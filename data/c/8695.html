To Be Perfect-Ten Nuns

Part 2

6. Maternal Attitude

Acting that must always be accompanied by maternal unction.

This maternal attitude in dealing with souls, which must be especially characteristic of you as superiors, is something John Paul II –in one way or another– always reminded the superiors of.

Thus, for example, in 1987 he told them that it was the primary responsibility of the superiors to assume the “daily maternal function of spiritual animation of consecrated souls. Here is the primary role of your service –he reminded them–: no one can replace you in the fulfillment of this mission, which invites you to be welcoming and full of affection toward the persons entrusted to you”1.

Your Directory of Consecrated Life is quite extensive on this subject (and it should be a subject of constant study for all, but especially for those who serve as superiors in the Institute) because it is what “defines the essence and the function of the woman religious in the Church”2. There we find expressions such as “to consume their lives serving”3, “they would never forget any [of their daughters], because their love is exceedingly authentic”4, and the constant invitation to find in the Most Blessed Virgin Mary their most perfect model of a spiritual mother5.

And “if she is truly a mother,” says Fr. Buela, “she has to do everything a mother does.” Which implies a whoooole bouquet of virtues and a long list of tasks. You know this better than I do!

I will be frank with you: I do not intend to give you a lesson on what a superior-mother should be, but I do want to tell you with John Paul II that this motherhood must be according to the example of the best of mothers: Most Holy Mary; who gave us the most perfect “example of that maternal love with which all those must be animated who, in the apostolic mission of the Church, cooperate in the regeneration of men”6, and which therefore makes these mothers “strong, gentle, intrepid, always sacrificed because they are consecrated, sometimes tired, and yet always generous”7, said the Pope.

Once, also speaking of spiritual motherhood, John Paul II quoted a writing of St. Edith Stein in which she details precisely what this mother's love consists in. It reads: “To give oneself to God, totally forgetful of self, not taking one's own individual life into account in order to leave room for the life of God: here is the deep motive, the beginning and the end of religious life. The more perfectly this is realized, the richer the divine life that fills the soul. But this divine life is love; overflowing love, which has no limits and which gives itself freely; love that bends mercifully toward every need; love that heals the sick and awakens to spiritual life what was dead; love that protects, defends, nourishes, teaches and forms; love that grieves with the grieving and rejoices with the one who is glad; that is ready for the service of each one in order to fulfill the design willed by the Father; in a word: the love of the divine Heart”8. That is the maternal love expected of you.

This love has nothing to do with the spirit of the “vinegary nun”9, as Fr. Buela calls her, or with the nun who in her dealings is “wrathful, impatient, angry, harsh, who talks much and vainly, who is a muddler and a schemer”10; nor is it the attitude of the superior who only pays attention to the nuns who smile at her and agree with everything she says. Her solicitude is for all the sisters under her care and, to tell the truth, for all the members of the Religious Family. That is why it is of capital importance to nourish supernatural charity and to know that this spiritual motherhood must be exercised as “an apostolic service”11. All the superiors, from the first to the last, should be an example in the exercise of this spiritual motherhood toward the sisters and toward the members of the IVE, whoever they may be: seminarians, chaplain, professor, novice… it does not matter; we are all members of the same family and you are the ‘mothers’.

Your Directory of Consecrated Life has a paragraph that ours does not have, but which I consider it fitting to quote here, since we are speaking of spiritual motherhood, which is ultimately an exercise of charity and requires a certain formation. A paragraph that should be kept in mind when selecting future superiors.

The text says the following: “A religious who does not order her affectivity, who seeks to be loved by others in a selfish way, who wants to be the center of attraction and, rather than serving, seeks her own comfort and to be served, who takes possession of her neighbors, not only seeking affection in a disordered way but wanting to exercise her dominion proudly –worse still if it is a matter of superiors–, who shows favoritism, surrounding herself with obsequious people even though they are evidently not the most suitable or the best, but only those who flatter and never contradict, who cannot bear the slightest slight or correction –even when she is right–, etc.,”12 cannot be a superior. It would be a disaster, “even if she fulfills perfectly –which will be quite rare– all the ‘material’ observances of religious life”13. And the same Directory says: “it would be a living lie.”

Watch out for that!: external observance does not necessarily imply capability for command, much less having the maternal spirit that must prevail in a superior.

The Proper Law defines a superior as one who has “the spirit of a mother, has patience in dealing with others and in bearing their defects, must be just in reproving and punishing faults, have confidence and also hope of their amendment, and above all must have the exquisite charity that seeks only the good of her subjects”14.

“Now, in order to obtain sisters adorned with these qualities, it is of great importance to have superiors of good judgment at the head of the Institute –says St. John Bosco–; superiors who thoroughly possess, and themselves practice, the virtues they must instill in the sisters. It is necessary –the saint goes on to say– that the superiors love all without distinction…; but to this patient and kind charity they must join a certain firmness of character which, when there is need, knows how to prevent abuses and the non-observance of the Constitutions without the least violence, but also without human respect”15.

I end this point with a short quotation from Don Orione speaking of the charity that must animate all religious-mothers: “Charity has nothing arbitrary, nothing hard about it; it finds its happiness in spreading and radiating around itself goodness, sweetness, kindness; one thing it desires: to immolate itself in order to bring about the happiness and salvation of others, for the glory of God”16.

Truly, it is my wish for all the Servants, present and future, that they exercise their spiritual motherhood in that spirit.

7. Exercise of authority

Intrinsically associated with this theme of spiritual motherhood comes the exercise of authority on the part of the superiors. Which is of the first importance, says John Paul II. And so he goes on to say: “The exercise of authority, with a spirit of service and love for all the sisters, is a vital subject, even though it is difficult and demands no little courage and dedication. The superior has the duty to help the religious so that she may fulfill her vocation ever more perfectly. The superior cannot evade this obligation, which is certainly arduous but indispensable.

The fulfillment of this duty demands constant prayer, reflection, consultation, but also courageous decisions, with awareness of one's own responsibility before God, before the Church and before the religious themselves who expect this service. Weakness, like authoritarianism, constitutes deviations equally harmful to the good of souls and to the proclamation of the Kingdom”17.

That is why the Proper Law defines the office of governing as a sacred office18, as a Cross19, and ultimately as a service20.

Our Founder has bequeathed to us by example and by word a very rich patrimony from which to learn the way authority is exercised in our Institute, and all the superiors of the SSVM must be fluent in the knowledge of this doctrine. “The system of government there must be,” says Fr. Buela, “is an evangelical system, proper to a family, where the superior, man or woman, is not a despot, is not a dictator, is not a sergeant. That is a mockery of the Gospel and of Jesus Christ. Yes, we have the religious vows, but according to the Gospel. We must not imitate formal, external types of obedience that are carried out in other spheres”21. “There does not exist in the Catholic Church a military-type system of government; that does not exist, it must not exist. We are the ‘family of God’, says St. Paul”22.

Hence the Proper Law affirms that the exercise of authority in our Institute “must be above all fraternal and spiritual, and must know how to involve the sisters in the decision-making process through dialogue; nevertheless, the last word belongs to authority, to whom it also belongs to see that the decisions taken are observed”23.

Regarding the exercise of authority as service, Fr. Buela goes on to say: “Those who have responsibilities in the community [such as those who are superiors] must flee the temptation to consider themselves ‘great’ and to make their authority felt. The first must make himself the last; the great, small; the leader, servant”24.

This means that one must “govern subjects as children of God; and those who are superiors must project onto their subjects the same charity with which God loves them as daughters”25; it means “showing veneration for the human person, specifically for their subjects, based on the dignity of every person”26 (because there may be superiors who claim this veneration only for themselves, and then instead of daughters they have a retinue that waits on them). Moreover, this office requires knowing how “to listen willingly” –without hurry, placing oneself within the other person's frame of thought27– and fundamentally “to decide and command what must be done”28. But very principally it also implies “promoting voluntary obedience, encouraging willingness in obedience, submissive freedom”29.

Therefore, all the superiors of the Institute of the Servants must be masters (pay attention to what I am telling you), “masters of Don Bosco's preventive system”, which is so well explained in the Directory of Oratories. Why? Because the oratorian spirit is the embodiment of our spirituality and therefore must be lived in all our houses, not only in the houses of formation, not only in the Hogarcitos, but in every community of sisters. There one finds concrete and excellent guidelines on how authority is exercised in our Religious Family. How can there be superiors who have never read it?

Look, there the superiors are taught “always to show themselves as mothers”, “to try to make themselves loved rather than feared”, “to foster trust between subjects and superiors”, “to show a liking for what the subjects like”, “to promote that family spirit by which ‘familiarity leads to affection and affection leads to trust’”, and so we could go on with a long list that would make life easier for all.

Few things bother subjects as much as the superior who is distant, haughty, who never lowers herself to them, who seems to govern ‘sitting behind a desk’ and never mingles with the tasks of her daughters. Do you want the sisters to talk with you, to open their hearts to you, to obey you promptly? The secret lies in familiarity and trust, which is not obtained except in closeness to the other person, in an attitude of service on your part –in fact and in deed– (genuine and not only in word). So if a sister asks to see you, you must immediately make arrangements for that to happen within the possible limits of time and space, never limiting yourselves to closeness by WhatsApp (not that it is wrong; I only say that one must not limit oneself to that). If the sisters perceive you as inaccessible, if to speak with you one has to ask for an appointment a week in advance (or more), if they write you an email and with luck you answer within a month, if the dialogue between you and the sisters is restricted ‘solely and exclusively’ to the canonical visitation, if you do not know how to make the others co-responsible by sharing with them what you know about the Institute or about the missions, if you never ask them whether they need something or how you can help them, if they speak with you and within a minute or two the rest of the community knows what you talked about, if they hear you criticize other religious (something that should not happen)… all that, sisters, uproots –I would say at the root– the trust and familiarity that ought to exist between you. And it sows such discredit with respect to authority that it can hardly be remedied some day, with the great harm this entails for religious life.

I know, and I am well aware, that it is not easy in our day to fulfill this mission of superiors. We have just said that it is a Cross. That is why, making the words of John Paul II my own, I encourage you not to abdicate your duty and the exercise of authority; to exercise it with a deep sense of the responsibility that falls upon you before God and before the sisters. With all understanding and fraternity, do not give up practicing, when necessary, patient correction, so that the life of the sisters may fulfill the purpose of religious consecration. These inescapable difficulties of your mission are part of your own vocational self-giving30.

Without realizing it, you are forming the future superiors, those who one day will govern the Institute. Do not fail to keep it in mind.

8. Formation

In the Conclusions of your last General Chapter are recorded the many advances that have been made with regard to the Formation of the sisters, and from the length of the treatment, the importance this has in the Institute becomes evident. One must continue in that direction. In all the areas and stages that a thorough formation embraces, according to the identity and mission of the Institute.

Now I simply wanted to mention to you just two little things St. John Paul II says on this subject:

  1. The formation of the formators.
  2. A formation oriented toward “biting into reality”.

Regarding the first, John Paul the Great affirmed: I hope that the institutes that receive numerous candidates (as is your case, by the grace of God) will prepare the formators who will accompany them with competence, patience and effectiveness throughout their initial and ongoing formation31.

About this effectiveness, it seems important to me to point out that it is imperative that those who serve as formators be fully imbued with the spirit of the Congregation. It is in this sense that the Proper Law says: “The formation of formators has a priority character32. [It is necessary that the] formators be capable of ‘forming young people of great spirit, who know how to transmit to the new generations of our Religious Family the charism that the Holy Spirit granted to the founder’33”34. And therefore all the formators must be united in one same spirit and tend to one same end35. Remember that the future of the Institute depends fundamentally on the formation given to the members in the times of preparation36. And in that we must know how to invest our best energies and resources.

Sometimes one falls into the error of choosing formators who are only pleasant to deal with, but who have great failings as regards knowledge of the spirit of the Institute (perhaps because they are very new, perhaps because they were not seasoned in an apostolic mission, perhaps because that is what they learned…). Other times they are versed in the Magisterium of the Church and in doctrine, but they too do not know the patrimony of the Institute thoroughly. Other times they are so harsh and distant that they make any learning unbearable, or they are so attached ‘to what is their own’ that they make assimilation difficult for the candidate who is of another culture.

I mean, I know it is not easy, but one must seek a balance between the human virtues required for such an office together with doctrinal formation and a thorough knowledge of the patrimony of the Institute.

Regarding the second, that is, formation oriented toward “biting into reality”, it is something key for us. And as you well know, it belongs to one of the non-negotiable elements attached to the charism and therefore must be emphasized in the formation given in our houses. In this sense, the first thing is to conform our life to that of the Incarnate Word. That is clear. But this formation also aims to form women capable of facing reality with a supernatural vision, in order to transform it according to the spirit of the Incarnate Word and according to the mode of the Incarnation, that is: assuming the cultures that must be evangelized37. And, faithful to the mystery of the Incarnate Word, to undertake evangelization without diluting faith into the rational, without turning the sacred into the profane, without falling into insubstantial spiritualities, without accepting any dualistic and Manichean view of reality, nor that which considers reality to have a one-dimensional character38.

Hence the importance we give to Thomistic formation, and how important it is that the sisters learn St. Thomas not as a catechism, but that they be taught to think. A formation that must not be restricted to the years of initial formation, but must extend even to ongoing formation –as I believe is already done in several provinces– and that enables them to give a positive response to the current problems from the point of view of the Gospel.

That is why our Spiritual Father pointed out the need for a serious and authentic updating on the part of the religious in the various doctrinal fields, overcoming the dangers of superficiality and emotionalism. It is necessary, therefore, to watch attentively over the various means of renewal and orientation (books, newspapers, magazines, courses of study, etc.), so as not to let oneself be confused by false ideas nor lead the persons one deals with along mistaken paths. Every family [and every person] desires the truth from one who is consecrated to God; be, then, faithful and happy to be able to proclaim it and bear witness to it39.

9. Vocational ministry

One cannot “be a perfect-ten nun” and not work for vocations. That is why another of St. John Paul II's requests to the major superiors refers precisely to vocational ministry.

“Governing is a cross; true power is vocations”40, says the Directory of Government.

How much is done at the level of the General Government and in the Provinces for vocational ministry? And when I say vocations I do not mean only vocations to consecrated life, but also priestly vocations.

I know that in many places they hold open-house days or come and see events, as the format is known in some places, by which young women are invited to spend one or several days at the convent and get to know a little more about the Institute. And that is very good! One must keep on doing it, and ever better and with greater quality. But that is not the only means. The Proper Law speaks of “reactivating an intense pastoral action… of an enthusiastic youth ministry [which] gives the Church the servants she needs”41. If Fr. Buela says that every parish should have a good oratory, analogously every convent should at least have a group of children, of young people, etc. –whenever possible, of course; if you work in a home for the elderly, the thing will be more difficult– but something can and must always be done for vocations.

I think I once told this in a circular letter, but I will tell it to you again now. I do not know if you know the story of Blessed Jordan of Saxony, a Dominican. Only three years after entering religious life he replaced his Founder and became Superior General of the whole Community. And he remained in this office for 15 years, until his death in 1237. During his term 249 houses of the Congregation were founded and six new provinces of religious were made. Now, think about it: you have to have people to send to all those houses. And the fact is that they say Jordan of Saxony had great influence among university students. One of his early biographers, a companion of his, says: “The religious houses where Father Jordan lived looked like beehives, because of the many young men who entered to become religious, and the many who went out from there to be superiors of other religious houses. That is why, on arriving at a convent, he would order many religious habits to be made, trusting that God would send him very numerous vocations, and so it happened to him everywhere.”

Father Jordan not only went to the universities to find very well-educated young men to become religious, but he also went to the countryside and to the working-class neighborhoods to invite healthy lads, even if they were unlettered, to enter the community. And this earned him the accusation that he was receiving people who were not going to bring glory to his Congregation.

I tell you this first because of the importance of the university apostolate as a source of vocations, but also to point out what the Conclusions of your Chapter in 2016 say, namely: “to be very careful not to place obstacles to vocations, being on the contrary very patient in giving the necessary time, bearing in mind that the selection of the candidate, although it must be serious, must nevertheless be progressive, not being too demanding at the beginning, as we have always been advised, seeking the middle ground: ‘the selection must be serious but one must also have mercy’ (cf. annex Conclusions Chapter 2010)”42.

Certainly there will be no lack of those who say that because you have many vocations ‘there is a lack of discernment there’ or that ‘it is not normal’, setting limits to Divine Providence and setting themselves up as counselors of God, who is the one who calls them. Do not let that discourage you from continuing to do all you can for vocations. Each of you must feel responsible for vocational ministry, and you must pass this on to the sisters when you return to your countries, proposing concrete initiatives, among which the promotion of the Spiritual Exercises cannot be lacking.

But also always remember that the “center of all vocational ministry” is prayer. In how many Provinces is the apostolate of the 40 Hours still not promoted as it should be? By this I do not mean that you do not pray for vocations, but that it is always a matter of doing more and better for the cause of Christ, using all the means within reach. Become promoters of the apostolate of the 40 Hours. And you yourselves, whenever you can, offer at least one of those 40 Hours for vocations in your Province.

John Paul II told the superiors: “Pray without ceasing for religious vocations: [so that] the ideal of consecrated life, an immense and gratuitous gift of God, may exercise an ever greater attraction on numerous young women oriented toward the highest and noblest achievements”43.

Finally, Fr. Buela also says: the great secret of vocational flourishing is the Cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, the only one who has words of eternal life (Jn 6:68). Which must not be lacking in our proclamation.

10. Model: Most Holy Mary

Lastly, and as it could not be otherwise, again and again the Holy Pontiff exhorted women religious to find in the Mother of the Incarnate Word the Ideal Model of what a consecrated woman should be. And so already in ’87 he told them: “In carrying out your apostolic mission, you have the model of the figure of Mary, … May you discover in her, ever more deeply, the model of your consecration to God and, at the same time, that of your apostolic commitment in the service of your brothers and sisters”44.

Note that John Paul II points to Mary as a model 1) of religious consecration and 2) of service.

Regarding Mary as a model of religious consecration, the Directory of Consecrated Life has a section devoted to the subject. There it is mentioned how Our Lady is the perfect model of consecration that all of us –and very especially you, mothers– must contemplate and imitate. Because the Mother of God is “the obedient Virgin”, “the contemplative woman”, “the missionary”, “the only one sensitive to the needs at Cana; the firm witness at the foot of the Cross; the center of unity who held together the newborn Church in its expectation of the Holy Spirit”45.

But Most Holy Mary is also pointed out to us as the Model (with a capital letter) of spousal love and of maternal love, so imperatively necessary for a woman religious. For to be a religious implies being a spouse of Christ, and if she is to be a spouse of Christ she must embrace the same life of poverty and virginity as Christ, which means also assuming Mary's kind of life46.

Our Mother in Heaven is truly the star that shines as a model of virtue before the whole community of the elect47 And with those virtues of the Mother, says the Holy Pope Paul VI, “the children will adorn themselves, who with steadfast purpose contemplate her example in order to reproduce it in their own lives”48.

What, then, are those virtues that must stand out with special splendor in women religious? Paul VI also mentions them in his apostolic exhortation Marialis Cultus: “faith and the docile acceptance of the word of God; generous obedience; simple humility; solicitous charity; reflective wisdom; piety toward God, prompt in the fulfillment of religious duties, grateful for the gifts received, which she offers in the temple, which prays in the apostolic community; fortitude in exile, in suffering; poverty borne with dignity and trust in the Lord; vigilant care for the Son, from the humility of the crib to the ignominy of the Cross; delicate forethought; virginal purity; strong and chaste spousal love49.

Nothing is more inconsistent with this Model than the religious (or the superior, if you like) who is not obedient, or rather who obeys “depending on who commands or what is commanded”50, or “who obeys amid murmuring and complaint, and the ‘spirit of opposition’, or simply as a display of the servility and pharisaical obedience that animates her51. Nothing is further from the Model of consecration that is Most Holy Mary than the religious/the superior who is neither humble nor simple and who often conducts herself with arrogance, or does everything to be seen, so that people may have ‘a good opinion’ of her (in our Argentine way we would say: ‘she does it to make sure of her post’). Those also contradict this magnificent Model of consecration who are not grateful but demand everything (or think that “everything is owed to them”), who, far from possessing that reflective wisdom the Pope mentions, are impulsive, govern and make decisions according to the mood they are in that day.

Have you noticed that the Most Blessed Virgin is also the Model of that vigilant care one must have for all the children? Not only for this one or that one ‘because later I need her to help me with something’, but for all, including the priests. And this to such a point that, in imitation of Most Holy Mary, women religious, and above all if they are superiors, must exercise that virtue which the Pope mentions almost at the end and which is delicate forethought (in English it is her delicate forethought). It means that she anticipates, foresees in advance what the needs of her son/daughter will be, which implies putting herself in his place and, with great forgetfulness of self, knowing how to arrange the means, anticipating with the timely word, and so… doing those strokes of genius that mothers do that make one say: “I was just thinking about how I was going to manage and …” “or that was exactly what I needed …”, etc. Because Our Lady's maternal love is “a concrete love, which is not limited to words of understanding, but commits itself personally to authentic assistance”52.

That is why Our Lady is also the Model of service “to the ignominy of the Cross”. The image of the Most Blessed Virgin at the foot of the Cross –as Fr. Buela explains– “must remind you not only of the why, but of the place, the function and the mission of the Servants”53.

“May the Virgin Mary help you to love, at the cost of any sacrifice, even to the point of heroism, as so many of your sisters have known how to do. May her presence be for each of you guidance and help.”

Never forget that our spirituality wishes to be especially marked, with special prominence54, by our devotion to the Mother of God. Always have recourse to her. Distinguish yourselves by your devotion to Our Lady. And encourage and sustain the vocations entrusted to you, after the example of Mary, by the generosity of her fiat.

Know that Our Lady is not only your model but also your Mother. And therefore a sure recourse in need, a source of strength in moments of difficulty and danger, and the greatest thing we have in this valley of tears.

* * *

In short, as I told you at the beginning, I believe that these 10 points, if they are maintained, if they are made life in each of you and in the jurisdictions in your charge, will be decisive in the life of the Institute. Much is at stake and it is very important that you remain thoroughly and genuinely firm in the principles that have guided the Institute from its origins. The key word here is fidelity: fidelity to God, fidelity to the charism and patrimony of the Institute, fidelity to the Church that has approved it; all of which is ultimately also fidelity to oneself.

These are times of trial, and blessed be God that it is so! “For it is fitting that we not lack the Cross, as our Beloved did not”55. And because the Cross is absolutely and definitively part of the program.

Did not the Founder tell you so in one of the first sermons preached to the Servants? “The Cross on which are the Lord and the Virgin of Matará constitutes a most beautiful program of life which those who take pride in bearing that name must strive to attain, with the grace of God, which is never lacking if one does what must be done. To project to the new generations the high ideals expressed in this Cross must be a renewed endeavor of each and every member of our Religious Family through the ages, so that the Gospel of Jesus Christ may resound in every corner of the earth: Go into all the world and preach the Gospel (Mk 16:15)”56.

One last anecdote: one day, after a clothing ceremony at which he had handed out many crucifixes, St. John Bosco gave a talk to the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians, among whom were many superiors and directresses. On that occasion he gave them some advice which I believe is very fitting for you too.

St. John Bosco told them: “let us all seek to carry, not the cross we would want, but the one the holy will of God assigns us [as may be the office you hold and in the present circumstances the Religious Family is going through]. Let each of us carry it cheerfully, thinking that as the years pass, so does the cross. […] Yes, daughters, let each of us carry our own cross with love and not lean it on others. Let each one say to herself: ‘Certainly I am a cross for others, just as others are often a cross for me; but I want to carry my cross by myself, and I must not be a cross for others’. […] This applies especially to you, teachers and directresses, who are directly occupied with the salvation of your neighbor. […] Perhaps you work a great deal and, in spite of everything, do not succeed in making others content. Always work for the glory of God, and always carry your cross well, because that is how it pleases the Lord. Certainly there will be thorns, but thorns that in time will be changed into flowers that will last for all eternity […] Do good, do good works. Toil, all of you work hard and always for the Lord with good will. Yes. Do not waste time. Do all the good you can. You will never regret it”57.

“Let each one, therefore, fulfill the office entrusted to him; but let him fulfill it with zeal, with humility and trust in God, and let him not lose heart if he must make some painful sacrifice. Let it serve him as consolation to think that his labors will redound to the benefit of that congregation to which we have consecrated ourselves”58.

When one thus contemplates at a glance these 10 points that John Paul II pointed out, and examines oneself personally and also how one is carrying out the sacred office entrusted to one, not infrequently one realizes that we are unprofitable servants; but we must never be discouraged, nor retreat, nor come down one point with respect to the ideals and holy interests of the Institute.

“Have confidence in Jesus even in your discouragements, even when your body or your spirit is reduced to powerlessness and refuses to help you. Say then: I do not rely on myself, my God, but on you, and in you I can do all things if you help me. I have nothing, and I can do almost nothing, but even so, I am going to begin. You will finish. –And, having said this, set to work; do the little you can, and God will take care of the rest. God is pleased to multiply difficulties; he halts and nails one down in powerlessness. One would like to, but one cannot. It ends in an agony […]. But when one takes a step praying and invoking, God comes, gives wings of love, and you will be amazed to see how you fly with freedom and grace. Come now! our Lord is yours; go to him with confidence. Exercise yourselves in confidence. Always trust in him and distrust yourselves: a grace I wish you for today and for always”59.

Finally, all that remains is for me to give you much encouragement and wish you, as always, all the best. May Our Lady always protect you and bless you abundantly.

AMDG
