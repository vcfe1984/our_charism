Perfect model of a consecrated woman

[Exordium] Since we are essentially Marian1, and since our Proper Law has indicated in several of its documents the principal and preponderant role that the Virgin has in our spirituality and missionary activity, we cannot fail to consider and go deeper into the example of our Most Holy Mother. In fact, our Proper Law presents her to us as the “perfect model of a consecrated woman, whom every religious must always contemplate and imitate”2. That is why today I would like to highlight some aspects, or rather attitudes, of our Mother which, although they challenge every Christian, are –as I see it– essential in a Servant.

1. Service

And the first attitude or quality that stands out in the Virgin is precisely that of service. Many times we have read in the Gospel that passage of the account of the Visitation that says: In those days, Mary set out for the hill country…3. The words of the Gospel account make us see with the eyes of the heart the young Virgin of Nazareth on her way to the town of Judah where her cousin lived, in order to offer her services. Analogously, each Servant sister, because she is a missionary –which is another essential aspect in us– is or will be sent to another town to offer her services. This implies availability, forgetfulness of self in order to serve the other, humility, zeal for souls… However, what strikes us above all in Mary is her attentiveness, full of tenderness, toward her elderly cousin. It is a concrete love, which is not limited to words of understanding but commits itself personally in authentic assistance. The Virgin does not simply give her cousin something that belongs to her; she gives herself, asking nothing in return. She has perfectly understood that the gift received from God, rather than a privilege, is a duty that commits her on behalf of others with the gratuitousness proper to love.

That is why the quality that must stand out in each Servant sister must be precisely an attitude of service. If a sister does not serve others, she is of no use. It is a contradiction in terms. Because what is proper to every servant is precisely to serve, with humility, with concrete acts of service toward the members of her own community, toward the poor who come every day to the door of the convent, toward the priests and seminarians –members of our own Family– and of course toward the souls that have been entrusted to you.

You must be convinced that you have consecrated your lives to the service of Jesus Christ, the Incarnate Word. That is not something abstract, a hazy idea, or something utopian; it becomes concrete “in humble service and generous self-giving, in the gratuitous gift of yourselves through a love to the extreme”4 in the service of your neighbor. And saying gratuitous gift means without expecting reward, without expecting recognition, without expecting consolations, without demands… It is, moreover, a constant service; it is not something sporadic, but, following the example of Most Holy Mary, it is a self-giving that fills everything and every day.

If a Servant says that she wants to be humble or thinks she is humble, but does not serve others, however many resolutions she makes and prayers she raises to heaven, she is beating around the bush. That is why, intrinsically associated with service, the virtue that must most distinguish the Servants is humility.

And from this follows a second quality, which is:

2. Joy

Every day at Vespers we pray the canticle of the Magnificat, which says: my soul proclaims the greatness of the Lord and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior, for he has looked upon the lowliness of his handmaid.

It is precisely from that availability for service, from knowing herself to be ‘handmaid of the Lord,’ that the joy of the Most Holy Virgin springs. Similarly, if each Servant is ready to proclaim her fiat to the will of God –without reluctance, without slowness, without reservations– then she will be able to proclaim her magnificat and live her consecration to the full.

The joy of the Virgin springs from knowing herself looked upon by God, in spite of her lowliness5; it springs from the ‘service’ she is able to render, thanks to the wonders that the Almighty works in her.

It is interesting to note that the Magisterium of the Church says that “woman cannot find herself except by giving love to others”6, and therefore she will not find her joy, her fulfillment, except by giving herself. Which I –in my limited understanding– take to mean giving everything that love asks of us, with total availability, immediately, unconditionally, asking nothing in return, with joy and wishing that even more be asked of us.

For this what is needed –among other things, and as I see it– is to ask for the grace of that maternal wisdom which instills an affection that foresees everything, so as to anticipate and satisfy the unspoken needs of our own; which carries charity down to the last detail; which makes of its hidden and silent service to its own a luminous and joyful sacrifice; which defends its own even with a glance; which knows how to spare them sorrow and toil; which receives with a strong heart the worries and annoyances that cloud their faces; which never keeps a tally of the things it has done for those it loves, because its happiness lies in giving itself “without measure, to the extreme, to exhaustion, to the end”7.

Notice that after the Magnificat there followed those three months of service to her cousin Elizabeth, about which the Gospel says nothing. Or perhaps it tells us the most important thing: the good makes no noise; the power of love is manifested in the serene discretion of daily service. And that is a great lesson for all of us, who so often seek offices and conspicuous apostolates that will win us human glory. The Virgin served in silence.

3. Intercession

Finally, a third quality that we should see replicated in each Servant is that of being an intercessor. We see it in the dialogue between the Virgin and the Incarnate Word at the wedding at Cana. She is sensitive to the needs of others8 and intercedes before Christ. She is the one who asks graces and favors from heaven for us. St. Louis Marie says that there is no grace of God that does not pass through the hands of Mary. That is why the Most Holy Virgin is the Woman who intercedes.

Likewise, each Servant must implore with her sacrifices and prayers favors from heaven for all souls, especially for the Institute, for the whole Religious Family and of course for all souls. Our Proper Law tells us so explicitly: “we must have recourse to her merciful intercession”9 and ask for all the graces we need to be faithful to our consecration10, as our formula of profession of vows says, a grace that must be asked not only for ourselves but for all the members of our Institutes.

In this sense, one of the books of the Servants says that “it is fitting for the Servants… to direct everyone toward heaven by the example of their lives and by their intercession”11; that is why you must pray for every grace for yourselves, for the Institute, for sinners… and especially now, when these are not easy times for the Church, is when we must ask the most. The intercession of Most Holy Mary does not fail. Because, as a good Mother, she is not distant from our concerns, our problems or our struggles; rather, with maternal solicitude she seeks to help us, protect us, defend us, give us what she knows is good for us to be happy in time and in eternity…

That is why I invite and encourage you to pray fervently for our own, for our own Religious Family, that it may always remain united and rooted in the precious charism that has been bequeathed to us. And when some cross comes upon you, some sacrifice you must make –God willing– be so kind as to offer it –following the example of our Heavenly Mother– for the good of our Family.

[Peroratio] In short, dear sisters, the exemplary holiness of the Virgin must always move us to raise our eyes toward her. That is why, in this Holy Mass, let us ask that the contemplation of the virtues of our Most Holy Mother –especially her readiness to serve, her joy and her dedication to intercessory prayer– may adorn our souls, and that we may reproduce them in concrete acts in daily life. For thus and only thus will we be behaving as true devoted children of the Virgin and fulfilling our purpose of being essentially Marian.

May the Virgin bless you.
