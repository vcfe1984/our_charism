Homily preached by Rev. Fr. Carlos M. Buela, I.V.E., at the Youth Mass on Sunday, October 15, Mother’s Day, and at the Holy Mass for the family, celebrated for the children of the «Isabel la Católica» School and for their parents, on October 20, 1995

We are gathered to celebrate the Holy Mass for the Family. A few days ago, on October 12, we recalled the example of that great woman of whom it can be said that she changed the course of history, a woman who stood out as a Christian, as a wife, as a mother and as a queen: Isabella the Catholic!

Today I want to speak about the one who is the soul of every family, the mother1.

A Hindu legend tells that when God wanted to create a mother, He thought long about how to make her… From the reed He took slenderness, from the grasses their gentle trembling, from the petal of the rose its enchanting velvety softness. From the rays of the sun He took joy, from fire its heartfelt warmth, from the clouds tears, and the confiding chatter of turtledoves. With all these exquisite things He formed the mother. That is why sorrow nests in her soul, for there is nothing more vulnerable than the breath of the rose, nor more timid than the trembling of the grass. Weakness, however, turns into strength in her. The mother, all sweetness, becomes fiercer than a hyena when she has to defend her children.

The mother is the guardian angel of our childhood! The soul trembles and the heart beats faster when we say the word: MOTHER!

Who does not feel tears well up in their eyes when thinking of the one who has never stopped loving them from the first instant of their life, the one who smiled -like a sun- over the cradle, the one who wept and grieved when her little child fell ill and had to suffer?

1. What is a mother?

Who can say what it means to be a mother? If we gathered together everything that has been written about her, the songs that speak of the mother in the home, the sighs that rise from the hearts of orphans in the cemetery… If we could see through to the torn soul of the mother speaking with her wayward son through the bars of a prison, if it were possible for us to count the hours a mother spends keeping watch beside the bed of her sick child, if we managed to call to mind the proud figure of the mother of the Gracchi, the tears of St. Monica, the loving heart of Blanche of Castile, the fortitude of the mother of the Maccabees, the incomparable spiritual stature of Isabella the Catholic, the piety of Mamma Margaret, or that of the modest woman who works day and night to support her family, and, above all, if we set before us the eminent figure of the Most Holy Virgin, Mother of God… we would have only a faint inkling of what a mother is!

Do you know what a mother is? She is the mirror of the soul who is always at our side! My mom is elderly now, she is 85, but she still cooks, washes the dishes, works… all with great joy. When I was in the Seminary, once, 25 years ago, she wrote me the following in a letter:

“Carlitos:

You write to us so that we may share your joys and emotions, but even if you had not explained your satisfactions to us, I am always at your side; this brought back to me the memory of a reading from when I was in 3rd grade, which stayed ‘in mente’: «My son, if you look at yourself in the mirror of your bedroom, you will see yourself as you are, with a smiling or a sad little face, and so… your dear little mother laughs if you laugh, cries if you cry… because she is the mirror of your soul…». That is what a mother is! The mirror of our soul! The one who is always at our side, beside us!

2. The most beautiful reality

A mother cannot be enclosed in intellectual categories. She is not to be understood, but loved.

Her kisses, the tears that spring from her eyes, her prayer, tell you what a mother is… Mother, dear mother!

How much these words say! A spellbinding power throbs in them. A sweet word that sounds like a lament, like the sound of a distant and mysterious bugle, it produces a joyful tingling in our heart, and when we pronounce it, our whole soul vibrates on our lips. Is there any creature to whom we are more united, in heart and soul, than to our mother? For nine months we lived beneath her heart! After God, the greatest grace and the greatest good we possess is our mother.

The word mother! speaks to us of admirable acts of perseverance, of goodness, of love, of sacrifice.

3. Her work

The mother gives us life, holds us close to her heart, looks at us with tenderness, teaches us to speak, to love, to laugh, to walk.

When we are little she feeds us with her own substance; she gives us bread when we are older. She takes care of us, protects us, warms us with the warmth of her own heart. Her love has no boundaries. She can generously give up her own interests and suffer with a smile on her lips because she loves; she is capable of renouncing, out of love for her child, all the grandeurs of the earth. She devotes her life to struggling for her children and rejoices to see them happy.

A good mother is a blessing from God and the supreme consolation that has been given us in this valley of tears. She shows us the way when we go astray. She lifts us up when we fall. She encourages us when we are downcast. She sweetens life on earth and, as if by magic, turns thorns into roses. God transmits to the world the consolation of love through the soul of the mother. As a poet said:

A mother’s hands
carved out of oak
lined with silk
that suffer joyfully
that pray in silence.

That work so much
that caress so gently
that educate so firmly
that rest so little.

A mother’s hands
that live loving
that sweetly, so sweetly
die forgiving…
I kiss them, I kiss them
a thousand times, praying;
blessed forever
be the hands of mothers.

The mother comes from God and is one of the most adorable and moving divine revelations. We cannot help being moved when we consider this marvel of God, and praising God for having placed His love in the heart of the mother.

Throughout history we can see the endless series of mothers who pass the torch of life from hand to hand, a torch that will one day return to the bosom of God, from whence it first came forth.

Dear brothers and sisters:

* Those who no longer have their mother on earth, remember that she is nevertheless closer than ever to her children. Remember also that there are duties to fulfill: to pray for her -especially the Holy Mass-, to visit her grave, to fulfill what was promised…

* Those who still have her alive, remember your duties toward her:

– to love her: not only in words, but in deeds, knowing how to show her that you love her!

– to respect and honor her, before your own and before strangers. In the Sacred Scriptures we are warned: «Oh, how infamous is he who forsakes his father! And how accursed by God is he who exasperates his mother!» (Sir 3:18); «If one curses his father and his mother, his lamp will go out in deep darkness» (Prov 20:20); «The eyes that mock the father and do not look with respect on the mother, may the ravens of the brook pluck them out and the young eagles eat them!» (Prov 30:17). This severe language should not surprise us. Parents are God’s vicars for their children.

-That is why parents must be obeyed «in the Lord» (Eph 6:1), and helped in their spiritual needs: company, conversation, prayers…, and also in their material needs: errands, household chores.

On Mother’s Day all children give her a little gift. But always, every day, we must give her a great gift, the best of all: to study, to work, to behave well, to be obedient, responsible, to listen to her, in a word, to be good. This is what our mother wants from us!

That is why the poet sang well of the mother, saying:

A mother who is light is life itself!
She is the only love that never ends,
that within the heart, like an essence
that purifies, flows out spreading.

When grief casts down every belief
this belief is never destroyed;
and it stays clinging so to our souls
that it seems the ivy of life.

Everywhere, always the same, it comes with me,
like a heavenly, unknown harmony;
my heart has your name engraved upon it
and so too does my imagination.

It will be the last echo to sound
on my dying lips, mother of mine!
And in my tomb it will be a reliquary
that will guard my gravestone and my shroud!2.
