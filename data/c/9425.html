The reality of the Incarnation

Recalling the thought of our Founder
During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the second day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in the mystery of the Incarnation, we call to mind Fr. Buela’s thought on the subject, taken from his book “El Arte del Padre.”

The reality of the Incarnation

The fact of the Incarnation of the Word is known through God’s revelation, and to recognize it is a grace of God: «flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father who is in heaven» (Mt 16:17). «The first and fundamental mystery of Jesus Christ [is] the mystery of the Incarnation of the Son of God». And «God was never so close to man –and man was never so close to God– as precisely at that moment: At the instant of the mystery of the Incarnation!».

It is a supernatural mystery «quoad substantiam» which, even after being revealed –given the limitation of our intellect– leaves its intrinsic elements in a zone beyond the horizon of mere human reason.

God teaches the Incarnation of His Son in various passages of Sacred Scripture. We will recall some:

– The angel Gabriel announces to Joseph that: «what has been begotten in her is of the Holy Spirit» (Mt 1:20).
– The angel reveals to the Blessed Virgin: «you will conceive in your womb and bear a son» (Lk 1:31).
– St. John expresses it in a concise and sublime way: «And the Word became flesh» (Jn 1:14).
– The apostle St. Paul does so by saying simply, with enormous force and richness of content, that Christ Jesus «emptied Himself» (Phil 2:7).
– In Heb 1:6: «when He brings His Firstborn into», about which St. Thomas explains that «he calls the Incarnation an introduction into the world».

Tradition confessed this august mystery under various names: economy (as a disposition or ordering of God), susception, assumption, inhumanation, incorporation, convention, exinanition, union, self-emptying, incarnation (sarkosis, of clearly biblical origin, which prevailed in the Creeds: «He emptied Himself», or «He became incarnate»).

We consider that the eloquence of these texts is such, and of such impact, that they are more than enough for our purpose: Sacred Scripture and Tradition teach, clearly and categorically, the Incarnation of the Word, and the Magisterium of the Church has always confessed it as an immutable dogma of faith. One cannot fail to recall how St. Peter of Alcantara would be enraptured when asking himself: «That God became incarnate? That God became man? That God came to take flesh? That God took human flesh?…» and he would enter into the sweetest ecstasies. Not even the angels themselves know some things concerning the mystery of the Incarnation.

In addition, there are the things Jesus took together with human nature, which relate fundamentally to four realities: grace (S. Th., III, q. 7-8), truth (III, q. 9-12), power (III, q. 13) and imperfections (III, q. 14-15), that is, the perfections and the defects assumed. Perfections assumed in the soul: a) in its very essence; b) in its intellect; and c) in its will; and d) the imperfections of body and soul, which do not imply any moral defect.

[…]

Today people do not want to accept the truth and the reality of the Incarnation: – the action of the Holy Spirit; – the divine maternity; – the hypostatic union; – perpetual virginity. These are all things that speak of God’s lordship over men. But many today do not even want to hear about these things. That is how things go for men. St. John Paul II recalled it: «Man, from the beginning, is tempted to want to be like God (cf. Gen 3:5)… but without God! Without the mystery of the Incarnation. Without the night of Bethlehem».

I believe that on this point the ultimate reason for this rejection is the hatred some progressives have for what is genuinely Catholic. It is not a misunderstanding, it is not a passing distraction, it is not an unconscious oversight; there is a theological reason in their persecution. Hence:

– We are the backward ones; they the forward ones.
– We the retrograde; they the advanced.
– We the ones of the past; they the ones of the future.
– We the conservatives; they the progressives.
– We the medieval ones; they the ones of the coming century.
– We the closed; they the open.
– We the obscurantists; they the enlightened.
– We the cave-dwellers; they in spaceships, but they only repeat very old errors.
– Everything negative is ours; everything positive is theirs.
– They the good; we the bad.
– No vocations in diocese ‘X’? It is our fault.
– Priests apostatize? It is our fault.
– The sects advance? It is our fault.
– Fewer and fewer people at their Masses? It is our fault.

We are the mangy sheep to blame for everything.

«But fear not, little flock: it has pleased the Father to give you the Kingdom!» (Lk 12:32).

For all this, and in order to be faithful to God today and always, we must renew ourselves in the charism of our religious family. In a particular way in the face of the enormous graces of the Holy Spirit which are the new foundations, the new conversions, the new vocations and the new missions.

First, by renewing in ourselves the urgency of the witness to the Incarnation of the Word: «We must bear witness that the message of the mystery of the Incarnation retains all its vigor, which wants all men to be children of God and in solidarity with the lot of their brothers». «… the Church, willed and founded by Jesus on Peter, the Apostles and their successors, has as its end the proclamation of the incarnation of the divine Word, who came into the world to reveal the truth and to save all men».

Second, by remembering that living this truth must be palpable in our lives: «we are in the world, but we are not of the world» (Jn 15:18-19).

Trying not to fall into the falsehood of those who:
– are not in it but are of it: «angelism» (they are not in reality).
– are in it and are of it: «temporalism» (they have nothing to give the world).
– are neither in it nor of it: «fence-sitters» («because you are neither cold nor hot…»; Rev 3:16).
– are not, but pretend to be: religious in disguise.

Mary is the living emblem of what the Christian must be, the synthesis of two humanly irreconcilable grandeurs: motherhood and virginity: she is the certificate of the truth of the Incarnation. [We sing to her, renewing the intention of this day of the novena]
