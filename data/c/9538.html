[Exordium] In our Directory of Spirituality we read: “We could say that our spirituality must be that of the hymn of the Kenosis”1, referring to the text of Philippians 2:6-7: He, who was of divine condition, did not consider this equality with God something to be jealously guarded: on the contrary, He emptied Himself, taking the condition of a servant and becoming like men. And in an even more challenging way we read in another place of our Proper Law that what is properly ours is “to imitate Christ in the self-emptying of His Incarnation”2.

Perhaps that is also what makes our point of focus, as members of this Religious Family, the text of the kenosis, given that we are called to replicate in our lives the example of the Incarnate Word and to prolong the Incarnation3.

1. The self-emptying of the Word

There is no way to weigh justly what the self-emptying of Christ means. Some used the example of toads to explain the self-emptying of the Word in the Incarnation4. Others, like Fulton Sheen, use that of dogs. The idea is the same. Sheen says5, after narrating a whole hypothetical case in which we had to lower ourselves to live like dogs in order to save them, that the humiliation is twofold. First, in assuming the body of a dog, it is as if that man had decided to confine himself to the limits of a canine body. He could speak, but he only barks. He could reason and count the stars, but he is only guided by instinct and would have his snout fixed to the ground. The second humiliation would be that this man would have to spend the rest of his life with dogs, knowing that he is a million times better than the dogs. He would always have to run with them. The futility of his life among dogs would be incredibly degrading. And in the end, the dogs would turn against you and tear you to pieces. And one would think that the humility of such a man is very great, because of his spirit of poverty, of sacrifice, of service, of love for dogs, by the fact of having become canine.

“However, says one author, that self-emptying would be within the order of creatures, within finitude; it would be the passage from a higher creature to a lower creature. This is not, even remotely, the case of the Incarnation, in which the self-emptying is that of an entire infinite God who assumes a finite nature. This is abysmal. It is an infinite self-emptying and an infinite abasement. One is not in the same order as in the hypothesis we were speaking of; rather, in this reality there is a lowering from the order of the Creator to the order of the creature. Therefore, abysmal too is the example of the virtues that Jesus Christ teaches us by His Incarnation: abysmal is His humility, His poverty, His obedience, His sacrifice, His patience, His sorrow, His service and His love for man. It makes one shudder to think of it! From transcendent to immanent, from powerful to weak, from majestic to humble, from immortal to mortal, from infinite to finite, from most pure Spirit to material flesh, from eternal to temporal, from impassible to passible, from immense to small, from unlimited to limited, from all-powerful to servant, from rich to poor, from Lord to slave, from King to subject, ‘from eternal life to temporal death’6. And all that, without ceasing to be God! It is an abysmal self-emptying!”7.

God became a servant and lived with servant men (slaves).

2. Implications

Now then, notice that we have been called to be other Christs8, to imitate −as we said before− Christ in the self-emptying of His Incarnation”9.

Therefore, it is fitting to ask: What are the implications of the kenosis of the Son of God for us who are called to be priest-victims, or priests of the Incarnate Word? Fulton Sheen answered

– As priest, it will imply the emptying of oneself in order to be filled with holiness;

– As victim, it will imply that one empties oneself in the service of others.

It is the challenge of our vocation. This kenosis in a priest of the Incarnate Word is manifested specifically in two resolutions:

  • Humility: because the measure of the increase of the Spirit of Christ in the soul is in direct relation to the decrease of self-love, of one’s own judgment, of attachments, etc.10
  • The other is the emptying of oneself, which also comes in direct proportion to compassion toward others. The less emphasis there is on the ego, the more care and the more attention there is toward one’s neighbor.

Our Founder says this same thing in these words: “The priest, like Christ… is a man who possesses nothing, because he possesses Him who is everything and is possessed by Him who emptied Himself, who made Himself nothing”11.

The more the religious or the priest empties himself of his own plans in order to carry out God’s plan, of his comfort in order to sacrifice himself for Christ, when he empties himself of that excessive desire to be appreciated, to be praised, to be taken into account, to be consulted, to be preferred to others, he ceases to be bound hand and foot by anxiety. As the humiliation of Christ becomes his model, then even the very sufferings he endures become part of the interior joy and of the service they offer outwardly.

3. Look at Him also made man12

Our Constitutions have in number 7 that marvelous paragraph that we know almost by heart: “We want to found ourselves on Jesus Christ, who has come in the flesh (1 Jn 4:2), and on Christ alone, and Christ always, and Christ in everything, and Christ in everyone, and Christ All.” Those very brief lines of our Proper Law, which so explicitly refer to the mystery of the Incarnation, have an immense depth, and I think they are of great fruit for our spiritual and priestly life. It is clear that it is to Him that we must look, it is to Him that we must return again and again, and to Him alone.

St. John of the Cross has a chapter in his book of the Ascent in which he speaks precisely of this, and it is where he speaks of Christ as the All of the heavenly Father.

Thus the saint says that “in giving us [the Father], as He gave us His Son, who is His one Word −and He has no other−, He spoke everything to us together and at once in this single Word, and He has nothing more to say”13.

“What God formerly spoke to our fathers through the prophets in many ways and in many manners, now at last, in these days, He has spoken to us in the Son all at once. By which the Apostle gives us to understand that God has become as it were mute and has nothing more to say, because what He used to speak in parts to the prophets He has now spoken in its entirety, giving us the All, who is His Son”14.

Christ is the definitive Word, the one and best Word of the Father. A present, a gift that has been given to us. This being so, says the Mystical Doctor, “anyone who would now wish to question God, or desire some vision or revelation, would not only be acting foolishly, but would be offending God, by not fixing his eyes entirely on Christ, without wanting any other thing or novelty”15. With which he is already telling us that precisely what must be done is to fix our eyes firmly on the Incarnate Word and realize the Treasure we have in Him.

Then he places on the lips of the Heavenly Father the following: “If I have already spoken all things to you in my Word, who is my Son, and I have no other, what can I now answer or reveal to you that would be more than that? Fix your eyes on Him alone, because in Him I have said and revealed everything to you, and you will find in Him even more than what you ask for and desire”16.

This should be applied to everything in our life, whatever the circumstance in which we may find ourselves, whatever our anxiety and whatever our uncertainty or need; to all He answers: “He is all my speech and answer, and He is all my vision and all my revelation. Which I have already spoken, answered, manifested and revealed to you, giving Him to you as brother, companion and master, price and reward”17.

I cannot extend myself much on this now, but we can mention how healthy it is for the soul to think of the Incarnate Word as our brother, as our companion, as our master, as our price and as our reward… And the Heavenly Father goes on to say: “If [still] you would wish me to answer you some word of consolation, look at my Son, subject to me and bound by my love, and afflicted, and you will see how many He answers you. […] look at Him also made man, and you will find in that more than you think […] [Because] in Christ dwells bodily all the fullness of divinity”18.

Look at Him also made man, look at Him emptied, look at Him suffering the same pains as, and even greater than, those you are going through, look at Him forgotten, look at His Heart saturated with ingratitude, look at Him with arms outstretched to embrace you… look at Him also made man, because in Him dwells the fullness of divinity, that is, all rest, all goodness, all sweetness, everything, and the only thing that gives true satiety to the soul, even if everything else is lacking.

[Peroratio] Each one of us, by being a member of the Institute and by being a priest, is called to “prolong the Incarnation.” That is our specific end19, our apostolate20, our charism21, our vocation.

For that, we must first prolong the Incarnation in ourselves. The Virgin Mother gave God a human nature through which He taught, governed and sanctified. Each time we genuinely renew our consecration to Jesus Christ (through Our Lady, as is also “what is ours”), we give Christ our human nature so that He may analogously use our priesthood to continue His teaching, His governing, His sanctifying work.

I conclude once more, so that it may be engraved on our souls: what is properly ours is “to imitate Christ in the self-emptying of His Incarnation.” It is a great grace, almost unsurpassable if we see who we are, and one that we must always implore from our Lord; that is why we can conclude by asking for this grace by means of “the prayer of the soul in love” of the mystical doctor of Fontiveros.

Lord God, my beloved! If You still remember my sins, so as not to do what I keep asking of You,
do Your will in them, my God, for that is what I most desire,
and exercise Your goodness and mercy, and You will be known in them.
And if You are waiting for my works, so as by that means to grant my request,
give them to me Yourself and work them in me, and the pains You would wish to accept, and let it be done.
And if You are not waiting for my works, what are You waiting for, my most clement Lord? Why do You delay?
For if, after all, it must be grace and mercy that I ask of You in Your Son,
take my little mite, since You want it, and give me this good, since You also want it.Who will be able to free himself from base ways and limits
if You do not lift him up to Yourself in purity of love, my God?
How will man, begotten and raised in lowliness, rise up to You,
if You do not lift him up, Lord, with the hand that made him?
You will not take from me, my God, what You once gave me in Your only Son Jesus Christ,
in whom You gave me all that I desire.
Therefore I will rejoice that You will not delay if I hope.

Let us remember what our Proper Law says: other congregations will shine for their renown, for their great influence, for the magnificence of their works. We only want –and this has always been our declared intention– to stand out by imitation in the kenosis of the Incarnate Word22, by configuring ourselves to His style23, by passing through this world doing good24 and bearing witness to the Truth25. That is why our passion must be “to ‘assume’ cultures, purify them and elevate them on the basis of Christ and His Gospel, understood ‘in the Church’26”27. This is and this has always been our intention, our goal. Even when they contradict us or persecute us, even in the midst of the harshest poverty and in spite of our great miseries. Because our faith and our trust are fixed on Him who said: Whoever acknowledges me before men, I too will acknowledge before my heavenly Father28; yes, in the world you will have tribulations, but do not fear: I have overcome the world29 I will be with you always, until the end of the world30.

May God our Lord and His Most Holy Mother grant us the grace always to have before our eyes the mystery of the Incarnate Word that we celebrate today. May it be so.
