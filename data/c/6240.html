Exaltation of the Holy Cross

Homily on the occasion of the 50th anniversary of the ‘discovery’ of the Cross of Matará

[Exordium] Dear Mother and Sisters: The solemnity we celebrate today has an altogether particular character for all Christians and very especially for the Servants. It is the mystery of “the cross of Christ the Bridegroom”1, as St. John of the Cross calls it.

And, incidentally, while I was preparing this sermon, I was pleased to see that six times the Monastic Rule of the Servants calls Christ: Bridegroom. For each contemplative nun enters monastic life precisely “to share the solitude of [her] Bridegroom, who through the Eternal Spirit offered himself to the Father”2 on the Cross.

Therefore, if the Cross is coextensive with the Christian life, it is all the more so for a bride of Christ, since the Cross is the “spirit” of one who consecrates herself to God, and that is also her path: that of the Cross, that is, of self-denial. “Whoever takes any account of himself neither denies himself nor follows Christ”3, wrote St. John of the Cross with his usual radicality.

This is one of the causes, if not the principal one, why today so many Christians and religious live deceived [examples] and think “that any manner of withdrawal and reformation in things is enough; and others [who] are content with practicing the virtues in some manner and continuing in prayer and pursuing mortification, but do not arrive at the nakedness and poverty, or detachment or spiritual purity”4; “and they think that this and that will be enough for them to come to union”5. To these the Mystical Doctor gave warning, saying that this “is beating about the bush”6, it is “fleeing from imitating Christ”7, and therefore they will not make progress in perfection. Ours, what is truly Christian, is this: to walk the way of the Cross of Christ the Bridegroom8.

Today, moreover, this solemnity shines with a special light because it marks 50 years since the ‘discovery’ –so to speak– of the Cross of Matará, and 40 years since the Argentine Episcopal Conference reproduced the “carved Cross of Matará” on its cover. I say ‘discovery’ to refer to the finding, by the researchers Martínez Moreno, of the Cross of Matará, which was in the possession of the Sosa Ruiz family in Santiago del Estero9, and which allowed the world to come to know the Cross of Matará.

There is a part of the Cross of Matará to which perhaps we rarely pay attention. It is the figure at the base of the Cross of Matará, a figure that researchers think “could be an indigenous chief, or cacique”10.

Speaking of this figure, Martínez Moreno says in his work Cruces Catequísticas de Santiago del Estero that “its position in an attitude of supplication, with the arms crossed over the breast, and at the base or beginning of the work [that is, at the base of the cross], would indicate the following: The cacique implores the intercession of Most Holy Mary… through the martyrdom of Christ”11 for his people, which is what is centrally represented on the Cross.

That is why in that supplicant figure, in a certain way, I seem to see represented the mission of the contemplative sisters and religious of our Religious Family, and therefore your mission. Because you, too, are at the base, at the beginning, in the vanguard of the work of evangelization of the Institute. And because you too have been called to intercede before our heavenly Mother for all souls, but especially for the members of our Religious Family12. This is how we must always be, like the cacique, at the feet of Jesus and of the Most Holy Virgin of Sorrows, very close to the cross! That is our vocation!

This is what our Proper Law says, quoting St. Teresa: “For this [to implore Mary’s intercession before the throne of God] Christ has gathered you here; this is your vocation and your desire, this is the reason for your tears and your prayers… The day your prayers, disciplines, longings and fasts are not devoted to this which I have told you, you will not attain –know it well– the end for which the Lord has gathered us here”13.

This is supremely important! Crucial! Because to the prayers and holiness of life of each one of you our Religious Family is entrusted in a particular way, with all its patrimony, with all that magnificent spirit our Founder has bequeathed to us, with all its missions…

And since the purpose of the contemplative life is to plead for our Family before the merciful Heart of the Mother of God, it is your responsibility, and it is the noble office God has assigned you, to contribute by your holocaust to keeping our Religious Family unscathed and to gladden it with the fruits of a loving faith. That is no small task! It is no trifling vocation!

That is the mission God has been pleased to grant you, as to the cacique on the Cross of Matará, and one must be faithful. And as with his, so too in us, the authenticity of prayer is verified when it is closely united to the cross of Christ and to the Virgin of Sorrows at the foot of the cross.

You all know well that witness means martyr, and the Church is faithful in martyrdom, in witness. I tell you this because when the mystery of the Cross touches our lives, with its nights and its aridities, to the point that the soul “comes to believe that all its blessings are ended forever”14, you must feel yourselves privileged missionaries of our Lord.

Particularly when fidelity to one’s vocation becomes difficult or becomes a Cross, and therefore prayer itself is crucified. We know well that God has placed great power in the joined hands of those who suffer. We see it in the very mystery of Christ that we contemplate today: Christ annihilated on the Cross, “annihilated in his soul without any consolation or relief”15. But in this moment “of the greatest abandonment,” “he accomplished the greatest work of his whole life,” which was “to reconcile and unite the human race with God through grace”16. Analogously, the contemplative nun, in union with Christ Crucified, when she denies herself, when it falls to her to suffer something, by her prayers and her sacrifices, can do more for the Church, for the mission of our Institute, for all souls, than others who carry out missions and tasks of great importance.

This is how the mystery of the Cross that we contemplate today takes shape, becomes reality in our lives.

St. John of the Cross, in his writings, identifies the “cross” and “love.” And both with the following of Jesus. Love is denial of self, inasmuch as it is the affirmation of the other as the reason and center of one’s own life. And this denial, “in which the pure spiritual cross consists”17, “must be a death and annihilation”18. That is why, paraphrasing two texts of the Gospel of St. John, he will say that “whoever wishes to possess something or seek it for himself, that one will lose it [his life, his soul].” Whereas “whoever renounces for Christ all that he can desire and enjoy, choosing what most resembles the cross… that one will gain it”19.

“Knowing how to lose one’s life means acting in communion with the whole community, through sincere obedience and mutual solidarity. It means adhering to Christ with an undivided spirit in the offering to Him of one’s body and soul; it also means placing greater trust in poverty than in riches, without concerning ourselves with anything other than serving God and others”20.

And sometimes, when we see that several years of religious life have already gone by and we still seem to be just beginning, we ask ourselves: will I be able to do that? We do not know.

The love and the science of the Cross is an enormous grace, but above all ‘it is a grace’ that we must ask of Jesus insistently, convinced that it is the greatest of graces and the one that in some way brings about the perfection of our vocation, even when it seems totally to surpass our strength.

It is then that, with all humility and trust, we can repeat the Prayer of the Enamored Soul with our arms crossed over our breast like the cacique of the Cross of Matará, and say:

“Lord God, my beloved! If you still remember my sins, and for that reason do not do what I keep asking of you, do your will in them, my God, for that is what I most desire, and exercise your goodness and mercy, and you will be known in them. And if you are waiting for my works in order by that means to grant my request, give them to me yourself and work them in me, and the sufferings you would be willing to accept, and let it be done. And if you are not waiting for my works, what are you waiting for, my most merciful Lord? Why do you delay? For if, after all, it must be grace and mercy that I ask of you in your Son, take my little copper coin21, since you want it, and give me this good, since you also want it.

Who can free himself from lowly ways and limits if you do not lift him up to yourself in purity of love, my God?

How will man, begotten and reared in lowliness, rise up to you, if you do not raise him, Lord, with the hand that made him?

You will not take from me, my God, what you once gave me in your only Son Jesus Christ, in whom you gave me all that I desire. Therefore I will rejoice that you will not delay if I wait.”

[Peroratio] The Cross on which the Lord and the Virgin of Matará appear constitutes a most beautiful program of life which we must strive to attain, with the grace of God, which is never lacking if one does what must be done22, as our Founder wrote. Likewise, it is the mission of each Servant to project to the new generations the lofty ideals expressed in this Cross, so that throughout the length and breadth of this world the Gospel of Jesus Christ may resound: I have overcome the world23.

May the Most Holy Virgin, the Madonna delle Grazie, always console us in our daily labors and nourish our hopes.

To conclude, it may be useful to recall one of the counsels that the Master of Fontiveros gave to Mother Ana de Jesús: “Meanwhile, now… occupy yourself in exercising the virtues of mortification and patience, desiring to become in suffering somewhat like this great God of ours, humbled and crucified; for this life, if it is not for imitating him, is not good”24.
