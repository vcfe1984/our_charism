Reflection of the Founder on the occasion of the feast of St. Louis Marie

The Lord says: “Whoever loves me will be faithful…” (Jn 14:23)
One of the forms of lack of fidelity into which people very often fall is the one proper to the “chanta.” The “chantas” are among those chiefly responsible for the mediocrity of communities. And in some, “chantism” is an attitude of life.

We will look at: First: What is it?
Second: Its remedies.

* * *

1. What is it?

Popular speech calls certain people “chantapufi,” which comes from the Genoese “ciantapufi” = one who does not pay his debts or keep his promises. The shortened form chanta is common with the same meaning, as are the augmentative chantún and the superlative chantumazo.

He is a mixture of show-off, conceited fellow and braggart.
The show-off is the one who pretends to be different from what he is.
The conceited fellow is the one fond of giving himself importance.
The braggart is the one who makes a manifest display of what he is not.
The chanta is a combination of all of them, identical in their essential inauthenticity.
His essence: he seems, but he is not.
He is concerned with what people will say; that is why he needs an audience to look at him and admire him, and before which he acts according to his own poses.

There have been famous ones:

– Tartarin of Tarascon… imagines himself to be what he is not.
– The master of Lazarillo de Tormes… appears before others in a way that does not correspond to reality.
… he is the one who nods with a knowing look without having the slightest idea.
… he is the one who makes impossible or simply fantastical plans.
… if he is a cook, he over-salts the food on several occasions.
… when it is time to work, they always find an excuse.
… during study time he gets up for any reason at all.
… at adoration, he always leaves for physiological needs.
… he commits himself to something, and then disappears…
… intellectually, he talks a lot and says nothing.
– He carries around under his arm every book he finds.
– He never finishes reading any of them.
– He does not go deeply into any subject.
– He is full of plans, to write in the future.
– He is never on time to hand in the monthly paper on the set day, nor does he make it to the Thomistic Conferences. He always asks for an extension.

He acts as if he were interested in a subject, but the only thing that interests him is to appear and be noticed.

… he improvises everything and expects to “pull rabbits out of the hat” – e.g., there is half an hour left before the triduum and only then does he remember the prayers… he has to lead the singing… and he chooses the songs on the fly…

2. Remedies

Reject appearances.

Show ourselves as we are, with simplicity, without hypocrisy, but without the impertinent brashness that confuses authenticity with spontaneity… killing can be spontaneous…

Be humble, acknowledge our limitations.

Be “faithful” to the Word of Christ, to the Will of Christ.

* * *

Dear brothers, in one way or another we are all more or less chantas. All of us… except the saints.

We are chantas to the extent of our own inauthenticity.

Let us imitate St. Louis Marie.
In him there was nothing of the chanta.
That is why they persecuted him.
That is why they hated him.
That is why he was a saint.
Let us ask him to obtain for us the grace of authenticity.
