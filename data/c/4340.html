Eucharistic Profession of Faith

Public manifestation of faith in the august sacrament of the altar

Feast of Corpus Christi, 1992

Dear Brothers:

On this Solemnity of the Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ we wish to manifest, as a community and publicly, the faith of the Catholic, Apostolic and Roman Church concerning the august sacrament of the altar.

Institution

1. How do you know that the Eucharist is a sacrament?

Because the Magisterium of the Church / solemnly declares it so in her Councils.

2. In which Councils?

In that of Florence: / “The third is the sacrament of the Eucharist, / the matter of which is wheat bread / and wine of the grape…” (Dz 698); / and, moreover, among others, / the Council of Trent: / “If anyone says / that the sacraments of the New Law… / are more or fewer than seven, / namely: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist /…, let him be anathema” (Dz 844).

3. Who instituted the sacrament of the Eucharist directly, and where?

Our Lord Jesus Christ instituted it at the Last Supper; / the Council of Trent declares: / “All our forefathers /…expressly professed that our Redeemer / instituted this so wonderful sacrament at the Last Supper” (Dz 874)

4. What are the sources in which the Church finds this doctrine?

These sources are three: / first, the three Synoptic Gospels; / second, the Gospel of St. John; / and third, the Apostle St. Paul.

5. What do the Synoptic Gospels teach?

St. Matthew (26:26-29), / St. Mark (14:22-25) / and St. Luke (22:15-20) / unanimously teach three things: /

– First, that Jesus Christ instituted the Most Holy Eucharist / by giving his body and his blood as food and drink, / thus replacing the meal of the paschal lamb. /

– Second, that he commanded his supper to be repeated: / “Do this in memory of me” /

– Third, that he commanded that the Eucharist be eaten and drunk: / “…eat… this is my Body; …drink… this is my Blood”

6. What does St. John (6:55-59) remind us of?

He reminds us of the teaching of Jesus: /

– First. That the celebration of the Eucharist is to be repeated, / because no one will have life / without eating the Body of Christ and drinking his Blood; /

– Second. That by the institution of Christ / it remains at the disposal of men / so that all might partake of his life-giving Flesh.

7. In what words does Jesus say this?

“I am the living Bread come down from Heaven / Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood / has eternal life / … for my flesh is true food / and my blood true drink /… whoever eats my flesh will live because of me /… whoever eats this bread will live forever”.

8. Does this also teach that the Eucharist gives us sanctifying grace?

Of course. / The union and abiding of man in Christ / and of Christ in man, / the participation and communion between man and Christ, / and eternal life, / cannot be obtained without sanctifying grace.

9. What does St. Paul teach? (1 Cor 11:20-25)

First of all, the Apostle St. Paul declares / the historical truth of the institution, by our Lord, / of the Holy Eucharist, / presenting it as a banquet, / as the real presence of Christ, / as a sacrifice, and as perpetuated until his Second Coming / by means of his ministers.

Essence

10. What is the matter of the Eucharist?

The proper matter of the Eucharist / is wheat bread and wine of the grape.

11. What is the form of this sacrament?

The proper form of the consecration of this sacrament / is the words of Christ: / “This is my Body” / and “This is my Blood” / (or “This is the chalice of my Blood”).

12. Why is it the proper and fitting form of the sacrament?

It is the proper and fitting form / because it signifies what is done in the sacrament, / since the words declare the conversion of the bread into the true Body of Christ / and of the wine into his Blood.

13. How does the celebrating priest utter the words of consecration?

The celebrating priest utters the words of the Consecration in two ways and simultaneously: /

– First, recitatively, narrating the historical event of the Supper: / “Who (Christ), on the eve of his Passion…”; /

– Second, and principally, assertively, / affirming the words spoken by Christ as his own, / that is, speaking “in Persona Christi,” / with those words bearing upon the matter that the priest has present on the altar.

Real presence

14. How is our Lord Jesus Christ present in the sacrament of the altar?

Our Lord Jesus Christ is truly, / really / and substantially present / in the Most Blessed Sacrament of the altar.

15. What does truly mean?

Truly means that his presence is not merely in figure, / as Zwingli held, / but in truth.

16. What does really mean?

Really means that his presence is not mere subjective faith, / as Oecolampadius held, / but in reality.

17. What does substantially mean?

Substantially means that the presence of the Lord in the Eucharist is not merely virtual, / as Calvin held, / but substantial.

18. What does the Council of Trent teach on this as a defined dogma of faith?

The Council of Trent teaches: / “If anyone denies / that in the Most Holy Sacrament of the Eucharist / there are contained truly, / really, / and substantially / the body and blood / together with the soul and divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, / and therefore the whole Christ; / but says that he is in it only as in a sign and figure / or by his power, / let him be anathema”. (Dz. 883)

19. With what body and with what blood is Jesus Christ present in the Eucharist?

Our Lord Jesus Christ is present in the Eucharist / with the same body and blood that was born of the Virgin Mary, / the same body that hung upon the cross / and the same blood that flowed from his side.

On Transubstantiation

20. What is the conversion of the bread and wine that occurs at the moment of the Consecration called?

This wonderful and singular conversion is properly called “Transubstantiation,” / not consubstantiation, as Luther held.

21. Why is it called wonderful?

It is called wonderful / because it is a most lofty mystery, / beyond the capacity of every created intellect. / It is the Mystery of faith!

22. Why is it called singular?

It is called singular / because in all creation there exists / no conversion like this one.

23. What happens in transubstantiation?

In transubstantiation the whole substance of the bread and the whole substance of the wine disappear / as they are converted into the Body, Blood, Soul and Divinity of Christ.

24. In such a way?

In such a way that under each of the species / and under every part of the species, / before separation and after separation, / the whole Christ is contained.

25. It is of faith, therefore, that the whole and only substance of the bread and wine is transubstantiated into the whole and only substance of the body and blood of Christ. Now then, what is it that remains?

What remain in the Eucharist, without a subject of inherence, by the power of God, / are the accidents, species or appearances of bread and wine.

26. What are they?

The accidents that remain after transubstantiation are: / the weight, size, taste, quantity, smell, color, flavor, shape, measure, etc., / of bread and of wine. / Only the substance changes.

27. What is contained under the species of bread by virtue of the words?

By virtue of the words, / under the species of bread is contained / the body of Christ.

28. And by virtue of accompaniment or concomitance?

Together with the body, / by natural connection, / are contained the blood and the soul, and, / by the wonderful hypostatic union, / the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ.

29. What is contained under the species of wine by virtue of the words?

By virtue of the words / the blood of Christ is contained / under the species of wine.

30. And by virtue of concomitance?

Together with the blood, / by natural connection, / are contained the body, the soul and, / by the hypostatic union, / the divinity of Christ.

On the Sacrifice

31. Is the Mass a true and proper sacrifice?

The Council of Trent teaches as a defined truth of faith: / “If anyone says that in the sacrifice of the Mass / a true and proper sacrifice is not offered to God, / or that to offer it is nothing other than Christ being given to us to eat, / let him be anathema”. (Dz. 948)

32. Did God foretell the Eucharistic sacrifice?

Yes, / already in the Old Testament God foretold / the sacrifice of the Mass by figures and prophecies: / through the prophet Malachi (1:10-11), / through the sacrifice of Melchizedek (Gen 14:18 ff.), / and through the sacrifice of the Paschal Lamb (Ex 12:3 ff.).

33. When did he teach that the Mass is a sacrifice?

He taught that the Mass is a sacrifice / when he instituted it: / “… this is my body which will be given up…” (Lk 22:19); / “… this is my blood… which will be poured out…” (Mt 26:28; Mk 14:24; Lk 22:20). / To give up his body and pour out his blood for us / for the forgiveness of sins / shows that he offers himself in sacrifice.

34. When does he command that the Eucharistic sacrifice be perpetuated?

He commands that the Eucharistic sacrifice be perpetuated / when he adds: / “Do this in memory of me” (Lk 22:19; 1 Cor 11:24‑25) / and the Apostle further adds: / “Each time you eat this bread and drink this cup, / you proclaim the death of the Lord until he comes” (1 Cor 11:26). / This proclamation or living representation / is in fact / carried out in every Mass, / thus perpetuating the sacrifice of the Cross.

Essence of the Eucharistic sacrifice

35. What does the Mass represent and what does it commemorate?

The sacrifice of the Mass is representative / and commemorative of the sacrifice of the Cross. / Therefore: “As often as the memorial of this sacrifice is celebrated, / the work of our Redemption is renewed”. (Prayer of the assembly of the 2nd Sunday in Ordinary Time).

36. What does representative mean?

Representative means that it makes present again the sacrifice of the Cross. / It is not an empty and lifeless representation, / nor a subjective recollection, / but an objective commemoration / and a living and full representation, / true and wonderful, / containing Christ, the Victim of the Passion, / and the separated species of bread and wine / representing the Passion itself, / through the mystical separation of the body and the blood.

37. What does the Church teach in her solemn magisterium?

The Council of Trent teaches: / “If anyone says / that the sacrifice of the Mass is only one of praise and thanksgiving, / or a mere commemoration of the sacrifice accomplished on the Cross, / but not propitiatory; / or that it benefits only the one who receives; / and that it ought not to be offered for the living and the dead, / for sins, punishments, satisfactions and other necessities, / let him be anathema”. (Dz. 950)

38. Is Holy Mass a sacrifice only by its relation to the sacrifice of the Cross?

The sacrifice of the Mass is not only an objective, / living and express representation of the sacrifice of the Cross; / it is not constituted solely by this relation / —which is essential to it— / but this relation is so essential to it / that Christ the Priest, by an unbloody immolation, / does on the altar what he did once and for all on the Cross, / offering himself to God the Father / as Victim for the salvation of all men.

39. Are the sacrifice of the Cross and the sacrifice of the Mass distinct?

No. / The sacrifice of the Cross and the sacrifice of the Mass are one and the same, / because the Victim is one and the same, / the Priest is one and the same, / and the oblative act is one and the same.

40. Do they differ in anything?

Yes. / They differ in something accidental: /

-First, on the part of the Victim: / on the cross the sacrifice was bloody; / in the Mass the sacrifice is unbloody; /

-Second, on the part of the Priest: / on the Cross Christ offered himself alone and visibly; / in the Mass he offers himself invisibly, through the hands of priests; /

-Third, on the part of the effect: / on the Cross he made satisfaction and merited superabundantly for all men of all times; / in the Mass the merits / and the satisfaction for sins accomplished on the Cross are applied to new generations.

41. What is the thing offered in the Mass?

The Victim offered in the Mass / is the body and blood of Christ as poured out in the Passion. / In other words: / it is the same suffering Christ, the Christ who has suffered.

42. Is the thing offered only the suffering Christ?

Not only is the suffering Christ offered, / but with him all his members are offered, / the whole Church, the Mystical Body of Christ.

43. What is required for the sacrifice to have its full effect?

For the sacrifice to have its full effect / it is necessary that the faithful immolate themselves as victims; / that is why the celebrant asks: / “May he make of us an eternal offering” (Euch. Prayer III), / or “that we may become in Christ a living sacrifice in praise of your glory” (Euch. Prayer IV).

44. What is the essence of the sacrifice of the Mass?

The essence of the sacrifice of the Mass / is found at the moment of the double consecration of the species, / ordered to communion / as an integral part.

45. Why is the essence of the Mass found in the separate consecration of the species of bread and wine alone?

Because there is found the true representation of the sacrifice of the cross, / in the separation of the two species, / under which the body and blood of Christ are present / by virtue of the words of consecration, / a mystical or sacramental immolation being carried out, / wonderfully suited to represent Christ immolated, that is, / with his body and his blood separated, / as they were on the Cross.

46. Why does the consecration bring about the sacrifice?

The Eucharistic consecration brings about the sacrifice for two reasons: / First, it is the unbloody, mystical or sacramental immolation of Christ / under the separated species of bread and wine, / which represent and perpetuate in an unbloody manner the bloody immolation of the Cross; / and second, it is at the same time a ritual oblation / made by Jesus Christ, the principal Priest, / through the hands of the visible priests, / in honor of the majesty of God.

On the ministers

47. Who is the principal offerer of the sacrifice of the Mass?

The principal Priest of the sacrifice of the Mass is Jesus Christ, / our Lord, / as is seen from the very words of the consecration, / in which the visible priest makes present the body and blood of Christ in the name of Christ: / “This is my body… This is my blood,” / acting ‘in Persona Christi’.

48. How is he the principal Priest?

Jesus Christ is the Principal Priest of Holy Mass / inasmuch as with an actual will / he wills and offers each and every Mass / celebrated on earth.

49. How does he offer them?

Jesus Christ as principal Priest / offers each and every transubstantiation by a single interior oblative act, / without innovation or succession, / not by successive acts of oblation. / This oblation, which continues without interruption, / is the same interior oblation of the sacrifice of the Cross, / though not gaining new merit, / but applying the merits gained on the Cross.

50. Who are the ministers of the Eucharistic sacrifice?

All validly ordained priests, and they alone, / are ministers of the sacrifice of the Mass.

51. Furthermore, does the whole Church offer the Mass?

Yes, the Church, comprising all the faithful who make it up, / offers each Eucharistic sacrifice.

52. How do the faithful offer the sacrifice of the Mass?

The faithful participate, not in the consecration, but in the oblation. / They participate in it, without performing the visible liturgical rite, in two ways:

– First, by offering the Victim through the hands of the priest, / who was publicly chosen by the bishop to offer the sacrifice in the name of all of them; /

– Second, by offering the Victim together with the priest, / in such a way that in the very oblation of the Victim / —made by Christ and by the visible priest— / they offer themselves to God the Father / as “living sacrifices, holy and pleasing to God” (Rom 12:1), / offering also their own spiritual sacrifices, their concerns, their sorrows, anguish, miseries and needs, / uniting their prayers or desires of praise, petition, expiation and thanksgiving / to the intention of the priest and of Christ himself, the principal Priest, / so that they may be offered to God the Father in the same oblation of the Victim.

53. When does the offering of the faithful begin?

The offering of the faithful begins with the procession of the offerings for the presentation of the gifts of bread and wine, / reaches its fullness when the priest places the Victim upon the altar, / is sustained throughout the memorial oblation / and is solemnly affirmed with the “Amen” of the final doxology.

On the sacrificial Banquet

54. What is the most perfect participation in Holy Mass?

The most perfect participation in the Mass / “…consists in the faithful receiving the body of the Lord from the same sacrifice” (Vat. II, S.C., 55).

55. What conditions are indicated for receiving the sacrament of the Eucharist fruitfully?

For receiving the sacrament of the Eucharist fruitfully / three things are indicated: /

  1. To be in the grace of God, without awareness of mortal sin; /
  2. To have fasted for one hour beforehand; /
  3. To know Whom one is going to receive and to approach Communion with devotion.

56. What images does the Second Vatican Council use to impress upon us the importance of the Mass?

The Council says that it is summit and source: / “it is the summit toward which the activity of the whole Church is directed and, / at the same time, / the source from which all her power flows” (Vat. II, S. C., 10)

Dear brothers:

Come! Let us adore so great a sacrament! Let us participate ever more actively, more consciously and more fruitfully in the one true sacrifice for the salvation of all men!
