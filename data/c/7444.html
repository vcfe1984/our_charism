Spiritual formation must prevail

[Exordium] On this day I would like us to reflect on a very short sentence of our Constitutions, which says: “In the novitiate spiritual formation must prevail”. It is in number 245 of the Constitutions. This sentence, which so often goes unnoticed, is of great importance, because we “have wanted to give an absolute preponderance to the spiritual aspect, because we understand that this is what our charism demands”1.

What does this mean?

First of all, we must say that you have not entered religious life “to train yourselves for the apostolate”2 the way someone learns the trade of a mechanic; you have not entered to learn languages and travel the world, nor to study philosophy or to live for free… but you have entered principally –listen carefully– in order “to seek God”3; that is the principal occupation of the novice4.

That does not mean that one should not do apostolate, or should not put ʽgritʼ into one’s study, or should not learn other things, because “it is true that we must serve God with the talents He gives us, but all those things are only means to a higher end, and our end is God Himself”5. Is that clear? All the activities carried out in the novitiate and, in fact, from now on and throughout the whole of religious life, “are means to find God”6.

That is why one should not even set perfection in action as the goal7: “may the food never get burned on me”, “may I never fail a subject”, “may I be the best organized in the apostolate”… all those things are very good –careful!– I am not telling you to be lazy, to say ‘anything goes!’, not to organize anything, no! Everything God asks us to do –an office, sports, study… whatever it may be– must be done in the best possible way, but that alone is not enough; that alone would be nothing more than an exterior ornament8, and the end we seek is principally interior, namely: to know and love the Incarnate Word9, and not to stop until we attain a “holy familiarity”10 with Him. That is also why we must not be discouraged when things do not turn out well for us, or do not turn out as perfectly as we would have liked; it is not as though we are out of the championship because of it, because the main thing for us is to love God, “to unite ourselves to His Person”11, and very probably the humiliation that this mistake, this carelessness, this fault brings me is a beautiful occasion to unite myself to God. “Jesus –says St. Thérèse– has no need of our works, but only of our love”12 (and this is in the Directory of Spirituality, 220).

Notice that this is so important that the Constitutions, in their first pages, point out to us that “in our lives and actions there must have primacy ‘the name, the teaching, the life, the promises, the Kingdom, the mystery of Jesus of Nazareth, Son of Godʼ, lived in such a way that we put nothing before His love”13.

That is why our proper law emphasizes that in the novitiate spiritual formation must prevail, because no one is born knowing these things; one has to learn them, and the sooner one learns them the better. The primacy of Christ is key in our spirituality as religious of the Institute, because our religious life according to the charism of the Institute is Christocentric, and that Christocentric stamp must be branded into us and into our apostolate14.

Now, this spiritual formation is imparted in the novitiate in many ways. To look no further, the Holy Mass itself is catechetical in itself, and one has to learn to participate in it –as our Founder and the Constitutions themselves say– “ever more consciously, more actively and more fruitfully”15; there it is Christ Himself who teaches in the secret of the soul, and the Mass is, as you know, “the principal thing, the most important thing we must do each day”16. The subjects you take also have a clearly spiritual tint17, and that is why one must know how to make good use of the time of study and of spiritual reading, because that opens doors for us; it helps us to find the path to finding God.

Two specific ways

But there are two ways clearly specified in the Directory of Novitiates by which the novice of the Institute of the Incarnate Word must seek God: “prayer and penance, in the atmosphere of fraternal charity in which one must live”18. These are “two wings of that search”19. Interesting, isn’t it?

  • Regarding prayer: one must learn to pray well. Sometimes some religious think that because they wear the cassock they already know how to pray, or they are embarrassed to ask if they have difficulties, if they experience temptations, if they feel reluctant to pray. So the first thing is to be willing to let oneself be taught; one has to accept that one does not know everything, that perhaps the way one used to pray when one was in the youth group ʽdoes not work anymoreʼ now that I am a religious; therefore, one simply has to know how to ask and let oneself be guided with humility.

What should the prayer of a novice of the Incarnate Word be like? It must be “unceasing prayer”20. It does not mean that one is in the chapel all day, or that one is with hands joined in silence in a corner while the others play sports. It means that whatever one does, one always keeps one’s soul recollected in God, for which it often helps to say a prayer beforehand, to set one’s thoughts on God. When I was a novice, we were always taught to pray before beginning an activity, so that the remembrance of Christ would never depart from us.

That is also why the prayer of the novice, especially that of adoration, must be a “remaining with God in prayer”21, because prayer does not consist “in thinking much, but in loving much”22. Remember that.

  • Regarding penance in community life: anyone who lives a few months in community learns very soon that “community life is the greatest penance”23, and this is an excellent means of finding God. Because life in community implies a certain denial of oneself in order to help the other, to encourage him, to ʽbear with himʼ, not to go around looking at the defects of others… that is part of the life of any family. One has to deny oneself many times in order to keep the schedule, to come back tired from the apostolate and still do one’s service with a smile, to eat quietly the food that did not turn out well for one’s companion, to know how to keep silent even when one is right and let the others win… but in all these occasions of ʽpenanceʼ one has to find wings to reach God. For these are the occasions that most allow us to imitate the self-emptying of Christ. Because you should know that ours is neither command nor honors, but humble service, generous self-giving, the gratuitous gift of oneself through love to the end24. Be convinced, then, that charity is the essence of holiness, and take advantage of all the opportunities that community life gives you to practice this virtue.

St. John of the Cross, who was a master of novices, has some brief counsels that he gives to a religious who asks him for advice about how to attain perfection (and which I recommend you read). There the saint tells him: “And all these mortifications and annoyances he must suffer with interior patience, keeping silent for the love of God, understanding that he did not come to the Religious Order for anything else but to be worked upon in this way and so become worthy of heaven. For if it were not for this, there would be no reason to come to the Religious Order, but rather to stay in the world seeking one’s own comfort, honor, reputation and ease… [therefore] let him always try to bear them [those annoyances of community life] with patience and conformity to the will of God, and not bear them in such a way that, instead of God proving him to his profit in this trial, He comes to reject him for not having been willing to carry the Cross of Christ with patience”25. So far St. John of the Cross.

Now, this is not easy; that is why one must be well grounded, spiritually speaking, in our Lord Jesus Christ and in the spirit of our Institute. In this Religious Family we have to be ready to live with all radicality the demands of the Incarnation and of the Cross –our proper law says it very clearly–; that is why we cannot expect a life with folded arms, where everything is ʽdrifting aboutʼ, where everything is consolation… our spirituality is the robust spirituality of the Incarnation, where there is self-emptying, where there is humiliation, where there are sacrifices… All that is part of the program. Since our call, as our founder says, “is not a call to have a good time, but to have a hard time”26.

But pay attention to this: the Incarnate Word does not leave alone any of those He has called, but always accompanies them and, through the action of the Holy Spirit, predisposes them for the mission for which He destines them. That is why, from now on, look always with confidence to Christ, who has called you to follow Him in our beloved Institute.

There is a sentence of our proper law that I find very consoling, and I like to repeat it especially when I visit the houses of formation; it says: “holiness is attainable because it is, above all, the work of God”27. Therefore, one must abandon oneself into the hands of God. Think of that when study is hard for you, when praying is hard for you, when obeying is hard for you, when you experience difficulties of any kind, or after you commit some fault, some mistake. Repeat to yourselves again and again: “holiness is attainable because it is, above all, the work of God; I only have to be docile to what God disposes for me, which will always be the best”, and keep going forward with hope!

[Peroration] That is why, I repeat, spiritual formation is so important, because one must become accustomed from now on to discern and to decide in the light of supernatural motives, that is, always seeking the greater glory of God, and also, and above all, to act and to live as faithful religious, never tiring of doing good, like the Incarnate Word.

May the Most Blessed Virgin obtain this grace for us from her Divine Son.
