October 18

The Saint

Feast of Saint Luke the Evangelist, who, according to tradition, was born at Antioch into a pagan family and was a physician by profession. Converted to faith in Christ, he became a beloved companion of Saint Paul the Apostle. In his Gospel, as a scribe of Christ's gentleness, he set down in order all that Jesus did and taught. Likewise, in the Acts of the Apostles he recounted the beginnings of the Church's life up to Paul's first arrival in the city of Rome in the first century.

The Institute's Proper Law

Directory of Novitiates

“1. When Saint Thomas speaks of the evangelical counsels, he insists on the adverb ‘totally’ as the distinction between counsels and precepts. The history of the Apostles, the first religious, opens with the relictis omnibus—having left all things—which Saint Luke's Gospel emphasizes (cf. 5:11). Turning aside toward worldly things poisons the love that we must direct to God in all its purity. […]”

Working translation of the excerpt from the Directory of Novitiates; no corresponding published English passage has yet been verified.

The Founder

“In Jesus' life, all events are related, not through accidental convergence, but through an inner orientation toward the sacrifice of the Cross. Saint Luke revealed it in a mysterious phrase: ‘he steadfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem’ (Lk 9:51, excerpt, Douay–Rheims).1 The constant attitude of his whole life was to go to Jerusalem: the Priest was making his way toward his altar.”

Excerpt from Sacerdotes para siempre. The Spanish source also gives the Latin et ipse faciam suam firmavit ut iret Ierusalem; its apparent transcription error is recorded for review rather than introduced as a separate English reading.

“Saint Luke expresses total self-giving: ‘And having brought their ships to land, leaving all things, they followed him’ (Lk 5:11, Douay–Rheims). Someone might say: ‘What did they leave? Just a few nets, which would have been rotten …!’ But that is not what matters. What matters? What matters is the spiritual disposition: God calls …! My child, burn everything! That is the great task. It is not only the great task of leaving things behind, but the task of always being ready to abandon everything—everything!—absolutely everything for God.”

Excerpt from Vox Verbi 171 — Homily. The Founder excerpts remain working translations; Scripture uses the published English edition identified below.
