Transcription of the good-night talk that Fr. Buela gave to the seminarians of the Institute on Sunday, March 26, 1995

“One of the convictions that has been strengthened in me on this pilgrimage, which thanks be to God I was able to make, is that the great enemy in these times continues to be the so-called ‘Christian progressivism.’ Although there are auspicious signs, it nevertheless continues to wreak havoc in the West, and also −as a backlash− in the East itself/ for example, a certain bishop −quite casually− told me that celibacy had to be abolished, that it was no more than an idea, that the Pope is all-powerful and can do whatever he wants (laughter), and I don't know how many other things.

At the end he says to me: − And what do you think?

I answered him: − “That would have to be studied…” What was I going to say!

Another thing, for example, that he told me there was: − “Muhammad was an epileptic/ he was a merchant who married a rich widow, much older than he was; the matter of the religion that God supposedly revealed to him has never been able to be proven, since it seems they were his illusions when those epileptic fits seized him. They do not want to allow a critical analysis of the Koran, because they already know that if they do that, they are going to discover that there were several hands that wrote the Koran, and not just one as they maintain, that of Muhammad or Mahomet (and that is also why they are against any scientific study of religion, soundly scientific, such as archaeology, for example); and yet, immediately afterwards, that same bishop, a victim of the progressive heresy, says to me: “And the Pope, in order to draw closer to the Muslims, ought to recognize that Muhammad was a prophet” (laughter). Go get yourself cured by Lolita Torres! He added that the Koran has very good things, which he completely shares, and that we should say as soon as possible: “This which is in the Koran is very good.” Well, what will the oracle say next? That hell does not exist!!! (laughter) What do we want religion for if we are all going to heaven? There is no need for Jesus Christ, nor for keeping the commandments, nothing! The whole supernatural order falls, the whole moral order falls, everything falls! Besides, if Jesus Christ taught it, Muhammad would be more than Jesus Christ.

Progressivism continues to ravage the Church, and still occupies key posts within the Church. We just sang to the Martyrs of Barbastro. When we were in Barbastro with Fr. Pombo, one of the priests –a very good man!– who wanted us to stay the night, to stay for a meal, whatever we wanted… said to me enthusiastically and emphatically: − “Wow, these young men died for refusing to take off their cassocks!!!” And he? He was in civilian clothes… (laughter) yes, yes! Not even a clergyman! I couldn't believe it; I didn't say anything either, but I was left blinking because… They have no vocations/ the Claretians don't either. They have martyrs and they have no vocations… nor will they have any as long as there is not a total conversion, from the heart, and as long as they do not truly place themselves at the service of the Gospel! Let them live as one ought to live…! There will be no vocations… That is how it will be, and in fact they are already starting to suspect it. Progressivism is truly still ravaging. In the United States, unfortunately, for example, many hospitals, schools, orphanages continue to be abandoned… The nuns there caught the silly pox of feminism, and so, bye-bye habit!… They go to the hairdresser, they have their unions, they have their health plans, they live and get together like old spinsters, nobody is in charge, there is no superior because: “How can there be a superior if we are all equal? Equal!” That is no witness at all, and so young women do not go to those congregations… Why? Because they want to live poverty! I read it in some statements of Mother Teresa of Calcutta, where she says: “The young women of the first world, the young women of the rich countries come to our congregation because they want to live poverty just as we live it…”, all the way! And so, what do these religious women do? They hold courses, a sort of retreat, seminar-style, where there are specialists who come to give conferences.

What for? So that they may die under anesthesia; that is, they die as a congregation, but with a sweet death: “Well then,” − they conclude −, “if God does not give us vocations, then we have to know how to act: sell the less important properties, live off what was sold, then sell the other properties.” That is how they teach them to die well: the sweet death! Precisely, when I was coming to Buenos Aires, there was traveling with me a man who had been General Councilor in Rome for 8 years, of a very important congregation, which here in Argentina is also very important because it has many schools.

− How many vocations do you have? I asked him.

− I think one in the whole country…

And aren't they ashamed? One becomes indignant… What do they have the schools for? Of course! They have the schools to make money, even though they say they don't… And so, in those schools the Christian faith is not lived as Christianity is: with enthusiasm! with fire! with verve! with zest! It is a thing for fat ladies, it is a bourgeois thing, it is a thing where the religious, or the priest, or the brother appears as the one who collects payment at the end of the month. The students always hear their parents speaking ill of the teachers at their school because they have to pay the fees, and they see the religious as the guys who come down hard on them and give them reprimands.

And so I say to him: I knew the place where you used to have your seminary, your novitiate; it was full of vocations… Full!… It was the time of “so-and-so.”

− “Ah! Yes, of course, that's true, but now… And if that worked for you before, why don't you apply it now?

−No, well, of course, uh, uh… We are studying the matter. Now we have a plan…”

All of it, all of it rubbish! It is good for nothing, it is all a failure. This religious was precisely the one who had called me −because of an article of mine that I had published on the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ− to teach at a very important teacher-training college in Buenos Aires. I taught there for a year and then I left, because it was a hotbed of progressivism. I said to myself: no, why should I waste my time here! They wanted to go about correcting it, but correcting it the way they always do, the way progressivism does: with half-measures/ the man is dying of cancer, but… “we'll give him an aspirin and he'll be fine, he'll be fine!” He'll be fine nothing! He'll go down, over the cliff. So, to see how far the thing went, since I was dealing with a person very well connected with the whole ecclesiastical milieu after having spent eight years in Europe, I asked him, to get him talking (laughter):

−How are things? How do you see the Church in Argentina?

First thing: −“Well… I'm just landing,” he answered me.

He returned a year ago and is on committees of the Argentine Episcopal Conference…! And then he realized that his answer was weak, because I looked at him as if to say… come on! Then he says to me: “I see the Church in Argentina doing well.” For my part, I was not going to say anything. “The only problem,” he added, “is that, except for some congregations, there are no vocations.” Well then, brother, the Church in Argentina is not doing well, it is doing badly/ Brother, there are no vocations! It's doing badly! Why? Because we are heading for death! Of course, the Church in Argentina is doing well in the same way their congregation is doing well: they have no vocations! That is progressivism: not calling things by their name. It was then, before or after, I don't know, that I said to him: what you are doing is putting them to sleep with the sweet death, the syringe. They go on dying, but without pain, without realizing it… The car is heading for the abyss, but they close their eyes… while the abyss awaits them. That is why I say: one is confirmed in the conviction that Christian progressivism, progressivism within the Church, although it does not have the virulence of former times, continues to wreak havoc. In fact you will have heard −I imagine you are informed− that the Pope removed a French bishop, precisely because of his extreme progressive stance, which is something quite serious. Last year he removed a bishop in the United States, also for serious statements on moral matters.

But one asks oneself: this brother is not a bad person, he is a good person; where is the problem? The problem is here (he points to his head)… it is the doctrine that they put into his head, it is progressivism. Instead of being moved by faith, they are moved by expediency, as if the world were going to save the Church, and they say that one must adapt to the world. For example, since they have to deal with young people (and they are all decrepit, all with arthritis, with rheumatism), they get the idea of wearing a toupee, dyeing their hair, wearing blue jeans, having… and they do not realize that this very thing is repulsive to young people, because, after all, for a person to grow old is the most normal thing there is, since the same thing happens to all of us. To pretend otherwise, being a religious, is no longer so interesting. That is why we must always ask Jesus Christ for the grace of fidelity to him, which takes concrete form in fidelity to the living Magisterium of the Church −what Peter teaches− and ask him for the grace not to fall into that labyrinth which is progressivism, manifested in a thousand forms and a thousand different ways. I have always asked for the grace to maintain, preserve and increase the authentic Catholic faith. And in the midst of all that havoc, God glories in raising up holiness and works great things. For example, what World Youth Day in Manila (Philippines) was. For the first time in the history of the world −since the world has been the world, since man has walked the planet earth− for the first time, for a religious event, the number of people gathered that gathered in Manila. According to L’Osservatore Romano, which is not given to exaggeration, five million people attended!, that is, five times the number of people who gathered in Buenos Aires when the Pope came to Argentina. Never has any politician, never ever, been able to gather such a number of people. And it was a man who called them together, a weak man who falls getting out of the shower, who trips over his cassock and fractures his shoulder, who was once shot, a weak man… but one to whom Jesus Christ promised, in the person of Peter, that the gates of hell would not prevail against the Church founded upon him. That man called them together, the man who continues to preach an unpopular doctrine, because to say nowadays that divorce is wrong is unpopular, to say that abortion is wrong is unpopular, to say that premarital relations are wrong is unpopular, to say that spouses in marriage must perform the act open to life is unpopular. Nevertheless, one sees that in the depths of the conscience of humanity, in the depths of the conscience of Christians, the thirst for truth still beats, even for truth in the moral order, even though it may be hard for one to live up to it. We have this example, unique in the present-day world.

There is not a single leader in the present-day world of the spiritual and moral caliber, of the leadership that John Paul II exercises, and this because he is faithful to Jesus Christ and because he is a true Son and Slave of the Blessed Virgin.

Let us ask her to know how to imitate, in spite of our limitations, in the place where God puts us, this giant whom God has raised up, this “Viking of God,” as they called him on the trip he made to Mexico in ’79, so as not to give in before all the confusion of Christian progressivism, which is ravaging the Church. Let us sing to Our Lady.”
