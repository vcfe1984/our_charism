Interview with Fr. Carlos Buela conducted by the young people of the Press Workshop of the Festive Oratory
Blessed Pier Giorgio Frassati.
We take the interview from the bulletin “Chispas” no. 1, publication of the Oratory.

For what purposes is the Festive Oratory carried out?

-The Festive Oratory has several specific purposes: it is so that young people can meet one another, enjoy themselves, be instructed and help one another.

How do you define the Oratory?

-It is very difficult to define, because it is something that embraces religious aspects, cultural aspects, musical aspects. St. John Bosco defines it, in a way, by saying: “it is a church, a school, a home (as a family home), and a playground (in the sense of a place of recreation, of fun).” It is difficult to define; it is described.

What does the “spirit of the Oratory” consist of?

-The spirit of the Oratory is a spirit of great joy, and in joy to find the meaning of life, why we are here in this world, and to learn to direct ourselves toward the other life.

What importance does the Oratory have?

-The Oratory is so important because, as St. John Bosco says, “to change a town you have to have an Oratory”; that is why, in fact, there should be an Oratory in every parish, so that young people could meet one another, because many times it is not that young people do not want to, but that they are not given the opportunity.

What qualities should those who work in an Oratory have?

-Simply to want to do good.

For whom is the Oratory carried out?

The Oratory is for every young person, boy or girl, whether in early adolescence, in late adolescence, or already fully into youth. As long as one has good will, it is for everyone.

In the spirit of which saint is this Oratory carried out?

-The Oratory begins with someone the 350th anniversary of whose death was celebrated not long ago: St. Philip Neri. He was a very joyful saint; he was a Florentine but lived in Rome. Over time, following his custom, Oratories continued to be held. In the last century it was Don Bosco who took up the idea of the Oratory, but transformed it, making something very particular, very personal to him, which is what we try to do here. So I think these are the two great saints who mark out what an Oratory must be: St. Philip Neri and St. John Bosco.

Tell us a little about St. John Bosco.

-St. John Bosco has been named patron of youth because from his earliest days as a priest he began to work with young people, and for that he also knew how to gather around him those who had a vocation to work with young people, and for that purpose he founded the Salesian Congregation, which is dedicated especially to the education of youth in schools and which also continues the custom of the Oratory, sometimes with greater skill and other times with less, because it turns out that it is not easy nowadays to run Oratories for young people; for us it is easy because we have many young seminarians: there are some who are capable in painting, others in music, others who like journalism, others who know how to do impressions, others who sing… So we can run an Oratory more easily, because one of the necessary things -since attendance at the Oratory is voluntary- is that the activities of the Oratory have to have a hook, for if they have no hook or are a bore, you don’t go. If a priest is alone it is very difficult to organize an Oratory today, because many people have to be involved. That is why I think that because of this very special situation, that we have such a large Seminary with so many young men, we can run an Oratory more easily, something that is more difficult to do elsewhere.

-In what way is the family integrated into the Festive Oratory? How can it take part?

-I think we must seek to have the family integrated as much as possible, both in accompanying the liturgical participation and also in the recreational part or when the campfire is held. That is why your dads and moms often come, because they want to see; it strikes them that you are enthusiastic about something, because today it is a serious problem that there are many young people who are not enthusiastic about anything. It is as if their arms were hanging down. It is terrible, but it is so.

I see that families must be integrated, although the older people have more problems than the young. We have the difficulty in schools -at least I had it in Buenos Aires- that the problem was not with the young people but with the parents; many times the young person still has fine ideals and yet it is the grown-ups who discourage them.

-Why did you decide to form an Oratory when there was already another one in San Rafael?

-Because, as I said before, there should be an Oratory in every parish. It is not that it is an Oratory for this city. Just as there are many parishes, there should be many Oratories.

In what way can the community help?

-The community can help in many ways, by making transportation possible and other things that are needed to make the Oratory different each day. A successor of Don Bosco used to say that “every Sunday the Oratory has to put on its festive clothes.” In this way the young person feels at home, and putting on festive clothes is not difficult: it is a very simple thing!
