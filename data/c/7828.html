Directory of Contemplative Life, 172; op. cit. Perfectae Caritatis, 7

[Exordium] I have come very gladly to this beloved monastery to show you not only our esteem and affection, but also our deep gratitude for the prayers of all of you. That in the first place.

Secondly, and given the expansion that the contemplative branch has had in these last years –consider that in 2016 the Institute had only 6 monasteries; by mid-2019 it had 11 monasteries, and at present there are 16– it seems important to me to highlight (although you already know this) the importance of contemplative life in mission lands. By the grace of God, today we can say that our contemplatives are on all 5 continents, since the monastery in Papua was founded. However, there are still 6 provinces, such as those of Peru, Russia, Ecuador, Northern Europe, Tanzania, and the delegation in Tajikistan, that do not yet have contemplatives. And besides, requests for the foundation of monasteries keep arriving: for example, there is a request to found the first male contemplative monastery in a diocese in Lithuania… Now of course, all this depends on what happens with us, but I wanted to tell you anyway so that you can help us pray.

In these times men –and women– in the various parts of the world and in the diverse cultural contexts in which their lives unfold seek God with anguish, often seeking Him, as St. Paul would say, “groping, though He is not far from each one of us”1. And that is why it seems to me that we must see this expansion of the contemplative branch of the Institute among peoples so different in race, in mentality, in cultures, as a proof of the validity of the charism of the Institute and of the universality of the message we bring. It is indeed a providential fact that God leads our members to pray and do penance in places to which, if it were not for us, very probably no one else would go. Consider that we are in Turkey, in Papua, that we want to open a monastery in Iraq, another in Taiwan… believe me, there are not many who want to go to those places. But for the mission it is imperative to go. And certainly we would give great consolation to the Heart of Christ.

Our proper law says it and today I want to repeat it to you: contemplatives “are the honor of the Church and the torrent of heavenly graces”2. The Second Vatican Council, for its part, in two of its documents –Lumen Gentium3 and Perfectae Caritatis4– also praises the life of solitude with which you have chosen to follow more closely Christ praying on the mountain. And canon law also confirms this when it says that contemplatives “always have an outstanding part in the Mystical Body of Christ”5.

Honor of the Church

Why does it say that they are the honor of the Church? Because Christ Himself has given us the example that the whole honor of love lies in loving, in giving oneself, in sacrificing oneself, and that is precisely what the monks of the IVE do. “To live the life of the Eucharist –says St. Edith Stein– is to go out completely from the small circle of one’s own life and to grow into the infinity of the life of Christ”6. To live the life of the Eucharist is precisely the life of the contemplatives of the Incarnate Word. They are moreover the honor of the Church because you have chosen the better part and it will not be taken from you7. They are the honor of the Church because God has chosen them to consecrate them to honoring exclusively His adorable Person and His real and living presence among us. They are the honor of the Church because they dedicate themselves to honoring the sacramental state of the Savior, copying in their lives the virtues that Jesus practices in the Eucharist8.

Torrent of heavenly graces

But they are also a torrent of heavenly graces, or as the Directory of Spirituality says: “magnets of the grace of God”9. How many graces the contemplatives obtain for the mission, for our own missionaries, for the conversion of souls; how much the monks help the faith to take root in the various peoples; how much the prayer and sacrifice of the contemplative sustains the priest in his mission field, and how much it is needed that all this spread and multiply! The whole Church greatly needs the collaboration of the monks.

That is why the most characteristic aspect of the apostolate of the monks continues to be the holocaust of yourselves hidden in penance, in the observance of monastic discipline according to the genuine spirit of the Institute and, above all, in the offering of prayers and sacrifices, both in the divine office and in the Holy Mass10.

So then, the interior life of the monk, by virtue of his monastic profession, is not only a private exercise of spirituality, already in fact highly commendable, but is mission and ecclesial service for the benefit of all the members of the Mystical Body of Christ and, in reality, of all humanity.

St. John Paul II, using a metaphor of St. Bernard, said that “just as the digestive system works for the benefit of the whole body, so the monks, by means of lectio divina and contemplation, assimilate the spiritual nourishment of the Word of God and of sacramental grace and transform it into vital juices for the benefit of all the members of the community”11. And that is why today, when so many people in the world are tossed about by confused intellectual currents, disturbed and thrown into spiritual crises by so many false spiritualities promoted everywhere, mainly by the media, there is great need not only of the prayer of contemplatives but of the example of the importance of the transcendent12.

The recent canonization of St. Charles de Foucauld, the martyrdom of the monks of Tibhirine in Algeria13, the martyrdom of more than 70 contemplatives of various religious orders in Spain during the Spanish Civil War14, speak of how important the radical self-giving of the contemplative continues to be today when it comes to evangelization.

[Peroration] You see, a moment ago we mentioned the number of monasteries the Institute has today. Who would have thought that a handful of monks from here, from San Rafael, would carry contemplative life to the whole world! God has entrusted the whole wide world to your prayers and to your holiness of life. Let us take as addressed personally to us this longing of the Heart of the Incarnate Word expressed in the Directory of Spirituality, when, speaking of the monasteries, it says: Would that we could multiply them throughout the whole world! 15.

Let us ask in this Holy Mass that both you and all the contemplatives of the Institute throughout the world may contribute with the strength of prayer to sustaining the Church and gladdening her with the fruits of an ardent faith, and that all our monasteries may truly be “seedbeds of edification for the Christian people”16.

May Our Lady bless you and protect you always.
