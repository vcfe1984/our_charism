1950 years ago, the land of Rome, founded by Romulus and Remus, would witness the passage of two new founders, the apostle of the key and the apostle of the sword, who, following the command of their Master with their total witness, would become the pillars of the City of God among men.

St. Peter, on the one hand, was the Fisherman to whom our Lord said, ‘Do not be afraid; from now on you will be a fisher of men’ (cf. Lk 5:5-6, 10)

St. Peter was the Witness who was present at the Transfiguration of the Lord, at His Agony in the Garden, and at His Resurrection.

St. Peter was the Preacher of True Freedom, who taught in his letter: “Act as men who are truly free, obeying God, and not as those who make freedom an excuse for their malice” (1 Pet 2:16)

St. Peter was the Wonderworker to whom multitudes flocked so that, as he passed by, at least his shadow might fall on some of them, and all were healed. (cf. Acts 5:14-16).

St. Peter was, finally, the Pope, the Vicar of Christ on earth, because Christ Himself said: You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church (Mt 16:18).

St. Paul, for his part, was the Apostle of the Gentiles who most gladly spent himself and was spent for souls. (cf. 2 Cor 12:15).

St. Paul was the Tireless Traveler, making three missionary journeys and five visits to Jerusalem.

St. Paul was the Persecuted One, teaching that we are “persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed (2 Cor 4:7).

St. Paul was the Formator who instructed the Romans, saying: “Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind” (Rom 12:2)

St. Paul was, finally, the Missionary who taught with his life that “The love of Christ urges us on” (2 Cor 5:14)

On a day like today, both gave Christ the supreme witness of love: St. Peter shedding his blood as he died crucified head downward, and St. Paul dying by beheading. St. Augustine would say: “Merit made their passion equal, and charity made it fall on the same day. Thus did He accomplish it in them who was in them, and who suffered in them and with them, who helps those who fight and crowns the victors”1

That is why even today, after 1950 years, it is our ideal to be faithful to their glorious legacy.

Because to love the Incarnate Word is to love His Successor and His Church, “founded on the Apostles and perpetuated by apostolic succession”2, “our motto” being “‘with Peter and under Peter’”3.

Because we are convinced that: “Where Peter is, there is the Church”4, and with loving impetus, like Peter, we have left everything5 and must go throughout the world proclaiming with him: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God (Mt 16:16) and there is no other name under heaven by which we can be saved (Acts 4:10)6.

Because we seek to be formed with a Roman spirit, the spirit of the Eternal City of the Apostles Peter and Paul, which “means being witnesses, day by day, to the living tradition of the faith, as it is proclaimed by the See of Peter”7

Because, in imitation of the Apostles who, obedient to Christ’s command, Go and teach all all nations…, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you, it is our desire to undertake with joy and courage the missionary adventure, announcing to all men that Jesus Christ has become for you, from God, wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption (1 Cor 1:30)8. And we wish to make our own the Apostle’s cry: Woe to me if I do not preach the Gospel! (1 Cor 9:16).

Because that is what the Apostles did, according to the words of St. Paul to the Corinthians: “all our missionary and apostolic work is founded on the conviction that He must reign (1 Cor 15:25)”9. That is why “we do not want ‘to leave anything untried so that the love of Christ may have the supreme primacy in the Church and in society’”10.

Because today the words of the Apostle of the Gentiles, God wills all men to be saved and to come to the full knowledge of the Truth (1 Tim 2:4), continue to be for us the rule and foundation of all our missionary activity11

Because, inspired by the great Formator of Apostles that St. Paul was, we want “to imitate Jesus Christ as perfectly as possible; we want to strive fervently and resolutely to have the same mind that was in Christ Jesus… (Phil 2:5), so that we may be the aroma of Christ (2 Cor 2:15), ambassadors of Christ (2 Cor 5:20) … of the mystery of the Gospel (Eph 6:19), a letter of Christ (2 Cor 3:3), clothed with Christ (Gal 3:27), firmly convinced that we are predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son (Rom 8:29), becoming like Him (cf. Phil 3:10), and being configured to Him (cf. Phil 3:10). […]Until we can say, in all truth, with the Apostle, Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ (1 Cor 11:1), and it is no longer I who live, it is Christ who lives in me (Gal 2:20)”12.

Because today, just as in apostolic times, our missionaries on the five continents must repeat with Peter to so many others: Silver and gold I have none; but what I have, I give you, and, in the name of Jesus Christ, devote themselves eagerly to the works of mercy.

Because today, following the counsel of St. Paul, Persevere always in prayer, keeping watch in it with thanksgiving (Col 4:2), our monks, with their sacrifices and prayers, must be “magnets of the grace of God”13.

Because today, 1950 years after Peter and Paul gave their supreme witness of belonging to Christ by the shedding of their blood, we too must be ready, out of loyalty to God, for martyrdom14, and with the same Apostle we must say: for me to live is Christ and to die is gain (Phil 1:21)15.

Because today, from East and West, on the five continents, we religious of the Incarnate Word desire to keep marching on rejoicing in hope (Rom 12:12)16 and with intrepid ardor we say: Possumus! since we know in whom we have put our trust (cf. 2 Tim 1:12)17, and with the Apostle we exclaim: I can do all things in Him who strengthens me (Phil 4:13). That is why we must advance without fear, with the firm conviction that all things work together for the good of those who love God (Rom 8:28).

Because the passion of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul has made this day sacred for us. Following their examples: “Let us love their faith, their life, their labors, their sufferings, their confession and preaching of the faith. Let us make our way pass before the gaze of the Lord. A way that was narrow, steep and full of brambles, but the passage of so many others has made it smooth. For the Lord Himself was the first to pass along it; the intrepid apostles also passed along it… and with them He who said: Without me you can do nothing. (Cf. St. Augustine, Sermon 295, 8)

To the Queen of Apostles we entrust ourselves, so that She may make of us:

  • “true apostles to whom the Lord of hosts will give the word and the strength needed to work wonders and to win glorious spoils from His enemies.
  • Apostles who have the silvered wings of the dove, to fly, with the pure intention of the glory of God and the salvation of men, wherever the Holy Spirit calls us.
  • Apostles who leave behind them, in the places where they preach, only the gold of charity, which is the fulfillment of the whole law (cf. Rom 13:10).
  • Apostles who carry in their mouths the two-edged sword of the word of God (Heb 4:12); on their shoulders, the blood-stained standard of the cross; in their right hand, the crucifix; the rosary in their left; the sacred names of Jesus and Mary in their hearts, and in all their conduct the modesty and mortification of Jesus Christ”18.
  • Apostles, in short, who may, by the power of divine grace, imitate and carry out in their lives the glorious example and indelible teaching of the Holy Apostles, Patrons of Rome

Sts. Peter and Paul, pray for us and for the whole world.
