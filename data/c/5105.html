Homily for the Religious Brothers of the Institute of the Incarnate Word

[Exordium] One of the great riches of our beloved Institute is certainly that it counts the Religious Brothers among its members; that is, that you are part of our Religious Family, true religious, as much as any of us. That is why I am very happy to be able to celebrate this Mass with you. Know that for me this meeting has a special importance, both in meaning and in affection.

That is why I would like to take advantage of this sermon to develop briefly two points that the Capitular Fathers mentioned at the last Chapter regarding the Religious Brothers: 1) becoming aware of the gift and the specific character of the vocation of a brother; 2) the contributions they are called to make to the mission of the Institute in the Church1.

1. The vocation of a brother in the Institute

St. Marcellin Champagnat said of the vocation of the coadjutor Brothers in his order (and we can say the same): “the religious vocation is always an extraordinary grace. After baptism, it is the most excellent that God can grant to a soul. It is a sign of predestination, a second preference and choice for the glory of heaven”2.

You have been called to walk toward perfection by way of the evangelical counsels, which one day you professed with a generous totality of commitment, and that is why you are called, and in fact are, religious in the full sense of the word. Because you have been chosen and consecrated by God for the excellent office of “loving and serving, and making Jesus Christ loved and served”3, as the Constitutions define our vocation as religious. And not to be just any religious, but religious of this magnificent Institute of the Incarnate Word, which in its manifold charity has seen in the vocation of the Brothers a key piece4 for the good of the Institute (the holiness of its members) and for evangelization. You, in fact, are called to the perfection of union with God -the perfection of charity-: that is your vocation! Just like that of the priest members of the Institute.

But moreover, in fact, it is you and not we –the priests– who are called key pieces of the apostolic endeavor of the Institute. And that is because the Incarnate Word himself –always fascinated with the little and the humble– has seen in the vocation of a brother a “greater participation in and imitation of the radicalism of his self-emptying, that by which, moved by love for men, he became a servant obedient unto death on a cross to give his life for many5 in order to transfigure the world6.

And so, for 33 years now7, our precious charism of evangelizing cultures has counted you among its most powerful allies. To the point that both you and we priests are indispensably associated in a single evangelizing vocation, that is, the vocation “to bring under the lordship of Jesus Christ all that is authentically human, even in the most difficult situations and in the most adverse conditions”8; each one in his own way, of course.

Notice that it is now unthinkable to carry on such apostolic works as the Hogarcitos, for example, without your help. And how many of the Institute’s apostolates in parishes, in monasteries and in the various missions would become difficult or perhaps be cut short without your help: I know that there is one who teaches Bible courses9, others who help with various works of service and hospitality in the missions, and so we could mention several more examples… That is why our Proper Law states: “The Religious Brothers participate in their own way in the pastoral solicitude of the [Institute]”10. And this way, it seems to me, is that of complementarity within the same religious life and under the same founding charism.

So, on the one hand, there is the help with the apostolic activities of the Institute, which in itself is a great thing. But in addition, I consider of capital importance and great value the contribution of the Brothers to community life, made up mostly of priests, and the great evangelizing witness they thereby give to others. For our Proper Law says that through “the fraternal life of the missionaries, that alone is already doing mission. Because there is no better witness than that of Christian charity”11. And elsewhere it says that “all the fruitfulness of religious life depends on the quality of fraternal life in common”12. Therefore, your role within community life is a very important factor for the mission and requires no small amount of work in virtue.

John Paul the Great said: “I want to remind all religious –brothers and priests– of their complementarity. To the religious priest, committed to many pastoral activities, the brother recalls that religious life has a community dimension that he must not forget. To the brother, committed to humble domestic work or to tasks of service, the priest recalls the apostolic dimension of what he does”13. Something we must always keep in mind.

It also seems important to me to recognize the specific role and the great influence that the Religious Brothers of greater experience can exercise over the younger Brothers. All of which certainly redounds to the benefit of the whole Institute.

Let us move on, then, to the second and last point of this sermon:

2. Contributions of the Brothers to the mission of the Institute

In a world accustomed to using words like “the greatest,” “super,” “the top of the top,” where it seems that value is given only to what is splendid, to what is great, to what is most showy, works of service are without doubt of incalculable value when it comes to mission and convey a very eloquent witness to the people who see them, and above all to those who benefit from your attentions: for it brings them the message of Christ, who said I have come to serve, not to be served, and who is the same one who promised that a cup of water will not go without its reward.

The splendid charism of our Institute has impelled us from the beginning not to shy away from the missionary adventure. But that is not something of the past, nor is it something that concerns only the youngest. I know that there are some among you here who long ago left their own country to go and put themselves at the service of peoples where the mission was just beginning (Guyana, Kazakhstan, Peru…), or who, even while remaining in their countries of origin, helped in the arduous beginnings of several missions.

Today too our Institute needs you, and very much. Because you continue to be “co-responsible for the missionary activity”14 that has been entrusted to us. That is why the Capitular Fathers, speaking specifically of you, said that “there are many apostolates, including intellectual and teaching ones, to which you can devote yourselves with much fruit, after due preparation. There are also trades you can learn and take on, which will be a gift of Providence in any mission”15.

So on this occasion I also want to encourage you warmly to give an ever more vigorous impulse to the missionary commitment of each one of you in the service of the Institute and of the Church. The proclamation of the Gospel is more necessary today than ever. And many Brothers are needed who, forgetful of themselves, want to spend themselves for the good of others, to bring souls closer to God and God closer to souls.

I also take this opportunity to exhort you to collaborate in vocational ministry, not only for the priestly life, but also and very especially for the life of a Religious Brother within the Institute. So do not hesitate to propose that way of life of a Religious Brother of the Incarnate Word that makes you so happy. Through your enthusiasm, help others discover the joy of giving themselves totally to Christ in a religious community, in order to share better the gifts of God and to proclaim more forcefully the message of the Word made flesh.

[Peroratio] In short, what I would like to tell you is this: rekindle in yourselves the awareness and the joy of your state as consecrated persons, as Religious Brothers within this Institute of ours, with all the richness and breadth of the service you can render. Truly, much is expected of you… all the Brothers of the Institute can and must play a most important role in the missionary work of the Institute; that is why you are called “key pieces.” Realize the special mission that God has entrusted to you in the life of the Church.

Know how to use all the resources of your insight to grasp the needs of people, whether priests or ordinary people in the apostolate, so as then to try to respond with all the richness of your heart. From this also follows the need to form yourselves so that the service you render may be ever more worthy of that Jesus whom you serve in each person. Live always content in the daily exercise of your tasks, because it is written that God loves a cheerful giver16.

Let us ask the Blessed Virgin, especially under the title of Our Lady of Luján, to obtain from her Son numerous and generous vocations of Brothers for our Institute. And to Her we also entrust each one of you, so that She may be for each one of you “the Virgin of the yes,” as St. John Paul II said, “the Virgin of fidelity and of total self-giving to the mission; and may She also be the Virgin of hope, which you must proclaim to all men, first making it a reality in your hearts”17.

All this we ask as we continue to pray in the Holy Mass.
