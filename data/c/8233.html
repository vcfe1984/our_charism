March 7, 1984 – March 7, 2024

Medallion of St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort as it is found today
in the Basilica of Our Lady of Luján
Buenos Aires – Argentina

In his last book, El Señor es mi Pastor. Memoria y Profecía, Fr. Buela wrote: “On May 3, 1981, came the founding grace (that is, in the second year after I began to be in Villa Progreso). I was there in 1981, 1982 and 1983 until March 1984”1. And he continues: “On March 7, 1984, we fulfilled a vow we had made to Our Lady of Luján in thanksgiving for the possibility of being able to begin our experience of religious life” 2.

That is what we remember today with heartfelt gratitude, first of all to our Mother and Queen and, in the second place, to our Founder. For on a day like today, 40 years ago now, Fr. Buela and all those who had joined him to begin the foundation of the Institute placed, in fulfillment of that vow, the medallion of St. Louis Marie in the Basilica of Luján.

And Fr. Buela himself gives us details of it, both in the book María de Luján and in the book we quoted earlier, in which we read: “The artist Amado Armas made a beautiful medallion of black Lunel marble, and on it, in white cement, the profile of St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort with the legend ‘Totus tuus.’ It was for many years in the first chapel of the first ambulatory on the right of the Basilica of Luján, but afterwards it was moved to the left entrance of the Basilica, past the door on the right. The sculptor was from the parish of Versalles, in the city of Buenos Aires, and he made me a replica, which is in the chapel of the Annunciation at the Seminary of the Finca; I had it with a large relic of St. Louis Marie, of red cloth, but the relic disappeared”3.

Seventeen days after the fulfillment of that vow, there began in San Rafael “this missionary reality” which is the Institute of the Incarnate Word. That is why this is a day that cannot pass unnoticed among us. For certainly each of our vocations has been begotten by the Most Pure Virgin of Luján4, but even more, she has called us to consecrate ourselves in an Institute that is distinguished by being essentially Marian5, whose spirituality is identified with the “Hail Mary,” with the “Angelus”6, and in which all its members are called to “be Apostles of Mary, giving ourselves to her in the maternal slavery of love and doing ‘everything through Mary, with Mary, in Mary and for Mary’”7. “She is the Mother of Jesus who has willed to be concretely ours”8, our Founder used to say.

What is significant is that Fr. Buela wanted us to consecrate ourselves to Our Lady not in just any way but, as he himself said, “by the grace of God, by the inspiration of Our Lady, we consecrate ourselves to her with a fourth vow according to the letter and the spirit of St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort”9.

It has been said on some other occasion that he is the first saint named in the Constitutions10 and that the only book by a saint that our Proper Law recommends must be read is precisely the Circular Letter to the Friends of the Cross11, written in his own hand by our beloved St. Louis Marie. Even more: the Directory of Spiritual Direction says that this “wisdom of the cross” in which we must be specialists12 is the wisdom of the cross as St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort (and St. John of the Cross) understood and taught it. We mention this in order to gauge the importance of both things: the vow to Our Lady of Luján and Fr. Buela’s choice that the medallion to be offered to Our Lady should be precisely of the great St. Louis Marie.

The name of St. Louis Marie appears explicitly thirty times in the body of the Father’s last book. And his works are quoted just as many times.

Strengthening hope

Now it is worth highlighting that Fr. Buela devotes the last three chapters of his book to treating the second coming of Christ, but from the perspective of “the eyes and heart of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary”13. And he says that he chooses to do so in this way for two reasons: 1) to cut off at the root the fatalistic and catastrophist tendencies, without hope, of some; 2) to strengthen the hope of many who know how to face with courage those who, in different ways, are children of the Antichrist.

Our Directory of Spirituality, 315, gives a succinct but precise description of the signs preceding the second coming of Christ, saying: “There will therefore be a time when men will turn away from the truth and take pleasure in iniquity14, a time in which the majority will not have authentic faith in Jesus Christ. The certainty that this universal apostasy will one day occur must lead us not to hitch ourselves to the triumphal chariot of any movement or ideology that is set up apart from Christ, even if it seems that the whole world follows it. We must always follow Christ. And even if the enemies seem to be the majority, we must say: ‘we are surrounded on all sides; let us not let them escape.’”

Fr. Buela, then, seems to suggest to us—forcefully—that in order to follow Christ always, what one must do is be closely united to the Most Blessed Virgin Mary. And he gives the reason for this using several texts of the illustrious French saint, from which we select a few:

– Because “Mary is entirely relative to God, and I would very well call her the relation to God, she who exists only in relation to God”15.

– “Because she is the dawn that precedes and announces the Sun of justice, Jesus Christ”16.

– “Because she is the way by which Jesus Christ came to us the first time, and she will be so also when He comes the second time, though in a different manner”17.

– “Because she is the sure means and the direct and immaculate way to go to Jesus Christ and to find Him perfectly”18.

– “Because Mary must shine forth, more than ever, in the latter times in mercy, power and grace: in mercy, to gather and lovingly welcome the poor sinners and those gone astray who will be converted and will return to the Catholic Church; in power against the enemies of God, the idolaters, schismatics, […] the hardened impious, who will rise up terribly to seduce and make fall, with promises and threats, all those who oppose them; and finally in grace, to encourage and sustain the valiant soldiers and faithful servants of Jesus Christ, who will fight for the interests of the Lord”19.

And quoting the words of the Incarnate Word: You are those who have persevered with me in my trials, and I prepare for you the kingdom as my Father prepared it for me, so that you may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom, and sit on thrones to judge the twelve tribes of Israel20, Fr. Buela comments: “If the Parousia ends in a great banquet, we must act in such a way that we may be at it, instead of being catastrophists without hope. Everything of God is always positive, and very positive! Heaven is an ‘exuberant extravagance’!, exclaims Cornelio Fabro!”21.

Mary, our Hope

In the Salve Regina, which we pray so often, we call the Mother of God ‘our Hope,’ and St. Louis Marie applies to Our Lady the symbol of the anchor, saying that she is the firm anchor that holds fast and prevents those who entrust themselves to her from being shipwrecked on the stormy sea of this world, where so many perish for not clinging to her22. Let us understand well what we have so often heard Fr. Buela say: “in the future, she is all our hope, because she is the Mother of Him who is ‘our hope’”23. And as St. Louis Marie says, let us too “tie our souls to her hope as to a firm anchor”24.

“The saints who have been saved were firmly attached to her, and to her they bound others so that they might persevere in virtue. Happy, then, a thousand times over, are the Christians who now cling faithfully and entirely to Mary as to a firm anchor! The stormy onslaughts of this world will not be able to submerge them or make them lose their heavenly treasures. Happy are those who enter into Mary as into Noah’s ark! The waters of the flood of sins that drown so many people will not harm them. Happy are those who cling to the faithful Mother and Virgin, who always remains faithful and always responds with love to those who love her!”25.

*****

On this day, then, once again, we entrust ourselves to Our Lady; we ask her for each and every one of us, for all the members of our Congregation, even for those who were, and in a special way for those who will be… whom we do not know, but in Heaven she knows them. And let us pray for the missionaries, men and women, especially for those who are working in places of great difficulty. And may love, tender devotion to our good Mother, always grow in us.

May St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort, who in a particular way showed his greatness of soul in tribulations and difficulties, which were many and great, intercede for each one of us and for our Institute and keep us firm in the hope that does not disappoint.

As true devotees of Mary, treasurer and dispenser of all graces, let us recite together the Magnificat to thank God for the graces He has granted us throughout these 40 years. Let us celebrate!

Medallion of St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort as it is found today
in the Chapel of the Annunciation of the Seminary Mary, Mother of the Incarnate Word
San Rafael – Mendoza – Argentina
