From his fullness we have all received grace upon grace

[Exordium] Dear brothers and sisters, as we reach the end of the year and look back on everything that has happened in our lives, in the life of the Institute, in the Church, we see how, if we try to have a real view of things, all the events of history, and particularly those that happen in our own lives, are interwoven with one another as if forming a golden chain that always leads us unfailingly toward God, from whom all good proceeds1. We must always be very aware of this, because it is, in the final analysis, the truth of life and of existence… of our life and of our existence.

And that is why, finally, we must conclude the year saying, and marveling at, how many graces we have to be thankful for. And we must be very aware that, even though some of the things that have happened have been adverse to us, they have been ordained by God for our good –because that is what is most fitting for all of us– and therefore we can truly say with the Evangelist: From his fullness we have all received grace upon grace2.

It is important, I would say fundamental, to keep this clearly present and well illuminated in the soul.

We may suffer persecutions, great misunderstandings, apostolic tribulations that truly grind down the soul, unfounded suspicions, senseless accusations… and thousands of other trials. And this not only on a personal level but also at the level of the Institute.

Notice that every tribulation, and especially persecution, in order to be blessed –says the Directory of Spirituality– must necessarily fulfill two requirements: that we be “reviled for the sake of Christ,” and that “what is said against us be false”3. As long as that is the case, on our part we must rejoice that God tests us in that way.

That is why, when some say “the IVE has problems,” we should smile and feel sorry for whoever says it, because in reality we have the happiness of ‘suffering something’ out of loyalty to God4, and that is an enormous grace, which –as our Proper Law also teaches– implies “the full and total rejection of the evil world”5.

At times we might ask ourselves: why do we have ‘problems’? (if indeed they can be called problems). We have ‘problems’ for defending the truth, for not being tributaries6, for defending the Patrimony of the Institute7, for being Thomists, for living forgetful of ourselves in order to spend ourselves and wear ourselves out in the care of those who are so often forgotten or rejected by their own families or by the State and who depend on us to live, for proclaiming the truth of Catholic dogma and morals with our efforts and our lives, and for so many other things… How can anyone think we are not going to have problems? It would be worrying if we did not have them, and God save us from not having them…

We might think that those who cause us problems (let us realize this too) are in general those who do not love these things, those who do not love religious life (that is why they attack it or abandon it), those who do not love the perennial philosophy, nor the poor and the needy, nor the missions or the new foundations, nor formation in Thomism, nor the increase and perseverance of so many young vocations.

That is why our Proper Law warns us very clearly “to be very careful not to turn over and over our ills”8, that is, never to let the fatalistic and negative thought that ‘we have problems’ settle in among us, because in reality what we have, the so-called ‘problems,’ are great blessings for which to rejoice and which move us to love God even more. Paraphrasing St. Edith Stein, we too can and must say that from crosses (or from ‘problems,’ if you like) we always come out renewed and strengthened.

* * *

So the truth is what it always is (and these are principles to which we must cling): it does not matter much what happens in our lives, but rather how one reacts to it. That is why, in view of the new year that is beginning, I wanted to offer you three considerations on divine providence (which are very well known, but which it is good always to have engraved in the soul) in the hope that they may help to reaffirm the firm hope we must always have in God and the conviction that everything that happens, happens for our good.

So that –as St. Paul says– the God of hope may fill you with complete joy9.

First consideration

1. We must always keep in mind that everything that happens has been foreseen and known by God from all eternity and has been willed by God, or at least permitted by Him.

It is healthy to think about this. And it is also very important. God’s knowledge does not increase like ours, which passes from ignorance to wisdom. He knows everything and everything is present before His eyes. The fall of Adam and Eve in Paradise, so to speak, did not catch God taking a nap. God is Knowledge, He is not a scientist: God knows everything, He learns nothing by experience, as we do.

Put another way, He does not look at us the way we look at an anthill, watching us go in and out of the house, going to work, and then telling a secretary angel to note down that we were unkind to the sister we just passed.

One author asked: Why is it that we always think God looks only at the bad things we do and never at the good ones? And he answered: God does not keep a file of all the acts we perform. We keep the accounts of all of them in our own books. Our conscience takes down the dictation we give it.

God knows all things not because He looks at us from up there, but because He looks within Himself, as the Cause of all things. God never tries to read over our shoulder. An architect can tell you how many rooms the house will have and the exact size of each one long before the house is built, because he is the ‘cause’ of that house being made. God is the cause of the being of all things. He knows them before they happen. Like those old film reels that contained the whole story of the movie before it was projected on the screen. The same happens with God: He knows everything before the events take place on the stage of life.

What one must not think is that because God knows everything, He has predestined some to go to heaven or to hell independently of their merits and overriding the freedom of each person. That is why the study of freedom is so important.

For the knowledge that someone is going to act in a certain way is not the immediate cause of our acting. The fact that you now know you are seated is not the cause of your being seated, nor does it prevent you from standing up. The Blessed Virgin could have refused to be the Mother of God, and Judas could have resisted the temptation of betrayal. The fact that God knew how each one would act did not make them act the way each one did.

Now, since in God there is no future, the fact that He knows beforehand what is going to happen does not mean that He pre-causes it. You could be standing on the roof of this house and see a person approaching the house. You know that before entering, he has to pass through the garden gate, go under such-and-such a tree, walk along a path, knock on the door, etc. You see all those possibilities, but you are not the cause of that person going through all those areas.

That is why, when a person asks us: “If God knew I was going to be damned, why did He create me?” The answer is: “God did not create you so that you would be damned. You do that yourself.” The universe is moral and therefore conditional: Behold, I stand at the door and knock10. God does not break doors down. The latch is on our side, not on God’s side.

This is the first thing one must know in any circumstance of life. Now, the second.

Second consideration

2. God allows or permits evil, but always for a greater good related to His love and to the salvation of souls.

This is extremely important. The doctrine of evil, of providence and of predestination, far from being a subject to avoid, was of great profit for him to consider. And truly it is. God permits evil. Let us remember that our Lord said to Judas: This is your hour11. Evil has its hour, but God has His day, Bishop Fulton Sheen used to say.

When some evil befalls us, we must always remember that God can draw good out of evil, because while the power to do evil lies with men, the effects of our evil acts are beyond our control and therefore in the hands of God. Joseph’s brothers were free to throw him into the pit, but from that moment on Joseph was in the hands of God. You meant to do me harm, but God disposed it for good, in order to preserve the life of many people.

We must never judge the evil that God permits by its immediate effects but rather by its final effects. To complain, to become sad or downcast in the face of these evils is foolish and is a failure to realize the hand of God in all things. It is failing to realize that our miniature minds cannot grasp God’s plan.

A doctor would not allow an operation if he could not restore the patient’s health through it, and God would not permit an evil if He could not draw a good from it. One must never forget this.

Third consideration

3. We must do everything in our power to fulfill the will of God as it is made known to us through the Church, the commandments, the Constitutions, our legitimate superiors and the obligations of our state of life. And everything that is beyond our power we must surrender and submit to the will of God.

Another truth that, in my view, is fundamental. One must clearly note the distinction between what is within our power and what is beyond our power. We must never have a fatalistic attitude. Some things are indeed under our control. The one who is negative in the face of evils fails to recognize that –within certain limits– his will can affect the events of life. It is God’s will that men have free will to use it under His subordination and thus to be happy, always, whatever happens.

But here we want to deal with those things that are beyond our reach (which is most of what happens to us), for example, illnesses, false accusations, the death of a loved one, a sickness, the contradictions one does not expect to have, and you can continue mentally adding your own examples. God could have prevented all these events, could He not? But if He did not, we must always know (because it is the truth of things) that it is for a higher reason. And therefore we must learn to say with Our Lady: Fiat.

We must learn to be docile and to submit to the will of God…and everything will be ours. Even the most irritating things of this life can be stepping stones to our salvation, and a cause of joy for our souls. We must never complain, still less lament, but see providence and the hand of God in everything. Everything that happens, happens for our good (and it is good to give due weight to each of these words). A saint used to say that one act of thanksgiving when things do not go as we like is worth far more than a thousand acts of gratitude when things go according to our will.

* * *

[Peroration] That is why, on this beautiful day, the eve of the Solemnity of the Mother of God, who is our most sublime example of what humility is and of the most perfect fulfillment of the will of God, I would like to read you what St. Luigi Orione used to say to his own: “Fiat! Pronounce this gentle word, O my sons and friends, pronounce it with every breath, with every heartbeat, with every movement of the lips. God will always understand it in the way in which you want Him to understand it, now as a prayer, now as an act of faith in doubt, as an act of hope in fear, and always as an act of love.

Fiat! Into your hands, then, into your hands, O my God!… Work, work this clay, O my God, give it a shape and then break it to pieces again; it is yours and belongs to the one who takes your place, and it will never again have anything to say… Suffering, raised up, brought low, useful for something or useless to all, I will always adore you and I will always be yours, O my God! No one will separate me from you! In joys and in sorrows I will always be yours, O my sweetest love, Jesus. Solitary and unknown like the flower of the desert, wandering like the bird without a nest, always, always, Lord and most gentle love of my soul, there will come from my lips the submissive word of her whom you gave me as Mother: Fiat! Fiat! Be it done unto me according to your word!”12.

In view of all the events of this year that is now ending, knowing how to have a supernatural outlook on all of them, but also clothing ourselves with a deep vision of faith to begin this new year, the invitation is this: to live in our lives the mystery of the Incarnation; that, after all, is our charism.

That is to say: like Our Lady, to give our whole heart to God as if we no longer possessed ourselves –because in truth that is how it is–; our will is ours only to do His. Let us always pray, not that God may change His will, but that He may change ours. Let us not measure the Goodness of God by His readiness to do our will (that would be a very grave error, almost a blasphemy). Let us always trust in God, who makes all things work together for good.

We call God Father every day when we pray the Our Father, and do we not believe that He wants the best for His children? Let us beware of always thinking that we could do more good for souls if we were not sick, if we had another position, if we had the opportunities, if we had the gifts another person has, or if what happens to us did not happen, or if people did not speak ill of us… What matters in life is not where we are, but whether we are doing the will of God. The holy abandonment the saints and mystics speak of.

It is worth recalling here, it seems to me, the counsel St. John of the Cross gave to a nun: “[Remain] well forgetful of all things so as to be able to enjoy God well in solitude, not caring that they do with you what they will for the love of God, since you are not your own but God’s”13.

It is always edifying to read that passage of St. Louis Marie which speaks of how God always exalts the humble, and that is why He made Mary sovereign of heaven and earth, treasurer of His riches, dispenser of His graces, worker of His wonders, mediatrix of men, exterminator of the enemies of God and faithful companion of His greatness and His triumphs14.

Certainly all the graces received during this year have passed through the precious hands of our Most Pure Mother, and we must not forget that. This, as our Constitutions say, “makes manifest the dominion and maternal providence that Mary has over all things, but especially over faithful souls”15. May this very thing be one more motive of hope and confident courage for the year that is beginning.

May the Mother of God grant all of us, today and always, to live each day rejoicing in hope16.

Let us continue to give thanks to Her during Holy Mass.
