Homily preached on the occasion of the
1stcentenary of the Birth of St. John Paul II

We have the grace of celebrating the first centenary of the birth of our beloved St. John Paul II in this shrine dedicated to the Mother of God, where he himself, 42 years ago now, said: “I wanted to come here, to these mountains, to sing the Magnificat following in the footsteps of Mary”1. That is why today, in thanksgiving for his birth, we too have come here, to these mountains, to sing the Magnificat following in his footsteps.

St. John Paul II was without any doubt a man of great soul, of magnanimous spirit, and that is why, upon being elected successor of Peter in 1978, to the question of Cardinal Jean Villot: “Do you accept the election?”, he answered: “In the obedience of faith before Christ my Lord, abandoning myself to the Mother of Christ and to the Church, and aware of the great difficulties, I accept”2. And seven days later he came here, to this Marian shrine which in his own words “attracted him in a special way”3. That gesture was the “first, humble response”4 to God’s call: he came to pray to Our Lady. Because, as he himself said in this place, when man is “extremely burdened and harassed by the contingent conditions of daily life, by all that is temporal, by weakness, sin, dejection… Prayer gives meaning to the whole of life, at every moment and in every circumstance”5. Thus he began his pontificate, invoking the maternal help of Mary, and with her name on his lips he ended it.

As Fr. Buela said: St. John Paul II “was great because he showed the Church the need to abandon oneself into the hands of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary, by his word and especially by his filial devotion to the Mother of God”6. Indeed, several authors maintain that Marian devotion was in many respects a particular charism of his pontificate, and that no Pontiff has promoted devotion to Our Lady as strongly and insistently as John Paul II7.

This Marian charism showed itself very clearly in his life. Already as a child he was as if captivated by Our Lady, and after losing his mother, Emilia8, he would visit Most Holy Mary in his parish in Wadowice, where he also received the scapular. His childhood is dotted with pilgrimages to the shrines of Kalwaria and Jasna Gora, where he went with his father. And one cannot speak of Karol Wojtyla without saying that as a young worker he read the Treatise on True Devotion, and without highlighting the immense influence that reading had on the spiritual life of the one who would later be called the “Marian Pope.”

This so filial and so authentic devotion of Karol Wojtyla to the Mother of God became evident to the eyes of the world in his Petrine mission, through his works and his gestures, in his Magisterium and in his heartfelt words. And it is to this that I wanted to dedicate this homily in his honor.

On his gestures

Proof of John Paul II’s love for the Mother of God is that of the 102 pastoral journeys he made outside Italy, most had as their high point his visit to some shrine of Our Lady. In fact, titles of Our Lady all over the world have seen him on his knees, have heard his expressions of affection, have received his petitions for the Church and the world. No Pope, before or after John Paul II, has made so many pilgrimages to Marian Shrines around the world, consecrating each country, each continent, each family and the whole Church to the Mother of God. Note that in just the first 5 years of his pontificate he consecrated to the Most Blessed Virgin Mexico, Poland, Ireland, the United States, Zaire, Ghana, Brazil, Germany, the Philippines and all of Asia, Nigeria, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Argentina, Spain, Honduras and all of Central America, Haiti, France and Austria9.

Of the 8 golden roses he gave as Vicar of Christ, 7 went to the Mother of Heaven: at the Shrine of Jasna Góra, Poland (1979); at the Shrine of Knock, Ireland (1979); to Our Lady of Luján, Argentina (1982); at the Shrine of Matka Boża Kalwaryjska, Poland (1987); to Our Lady of Evangelization in Lima, Peru (1988); to Our Lady of the Conception of Sameiro, Portugal (2003); and to Our Lady of Lourdes, France (2004). And the eighth rose he offered to the spouse of Our Lady, at St. Joseph’s Oratory in Quebec, Canada (2004). On the other hand, we cannot fail to mention the gift to Our Lady of Fatima of the bullet that pierced him in that attack of May 13, 1981.

His Marian works

Another fact that reveals to us the Marian imprint John Paul II wished to give the Church was his having declared 2 of the 3 Marian years there have been throughout the history of the Church: the first was declared by Pope Pius XII (1953-1954); the other 2 by Pope John Paul the Great: the first of them in 1987-1988; the second from 2002-2003, better known as the “Year of the Rosary”10. On the occasion of that first Marian year he published his sixth encyclical, Redemptoris Mater, on the Blessed Virgin Mary, and within the framework of the Year of the Rosary he published an apostolic letter, Rosarium Virginis Mariae, coinciding with the beginning of the twenty-fifth year of his pontificate. In that apostolic letter—as you know—he introduced the luminous mysteries of the rosary, after the mysteries of the rosary had remained ‘untouchable’ for almost a millennium.

During that Marian year of ’87-’88 he visited all the most important Marian churches of Rome, and he frequently took advantage of the moment of the Angelus or the Regina Coeli to commemorate some title of Our Lady from some other part of the world.

And so that we may see that his filial devotion to Mary reached even to the details: on December 7, 1981, he enthroned in St. Peter’s Square the mosaic of Mary, Mother of the Church, and on that occasion he said: “I am happy to inaugurate this testimony of our love and devotion, and I trust that she, with constant solicitude, will continue ‘caring for the brethren of her Son who still journey on earth surrounded by dangers and difficulties’11, allowing our generation too to experience the invincible power of her maternal protection.” Likewise, you will remember when he handed over the Icon of the Mother of God of Kazan to Patriarch Alexy II, as his gift to the holy Russian Orthodox Church and to the whole Russian people, on August 28, 2004—one of John Paul II’s last public Marian gestures.

Moreover, he renewed 5 times the consecration of Russia and the world to the Immaculate Heart according to the request of Our Lady at Fatima: The first was from his hospital bed on June 7, 1981, after suffering an assassination attempt, where in reality he makes an act of entrustment of the Church, of men and of peoples to Most Holy Mary12. The second was a repetition of the first on December 8 of the same year in the Piazza di Spagna13. The third was at Fatima on May 13, 1982, while he was on pilgrimage to thank Our Lady for having saved him from death the previous year. The fourth was during the Synod of 1983: the Holy Father repeated the consecration in essentially the same terms during Mass on October 17. And finally, as you know, on March 25, 1984, in St. Peter’s Square and before an image of Our Lady brought from Fatima especially for the occasion, in union with all the bishops of the world, he consecrated the world to the Immaculate Heart for the last time. Although ‘those who know’ say that only the acts of May 13, 1982 and of March 25, ’84 can be considered ‘official’ acts of consecration, and the others as preparation for those two14.

On his Marian Magisterium

In addition to the encyclical and apostolic letter we have just mentioned, John Paul II dedicated three years of general audiences—70 audiences, to be precise15—to imparting the most extensive Marian catechesis that any Pope had ever given in the whole history of the Church. He always concluded all his pontifical documents, his allocutions, homilies, addresses, etc. with a Marian invocation or by making a clear connection of the topic with the life of the Most Blessed Virgin. We could say that he wished to seal each topic by directing us to her who lived all these mysteries fully in communion with Christ. It is as if he had wished to sign each of his interventions with the presence of Our Lady.

Just to give us an idea of the singular contribution made by the Marian magisterium of John Paul II, Cardinal Ratzinger, speaking of the encyclical Redemptoris Mater, said: “The Pope’s fundamental thesis is the following: the specific character of Mary’s mediation consists in the fact that it is a maternal intercession, ordered to an ever-new birth of Christ in the world […] It is true that the Second Vatican Council had already used the title of Mediatrix and had also spoken of Mary’s mediation. However, until now, this theme had not been set forth so extensively in any magisterial document. As regards content, the encyclical goes beyond what the Council already said, while keeping to its terminology. But the encyclical deepens the conciliar elements and gives them a new weight for theology and piety”16.

So that, at a historical moment in the Church when many saw devotion to Mary as a need of the uneducated, of the simple, the Holy Spirit raised to the See of Peter a man of great human, spiritual and intellectual quality, who knew how to teach us all that kings as well as shepherds must prostrate themselves before Jesus, who is in the arms of His Mother.

On his words

His words revealed a Marian spirituality that was inwardly convinced, tender, constant and very holy. St. John Paul II was convinced that each person must learn to see himself in the words of the Master on the Cross: Behold your son; son, behold your Mother, and that these words, which were Christ’s testament, had to be welcomed—embraced, interiorized—by each soul, because that was the express desire of the Incarnate Word. That was decisive in his spirituality, I mean, the fact that he took on that filial love, letting Our Lady exercise her whole maternal mission. And that is precisely what he sought to transmit to all souls: a profound trust and filial love for Most Holy Mary.

His Marian piety, so intimate, so tender and so theologically enriched, led John Paul II toward a Marian spirituality of profound trust. It is in this sense that at Czestochowa, during his first pilgrimage to Poland, he said: “I am a man of great trust; here I learned to be so. I learned to be a man of profound trust here, in prayer and meditation before the great icon of Mary, the first disciple: Be it done to me according to your Word.”

With what expressions of deep-rooted filial love he spoke of the Mother of God in order to lead souls to that same trust: “Icon of God’s tenderness—he called her—she shows herself attentive to the needs of others, eager to respond to them, and rich in compassion”17. He calls her a “sign of contradiction” by her association with her Son and at the same time a “sign of hope”; that is why he says: “The woman who is venerated as Queen of the Apostles without being placed within the hierarchical structure of the Church, and who nevertheless made all hierarchy possible because she gave to the world the Shepherd and Bishop of our souls. […] This is the Woman of history and of destinies!, the woman who encourages us today”18.

Finally, and now to conclude, I would like to repeat those heartfelt words he addressed to young people at one of the last youth days, which suit us so well at this time. John Paul the Great said: “On the Cross, the Son can pour out His suffering into the heart of the Mother. Every child who suffers feels this need. […] Entrust yourselves to her with complete trust! […] Know, dear friends, that this mission is not easy. And it can even become impossible if you count only on yourselves. But what is impossible for men is possible for God19. The true disciples of Christ are aware of their own weakness. That is why they place all their trust in the grace of God, which they welcome with an undivided heart, convinced that without Him they can do nothing20. What characterizes them and distinguishes them from the rest of men is not their talents or natural dispositions. It is their firm determination to walk in the footsteps of Jesus”21, holding the hand of Mary.

*****

Everything for Jesus Christ through Mary. Thus he lived, thus he fulfilled his mission and thus he died, with the Totus Tuus on his lips and in his heart, this most worthy son of Mary. In his spiritual testament John Paul II, like another Christ on the cross, also wished, on leaving this world, to leave us in the hands of Mary: “Into these same maternal hands I leave everything and everyone with whom my life and my vocation have united me. Into these hands I leave above all the Church, as well as my nation and all humanity.” Through his intercession, may we too be worthy children of Mary and one day come to rest beside her heart. Deo gratias for the great gift of John Paul the Great!
