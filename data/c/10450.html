Sermon for the First Sunday of Advent

Today we Christians celebrate the season of Advent, that season in which we prepare ourselves in a special way to celebrate the feast of Christmas in a Christian manner. That is why it is a time in which one should try in a particular way to grow in prayer, in the Christian life, so that when that day comes, one more year, one may have a renewed heart and be able to fill it with the coming of the Savior.

Advent as preparation for the coming of the Messiah

That is why we must await the birth of Christ, await His coming and prepare ourselves for that day, in the same way that the world in its time prepared itself for the coming of the Messiah. Or better said, as God prepared the coming of His Son.

Reason tells us that if God really decided to become incarnate, the least God could have done to support His claim would have been to announce His coming beforehand. If God sent someone on His own behalf, or if He Himself came with a message of vital importance for all men, it seems reasonable that He should first let men know when His messenger would come, when he would be born, where he would live, the doctrine he would teach, the enemies he would stir up, the program he would adopt for the future and the kind of death destined for him. According to the measure in which the messenger conformed to these announcements, one could judge the validity of his claims.

Moreover, reason assures us that if God did not do such a thing, nothing could prevent some impostor from appearing in history and saying: “I come from God,” or “an angel appeared to me and told me to announce this,” as happens nowadays with so many sects and visionaries who appear day after day wanting to claim to be divine envoys. If a foreigner arrived in San Rafael and said he was a diplomat of some country, the least he would be asked for are his documents, that is, the credentials attesting that he does in fact represent a certain government. And if this is necessary for the delegates of other countries, with how much greater reason is it asked when someone claims to be a divine envoy.

In this Christ accredits His mission more than anyone. Socrates had no one to foretell his birth. Buddha had no one to announce beforehand his coming and his message. Every founder of some sect appeared in history without anyone expecting him; no one ever heard of them until propaganda spread their name. But the case of Christ was different. Because of the prophecies contained in the Old Testament, His coming was not an unexpected event. There were no predictions about Buddha, Confucius, Muhammad or anyone else who may be going around, but there were about Christ. Others came and simply said: “Here I am, believe in me” . These, therefore, were only men in the midst of men, and not an incarnate God.

Awaited by all

Christ was the only one who stood out from this line, saying: “search the scriptures of the Jewish people; they speak of me.” Christ was not a chance occurrence in history; rather, the world was waiting for Him.

I. The Jews were waiting for Him: it is true that the beautiful prophecies of the Old Testament can be better understood in the light of their fulfillment. But one need only read the prophets: it can never be doubted that these predictions point to Christ and the kingdom He established. In the Old Testament it is prophesied that through them all the nations of the earth would be blessed (as indeed happened); that the tribe of Judah would have preeminence over the other Hebrew tribes (and from this tribe Christ was born); in the Old Testament is prophesied the virginal birth of the Messiah, the accounts of the Man of sorrows are plainly there, which announced the death of the Savior, that He would be born in Bethlehem, that He would be humble and despised. In whom but Christ did these prophecies find their fulfillment? That is why in Christ there is a singularity that sets Him apart from all the other founders of world religions. He was the only person foretold.

II. But note that not only the Jews awaited Christ, but also the pagans; that is, without knowing divine revelation, the world had a certain thirst for the Incarnation, for God to become man.

Tacitus, a Roman writer, said: “People were generally persuaded,” on the basis of the ancient prophecies, “that the East was to prevail, and that from Judea would come the Master and Sovereign of the world.”

Suetonius said: “There was throughout the East an ancient and constant belief that, with the support of prophecies undoubtedly certain, the Jews were to attain supreme power” . How is all this to be explained?

In China writings are found in which the same state of expectation is shown, but because it was on the other side of the world, it believed that the great Sage was to be born in the West. The annals of the Celestial Empire contain the following account: “In the 24th year of Chao Wang, on the 8th day of the fourth moon, a light appeared on the southwest side that illuminated the king’s palace. The monarch, surprised by the brightness, questioned the wise men. They showed him books in which it was indicated that this prodigy signified the appearance of the great Holy One of the West, whose religion was to be introduced into their country.”

Even the Greeks, without knowing it, were awaiting Christ, since Aeschylus in his Prometheus, six centuries before His coming, wrote: “Do not expect an end to come to this curse until God comes to take upon His head the pains of your own sins, by way of expiation.”

And if not, how did the Magi from the East know that one day He was to come?

Certainly, not only the Jews awaited the birth of a great king, a sage and a savior, but also Plato and Socrates spoke of the Word and of the universal sage “who was to come.” Confucius spoke of the Holy One. Aeschylus of a Savior and Redeemer who would free man from the original curse. All these persons stood on the side of expectation on the part of the pagans.

What separates Christ from all men, what distinguishes Him, is that above all He was awaited; even the Gentiles felt a longing for a liberator or redeemer. This fact alone already distinguishes Him from any impostor who might wish to proclaim himself a religious leader.

***

In short, the world awaits Christ; the Jews await Him and so do the pagans; He is the most important Being in history; we cannot be the ones who remain indifferent before such an event. Quite the contrary, we must dispose ourselves so that there may also be a Christmas in our hearts, that they may be filled with God. May we find in this preparation the occasion to take up Christianity seriously, with renewed enthusiasm, so that God may be all in all.So that at Christmas the feast may be complete, because unlike many who know neither whom nor

why they are celebrating, may we know how to rejoice at the birth of Jesus.

Above all we must imitate Our Lady. In Peru there is an image that is very striking; it is found in the Church of the Jesuit Fathers in Lima, and the Peruvians call it the Virgin of the O; it is Our Lady of the Expectation, and it is an image that commemorates the immense joy of the moments before the birth of Jesus. May she grant us the grace to prepare ourselves fittingly for this new Christmas that is approaching.
