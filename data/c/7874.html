[Exordium] Dear Mothers and Sisters, first of all, thank you very much for the invitation to celebrate this Holy Mass with you. It is a joy to see that the sisters have increased in number (it seems to me) and accompany very closely the apostolate of the fathers here in Chile. That is why in this Holy Mass I invite you to give thanks to God for all these years of apostolate as a Family (with a capital F).

Now that we are among family, I would like us to reflect together on a very simple but very profound line of our Directory of Spirituality, which says: “To serve one’s neighbor, even, like Mary, in the simplest tasks”. It is in number 84 of the directory, where it explains what Jesus taught us when He was enclosed for nine months in the womb of Our Lady. Well, one of the things He taught us is precisely that: to serve our neighbor even in the simplest tasks.

The logic of the world vs. the logic of the Incarnation

Many of you here have the privilege of serving the poor directly, the most forgotten or most despised men and women, the children who often do not have access to the enjoyments that children elsewhere have; however, I believe that this is the visible part of the road to God. Yes, because the poor lead to the heart of God! The Incarnate Word Himself, poor among the poor, revealed a great mystery when He told us: “whatever you did to one of the least of these my brothers, you did to Me1; [that is why] serving the needy is the way to attend to Christ in His flesh”2.

Hence it seems very clear to me that the path of sanctification of the Servants throughout the world, but especially here, because of the work you carry out, passes very much through gestures of kindness and service to the poor, which is indeed a gift of God that fits squarely with the purpose of our Religious Family, which includes precisely “living with all radicality the demands of the Incarnation and of the Cross”3 and seeing Christ especially in the poor4.

The logic of the world invites to selfish greed for material things without giving anything in return, and even incites to greed for interior satisfactions or consolations without any sacrifice, or at most, to giving, but always expecting to receive something in return. The logic of the Incarnation, which is the logic of the Son of God, who emptied Himself, taking the condition of a servant5, is quite the opposite. According to the logic of the Incarnate Word, “to love means to give, to give everything that love asks of us, to give immediately, without complaining, with joy, and wishing to be asked for even more”6. That is the program; that is how the Most Blessed Virgin Mary served her Son and her spouse St. Joseph, and that is how we must serve our neighbor. This is a sentence of a Polish blessed, foundress of the Sisters of St. Felix of Cantalice, Blessed Maria Angela Truszkowska7, which seems to me to sum up the principal marks of how the service of a Servant should be.

Qualities of the service of a Servant

Let us look briefly at each of the qualities of this service.

First, it says that the service is a loving service, because “to love means to give”. Ours is not a social assistance service, like someone who gives alms but does not get involved with the good of that person’s soul; you know that better than I do. Ours is not a merely technical service of distribution, but that of well-ordered love of neighbor8, by which we fulfill the command received from our Lord to love our neighbor as ourselves; if it were not so, the apostle James could ask us: What good is it if one of you, on seeing a brother or sister naked or lacking the necessary food, says to them: ‘Go in peace, keep warm and eat’, and does not give them what they need for their body?9. That is why charity toward our neighbor is an exercise of faith that leads us to God. Do you understand me?

Secondly, it says: “to give everything that love asks of us”. And in this sense our proper law points out the following: “It is not enough to give to the poor; one must give oneself”10, like those who exercise a motherly office, forgetful of themselves. Even more, our Directory of Spirituality, speaking of the Servants, says –pay attention– that “all their wealth consists in giving themselves to the Word”11. And how do the Servants give themselves to the Word? “even in the simplest tasks”12, which is what we were saying at the beginning. That is, going out to beg in the market, serving food, cleaning the church, playing with the kids, teaching catechism, visiting families… Doing everything that love asks of us, even in those things that according to the logic of the world are unimportant, and that some might even consider demeaning; but we follow the logic of the Incarnation, which implies self-denial, sacrifice, humiliation.

And, thirdly, the service of a Servant implies “giving immediately, without complaining, with joy, and wishing to be asked for even more”. It is interesting to note that the Directory of Spirituality, among the things it mentions that the Incarnate Word taught us in the womb of Our Lady, includes precisely that of “doing the things of God promptly”13, “because ‘slow calculations are foreign to the grace of the Holy Spirit’14”15. That is why the Servants must be “docile and prompt in carrying out what the Holy Spirit asks of them, like Our Lady, who quickly set out on her way16”17; and serve with joy, without complaining, “working always against the temptation of delay, against the fear of sacrifice and of total self-giving, and against the temptation to take back what we have given by seeking compensations”18.

I know that you here in Chile carry out a magnificent work of charity in all the apostolates you have, and I encourage you to keep working and always doing more and better in this sense, because, as you know very well, “charity is indispensable to evangelize the culture”19, and even the most modest gestures can speak of Jesus Christ.

[Peroration] May the Most Blessed Virgin of Mount Carmel, Patroness of Chile, who holds the Child Jesus in her arms, always remind you that you are called to serve the Incarnate Word in the person of your neighbor, to carry the burden of the needy, of the weakest. And in this self-sacrificing service –as is the service of all mothers– may God fill you with joy, and may the unsearchable riches of Christ20 superabound in your souls.

A grace that we implore from our Lord through the intercession of Our Lady in this Holy Mass.

May God bless you today and always.
