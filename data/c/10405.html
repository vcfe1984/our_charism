Homily of Rev. Fr. Carlos M. Buela, V.E., preached at the Sunday Holy Mass to the young people of the “Blessed Pier Giorgio Frassati” festive oratory, in November 1995

In the Gospel we have just heard, we are told the famous parable of the Pharisee and the tax collector. The Pharisee –who thought himself righteous– enters the temple all full of pride, and speaks to God as one equal to another: “O God! I thank you that I am not like other men, thieves, unjust, adulterers, nor even like this tax collector” (Lk 18:11).

The tax collector, on the contrary –who considered himself a sinner because he was the collector of taxes– had stayed at the back of the temple. Unlike the Pharisee, he takes on a profoundly humble attitude. He keeps his distance, he does not even dare to raise his eyes to heaven, he strikes his breast –as when we pray the “I confess,” as a sign that we consider ourselves sinners and that we are repentant–.

He says from the back of the temple: “O God! Have compassion on me, for I am a sinner!” (Lk 18:13).

The Pharisees were a group of men who had certain characteristics that we are going to analyze. In man, each organ has its own disease: the disease of the eyes is called conjunctivitis; that of the stomach, gastritis; that of the liver, hepatitis; that of the bronchi, bronchitis. In animals the same thing happens: the most common disease in dogs is called rabies, in cows foot-and-mouth disease, and so we could go on with countless examples.

In religion, the disease proper to it is Pharisaism. It is, as it were, the cancer of religion, the syphilis of religion. Our Lord Jesus Christ fought tooth and nail against the Pharisees of his time –against the Pharisees of all times– who are the ones who finally brought him to death. And how does Jesus characterize Pharisaism? He characterizes it perfectly in the twenty-third chapter of the Gospel of St. Matthew. There he describes everything these men did. Let us base ourselves, then, on this chapter to describe the fundamental elements of Pharisaism.

  • First characteristic: hypocrisy.

On the outside, they pretended to be religious, but on the inside they were not. They wore phylacteries hanging on their cloaks, and on their foreheads inscriptions of the Law, of the Torah and of the commandments, because it is written: “…the commandments (of Yahweh) …shall be as a sign between your eyes…” (Dt 6:1.8). But what about the inside? Inside, interiorly, they were corrupt, putrid. That is why Jesus compares them to whitewashed tombs. For them, it was a sin to touch tombs; that is why they whitened them with lime: so that their presence would be noticed and no one would walk on them. From the outside, they looked clean because of the whiteness of the lime, but inside there was only rottenness.

Such were the Pharisees, and such are the Pharisees of all times, in our time as well.

I always say that the seminary is the factory of priests, because that is what it is. But if there is no good formation, it becomes a factory of Pharisees! Because it is very easy to put the emphasis of religion on external things and forget that the main thing is in the interior.

That is why Jesus says in the Gospel of St. Matthew: “They say, and do not do” (Mt 23:3). They said many things, they had hundreds of precepts, which they themselves invented, but they did not keep them. For example, to see the absurdity rabbinic legislation reached, it is enough to consider what the topics of their disputes were: they argued among themselves –in the time of Our Lord– over whether it was lawful to eat the egg that a hen laid on the Sabbath, because in laying the egg, it worked, and on the Sabbath –according to Pharisaic law– one cannot work. Ridiculous!

  • Second characteristic: ostentation.

They wanted to show others their goodness, their piety, their religiosity. “…they make their phylacteries very broad and the tassels of their cloaks very long, they want the first place at banquets and the first seats in the synagogues…” (Mt 23:5-6). It is religion turned into ostentation, an external thing.

  • Third characteristic: contempt for one's neighbor.

It is the case of the Pharisee in the temple: “I am not like other men, thieves, unjust, adulterers, nor even like that tax collector.” They consider others inferior. That is why they are like parsley: they get into everything (in my day parsley was used in every meal; now I think it is no longer used). In conversations they only talk about themselves, about their things, about their problems, about what they did, about what they are going to do, about what they think… They only talk about themselves, and they do not listen to others. Why? Because they despise them, because for them the most important ones are themselves. At bottom, they are the center of the world. They think they are the center of the world!

They despise their neighbor above all if he is ignorant, poor or a sinner. “This man eats with scribes and tax collectors” (Mt 9:11), they said of Jesus.

  • Fourth characteristic: a false delicate conscience.

In some things they are strict, exaggeratedly strict, and in others lax, exaggeratedly lax. As sometimes happens with some, who demand of people things that people cannot do. And they fail to do what they have to do, such as providing the means for them to have Mass on Sunday!

Jesus said it in a marvelous way: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who pay the tithe of mint, anise and cumin –some plants that grew in their gardens– and neglect the most important things of the Law: justice, mercy and faith! These are what you should have practiced, without neglecting the others. Blind guides, who strain out the gnat and swallow the camel!” (Mt 23:23-24).

The law dictated that they could not eat the animal, yet they did eat roast camel, which is a very tasty meat, although it was forbidden by the law. They strained out the gnat, but swallowed the camel!

  • Fifth characteristic: disguise.

It is those people who have a smile at every moment, but since you already know them, you are waiting for them to plunge the dagger into your back. On the outside, they wear sheep's clothing; on the inside, they are wolves. They are “wolves in sheep's clothing,” as Our Lord said.

They hide their lack of mercy toward their neighbor under the cloak of a false religion.

And so it is that the Pharisees bring about the crucifixion of Our Lord Jesus Christ on the pretext that he healed on the Sabbath. They kill the Only Son of God, the Incarnate Word. Why? Because he did not keep an external precept of their religion.

And this, dear brothers –I take this opportunity to say it especially to the young people–, is something that happens today, unfortunately! Many Christians, many Catholics, are Pharisees; they are Catholics in name only, on the outside. Sometimes without fault, because no one has taught them; but sometimes, in some cases, it happens because they fall into this evil, into this deep vice of the religious man, which is to make religion something merely exterior.

Hence also the seriousness of this evil, as Our Lord points out.

What is the greatest seriousness of the Pharisee? The greatest seriousness is religion without love.

The Pharisees are those who neither enter nor let others enter. That is why many times in our groups –of young people or adults– there are people who would like to enter, but do not, because there are Pharisees who do not enter but do not let others enter either.

As Our Lord Jesus Christ also said: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who shut the Kingdom of Heaven to men! You certainly do not enter; and those who are entering you do not allow to enter” (Mt 23:13).

  • What are the remedies?

In the first place, to study the virtues of Our Lord Jesus Christ: patience, humility, love, the spirit of service, self-giving to others.

All the virtues of Our Lord, in order to imitate them. These virtues are diametrically opposed to the vices of the old Pharisees. Our Lord taught an eminently interior religion, which is also manifested exteriorly, but is not mainly exterior, but mainly interior.

And the most important thing of what he came to bring is the New Law, which is faith working through charity in our heart, and that is interior, it is infused.

In the second place, we must manifest with our life, with our works, with our words, evangelical truth and simplicity, against Pharisaic falsehood.

In the third place, we must be humble, as Our Lord teaches: “Whoever humbles himself will be exalted.” And against Pharisaic pride, he teaches: “Whoever exalts himself will be humbled” (Lk 14:11).

In the fourth place, finally, to the contempt that the Pharisees have for the poor, for the ignorant and for sinners, we must oppose the love of Jesus Christ.

If he shed his Blood on the Cross for them, we certainly can and must sacrifice ourselves for that most needy part of humanity.

That is why we must do as the tax collector did, who, without raising his eyes to heaven, strikes his breast, saying: “Have mercy, Lord, for I am a sinner.”

This is the religion that Jesus Christ taught us. And I believe that young people understand this language, because they are looking precisely for that Lord who never fails, for the One who dies no more because he has risen, for the One who has the words of eternal life.

Let us ask Our Lady for this grace.
