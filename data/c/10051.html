Homily preached on Good Shepherd Sunday

[Exordium] Dear brothers and sisters. How the words we have just heard in the Gospel of this Sunday of Eastertide must resound in our ears! How much trust they must inspire in us! How much security for our life! Many times in the Gospel our Lord expresses Himself in these terms, giving us consolation, inspiring trust in us, encouraging us to keep going, not to lose heart, never to despair. These sentiments are, without a doubt, parts of the truth of the Gospel, of the message of salvation that Christ came to bring to men. That is why let us always remember and meditate on these words, on the strength they have and the security they offer us; let us think of the greatness they contain: My sheep hear my voice .. I know them and they follow me. And further on, with all that this means: I give them eternal life and they shall never perish, because no one shall snatch them out of my hand. And to give proof of all this, it is the Father who has given them to me, and He is greater than all … He is greater than all. Who, then, can doubt the value of these statements? From words so firm and clear, spoken by the Incarnate Word, spring all Christian peace and joy. Peace and joy that are founded on the most beautiful promises given by God to His followers, to His sheep. Before such great and concrete assurances, how can we not live in peace, how can we not be happy in this life, even in the midst of so many tribulations and sorrows… No one shall snatch them out of my hand… I will give them eternal life1…

These very beautiful words of today’s Gospel constitute the end of the parable of the good shepherd; the Church always places a passage of this Gospel on this day on which the World Day of Prayer for priestly and religious vocations is celebrated, asking God that, in accordance with that text of Jeremiah, He grant us shepherds after His own heart2.

1. To be a sheep of Christ

In the first place, then −and it is the basis of everything for making ourselves worthy of these blessings of the Lord− it is necessary, before all else, as the only indispensable condition asked of us, to strive with all our strength, as the greatest of our longings, to be a sheep of Jesus Christ… to belong to Him… to possess His spirit. Since these words are addressed to and fulfilled in my sheep3, it is therefore necessary, in order to be worthy of the consolation and the immeasurable good they contain … to belong, no matter what, to the flock of Jesus Christ. And this is our struggle and this is our combat in life: to belong to Jesus Christ. Every other aspiration should be subordinated to this one.

We must always strive for, and ask God for, that inestimable grace which is to be a sheep of His flock, a member of His body which is the Church, the grace −in the words of St. Ignatius− to “serve as a soldier under His standard”4. We must ask for the grace to be faithful to Him, not to be deaf to His calls, but to be docile to His sighs of mercy. To be faithful to His teachings, to the demands of His way of life, to His spirit of conquest; to be faithful in keeping His commandments, in the frequent reception and love of His Sacraments (particularly the Eucharist and Confession); to be faithful to the impetus and the fire of Pentecost in our life; to have the spirit that the apostles and the saints had. To be a sheep of Christ… there is our greatest aspiration, our only goal.

Why is this so? Because only in this way will we too be sharers in His victory, in the paschal victory which we celebrated with so much joy on Easter Sunday, in Easter week, and during these fifty days that we live until the celebration of Pentecost.

To achieve it, on our part −and this too is interesting− we are only asked to want to be faithful … to want it effectively … and consequently to strive to achieve it. That is all; the rest is God’s work. Certainly the help of our Lord for this will never be lacking to us, if it is precisely He who wants all of us to be sheep of His flock, it is precisely He who wants there to be one flock and one shepherd5… Sacred Scripture bears eloquent witness to this: God wants all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth6… God gives His grace to all7… God does not want the death of the sinner, but that he be converted and live8… To all of us He says: “Let them only stretch out their hand to me and I will lead them”9. That is why my yoke is easy and my burden is light10… You do not have to weep much, sinner11, says Isaiah, even a single teardrop is enough to set in motion all the love of a God eager to find you… Whoever comes to me I will not cast out12.

Let us think of all the biblical texts that speak of this; let us think of God seeking Adam in paradise; let us think of God forgiving the people for the betrayal at Sinai; let us think of the parables of mercy, of the love of the father for the prodigal son, of the tenderness of the good shepherd who finds the lost sheep, of the charity of the good Samaritan, eager to help and to heal the one who is sick. This truth is impressive: God seeks us more than we seek Him. It is not much that we have to do to be a sheep of Christ; even more, we must do only one thing, and it is this: be faithful.

2. We must trust in Him

It is to His sheep, then, that Jesus promises the most beautiful consolations, even in this life. We must think a great deal about this. About the greatness and the content of these promises. About what is at stake in our Christian life. The world has not heard and will never hear such words; no one is capable of expressing himself like this. That is why we must be attentive. Let us always think of, and let there always resound in our ears, in our daily life, those texts of Sacred Scripture that speak of the goods that will be given to the sheep of Jesus Christ.

  • Whoever comes to me will never hunger, and whoever believes in me will never thirst13…
  • I am the resurrection and the life; whoever believes in me, even if he dies, will live14…
  • I am the bread of life; whoever eats of this bread will live forever15...
  • Whoever is baptized will be saved16…
  • Your joy will be complete17…
  • Life does not end but is transformed18…
  • To those who follow me I will give a hundredfold in this life and afterward eternal life19…
  • I will not leave you orphans; I will always be with you20…
  • I will give them eternal life and they shall never perish21…

Who in the history of mankind has had the daring and the boldness to express himself like this? Who has had the courage to die for these truths and to give his life on the cross so that men, convinced of his extreme love, might turn back to him? How well the first Christians understood this; how well the apostles understood it:

  • St. Paul affirms in one of his letters: God is faithful and will not fail those whom He called to communion with His Son Jesus Christ22.
  • The author of the Letter to the Hebrews affirms that God says: I will never leave you, I will never abandon you23.
  • St. Peter expresses this unfathomable mystery with unexpected words: by His divine power, He has granted us everything that pertains to life, making us sharers in the divine nature and escaping from the corruption that is in the world24.
  • St. James counsels us forcefully: Happy the man who endures trial! Because, having passed the test, he will receive the crown of life that the Lord has promised to those who love Him25.
  • St. John makes us notice the love God has for us: See what love the Father has had for us, that we should be called children of God26… This is the message… God has given us eternal life27.

3. Trust in God

How, then, in the face of these great truths, can we not abandon ourselves to God, to His divine providence, to His merciful love, to His boundless tenderness, to His desire to save us!

In the face of words such as those of today’s Gospel, my sheep hear my voice28… I will give them eternal life… they shall never perish29… how can we not attain peace within ourselves, how can we not realize that this world passes away, with its sorrows, its tears and its tribulations; and that even in the midst of them we must have great interior peace, great tranquility, that we must be authentically happy, because an eternal weight of incalculable glory awaits us.

How can we not be joyful: rejoice always in the Lord; again I say to you, rejoice30... There is no greater joy than this: to know that we live in the truth31… That is why it has rightly been said that a Christianity without joy is inconceivable. Sadness, as such, is foreign to the Christian spirit. That is why, in the face of our tribulations, let us always remember the words of today’s Gospel… my sheep, no one shall snatch them out of my hand; I will give them life, and eternal life32.

How can we not be optimistic: if the glory of this world passes away. Hence the words of our Lord in the Gospel: do not fear, I have overcome the world33. If we have been promised life, and life forever. Who can promise something like that? Who can grant us such a gift? And yet it has been granted to us in Jesus Christ, the only name through which we can be saved34; the only one who has words of eternal life35.

How can we not find strength to keep going: how can we not be ready to fight, not to collapse, to keep going, to redouble our strength. There will always be trials in our life, and it is necessary that it be so, but in the words of Scripture we must find the strength to keep going and to pass with a firm step through the midst of them. Happy the man who endures trial!36. That is why the same St. James said: count it the greatest happiness to have to endure various trials; you know that when our faith is tested we learn to be steadfast37. And St. Peter: In this rejoice, even though for a while you may have to endure some trials. Your faith will come out of them tested like gold that passes through the fire38… Rejoice in sharing in the sufferings of Christ39… Since, as the Letter to the Corinthians teaches: God will not allow you to be tempted beyond your strength40.

How can we not abandon ourselves forever into the hands of God, who loves us and protects us;

who will never allow us to lack anything we need for our salvation. For, as St. Teresa said: “the Lord never abandons those who venture out for Him alone.” If we are faithful to Him, He will never leave us, He will never abandon us, and He will always help us. How well Don Orione exclaimed: “O Divine Providence, there is nothing more lovable and more adorable than you, who in a motherly way feed the birds of the sky and the flowers of the field: the rich and the poor! In you is all our trust, O Holy Providence of the Lord, because you love us, and love us more than we love ourselves. No, with your help, I never want to pry into you, I do not want to tie your hands, I never want to hinder you; I only want to abandon myself into your arms, serene and calm. Grant that I may always be before you with the simplicity of a child in its mother’s arms.”

[Peroratio] That is why let us ask God that in this Easter season we may find that trust in Him which the victory of the Lord’s resurrection inspires in us, since, if we are faithful to Him, nothing and no one will be able to take away from us the peace and joy we carry within. As the apostle St. Paul said −words we should always think about− with challenging questions: What then shall we say to this? If God is with us, who is against us? He who gave us His own Son, how will He not give us all things with Him? Who will accuse God’s chosen ones, knowing that it is He who makes them just? Who will condemn them? Will it perhaps be Christ Jesus, He who died, even more, He who rose and is at the right hand of God interceding for us? Who will separate us from the love of Christ? tribulation? anguish? persecution? hunger? nakedness? dangers? the sword?41… And he himself answers: in all this we come out victorious thanks to Him who loved us. And he concludes: for I am sure that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor things present, nor things to come, nor powers, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature will be able to separate us from the love of God made manifest in Christ Jesus our Lord42.

May the Blessed Virgin grant us the grace to possess this firmness and this unshakable trust in God our Lord. May it be so.
