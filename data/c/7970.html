Reading the table of contents of this somewhat uneven and disorderly collection of lectures, articles and sermons from very different periods of Fr. Julio Meinvielle, what Gilbert Keith Chesterton said about St. Thomas Aquinas comes to mind: “he thought pugnaciously… (which) does not mean bitterly or contemptuously, without charity, but combatively.”

Like St. Thomas, to whom he knew himself indebted, Fr. Julio thought pugnaciously, as is plainly evident. (The talented Cornelio Fabro admired his polemical “vis.”) Ramiro de Maeztu maintains that: “Culture is polemical. I know of no work and no life that has left a mark on the history of culture that has not been a work and a life of polemics.” Fr. Julio used to say: “To fight is a grace.”

And if, undaunted, for almost fifty years, he stood firm in the breach1, that is, always prepared and ready to fight the battles of God, it is because his soul was nourished assiduously in the contemplation of Jesus Christ, the Logos made flesh: in Himself, in His Church, in the Eucharist, in every man. If someone, in order to restrain his unbending fortitude, had advised him to content himself with saying Mass, he would have answered as Fray Francisco de Paula Castañeda did: “it is precisely the Mass that sets me on fire, and drives me on, and compels me to unceasing struggle.”

Hence he was able to detect and denounce from the housetops2 the progressive avalanche of the 1960s, a task that reaches its fullness, in my view, in his masterwork “De la Cábala al progresismo” (Ed. Calchaquí, Salta, 1.970, 470 pp.). It was impossible for him not to notice and warn us about the deviations of neo-modernism, because its principal end, like that of all the errors concerning the truths the Church professes, is “to diminish Christ in His dignity”3.

The liberal progressives diminish the dignity of Christ, since they do not want the reign of Christ the King over society, over politics, economics, culture, nations and states. All the progressives diminish the dignity of Christ, since they do not affirm the absolute necessity of grace for the solution of man’s problems -even temporal problems- Unless God guards the city, in vain do the watchmen keep vigil4; thus frustrating the coming of the Son of God in the flesh: Grace and truth come through Jesus Christ5. The Marxist progressives diminish the dignity of Christ, since they seek to put Christ and His Church at the service of the revolution, emptying them of their supernatural content.

They attack the dignity of Christ by attacking the faith and morals taught by Christ. Read the Declarations of the Sacred Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith: “to safeguard from some recent errors faith in the mysteries of the Incarnation and of the Most Holy Trinity” (21/2/1972), “on Catholic doctrine concerning the Church, to defend it from some present-day errors” (24/6/1973), “on certain questions concerning eschatology” (17/5/79), “on abortion” (18/11/1974), “on certain questions of sexual ethics” (premarital relations, homosexuality, masturbation, etc. 29/12/1975), “on the admission of women to the ministerial priesthood” (15/10/1976), the very recent one of 6/8/1983 “on certain questions concerning the Minister of the Eucharist”: does this not indicate that some have at times come to oppose Catholic faith and morals, even in fundamental matters? Did not the Credo of the People of God (30/6/1968) come out to confront those who were tearing down truths of Christian doctrine, like some Catholics “captive to a certain desire to change and innovate”?

The doctrinal errors about the Eucharist, which merited the Encyclical Mysterium Fidei of His Holiness Paul VI, are manifestly repugnant to the dignity of Christ, just as so much vulgar, crude, kitschy and tasteless “liturgy,” which destroys the “sacrum” of the mystery, degrades the dignity of Christ6. And must we not speak here of the internationally orchestrated campaign against the celibacy of the ministers of the Eucharist, which occasioned the encyclical Sacerdotalis Caelibatus (24/6/1967)? Will it be necessary to highlight “the Christological significance of celibacy”7?

Everything that lowers the dignity of His Most Holy Mother, the Virgin Mary, lowers the dignity of Christ. Paul VI did not hesitate to recall the doctrine of the cult of the Blessed Virgin in texts “to which it will not be useless to return in order to dispel doubts”8. A pastoral expert went so far as to state publicly that it was necessary to tear down the Marian shrines “because they were hotbeds of superstition” (sic).

Those who do not accept -in fact or in principle- the true and only visible head of the Church, the Pope, attempt to undo Christ, seeking to diminish Him in His dignity, because they patently dissolve the unity of the Mystical Body: it could not be one single Body if it did not have one single Head; nor one single community if it did not have one single leader, as its Founder taught: one flock and one shepherd9. Recall, for example, the deviations of Hans Küng and of so many others, and, following an apparently opposite path, the experience of Ecône, and, in its exacerbation, those of El Palmar de Troya, etc. See likewise, to give two very visible examples, the progressives’ lack of obedience to the only visible head of the only Church founded by Christ, in the desacralizing tampering with the liturgy and in not wearing ecclesiastical dress.

The progressives seek to diminish the dignity of Christ by preventing the development of His life in new human beings, by being against the transmission of human life, hence the need for the Encyclical Humanae Vitae (27/5/1968), and against the transmission of divine life, hence the need for Evangelii Nuntiandi and for treating at the next Synod, among other things, the irreplaceable importance of the sacrament of Confession. We have also read that “the sacraments are the dead weight of the Church” (sic), when in truth they are what drive the ship of the Church forward with impetuous and unstoppable vigor, with force, strength, resolution and onrushing energy, since they are, in the words of St. Thomas, “separated instruments of the Divinity” and “like divine relics of the Incarnation.”

They diminish the dignity of Christ by trying to baptize so-called modern philosophy -which connotes not a chronological reason but an ontological one-, inasmuch as it is closed to transcendence and therefore denies a priori the divinity of Our Lord Jesus Christ, “thus creating a new gnosis”10.

The progressives lower the dignity of Christ by plunging into the temporal while forgetting the eternal; by not bringing adequate solutions to the pressing temporal problems of lack of justice, of peace, of bread and of shelter; by keeping silent and not denouncing the true enemies of the progress and development of men and of peoples; when priests dilute their sacramental charism, which identifies them with Christ the Priest, by invading fields that belong exclusively to the laity; by not having the vigor to take a clear, firm and wise position in the face of modern culture; by not teaching that everything -culture, politics, economics, work, family, etc.- must be subordinated, while respecting legitimate autonomies, to the lordship of the one King of kings and Lord of lords. John Paul II cries out: “Open, indeed, open wide the doors to Christ! Open to His saving power the doors of States, economic and political systems, the vast fields of culture, of civilization and of development.”

In years now past, the cooling of missionary zeal, the emptying and liquidation of seminaries and novitiates, the abandonment of generous self-giving in so many consecrated souls, the absence of great converts, were unmistakable signs of the sterility and destruction that characterize the progressive phenomenon, as a just retribution for experimenting in an attempt to derogate from the dignity of Christ.

They have sought the dissolution of Christ by spreading errors in faith and morals that caused -and cause- a great many spiritual deaths, since “The Son of God by His Incarnation has united Himself in some way with every man”11. In short, they diminish the dignity of Christ by hindering the true progress, material and spiritual, of man, the image of Christ.

For a soul contemplative of the mystery of the Incarnate Word, as Fr. Julio was, it was absurd to excuse himself from bearing witness, behaving like a dumb dog12 before those who lower the majesty of Jesus Christ. He had the gift of thinking pugnaciously because he defended the dignity of Jesus Christ, and this as a fruit of charity. I heard someone say with a certain sarcasm: “What must he have said if he ran into Maritain in heaven!” I immediately thought: “He must have rejoiced exceedingly!” He loved his enemies and, because he wanted the eternal salvation of all, he hated error. He taught us that “love covers a multitude of sins”13 and that we must pray and do penance for those who are in error and for those who persecute us, forgiving them from the heart: Lord, do not hold this sin against them14. Finally, in fact, we owe them a great deal, since, in spite of themselves, they work for our good, if we truly love God: Everything works together for the good of those who love God15; and we must be grateful to them, since their errors are an occasion for us to clarify and deepen our faith, and their unjust persecution makes it possible for us to live the eighth beatitude, makes us gain many merits for eternal life and obtains for us an unsuspected supernatural fruitfulness. In a similar way, those who lower the dignity of Christ will thank us for not having let them carry out, with greater freedom and to a greater extent, their work in favor of error and deceit.

Therefore, as the Virgin of Fatima asked, we must always pray and do penance, especially in this time, for those who despise lordship and blaspheme the glories… feeding themselves; they are clouds without water carried along by the winds; autumn trees without fruit, twice dead, uprooted; wild waves of the sea, casting up the foam of their impurities; wandering stars for whom the gloom of darkness is reserved forever16. For them we say with St. Luigi Orione: “Place me, Lord, upon the mouth of hell, so that by Your mercy I may close it,” as Meinvielle lived it and as anyone who does not diminish the dignity of the love of Christ fully understands it.

On his tomb was engraved: He loved the Truth17, because he lived it in the strictest sense of the word. He loved the truth reflected in every particle of the universe, as a spark of the infinite Truth, which is God. He loved the truth, and for that reason he studied problems, even temporal ones, in order to arrive at solutions that would lead us to a better world for our Homeland and for all of suffering and anguished humanity; he knew that “truth is the first and fundamental condition of social renewal18. He loved the truth taught by the Catholic Church: “Sine illa peritur!,” as Blessed John Paul I recalled. He loved Jesus Christ, the Truth, and out of love for the Truth he argued against the grumblers, the malcontents, who live according to their passions, whose mouths speak arrogantly, who flatter people for the sake of gain19, seeking their salvation, snatching them out of the fire20 by refuting their errors, so that they might not diminish Jesus Christ, who is the Only One who has words of eternal life21.
