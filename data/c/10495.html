Sermon for the Fourth Sunday of Advent

Dear brothers and sisters: The passage of the Gospel we have just read is known as the Gospel that narrates “the trial of Joseph.” The verse that follows the passage we have read already tells of the birth of Jesus; the account of that central event the Church reserves for Christmas Day. For now she leaves us only here, with the expectation, with the enthusiasm, with the longing: He did not know her until she gave birth to a son. It is logical: we find ourselves already—practically—on the eve of the great celebration of the birth; the path of Advent (of intense prayer, of penance and purple vestments) is coming to its end.

It is therefore the moment to meditate on and consider the moments before the birth of Christ. The Church proposes to us—in the cycle of readings of the current year—the dream of Joseph, and more still: what is hidden behind the narrative of the dream, and what lies latent beneath the whole account we have heard and also beneath the first readings—let us recall the words of Isaiah and the beginning of St. Paul’s letter to the Romans—is the gospel of the virginity of Mary, our Mother. A precious dogma of Mariology and therefore a constitutive part of our faith:

The Most Blessed Virgin was a virgin before, during and after childbirth; that is why we speak of the perpetual virginity of Mary, the Most Pure, the Immaculate Conception. Let us consider this truth a little today in this homily; it is necessary, because today as always—as already happened in the first centuries of the preaching of the apostles—there are those who openly and brazenly deny it. And this right beside us: think of the Jehovah’s Witnesses, think of the evangelicals, sects of the twentieth century. It is, however, one of the most beautiful dogmas of our faith: Christ was born of a Virgin mother.

1. The Gospel we have read

We have heard, then, the revelation made to Joseph. The simplicity of the Gospel account we have heard does not, however, fail to show a violent tension: the fact is that Joseph must assume the legal paternity of Jesus and that Mary is a Virgin Mother, for she has conceived by the work of the Holy Spirit.

That is why it is called the Gospel of the trial of Joseph. Perhaps for us, in a world so distant from the Hebrew world and as carnal as ours, it is hard to understand what this meant. In the Gospel account, Mary was the betrothed wife of St. Joseph. Betrothal—according to the custom of the Hebrew people—entailed a real commitment, such that the bridegroom, already from the betrothal itself, was called “husband” and could break the betrothal only with a formal repudiation. Mary was found with child by the work of the Holy Spirit, and Joseph, who was just and did not want to repudiate her, decides to divorce her in secret. Joseph’s justice consists in the fact that, convinced of Mary’s virtue, he refuses to hand over this fact—which he does not understand—to the legal procedure, which was most rigorous. The law in fact prescribed—according to the book of Deuteronomy—that if, when the time of the marriage came, the young woman was not found in a state of virginity, Deuteronomy says, They shall bring her out to the entrance of her father’s house and the people of her city shall stone her so that she dies, because she has committed a great infamy (Dt 22:21). To repudiate the woman with legal repudiation was to expose her to defamation and also to death.

2. Mary was a virgin before childbirth

Mary conceived not by the work of a man, but by the work of the Holy Spirit. We have heard it in the Gospel we have just read; a Gospel that ratifies with all the authority proper to it the prophecy of Isaiah that we heard in the first reading: it is the prophecy of Emmanuel applied to Mary. They ask the prophet for a sign of when this will happen, and Isaiah says: Hear, House of David! The Lord Himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son who shall be called Emmanuel, God

with us. St. Matthew, as we have heard, announces the supernatural conception of the Messiah and says: All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had announced through the prophet, who says: Behold, the virgin shall conceive and give birth… and he repeats the prophecy of Isaiah. We also heard it in the first verse we read today: The conception of Jesus Christ took place in this way: when Mary, His Mother, was betrothed to Joseph, before they lived together, she was found to have conceived by the Holy Spirit. And what was happening was not foreign to the Scriptures nor to the hope of the people (today we read Isaiah); with such a conception the beautiful prophecy of Ezekiel was fulfilled: The gate shall remain shut. It shall not be opened, nor shall any man enter by it, because the Lord, the God of Israel, has entered by it (Ez 44:2).

3. Mary was a Virgin during childbirth

A French theologian explains it very well when he says: “Just as the light of the sun bathes the glass without breaking it, and with impalpable subtlety passes through its solidity and does not break it when it enters, nor destroy it when it leaves, so the Word of God, the splendor of the Father, entered the virginal dwelling and came forth from there, the virginal cloister remaining closed; for the purity of Mary is a most spotless mirror, which is neither broken by the reflection of the light nor wounded by its rays”1.

And Fray Luis de León says: “What was conceived in the holy womb, as the months went by, came forth from it without causing it pain and leaving it holy and intact. And as the one who was being born was, according to His divinity, a ray and a radiance flowing with purity and simplicity from the light of His Father, He also gave His humanity the properties of light, and came forth from His mother as the ray of the sun passes through the windowpane without harm, and we saw an admirable mixture: flesh with the properties of God and God with the properties of flesh, and divinity and humanity together, and man and God born of father and mother, and without father and without mother—without mother in heaven and without father on earth”2.

4. Mary was a Virgin after childbirth

That is why we speak of the perpetual virginity of Mary. This is what many modern sects deny (Jehovah’s Witnesses, Mormons, etc.). It is nothing to be alarmed about; it is not a new error, it is always there. Already the Ebionites and Cerinthus in the first centuries taught that Christ was conceived like everyone else … heretics. St. Thomas himself, speaking of the perpetual virginity of Mary, warns: “Without any doubt whatsoever we must reject the error of Helvidius Priscus (who was a Stoic philosopher and Roman statesman who lived in the first century A.D.), who dared to say that the Mother of Christ, after His birth, had lived with St. Joseph and had other children by him. This cannot be admitted in any way.” And he says that to admit this is not only heresy but also blasphemous, because:

  • It is offensive to Christ, for being the Only Son of God, it was fitting that He also be the only son of His mother.
  • It is offensive to God the Holy Spirit, whose sanctuary was the virginal womb of Mary, where He formed Christ, and it was not fitting that it be profaned by any man.
  • It is offensive to the dignity and holiness of Mary, who would prove most ungrateful if she were not content with such a Son and consented to lose her virginity, which had been so miraculously preserved for her.
  • It is offensive to St. Joseph himself, who would have incurred a most grave rashness had he attempted to defile her whom he knew to have conceived by the work of the Holy Spirit.

That is why we Catholic Christians, as good children, defend today and always the perpetual virginity of Mary: before, during and after childbirth. Mother and Virgin, as had been announced —you shall conceive and bear a son—. The sects deny this truth by arguing through a false and private interpretation of the scriptures, and by this means they deceive many people… and they will bring up the matter of the firstborn, the brothers of Jesus and who knows how many other things…. they may know the Bible, but it is a relative knowledge, for they err abruptly in its interpretation… for their study, far from being a scientific study, is tendentious… that is why they do not develop a body of thought, much less a theology.

Let us always, before them and before anyone, defend this, one of the most beautiful dogmas of our faith. Mother and Virgin. And with a vow of perpetual virginity; that is why, when the angel of God set about asking a maiden of Galilee to lend her nature for the Incarnation of the Word, the answer that was heard was: how can this be, since I do not know man?

***

Dear brothers and sisters: Now on the threshold of the feast of Christmas, of the celebration of the birth of Christ, the Church presents His Mother to us. And contemplating His Mother as the Dawn that precedes the rising Sun, let us also foretaste even now the greatness of the birth we are going to admire this coming 25th. Advent draws to its close looking at the Most Blessed Virgin; under the enraptured gaze of the mystery of the Virgin Mother, let us too await with longing the birth of the redeemer. So be it.
