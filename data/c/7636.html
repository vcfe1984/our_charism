25th anniversary of the

Missio Sui Iuris in Tajikistan

Dan 7:9-10.13-14 or Rev 12:7-12a / Ps 137:1-5/ Jn 1:47-51

I thank you, Lord, with all my heart; I will sing to you in the presence of the angels. I will bow down before your holy Temple. I will give thanks to your Name for your love and your faithfulness, for your promise has surpassed your renown.

The words of the psalm we have heard today turn out to be providential for the occasion that brings us together, for the psalmist expresses our need to give glory to God and to burst into thanksgiving for the 25th anniversary of the creation of the missio sui iuris here in Tajikistan. An event that has remained indelibly linked to the history of our Institute.

When St. John Paul II referred to the missio sui iuris, he said that a great hope had been kindled1 and that this constituted “the beginning of a promising era of evangelization”2. And truly, at the end of these first 25 years, we can see how the enormous labor of the missionaries of the Incarnate Word and of the religious sisters, who have done missionary work without letting themselves be overcome by obstacles −the difficulty of the language, the lack of resources, the small number of priests in the face of the many needs of the mission− has borne abundant fruit for the Catholic Church in this beloved country.

Thus we see today a community fervent in faith and in love for Our Lady, children and young people fond of their parish, the inestimable gift of having the first priests born in this blessed nation (a grace for which we will never be grateful enough), the grace of having two parishes: one here in Dushanbe and another in Bokhtar; today we have the presence of religious sisters who, with a spirit of authentic ecclesial solidarity, have left their homeland to do missionary work in these lands, and for some time now we have also had a contemplative monastery that implores God’s help day and night for all the souls in the missio sui iuris.

Truly, I believe we can say without fear of being mistaken that upon the sufferings, upon the prayers and arduous labors of the Tajik Christians and of their missionaries −those who are here now and those who are no longer here− this mission rises, in which the new life of the Christian community has flourished.

That is why we say again with the psalmist: I thank you, Lord, with all my heart… your promise has surpassed your renown.

1. To the Tajik Catholic faithful

I would now like to speak especially to you, the lay faithful.

As you well know, this anniversary we celebrate today was preceded by many years of harsh pressure and great crosses during the years of the regime.

How many Christians of those days would have wanted to see what you see today: a church with several priests, with religious sisters, with children and young people who receive the sacraments… and yet they did not see it.

That is why this feast teaches all of us to trust a little more each day in divine Providence, which never abandons us, above all in the hour of trial. The faithfulness and mercy of God that we sang in the psalm are the foundation of all our trust and of all hope.

This feast also speaks to us of the great power of the prayer of the humble, which ‘wrests’, we might say, the greatest gifts from the Heart of Christ. And God, for whom all things are easy because He is almighty, uses His power for mercy3 and draws from the most difficult situations, and from great evils, great blessings, like this one we are celebrating today.

Let us be certain that, however heavy and stormy the trials we must face may be, we will never be abandoned to ourselves, we will never fall out of the hands of the Lord, the hands that created us and that now continue to sustain us on the road of life.

That is why, while I congratulate you for your daily faithfulness in keeping the flame of faith alive within your families, I invite you also to be bearers of that hope of which John Paul II spoke. This means being responsible for ensuring that this magnificent gift is not lost, but that the flame of faith remains lit and spreads to many more souls. And so that the flame of faith does not go out, you must feed it with good catechetical formation, with the prayerful reading of the Bible, with the reception of the sacraments. That will help you to persevere in bearing witness to Christ, to forgive offenses, to be generous in the service of God and neighbor, and in this way you will be collaborating effectively in the service of the new evangelization. Know that you are the hidden pillars of the Church, to whom Jesus Himself addresses words of consolation: You will be blessed in the kingdom of heaven4.

I also see several young people and children here. Some years ago Fr. Orzu and Fr. Maxim were also sitting on that side. And now they are missionary priests, because they knew how to listen to the voice of Christ and they realized that He needed them to pass on to other kids the joy and peace they experienced when they were here in the parish. And it may be that today, too, God is looking among you for someone who wants to be generous and enthusiastic in drawing others to Him. My message for you, dear young people, is this: Trust in Christ! He will not disappoint your hopes or your plans; on the contrary, He will fill them with meaning and joy. Talk with Christ. Ask Him what He wants for you, especially when you receive Him in Communion or come to the church at some other time. But do not fail to do it.

2. To the missionaries of the IVE

Now, if you will allow me, I would like to address the missionaries, especially the priests.

Without any doubt this celebration could not be a reality without the generous commitment of all the missionary priests who have passed through this mission and who are in this mission. That is why, first of all, I want −in the name of the whole Institute− to thank you and congratulate you, because you represent here today all the missionaries who, with great joy, but also with crosses and sacrifices, strove not only to begin the mission but also to persevere in the effort undertaken.

Some of you have already been many years in this mission, which is the same as saying that for many years you have had the privilege of serving the Church of Christ in Tajikistan, and you have seen it grow.

Following Christ’s mandate, some have come from very far away, and little by little they have courageously faced the challenges of a mission that was just beginning and of a culture different from their own, and they have been cultivating the precious fruits that today we have the joy of celebrating.

My message is this: always look to Christ, our sure hope, and serve Him “with the fervor and enthusiasm of the saints”5, as is proper to the members of the Institute.

The Tajik people need you, because they need God, who is the fundamental reason for our lives. Therefore, be magnanimous and creative in launching apostolic initiatives that bring Christ and the Most Blessed Virgin to as many souls as possible.

May this anniversary not mean simply a goal reached, the finishing point, but on the contrary, may it be simply the beginning of that new era of evangelization announced by John Paul the Great.

I want to thank most especially Fr. Pedro López, superior of the missio, for his meritorious and acknowledged pastoral work, as well as all the priests present here for the service they render to Christ in the children and young people, in the sick and in families.

It only remains for me to entrust you to the intercession of Our Lady of Luján, who definitely is the Star of evangelization in this nation, and no one has done as much as she for the evangelization of the culture6 of the Tajik people.

Into her motherly hands I place the constant work of the missionaries, their expectations and plans, so that she may guide them and sustain them in their daily apostolic effort. May they be encouraged by the example and intercession of St. John Paul II himself, who showed so much zeal and affection for the people of Tajikistan.

In short, may this be a day of full celebration. For truly the Lord has done great things for us.
