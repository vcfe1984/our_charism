The Constitutions and the Incarnate Word

Recalling the thought of our Founder
During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the first day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in the preexistence of the Word from all eternity in the bosom of the Most Holy Trinity, we call to mind Fr. Buela’s thought on the subject, taken from his book “El Señor es mi Pastor. Memoria y Profecía.”

The Constitutions and the Incarnate Word

The first part, “Our Path,” was surely written between May 3 and August 1981, because in August we held the first meeting to report on what we wanted to do.

There (in “Our Path”) is a first great development of what the Mystery of the Incarnate Word is. A development that in some way reaches its summit in “El Arte del Padre.”

I drew my inspiration first and foremost from all the passages of Sacred Scripture where the Incarnate Word is spoken of (e.g. the Hymn of the Kenosis, the prologue of St. John…).

And in the Magisterium of the Church, there is a formula there that is fantastic. There are many citations of the Council, but there is one I like most: “Council of Ephesus,” D 111, St. Leo the Great, Dz 143, 144. And here comes, for me, the most important text: Dz 148, Council of Chalcedon: “… one only and the same Jesus Christ, without division, in a single hypostasis, one only and the same Only Son…”. Ephesus in 431. St. Leo the Great 449. Chalcedon 451.

“Hence the Magisterium of the Church has always defended the Catholic truth about the Incarnate Word: «Dogma says that in Christ there is only one person, namely, the divine person of the Logos, and two natures, both subsisting in one and the same divine person. The human nature has been assumed into the unity and dominion of the divine person, so that it is the divine person who acts in the human nature and through the human nature as through an organ of His own.

The Third ecumenical Council of Ephesus (431) confirmed the twelve anathematisms of St. Cyril of Alexandria, but without formally defining them. Later they were recognized by the popes and the councils as an expression of the genuine doctrine of the Church. Here, in condensed form, are their main points:

a) Christ with His own flesh is a single being, that is, one single person. He is God and man at the same time ( 2 and 6).

b) The Logos-God is united to the flesh by an intrinsic, physical or substantial union (An. 2 and 3). Christ is not a bearer of God, but truly God (An. 5).

c) The human and divine properties of which Sacred Scripture and the holy fathers speak to us must not be divided between two persons or hypostases (the Man-Christ and the Logos-God), but must be referred to the one Christ, the incarnate Logos (An. 4). It was the divine Logos who suffered in the flesh and was crucified, died and rose again (An. 12).

d) The Blessed Virgin Mary is Mother of God, because she gave birth according to the flesh to the incarnate Logos-God (An. 1).

The Council of Chalcedon (451) defined that the two natures of Christ are united “in one single person and one single hypostasis” (DZ 148)».

And leaping across the centuries we come to His Holiness Paul VI, in his Solemn Profession of Faith, on June 30, 1968, who once again confesses the now twice-millennial faith of the Catholic Church in the divinity of Jesus Christ: «We believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the Son of God. He is the eternal Word, born of the Father before all ages and consubstantial with the Father, homooúsios to Patri, and through whom all things were made. He became incarnate by the work of the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Virgin Mary and was made man: equal therefore to the Father according to the divinity and inferior to the Father according to the humanity, and one in Himself, not by an impossible confusion of the natures, but by the unity of the person»

With the approval of the same Pontiff, the Sacred Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, on February 21, 1972, published a very important «Declaration to safeguard the faith from certain recent errors concerning the mysteries of the Incarnation and of the Trinity». Among the Christological errors the Declaration points out three principal ones:

1) The denial of the preexistence of the person of the Son subsisting as distinct from the Father and the Holy Spirit from all eternity;

2) The abandonment of the notion of the one person of Christ; and

3) The denial of the assumption of the human nature of Christ by the Second Person of the Most Holy Trinity, asserting instead that it would exist in itself as a human person. The paragraph concludes with this lapidary sentence: «Those who think in this way are far from the true faith in Christ, even when they affirm that the singular presence of God in Jesus makes Him become the supreme and definitive summit of divine revelation; nor do they recover the true faith in the divinity of Christ when they add that Jesus can be called God because God is supremely present in what they call His human nature».

[With the hymn to the Virgin we ask her to obtain for us the grace we are asking for on this day of the novena and to keep us from erroneous doctrines about the mystery of the Incarnation]
