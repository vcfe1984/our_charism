A knot of relationships

Recalling the thought of our Founder
During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the sixth day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in the priesthood of Christ, we recall what Fr. Buela wrote in his book “Sacerdotes para siempre.”

A knot of relationships

The priesthood is, essentially, communion. That is why the priesthood is a knot of relationships. A knot made of bonds that tighten and close in such a way that it is almost impossible to untie them, and the more they are pulled, the tighter they become. A knot of many and manifold relationships. Hence living the priesthood as a mystery of communion is something never attained in total perfection. […]

Relationship with the Incarnate Word

The Second Person of the Most Holy Trinity made man: with all the mysteries of His life, Passion, Death and Resurrection. With what He did and taught. In a particular way with the Eucharist, as sacrificial sacrament and as sacramental sacrifice.

Sacramental root

«Through sacramental ordination, conferred by the imposition of hands and the consecratory prayer of the bishop, there is established in the presbyter “a specific ontological bond, which unites the priest to Christ, High Priest and Good Shepherd.”

The identity of the priest, then, derives from the specific participation in the priesthood of Christ, whereby the one ordained is transformed, in the Church and for the Church, into a real, living and transparent image of Christ the Priest: “a sacramental representation of Jesus Christ, Head and Shepherd.” Through consecration, the priest “receives as a gift a spiritual power, which is a participation in the authority with which Jesus, through His Spirit, guides the Church.”

This sacramental identification with the High and Eternal Priest specifically inserts the presbyter into the Trinitarian mystery and, through the mystery of Christ, into the ministerial communion of the Church, in order to serve the People of God».

Specific identity

«The Christological dimension −like the Trinitarian− arises directly from the sacrament, which configures one ontologically to Christ the Priest, Teacher, Sanctifier and Shepherd of His People.

To those faithful who −while remaining grafted into the common priesthood− are chosen and constituted in the ministerial priesthood, there is given an indelible participation in the same and one priesthood of Christ, in the public dimension of mediation and authority, as regards the sanctification, the teaching and the guidance of the whole people of God. Thus, if on the one hand the common priesthood of the faithful and the ministerial or hierarchical priesthood are necessarily ordered to one another −for each, in its own way, participates in the one priesthood of Christ−, on the other hand they differ essentially from one another46.

In this sense, the identity of the priest is new with respect to that of all Christians, who, through Baptism, participate together in the one priesthood of Christ and are called to bear witness to Him throughout the earth47. The specificity of the ministerial priesthood stands in relation to the need that all the faithful have to adhere to the mediation and lordship of Christ, made visible by the exercise of the ministerial priesthood.

In his particular Christological identity, the priest must be aware that his life is a mystery inserted totally into the mystery of Christ in a new and specific way, and this commits him totally to pastoral activity and fulfills him».

Relationship with Our Lady

«There exists an “essential relationship… between the Mother of Jesus and the priesthood of the ministers of the Son,” which derives from the relationship between the divine maternity of Mary and the priesthood of Christ.

In this relationship is rooted the Marian spirituality of every presbyter. Priestly spirituality cannot be considered complete unless it takes seriously into consideration the testament of Christ crucified, who willed to entrust His Mother to the beloved disciple and, through him, to all priests, who have been called to continue His work of redemption.

As to John at the foot of the Cross, so Mary is entrusted to each presbyter as Mother in a special way.

Priests, who are numbered among the disciples most loved by Jesus crucified and risen, must welcome Mary into their life as their Mother: she will therefore be the object of their continual attentions and of their prayers. The Ever-Virgin is for priests the Mother who leads them to Christ, while at the same time making them authentically love the Church and guiding them to the Kingdom of Heaven.

Every presbyter knows that Mary, because she is Mother, is the eminent formator of his priesthood: since she is the one who knows how to mold the priestly heart; the Virgin, then, knows how and wants to protect priests from dangers, weariness and discouragement: she watches, with maternal solicitude, so that the presbyter may grow in wisdom, age and grace before God and men.

Those who do not know how to imitate the virtues of the Mother will not be devoted sons. The presbyter, therefore, must look to Mary if he wants to be a humble, obedient and chaste minister, who can bear witness to charity through total self-giving to the Lord and to the Church.

Masterpiece of the priestly Sacrifice of Christ, the Virgin represents the Church in the purest way, without spot or wrinkle, wholly holy and immaculate (cf. Eph 5:27). The contemplation of the Blessed Virgin always places before the eyes of the presbyter the ideal toward which he must strive in his ministry on behalf of his own community, so that it too may be a wholly glorious Church through the priestly gift of his own life».

Love for Mary is visibly manifested in praying the Holy Rosary. […]

May Mary Immaculate obtain for us the grace to be faithful to the mystery of communion that we represent.
