October 281

The Saints

Feast of the Apostles Saints Simon and Jude: the former was surnamed the Cananaean or the Zealot; the latter, the son of James, was also called Thaddeus. At the Last Supper he asked the Lord about his manifestation and received this answer: “If any one love me, he will keep my word. And my Father will love him and we will come to him and will make our abode with him” (Jn 14:23, Douay–Rheims).2

The Institute's Proper Law

Directory of Spirituality

“We profess our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ (Jude 4), and that a reference to all creatures is contained in Him. He is the point of meeting and union between the comprehensor and the wayfarer (comprehensor et viator); the Master and the slave; the century and the moment; the universe and the atom; the from-now-on of salvation and the up-to-now of the promises; the not-yet of what we are waiting for and the already-realized. He is the point of meeting and union of God and man, of eschatology and the Incarnation, and of sidereal distances and the inch. For that reason, nothing escapes Christ, neither space travel nor the world of electronics and computers, neither the most current science nor the most sophisticated technology, nor recent discoveries, the family, work, culture, politics, economics, etc. Therefore, we want to live the exhortation of Saint Ignatius of Antioch, ‘Stop your ears, therefore when anyone speaks to you at variances with Jesus Christ.’”3

The Founder

“They embraced and departed. They would carry the witness of Fire and Wind. They would be witnesses to the Absolute and to the Greatest Love. In different ways, it seems, they would shed their blood rather than betray those truths: James the Greater was slain by the sword and Matthew beheaded; Thomas was pierced with a lance; James the Less was thrown down and finished off with a fuller's club; Jude Thaddeus was beaten to death; Simon the Zealot was sawn asunder; Philip was crucified, Peter crucified head downward, Andrew on a diagonal cross, and Bartholomew flayed … ‘If the grain of wheat … die, it bringeth forth much fruit’ (Jn 12:24, excerpt, Douay–Rheims).

“Thanks to them and their successors, the Lord's command still resounds in the world today: ‘Go ye into the whole world and preach the gospel …’” (Mk 16:15, excerpt, Douay–Rheims).

Excerpt from Vox Verbi 044 — Homily. Working translation of the quoted excerpt; the traditional accounts of the Apostles' deaths are retained as statements of the source.
