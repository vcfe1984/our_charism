Necessity of Major Seminaries

  1. Our Constitutions affirm the need to work at the turning points of culture, and among these they take into consideration “seminary” education1.
  2. How necessary and urgent this apostolic work is, is evident, and even more so in our days, for, as Pius XI already affirmed, the Church “knows well that the religious and moral condition of peoples depends in great part on the priest”2. Priests are the representatives of Christ the Head, charged by Christ himself with continuing his presence and saving work in the Church and in the world through their threefold office of preaching, sanctifying and For this reason, the Second Vatican Council, in the document Optatam totius on the formation of the clergy, affirms that “the desired renewal of the whole Church depends in great part on the ministry of priests”3. Conclusion

  1. To conclude, let us say that we want to form seminarians who live “lordship” over themselves, over men, over the world and over the devil; who enjoy the “freedom” of the children of God in full docility to the Holy Spirit, being convinced that all things are yours; whether Paul, or Apollos, or Cephas; or the world, or life, or death, or the present, or the future; all are yours, and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's (1 Cor 3:21-23); who have the “spirit of princes” and are noble718; who are courageous and totally resolved to attain holiness719; who overcome the temptations proper to the priestly state, such as being egocentric, a functionary, petty, inconstant, boring, an improviser, frivolous, tense, mediocre, parochial, affected, greedy, ill-humored, secularized, fearful, timid, a social climber and ubiquitous720.
