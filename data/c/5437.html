Rome, Italy, February 2, 2018.

Feast of the Presentation of the Lord

“The feast of the Presentation of the Child Jesus is also a feast of the Mother”

St. John Paul II, February 2, 1990

Dear Fathers, Brothers, Seminarians and Novices:

The mystery of the Presentation of the Lord leads us to turn our gaze to His Most Holy Mother, who carries Him in her arms, sheltered by her most tender Heart, to be presented in the temple forty days after His birth.

In this way, the Most Holy Virgin also becomes a figure of all the mothers who throughout history –including our own dear mothers– continue to offer their sons and daughters to the Heavenly Father, associating them with the one oblation of Christ, the cause and model of all consecration in the Church1.

This mystery of the life of Christ, which speaks to us of our vocation as consecrated persons as a most exalted heavenly favor, as an eternal and most merciful election on the part of God the Father, also speaks of the divine election of our mothers to be, precisely, mothers of a consecrated person, whether he be a priest, brother, missionary or monk.

They “came forth from the heart of God”2 to be our mothers! Which is the same as saying, to be “the supreme consolation in this valley of tears.”

That is why this letter is in some way also addressed to them: to your mothers, and I ask you, please, to be so kind as to pass it on to them, if you think it fitting.

First of all, I wish with all my heart to express a most particular gratitude to all the mothers of the priests, brothers, novices, monks and seminarians of our dear Institute, for each of them having been a providential instrument in our fulfillment of the Will of God, and for the unconditional accompaniment they offer us with their constant prayer and service, which speaks so clearly of their boundless love.

May a special and heartfelt thanksgiving also rise to our Lord for all the mothers who have already departed for heaven and who from there now fully contemplate the splendor of the Priesthood of Christ, in which we –their sons– have come to share. To all of them –among whom is my own most beloved mother– who from heaven continue to intercede for us in a unique and, mysteriously, much more effective way: infinite thanks!

Likewise, I would like to take advantage of this feast, which “is also a feast of the Mother”3, to dedicate a few simple words to give expression to the lasting affection and heartfelt homage that we, the sons, owe to our mothers.

For a mother, in a special way, is everything to a person (and I believe that we religious live this reality more fully and more beautifully than other children do). Because to a great extent, everything one is, one’s way of being, of thinking, of believing, all the good one has, one owes principally to one’s mother. Who could doubt that our mothers’ contribution to our lives has been decisive! That is why I dare to say that, among all human relationships, there is none that can equal this one. For as St. John Paul II taught, “although both of them together are parents of their child, the mother is the special ‘part’ and the more qualified one”4.

For every time a child is born, a mother is born as well, and the relationship established between the two is unique, most intimate, unrepeatable and eternal, since no other future relationship, however deep it may be, nor the most tortuous circumstances, nor the most humiliating human weaknesses, not even death itself, will ever be able to erase the sacred bond between mother and child. For a child, his mother will always be his mother, and for a mother, her child will always be her child.

A mother is, in some way, like our Providence on earth in the first years of life, our firmest support in the following years of childhood, our most tender and loyal friend in youth, and our most inseparable and unconditional companion throughout our whole life (and even when she is no longer among us). A good mother is a blessing from God and the supreme consolation that has been given to us in this valley of tears. She shows us the way when we go astray. She lifts us up when we fall. She encourages us when we are downcast. She sweetens life on earth and, as if by magic, turns thorns into roses. God conveys to the world the consolation of love through the soul of a mother. That is why a mother’s love knows no bounds. She can generously renounce her own interests and suffer with a smile on her lips because she loves; she is capable of renouncing, out of love for her child, all the grandeurs of the earth. She devotes her life to fighting for her children and rejoices to see them happy.

That is why for a child there is no place in the world, however beautiful it may be, like this one: his mother’s heart. There is no creature in this life to whom we are more closely united than the heart and soul of our mother. It is said of the Heart of Our Lady and that of Our Lord that they beat in unison, and one author says that this happens with all mothers, with the difference that in that beating it is always the mother who sets the rhythm, and that is why she always knows or senses what is happening in her child’s heart. Who has not experienced this? That is why Hebrew women would counsel one another, saying that making the decision to have a child is of crucial importance: “it is to decide forever that your heart will walk outside your body”; and the proverb says that “whoever takes a child by the hand, in the end, takes the heart of a mother.”

Therefore, it happens with good mothers as with the saints: the work she accomplishes in us is so great that all that remains is to praise God for the gift of having given her to us, because she has been, is, and will continue to be, even when she is no longer here with us, the image of God’s providence and love on this earth.

The mother is the heart of the family; it is the mother who in a certain way creates and constitutes the home and protects its unity. Who has not seen this? Around her, everything is ordered and unified, everything is harmonized and develops. That is why, when a mother departs, nothing is ever the same. She weaves the bonds among everyone; she sends the children toward the father and the father toward the children. The mother is in a particular way the one who listens, consoles, encourages, forgives, reconciles and gives each one his place. She spreads the balm of love and tenderness over all family relationships. She is constantly concerned for each one and does not rest until all are content.

How many of us can say of our mothers –to whom God Himself has entrusted us5– that they have been and are the precious gift through which we are given to see how full of love Divine Providence is! For they, considering “good example as their first mission”6, “with their maternal fiat (‘be it done unto me’)”7 taught us that there is no greater happiness than doing the Will of God. And in the shelter of the sanctuary of their hearts they taught us that what is most noble, pure and complete is generous self-giving, without conditions, without time limits, a limitless self-giving even to the point of forgetting oneself out of love. That is why our mothers will always be the most luminous brushstroke that God set down in the book of our lives, and the most tender caress through which we came to know the fatherly gentleness of God.

Therefore, any one of us, wherever he may be, cherishing his good mother in his heart, can say with St. John of God: “God has deposited the treasure of His love on earth in three chests: in the Eucharist, in Confession, and in my mother’s heart.”

Before so excellent a gift, all that remains is to praise God and burst forth in thanksgiving to Him who has been pleased to give us our mothers as the most tangible proof we have of the riches of His kindness.

Let us acknowledge with gratitude, and for the great consolation of our souls, that we will always have the hearts of our mothers beating in our own bodies, and that wherever we go we will not cease to experience that wave of love and sweetness with which maternal gazes are wont to bathe their children. Thanks to our mothers, all of us will always find in a corner of our hearts that ray of hope which inspires us, consoles us and encourages us to sacrificial love, such as theirs is. That is why our gratitude to our mothers will always be eternal.

*****

Together with this gratitude, I also wish to extend an invitation to you: it is the “40 Hours Project.”

St. Angela Merici used to say that “mothers, even if they had a thousand children, would always carry each one of them engraved in their hearts, and would never forget any of them, because their love is exceedingly genuine”8. That is why I know beforehand that they will accept the proposal.

The 40 Hours Project goes back to 1881 when, in Lu Monferrato –a town in Italy which today has some 1,500 inhabitants– a group of mothers, moved by that genuine love of which we were speaking and with a great spirit of Christian faith, decided to join together to adore Christ in the Blessed Sacrament, praying for vocations to the priesthood and to consecrated life, and every first Sunday of the month they received Communion, offering it for the same intention. The fact is that our Lord more than answered the trusting prayers of those fervent and loving mothers, to the point that 323 vocations can be counted, missionaries in 36 countries (all of them persevering by the grace of God), arising from that tiny village.

Inspired by the great example of faith and love of these mothers, a group of ‘our mothers’ have embraced the same initiative, which today I would like to extend to all our mothers.

They unite for 40 continuous hours on the 14th, 15th and 16th of each month, each offering one hour of prayer, preferably before the Blessed Sacrament, to pray for the increase, perseverance and holiness of priestly and religious vocations for our Institute.

St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort affirmed that “when we pray in common, the prayer of each person becomes the prayer of the whole assembly, and all together form but one prayer”9 and that “a person who prays the Rosary alone has the merit of only one Rosary, but if he prays it with thirty persons, he acquires the merit of thirty rosaries”10. It seems to me that what is said of the Rosary may well be said also of adoration of the Blessed Sacrament. That is why I want to invite all our mothers to join together in praying for us, their sons, the sons of the Family of the Incarnate Word.

For this purpose we have created a website, www.40horas.org, where they can register to pray at the time most convenient for them on the assigned days. The site is available in six of the main languages spoken by our religious, and it is our intention that in the future it will be available in a few more (it will be a way of seeing how many mothers in the different parts of the world join this project of praying for vocations).

I understand that many of our mothers are already elderly, or work, or have no church nearby, or bear in their bodies the precious cross of illness that keeps them in bed. This invitation is for them too! For these mothers can also offer their hour of work, the rosaries they silently pray bead by bead while the rest of the family sleeps, and all the sufferings of body or soul, for these, their dear sons of the Incarnate Word, scattered across the five continents.

And to make this call even broader and more inclusive, I also address personally all the Sisters “Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará,” all the women of our Third Order, all the female relatives of our religious, our sisters, all our ‘mothers of the heart’ who have adopted us as their sons and who are so numerous in our missions, and, finally, all women, especially those who desire that God bless their family with a vocation to the priesthood or to consecrated life, to join this magnificent project of faith and charity, which God will know how to reward a hundredfold11.

Now, since our beautiful Religious Family is present in places where access to the internet is still something ‘uncommon,’ and since on the other hand we know that many women would like to join this project, we have thought that the most fitting thing would be for the religious who come from those places or who are missionaries there to ‘register’ them themselves on the website mentioned above, so that as many as possible may join in the holy mission of imploring the Lord of the harvest12 to send more laborers, and to deign to grant the grace of perseverance and holiness of life to those who are already working so that He may reign13.

We are more than 800 members in the Institute of the Incarnate Word: what an enormous consolation it would be to know that there are more than 800 souls who, with the generosity and boundless love characteristic of the maternal heart, implore such excellent graces from heaven, collaborating almost imperceptibly –an attitude so typical of mothers– in the thrilling adventure of bringing the love of Christ to countless souls.

“Jesus always kept His divine Mother with Him: they were never separated from one another”14. May we never lack mothers who accompany their sons with ardent and constant prayer, which brings so much consolation in this life.

To the Most Holy Virgin, who became the Mother of our Light at the cost of the great sacrifice of her Son and of the maternal sacrifice of her heart, I entrust all of you and your mothers, as well as the fruits of this noble initiative.

In Christ, the Incarnate Word,

Superior General
