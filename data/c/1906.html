Homily during the Mass at the altar of the Chair in St. Peter’s Basilica, with the participants of the International Meeting of the IVE in Rome

Vatican,

Today we are gathered here around the altar raised over the very place that treasures the remains of the Prince of the Apostles, as members of one single Religious Family united in one same love for God and for his Church.

A love in which we always want to stand out, clearly showing by the witness of our life that for us love for the Incarnate Word and love for his Church are one and the same. Because we firmly believe and are convinced that the Mystical Body of Christ is Jesus Christ himself “continued, spread and communicated”1. Therefore, we are not speaking of two distinct loves or services; rather, our love and service of Jesus Christ are one with our love and service of the Church.

And just as Christ loved the Church and gave himself up for her2, so we too “want to love and serve, and to make Jesus Christ loved and served… both the physical Body of Christ in the Eucharist and the Mystical Body of Christ, which is the Church”3.

Now, “Jesus is loved and served on the Cross and crucified with Him, and in no other way”4. And we can say the same with regard to the Church as the Mystical Body of Christ: the Church is loved and served on the Cross and crucified with her, and in no other way.

This has practical consequences in our life, as we see in the life and in the teachings of the Apostle St. Peter himself in his letters.

Simon, son of Jonah, after his confession of Christ as the Son of the living God, was scandalized by the cross that following Christ implied, and as soon as our Lord tells him that he will have to suffer much and be condemned to death, Peter tries to prevent it, to keep Him away from the cross, saying: Far be it from you, Lord! This shall not happen to you5. To which the Incarnate Word responds by calling him Satan6. Because keeping us away from the cross will always be the devil’s business.

Peter was scandalized by the cross. Like so many Christians today who abandon the church with the excuse that “they will never be able to become anything with a cross on their backs.” And like so many others who keep their faith behind closed doors because of the commitment that friendship with Christ demands.

However, Peter learned the lesson, and we too must learn it today. That is why in one of his letters he beautifully warns us: Resist, firm in the faith7; arm yourselves also with the same disposition as Christ to suffer8. Serve one another, each with the gift you have received, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God9. And he clearly counsels us not to be ashamed of being Christians and of suffering something for Christ10; that is, not to be ashamed of carrying our cross held high.

For this reason, the mission of the laity of the Incarnate Word cannot be reduced to the pure and simple example of honesty, competence and faithfulness to duty. All of that is presupposed. Rather, there must also shine forth in you “an authentic witness of Christian life”11, a bold and firm witness to Christ, primarily through the radiance of faith, hope and charity12.

From you, the Church and the Institute itself expect “a living and burning witness of Christ alive in his Church; they expect you to speak and act by example, to stand out before others by the effective practice of the Christian virtues, as men and women who carry Jesus and Mary in their blood; they expect you to strive to fill your domestic, social and professional activity with Christian magnanimity”13.

That is, to be a lay member of the IVE is to be someone who truly loves and serves the Church, working hard for his personal sanctification, striving to be ‘another Christ’14. This translates into following the poor Jesus, without being dejected by scarcity or puffed up by wealth; imitating the humble Christ without craving vain glories, but in everything seeking to please God rather than men, always ready to leave everything for Christ; and even to suffer persecution for justice’s sake, always remembering the words of the Lord: if anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross and follow me15. That will always be the best service you can offer the Church. Because –my dear brothers and sisters– words move, but examples draw others along16; as St. Peter himself experienced, who died crucified head downward in this very place.

It is true that these are not easy times in which to live and practice our faith. It is not easy for parents to raise their children in a Christian way in a world that seeks to eradicate God. It is not easy for young people to live chastely in a world that preaches a sexual life without responsibility. It is not easy for professionals who strive for the primacy of Christ in society when their colleagues think that fraud is part of business, and greed the engine of society. Nevertheless, what is proper to our laity is to bear witness to Christ in all circumstances and situations. A witness that must be free of all fear. We must not be surprised at the violence that has been unleashed against you to put you to the test, as if something extraordinary were happening to you17, the Apostle Peter warns us in his first letter.

For note that “one of the most interesting observations made by our Lord concerning His Mystical Body was that it would be hated by the world, as He Himself was. The world loves the things of the world, but hates what is divine…but because you are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hates you18”19.

But in that very thing, says the Prince of the Apostles, we must rejoice: Rejoice insofar as you are able to share in the sufferings of Christ. Thus, when his glory is revealed, you too will overflow with gladness and joy. Blessed are you if you are insulted for the name of Christ, because the Spirit of glory, the Spirit of God, rests upon you20. Notice that the apostle mentions sufferings, insults… all of that is part of the program.

That is why today St. Peter tells us again, with all the power of the divine truth that abides in his word: Who can harm you if you devote yourselves to doing good? Blessed are you if you have to suffer for justice’s sake. Do not fear and do not be troubled; on the contrary, glorify Christ the Lord in your hearts. Always be ready to give a defense before anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that you have. But do it with gentleness and respect, and with a clear conscience. Thus all those who defame you will be put to shame for their slanders, because you conduct yourselves as servants of Christ. It is better to suffer for doing good, if that is the will of God, than for doing evil21.

We have been begotten for an inheritance that cannot perish, nor be defiled, nor fade away, and that is reserved in heaven for us22. That is why, with our gaze fixed on Christ, we must launch ourselves into the epic mission of transforming the world for Christ.

* * * * *

My dear all: “The law of the Body is the law of the Head: Crucifixion and Empty Tomb. […] Now Christianity is under attack. But that means that these are wonderful days to be alive. It is easy to drift with the current. Corpses drift downriver. But it takes living bodies to resist the current. […] True Catholic life is not made up of routine acts of piety, but of a crisis that sets a great choice before us […] That is why these are splendid days in which to live. We can make decisions that will echo in eternity”23.

We entrust ourselves to Mary, Mother of the Church, and to the prayers of the Apostle St. Peter, over whose tomb we celebrate this Holy Mass.
