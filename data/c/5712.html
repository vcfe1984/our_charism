Easter Morning 2019

Jn 20:1-9

[Exordium] Dear Brothers and Sisters in Christ, only once in the history of the world was a guard of soldiers posted before the tomb of a dead man. Only once in the annals of time has it been recorded that a stone was rolled in front of a sepulcher to keep a body from escaping, and that was on a Good Friday.

And yet here, in all seriousness, after the Man has been struck in the face, crowned with thorns, scourged to the point that his flesh hung from Him like scarlet ribbons, [after they] made him carry a heavy beam (which had been carved by members of his own trade) and forced him to drag it through the streets of the city up a hill, and then he was hung on the wood with nails for three hours, pierced with a lance until blood and water came out, found dead and then buried under the stifling weight of a hundred pounds of aromatic spices; after all this, the chief priests and the Pharisees went to the Roman governor and asked him for authorization to seal the stone and post a guard.

These precautions even seem humorous given the circumstances. Yet what deprives the scene of that note of foolishness is that his enemies were really afraid that He would rise. The request for a guard until “the third day” referred more to Christ’s words about his Resurrection than to the fear that the Apostles would steal the body and fake his ‘Resurrection.’ The enemies of Christ were expecting his Resurrection.

The Pharisees called Christ an impostor, and yet they were afraid he was not one; they asked him to come down from the Cross and were half afraid that he actually would; they ridiculed his prophecy that he would rise on the third day, but they sealed the door of his tomb because they were afraid that he actually would rise; they laughed at Him mockingly when he said that no other sign would be given to that unbelieving generation but the sign of Jonah, and yet they asked that Roman soldiers guard the whale of the earth, lest it throw off its weight and leave him on the shores of eternity.

[The enemies of Christ] were sure that he had been buried, and yet they were not sure that his life had been taken away; they were certain that He was in his tomb but uncertain that they could keep him there. What a strange mixture of belief and unbelief! They had nothing more to fear, but they were not going to take any chances; they had rid themselves of his Presence but feared that he might walk again; he was dead and yet he could live.

This unusual mixture of skepticism and faith is serious proof that when men rebel against the Godhead, people experience that uncomfortable feeling that somehow God might still be in the world. They will always be certain that they have killed Christ, and yet they will guard his tomb. While they celebrate their sweeping victory, they still keep their eyes on their enemies, because when God is the enemy, men can never be sure that they have carried the day.

Their fears were justified. The Resurrection was a fact. He said he would rise again. Resurrexit sicut dixit! But how? At the sound of trumpets? Walking with invisible steps that slipped past the Roman guard? No! At the point of the darkest shadow and in the deepest of silences they suddenly saw a figure, for an angel of the Lord came down from heaven, and approaching, rolled back the stone and sat upon it. His face shone like lightning, and his garment was white as snow. And for fear of him the guards trembled and became like dead men. (Mt 28:2-4)

The soldiers would have been brave amid the turmoil of a battle, but when the angel appeared the guards trembled and became like dead men. (Mt 28:4). Then the angel, turning to the women, who were seeing all this better than brave soldiers, said to them: “Do not be afraid; I know that you are looking for Jesus, the Crucified. He is not here, for he has risen as he said. Come and see the place where he lay… Suddenly Jesus came to meet them and greeted them, saying: “Rejoice.” They came up to him and, clasping his feet, bowed down before him. (Mt 28:5-6;8-9)

Since that first Good Friday, men must always fear that closed doors cannot shut in the Light of the world. Because when God is the enemy, men can never be sure that they have carried the day.

Now we turn the lesson of Easter around: when man has lost his day, God returns to save him. Man lost his day in Eden when he succumbed to the temptation of Satan, and God promised him a redeemer to save him from sin. Man lost his day on a Good Friday when he crucified on the tree of ignominy the only truly Good thing ever to be seen on this earth, and God came back on an Easter day to save him from his own deceptions. It is a peculiar fact of history that man never seems to need God so much as when his own strength fails him; God does the most for us when, like Peter, we have worked through a whole long night and caught nothing.

Our world is full of people who have lost the path; of souls whose sins have been psychoanalyzed many times but forgiven never; of bodies embittered by sorrow and never sweetened by the vision of the cross; of hearts that have chased after thousands of luxuries, only to be left completely disillusioned in the end; of poor people who think that riches will give them peace of mind; of desperate people who think a drink can make them forget.

But for all these souls the message of Easter rings out: there is no reason to despair. The Resurrection was announced to Magdalene: a soul like ours.

So not everything is lost. We need to realize that we have crucified the Only One who can save us. We need to discover, like the prodigal son, that there is no place like the Father’s house. We need to accept the grace of Christ’s Resurrection.

This is the invitation of Easter: to live in the hope of the risen Christ! Peace awaits you in the service of the God who made you; Redemption awaits you in the Mass; Forgiveness calls you from the Confessional; Love [wants] you in the Eucharist. As Pope Francis reminded us: “Let us not flee from the Resurrection of Jesus; let us never declare ourselves dead, whatever happens.” (Evangelii Gaudium, 3)

Because that is the message of Easter day: the Resurrection of the Dead One, the Triumph of the Vanquished, the Finding of the Lost; the Rebirth of the Earth; the Awakening of Life; the Trumpet of the Resurrection is resounding over the land of the living.

So, in conclusion, the thought I would leave you with is that no matter how hopeless things may seem, there is still hope, because Christ is the Resurrection and the Life. “No matter how dark things are, goodness rises up again and spreads,” said Pope Francis. He who can make snowflakes out of drops of dirty water, draw diamonds out of coal, and make saints out of Magdalenes, can also make you victorious if you confess Him, in his mystical life on earth, as the Christ, the Son of the Living God.

May the joy of our faith in the Resurrection of the Lord be rekindled as a quiet but firm confidence, because the unchanging love of our Lord never ceases. Bring your hearts to complete salvation, because the great storms of the Redemption are flooding everything! Bring your lesser calvaries to the Great Calvary, because the via crucis is the via pacis. The way to peace is the cross. Take up your cross and follow me. Then, beneath the muffled thunder of battle, the victory cry of the King of kings and Lord of lords will sound, because when man has lost the day, God returns to give him the victory!

[Peroratio] Dear Brothers and Sisters: on this Easter morning, each of us must pause before the empty tomb and meditate on the supreme miracle of the Resurrection of Christ.

May the Blessed Virgin Mary, silent witness of this mystery, strengthen us in our personal adherence to Him who died and rose for us. May She be our teacher and our guide in faith; may She support us in moments of doubt and temptation; may She obtain for us that interior peace which no one can disturb, because it is rooted in the certainty that Christ has truly risen.

On this beautiful Sunday morning, at the feet of our Blessed Mother, let us entrust our lives to the Mother of the Risen Christ, together with all our intentions and all our hopes. Let us ask her for the grace always to keep alive the hope and joy of this Easter morning, which reminds us that love has triumphed, that mercy has been victorious! Happy Easter to all!
