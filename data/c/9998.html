Homily preached by Rev. Fr. Carlos Miguel Buela on Sunday, June 18, at the Holy Mass of the Solemnity of Corpus Christi

I

“Eat, friends, drink, O beloved, be drunk!” (Song 5:1). St. Thomas applies this verse of the Song of Songs to the Eucharist1.

This is one of the effects of the Eucharist: TO DELIGHT. (“Delectat,” says St. Thomas2). Just as material food delights the body, this spiritual food delights the soul.

That is why: “Eat, friends, drink, O beloved, be drunk!” (Song 5:1).

This sacrament spiritually gives grace together with charity. Hence St. John Damascene compares it to the burning coal that the prophet Isaiah saw: “Just as the coal is not simply wood, but wood with fire, so the bread of communion is not ordinary bread, but bread united to the divinity”3.

O miraculous thing!
The banquet and the one who invites are one and the same4.

II

“Eat, friends, drink, O beloved, be drunk!” (Song 5:1).

St. Gregory the Great teaches that “the love of God is not idle, but, when it is possessed, it works great things”5; it follows that this sacrament has of itself the efficacy not only to give the habit of grace and of virtue –especially of charity– but also to stir up the act of charity, because, as St. Paul says, “the love of Christ urges us on” (2 Cor 5:14). With the love of Christ “the soul is strengthened, spiritually delights, and in some way becomes inebriated with the sweetness of the divine goodness,” teaches St. Thomas6.

The soul… “delights and in some way becomes inebriated!”

Hence: “Eat, friends, drink, O beloved, be drunk!” (Song 5:1).

That is why we exclaim in the “Anima Christi”: “Blood of Christ, inebriate us!”

O miraculous thing!
The banquet and the one who invites are one and the same.

III

St. Thomas calls this delight the actual effect or actual charity7, and also fervor, because it implies actuality, and a tense actuality. The grace of the Eucharist, which theologians call nourishing grace, among other things actually produces the sustaining of the spiritual life, its increase, its development, the repairing of the strength that is lost, giving greater habitual grace and greater habitual charity. But beyond the actuality of the habit there is the actuality of the act into which the possessed habit bursts forth. The Eucharist produces love of God in souls. That is why, when we are at Mass, we love more; that is why the Mass does us good, because it teaches us to love our neighbor more by teaching us to love God more.

This delight produced by the Eucharist is also called joy, because it comes from the actual perception of the good that one possesses –nothing less than Christ!– for which there must be no distraction in the reception –sacramental or spiritual– of the Eucharist. Many souls lose the actual delight of the Eucharist… because they are distracted at Mass or at Adoration! Set aside foolish distractions!: “Eat, friends, drink, O beloved, be drunk!” (Song 5:1).

IV

The delight produced by the Eucharist is not necessarily sensible, nor is it a sensible affection either. It is a spiritual joy, a deep joy of the soul, a supernatural joy that comes from the appreciation of the great good that is received: the Lord, Jesus Christ!, with all that he is and with all that he has. That is why Don Miguel de Cervantes Savedra says, in a poem:

O miraculous thing!
The banquet and the one who invites are one and the same.

The delight consists substantially in the promptness of the will for the virtuous works of the Christian life.

Besides actual distractions, that is, at the moment of communion, what other things impede the delight of the Eucharist? Venial sins. Actual venial faults impede the actual effect of the Eucharist; not the habitual effect, but certainly the actual one. The spiritual sweetness is infallible on the part of the sacrament, but actual attachment to venial faults or actual distraction at the moment of Communion –sacramental or spiritual– impede the effect of actual joy, of spiritual fervor, of delight or of actual love, which are all the same thing.

V

“Eat, friends, drink, O beloved, be drunk!”

(Song 5:1).

Urban IV said of the Eucharist: an admirable and stupendous memorial, delightful, sweet… in which every delight and every sweetness of flavor is tasted, and the very sweetness of God is savored…”8 And Leo XIII: “it pours into (souls) the sweetest joys, which far exceed anything that men can understand and appreciate in this matter”9.

That is why: Dear friends, “Eat, …drink, …be drunk!” (Song 5:1).

O miraculous thing!
The banquet and the one who invites are one and the same.

Panem de coelo praestitisti eis. Omne delectamentum in se habentem.

You gave us, Lord, bread from heaven. Containing in itself every delight!
