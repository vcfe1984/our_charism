The Crown, the Mantle and the Moon at the Feet of Our Lady of Luján

[Exordium] Dear brothers, I would like first of all to thank God for this beautiful gift of providence, that we can be here together as a Religious Family around our Mother, the Virgin of Luján. To honor and express our love for her who is the “excellent masterpiece of the Most High”1, the “paradise of God”2, as St. Louis Marie called her, and who is, at the same time… our Mother!

Our Directory of Spirituality says “that we were conceived in the most pure womb of Mary, and that is why she is our true and proper spiritual Mother”3. What is more, it clearly states: “We were born from the womb of the Virgin, in the manner of a body united to its head”4, the Head who is Christ, the Incarnate Word. That is why we say with all propriety that she is our Mother and that there is between us and the Most Holy Virgin a most intimate union, like that of a mother with her child, who expects everything from her, to whom he entrusts everything.

And as our Mother, the Virgin of Luján is in fact wholly devoted to the good of our souls, whether in the physical or material order, but above all in the spiritual order.

I would like, then, on this day, contemplating the beautiful image of the Most Pure and Immaculate Conception of Luján that we have here, to reflect together with you on the unlimited confidence we must have in the Most Holy Virgin, as true children of so blessed a Mother. And I would like to do so, if you will allow me, by looking at three elements that adorn her image: the crown, the mantle and the moon at her feet.

1. Crown

The original imperial crown of the Virgin of Luján was blessed by Pope Leo XIII and placed upon the precious brow of our Mother on a day like today, exactly 130 years ago, this being the first coronation of an image of Our Lady in America5. We can say that today we are also celebrating that event.

The Virgin of Luján is Queen. For Christ himself crowned his most worthy Mother with a crown of twelve stars and raised her upon a royal throne, establishing her as Queen and Lady of all creation. And if she is Queen, she is Queen of the Angels, of the Apostles, of the Martyrs, of the Virgins, of all the Saints, of families, and therefore also of our Religious Family. In fact, our Founder consecrated our Institutes and the Third Order to her6.

But let us not forget that, although she is Queen, she is a Queen all sweetness and clemency, inclined to do us not only good, but only good and the greatest good, to all and always. And that is why our dear St. John Paul II says: “The title of Queen does not replace that of Mother: her queenship is a corollary of her particular maternal mission, and simply expresses the power conferred on her to carry out that mission”7.

So, even amid crosses, persecution, setbacks of any kind, when human strength fails, and even under the weight of “our faults and sins, no one –that is, none of us– should distrust the mercy of God”8, because Mary is Mother and Queen of Mercy. And if she is Queen and Mother, her power is immense.

Hence we say that before the Most Holy Queen the powers of the principalities of this world vanish, adversities, however threatening they may be, wane, and the greatest sorrows and crosses are sweetened.

How many miracles the Virgin of Luján has worked to convince us of her formidable power! And although they are many, I would like to bring here the example that Fr. Salvaire himself, a French Lazarist missionary, recounts about himself. He says that it happened at the end of 1875, on the Argentine pampas, when he was 28 years old. There the Indians took him prisoner and were going to execute him because they accused him of having brought the plague of smallpox to those lands. Surrounded by the Indians, who rode in circles around him with lances in hand, he only prayed and promised Our Lady –before whom he had prayed in an oratory before beginning his mission– that if he was saved he would devote his life to raising a shrine to her and spreading her devotion. It was then that a young man appeared from the midst of the throng and interceded with the chief to set him free. And so, miraculously saved by the intercession of the Virgin of Luján, this holy priest built the Basilica in her honor (the first Gothic church in pointed-arch style in Argentina), wrote a magnificent two-volume book with the history of Our Lady, and it was he himself who petitioned the papal blessing of the crown for our Mother. It is good to recall this event today, as we celebrate one more anniversary of the coronation.

And so this example –like so many others we could cite here– shows us how all of us will find a ray of hope in a corner of our heart whenever we invoke the help of Mary. May contemplating the crown of our Mother always remind us of the sovereign power of the Queen of Mercy!

2. Sky-Blue and White Mantle

And this leads me to speak to you about the second element that adorns the Virgin of Luján, and that is her mantle.

Because “according to Spanish custom, from the earliest times she was dressed in garments. And since she is the Immaculate Conception, the attire is a white tunic and a sky-blue mantle”9.

This mantle of Our Lady is like the wings of the hen under which the chicks take shelter. And it must always remind us that, as a good Mother, she wants all of us in her lap, united under her mantle, close to her Most Pure Heart, so united to the fountain of Life born on Golgotha. That is why everything that causes division or dispersion, or hinders union, saddens her greatly. What mother is happy if one of her children is missing? Ah!… But if one should come to be missing, with what solicitude this Good Mother goes out in search of her children! And with a thousand delicate touches and motherly tones she tries to draw him close again.

An example of this very thing is the fact that, already from the time when the Black Manuel took care of the Virgin of Luján, many times at dawn he would find her covered with dew, and at other times with her mantle full of burrs and dust and some mud10, because she went out during the nights in search of her children to rescue them from their vices, or to draw near in the hardship of one of them, to free them from some danger or to help them die well. For she herself wants to lavish her care upon them and to feed them and defend them and give them everything they need. What is more, she does not allow anyone to leave her side disconsolate or to feel less loved. And so the saints of all times have seen in the mantle of the Mother of God an eloquent sign of her maternal protection, of sure defense against all evils, of shelter against the snares of evil. St. Alphonsus said: “Let us run to this divine Mother, let us take refuge under her mantle, and she will save us”11.

St. John Paul II affirmed: “Mary, glorified in heaven […] continues to be Mother of all men, […] until the end of the ages. In the light of the divine glory, she contemplates all and each one of her children, in all and in each of the moments of their existence”12.

The Virgin of Luján loves us as much as her power, and her power has no limits, because she is the Suppliant Omnipotence; and so she follows us with her tender gaze wherever we go and extends to all of us the mantle of her tenderness, which shelters us from the cold of loneliness, protects us from the heat of the passions, serves as our refuge against the evil one, and soothes the sorrows of this life.

How many times in our short history as a Religious Family have we experienced the sure help of the Virgin of Luján, who has even made us progress in everything! The same –I am sure– each of us can say in a most personal way: How many times Our Lady has saved us from imminent dangers, has brought us out of extremely difficult situations, and, even more, has granted us favors before we asked her for them!

How fitting, then, is the beautiful custom that our elders taught us of kissing the mantle of Our Lady, which is at once an invoking of her protection and a grateful response to so much affection.

That is why: “Let no one [of us] lose heart! Let no one be led astray in moments of difficulty and of eventual defeats! Let no one be overcome by the temptation that efforts are useless!”13. The colors of the mantle of our Blessed Mother must make us raise our gaze to heaven, where one day we hope, as our whole reward, to embrace her for all eternity. Her sky-blue mantle reminds us that heaven is the goal. Let us then always keep present and firmly rooted in our mind and heart that we have a Mother who watches over us and always accompanies us and who desires –even more than we do– to give us every good. Protected under the shield of her mantle, we have nothing to fear.

That is why our confidence in the Mother of Heaven must be unlimited, unshakable, unbreakable. Knowing that she does not remain distant, but becomes fully involved and follows step by step every detail of our lives.

3. Moon

Finally, at the feet of the Holy Image there is a silver crescent moon, “because she is Mediatrix between Christ –the Sun– and the Church –the Moon.” And if she is our Mediatrix, then she is a “sign of sure hope”14.

Pope Benedict XVI explained that “the Most Holy Virgin Mary has the moon at her feet, an image of death and mortality, because she has left death behind and is completely clothed with life, the life of her Risen Son. And so the Virgin Mary is a sign of the victory of love, of the goodness of God, giving our world the hope it needs”15.

We, members of the Church, who journey as pilgrims “amid the consolations of God and the persecutions of the world” in order to bring the Incarnate Word to men, have in the Virgin of Luján our Mother, who unceasingly implores for us before the throne of God the gifts of salvation16. That is why, although the Church suffers her setbacks, she always comes out victorious17 –Pope Benedict affirmed–, since through the most powerful intercession of the Mother, there cannot triumph over those who entrust themselves to her, even “in spite of their poor strength, in spite of their littleness, in spite of their weakness, either the Evil One or those who belong to the Evil One. It is a prayer of the Mother and, therefore, it is a prayer that the Son is, so to speak, obliged to hear”18.

And Our Lady certainly does hear prayers! Look around you. Each of us is the living confirmation and the palpable proof that the Virgin of Luján hears prayers. Was it not to her that Fr. Buela asked for the grace to be able to accompany many vocations? See what an attentive and generous Mother Our Lady is!

So, if Our Lady is not only our Queen and Mother, but also our powerful Intercessor, why be afraid? St. John Paul II, when he visited Argentina, referred to the Virgin of Luján, calling her the “Virgin of Hope”19, through whom “salvation and the hope of a new world came to us”20.

This image of the Virgin of Luján, present in our communities, in all our missions, in countries so distant and in cultures so diverse, helps us to fix in our soul this reality, which undoubtedly strengthens our hope: that of the “certainty of the maternal protection of Our Lady” wherever we go and whatever the circumstances, whether speaking individually or as an Institute. Her presence among us seems to say to us: “Am I not here, I who am your Mother?”

That is why, today and always, “now and in the future,” “she is all our hope because she is the Mother of the One who is ‘our hope’”21.

[Peroration] St. John Bosco wrote to one of his missionaries who was somewhat discouraged, saying: “We must work! Think of dying on the field of labor as a good soldier of Christ. Am I good for nothing? I can do all things in Him who strengthens me. Are there thorns? With the thorns turned into flowers, the angels will weave a crown for you in heaven. Are the times difficult? They always were, but God never failed with his help. Christ is the same yesterday and today”22. And we know that this help comes to us unfailingly through the virginal hands of Most Holy Mary. That is why let us always go forward, always doing good, without being intimidated, confident in the help and protection of Mary.

And the same saint, on another occasion, “in 1862 confided to Don Cagliero: ‘Such sad times are upon us that we need precisely for Our Lady to help us preserve the Christian faith.’ And St. John Paul II, reflecting precisely on Don Bosco's saying, commented: “[These] are grave and serious words that we too can repeat today, consolidating ever more our love and our confidence in Mary. Trust in Mary. Entrust your activities and concerns to her maternal care each day.”23

And that is exactly what we want to do: To her we entrust all the activities, all our missions and all the concerns of our Religious Family. In a particular way, to her we entrust the religious who today will make their perpetual vows, making a total offering of their life to Jesus, through his Mother.

May this always be our attitude: that of unrestricted confidence and of an immense and tender love for the Mother of God.

We ask the Virgin of Luján to obtain for us this grace from her Son. And we also ask it through the intercession of the Spiritual Father of Our Religious Family, our dear St. John Paul II.
