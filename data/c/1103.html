As all of you know, ongoing formation is fundamental for us1 “and knows no limits of age or situation”2. Because we must always be converted3, and because religious life itself “is a living grace which asks to be received and lived in conditions of existence that are often unprecedented”4, as you will have experienced so many times. This requires on our part “continually verifying one’s own fidelity to the Lord, docility to His Spirit, intelligent attention to circumstances and to the signs of the times, the will to be inserted in the Church, the readiness to be subordinate to the hierarchy, boldness in initiatives, constancy in self-giving, humility in bearing setbacks, etc.”5. That is why Fr. Buela speaks of the need “to evangelize oneself, because when one abandons that, relaxation begins, which can reach the point of scandal”6.

What is more, the very fact of wanting to be faithful to our charism, to the evangelical intention of our founder, cannot but impel us “to travel—as the Proper Law says—a demanding itinerary of ongoing formation”7. And in this sense, “the ‘doctrinal deepening’ of one’s own charism is extremely important”8. That is why the Directory of Ongoing Formation of the Servants mandates that, in the communities, the frequent reading and study of the Constitutions, the Directory of Spirituality and the other Directories, as well as the writings of Fr. Buela, be fostered9.

Thus ongoing formation is not and must not be merely intellectual, but must encompass “all the aspects of the person of the religious”10, as our Constitutions so aptly say: ongoing formation also includes the human, the spiritual and the pastoral dimensions11.

Today I would like—if you will allow me—to devote myself to a topic that perhaps has more to do with spiritual formation, but which definitely has a great influence on the human and pastoral dimensions as well. Therefore, in this little talk I propose to set out for you something I mentioned in the Circular Letter of the month of May, when I spoke of “the particular imprint that the preventive system exercises on our way of living religious life, and which becomes so incisive (in us and in everything we do) that the Proper Law itself calls it the ‘embodiment of our spirituality’12”. The phrase is very strong, and the truth is that I chose this topic because it seems to me that it can be of great help in living our own consecration in all its fullness, in “fidelity to the spirit and to the end intended by our dear Founder”13 and to the “the specific mission that the Church has entrusted to us”14.

Needless to say, this does not intend to be an exhaustive examination of all the aspects of our religious life that reveal the imprint of the preventive system, but it certainly does intend to show, from the Proper Law, how the ‘oratorian spirit’ forms an integral and inescapable part of the fabric of our way of living religious life and is, in short—as we said before—the ‘embodiment of our spirituality’15. [You can find the corresponding quotation in numbers 199 and 201 of the Directory of Oratories]

[Embodiment of our spirituality] We say that the ‘oratorian spirit’ is part of our spirituality simply because the preventive system is the evangelical charity that the Incarnate Word Himself came to teach us, and which He Himself practiced and commanded us to practice: I have given you an example, so that you may do as I have done to you16. From this arises, in a certain way, the relevance of the topic, since it is our obligation, according to what we declare in our Directory of Spirituality, “to want to imitate Jesus Christ as perfectly as possible”17.

Respect for the person

Let us begin, then, by saying that the Incarnate Word assumed an integral human nature18 “to show the human race its dignity”19, and He assumed it in a real way20, precisely in order to elevate it, dignify it and perfect it21; so that grace might act in that human nature, transforming it from within. This in itself entails a very great respect for the person, for his talents, for his gifts, for his freedom and even for his ‘shortcomings’ and the limits of his strength … as a sign of profound veneration for the action of God in the soul of each one.

The oratorian spirit itself echoes this in all its essential elements. Did not Don Bosco command them “never to order things harmful to health, or that prevent necessary rest, or that involve a conflict of obligations or with the orders of another superior”22? Did he not earnestly recommend to superiors that they share in the inclinations of their subjects in order to find the best way to win them over and lead them to the good23? And the reason for this is given by the Saint of Turin himself: “Thus did Jesus: tolerating in His apostles ignorance, roughness and even little fidelity”24.

You know very well that “the preventive system rests entirely on reason, religion and love”25. Therefore, it does not use “coercive means, but rather persuasion and charity (amorevolezza)”26, with the intention of “so disposing the spirit that, without violence, they bend to the will of God”27.

That is why the preventive system, or the oratorian spirit, which must reign in our communities and interpersonal relationships, requires a solicitude that is extremely respectful of the person28. Whether these persons are subjects, superiors, priests, or souls in the apostolate. Whether one is in a house of formation or in mission lands. Whether or not I happen to have the role of formator, because all of you here—as the spiritual mothers that you are—must be extremely interested in the education of your spiritual children, whether they are sisters in religion or not. And the attitude of respect is fundamental. But also because to act otherwise would be to contradict our being as religious of the Incarnate Word.

St. John Bosco wrote to his own: “Remember that education is a matter of the heart, and that God alone is the master of hearts”29. You will have heard it many times: we are not “masters of souls”30. We are at their service; therefore we must respect them and love them as children of God, fostering voluntary submission, so that they may cooperate freely and responsibly, of their own will, in the love of all that is good31, and this, which is commanded to superiors, every Servant can and must apply in all her relationships. Acting always with “the greatest respect for the freedom of each one”32. Because what is ours “is to live and to make others live under the action of the Holy Spirit, without coercion of any kind, scrupulously respecting consciences, promoting healthy pluralism, leading others to live fully the freedom of the children of God”33, as our Constitutions say.

And this has repercussions throughout the length and breadth of our entire Proper Law, and therefore on the way in which we live our consecrated life in this precious Religious Family. For example: “all are encouraged to feel co-responsible”34 and “the participation of all”35 is fostered, but no one is forced. All of us are persuaded to do good; we are “always shown the foundations and motives of the various norms and decisions, inasmuch as they are directed to the common good”36, so that we may be persuaded “of how necessary this is not only for achieving a true community life, but also for developing and strengthening the aptitudes proper to each one”37, and so that we may act out of conviction, spontaneously and joyfully, not “out of inertia or coercion, but because we have been persuaded intimately and charitably”38; but no one is trampled on. Quite the contrary, we are taught “to know how to judge all things, and as virtuous persons to move freely toward the good”39, and that is how we approach formation.

To act otherwise is to betray the spirit that animates our dear Religious Family. Which, in the words of our founder, takes on an even more serious connotation, for he says that it would be “a lack of respect for the dignity of the human being and a lack of respect for the dignity that every religious man and every religious woman must have”40.

This respect that we speak of for the human person in his integrity applies in every sphere, and on this you can also read the Directory of Missions Ad Gentes, of Evangelization of Culture, etc. But here I will only mention what the Directory of Fraternal Life says: “Cultivate mutual respect, by which the slow pace of the weakest is accepted and, at the same time, the emergence of richer personalities is not stifled, favoring creativity”41.

Don Bosco himself recounts that Michael Magone was a boy of “a spontaneous and lively disposition”, and when recreation came “he seemed to shoot out as if fired from a cannon”42. The saint, condescending to the boy’s way of being and seeing many others in him, managed to get them a good quantity of wooden rifles and swords, bocce balls, stilts, and everything that could contribute to giving joyful and free expansion to his personality. “Absolutely nothing is gained by having clones”43, says Fr. Buela.

Notice that even when it comes to correcting, Don Bosco invites us to be respectful toward the one who is to be corrected, setting before us the example of our Lord with St. Paul, about which the Saint comments: “I seem to see in this our norm to follow when we encounter hearts reluctant to our will”44. Our Lord did not shame him in public; He did not knock him off his horse suddenly, but after a long journey and after having given him the opportunity to reflect. It is then that, strongly but meekly, He enlightens his reason—something very important—so that he might recognize his error45, precisely out of respect for him, for his intelligence, for his freedom, for his conscience.

In this regard we can draw a direct line to the norms of the Proper Law, which explicitly quote the apostle of youth. It reads thus: “they are our children, and therefore, when we correct their errors, we must lay aside all anger… let us have understanding in the present and hope for the future, as befits true parents who are sincerely concerned with the correction and amendment of their children”46. And that is why we are commanded to correct “in private and out of pure charity”, in such a way that it leads to a serene amendment47; avoiding at all costs “those humiliating corrections that discourage and drive away”48.

“The human spirit is made in such a way that, when treated with rigor, it rebels. Everything with gentleness, nothing by force; harshness ruins everything, exasperates hearts, breeds hatred. Gentleness handles the heart of man at will and makes of it what it wishes”49. And you, as women, as mothers of souls, must be experts in this; in the likeness of Most Holy Mary, “the gentlest of women, the furthest removed from all anger, discourtesy and arrogance”50.

Joined to this comes a key theme in the preventive system: amorevolezza, which we have already spoken about in the circular letter. But on this occasion I only want to mention it in relation to our way of living our consecration in the Institute and to the family spirit that must reign in our communities and among our Institutes.

Spirit of authentic family

What is ours, dear sisters, is to live “in a spirit of authentic family51, in an oratorian atmosphere52, in serene and contagious joy”53. All of these are elements clearly taken from the preventive system.

There are a great many times when the Proper Law refers to this aspect, which tells us of the great importance it has for our religious life. We are not independent islands. We do not walk along parallel paths and—oh!—it just so happens that we have the same founder and the same charism, or that we wear the same habit. We are a family! Where there are mothers, fathers, children, brothers, sisters… and, therefore, there is (or there must be) mutual collaboration, fraternal affection, support, trust, “the generous self-giving of each one to the good of the others”54 … beyond the difficulties and frictions there may be, humanly speaking. What unites us is the Incarnate Word. Who, yes, it is true, called each one of us individually, but in order to form a Family. And a family is forever.

Always keep this in mind. God thought of us as a Religious Family. I challenge you to verify it for yourselves by reading the Proper Law. Therefore, every attitude, every project, or whatever it may be, that contradicts, hinders, ridicules, or seeks to distance us from this family spirit that must reign in our communities and among our Institutes, is not from God; it is not what God wants for us. That causes division, discontent, desires for one to predominate over the other, and that is not for us. The Incarnate Word called us together to be “a family united in His name”55, and our apostolic strength—in great part—comes from there and is strengthened from there. This exercises a powerful attraction on young people and becomes for us a source of vocations. Without going any further, how many seminarians, how many priests have been directed to the Institute by one of you, and how many of you have found the Institute of the Servants through the IVE. Think also of how many activities—on which we currently work together—would be unimaginable today without your help or ours.

This family spirit that must reign in our communities and among our Institutes “must increase and deepen that human sensitivity which allows us to understand needs and welcome requests, to sense unexpressed questions, to share hopes and expectations, the joys and labors of ordinary life; to be able to meet everyone and dialogue with everyone”56, to anticipate the needs of others, and to offer oneself willingly to do whatever needs to be done for the good of the Family. Be convinced that our Religious Family is a treasure! And we have to take care of it, to live fond of it; we have to be happy to bring it more and more everywhere; we have to defend it and be aware of how much we owe it!

There is a line from the Notes of the last General Chapter of the Servants that I find particularly apt. It reads thus: “pastoral work with the IVE brings the charism to its fullness and therefore produces great fruits of fruitfulness”57. Exactly so. The more complementarily we work together—so it seems to me—the more fruitful our labor will be, the more incisive, the more effective and the more lasting. And we will be happier, more at peace, stronger, more united, because we will be doing what God wants us to do and as God wants us to do it. And this gives our little Religious Family an incalculable potential.

Trust

Now, regarding this family spirit, I would only like to add one more element which I think is always worth revisiting, and that is trust, to which St. John Bosco gives great importance and which the Proper Law takes up as an inescapable element if we are to live in a spirit of authentic family. Because “trust is one of the things that most favors the smooth running of a religious congregation and the peace and happiness of its members”58.

Note that we can live with 10, 20 or 100 sisters, and follow the same schedule, and pray together in the chapel, but if there is no trust, then there is no familiarity, and therefore the family spirit bleeds out because it is wounded at its center, because there is no trust. Let these paragraphs that I quote below, taken from the Proper Law, serve as illustration:

  • “This familiarity or family spirit is what will make it possible to form the religious effectively, for ‘familiarity begets affection, and affection trust’, and only by having this trust with their Superiors ‘do the young reveal everything’, that is, they become docile in the Holy Spirit to their formative work”59.
  • “Don Bosco teaches in the same letter ‘that the young should not only be loved, but should realize that they are loved’. For ‘he who is loved loves, and he who is loved obtains everything, especially from the young’”60. This is also taken from the of Studentates, so the word young person can perfectly well be replaced by religious.
  • “The Superior moreover has the obligation to win the trust of her religious in order to be able to carry out her role as guide”61, since “if she does not know them because they do not open themselves to her, it is impossible for her to direct them and help them with her advice and suggestions”62. It is extremely important in this regard that the superior keep strict secrecy about what the sister tells her in confidence, since “a small indiscretion on this point may be enough to diminish and perhaps totally destroy the trust they had placed in her”63.
  • Now, do you want a clear example of this familiarity? St. John Bosco says it, and the Proper Law quotes it explicitly: “Jesus Christ made Himself little with the little ones and bore our infirmities. Behold the master of familiarity!”64.

This trust and familiarity, although always important, becomes imperative, so to speak, when we go through critical moments, whatever the factors that influence them may be: whether external, such as “a change of place or of office, a difficulty at work or an apostolic failure, misunderstanding, a certain feeling of marginalization… or for more strictly personal reasons, such as physical or psychological illness, spiritual dryness, bereavements, problems in interpersonal relationships, strong temptations, crises of faith, a certain sense of insignificance, or other similar factors”65. In any of these cases, look at what our dear John Paul II recommends: “When fidelity becomes more difficult, the person must be offered the support of greater trust and a greater love, both at the personal and community level. What is necessary, above all at such moments, is the affectionate closeness of the Superior; great comfort and encouragement also come from the qualified help of a brother or sister, whose availability and readiness will facilitate a rediscovery of the meaning of the covenant which God was the first to establish and which He will not fail to keep”66. It seems to me that the resonance of the preventive system in this is clear, is it not?

So much for trust. There is much more that you can go on reading and deepening. The riches that God has poured out in the Proper Law of our Institutes are truly countless, so I invite you to know how to take advantage of everything that is there.

Joy

Finally, and now drawing to a close, I wanted also to point out, as a characteristic part of our way of living consecrated life, joy, to which Don Bosco and the Proper Law give such a preponderant place.

Joy is an essential characteristic of the family atmosphere. Do not the Constitutions tell us: “let no one be disturbed or saddened in the house of God”67? Precisely because joy is an expression of love and the logical consequence of that oratorian spirit based on reason and on religiosity, which has its origin in peace with God and in the life of grace.

We have been called to live “with imperturbable joy, in the light and in the cross”68. A joy which, as you know, must “be spiritual and supernatural”69, but which is also helped by external elements that nourish it and transmit it, because obviously we are a union of body and soul. That is why the Proper Law points out: “It is very important to cultivate this joy in the religious community”70.

You must “know how to rejoice together, allow yourselves personal and community moments of relaxation, take some distance from your own work from time to time, rejoice in the joys of the other, pay solicitous attention to her needs, give yourselves generously to apostolic work, face situations with mercy, go out to meet the future with the hope of always and everywhere finding the Lord: all this nourishes serenity, peace and joy, and becomes strength for apostolic action”71.

And we have a great many things taken from the magnificent example of Don Bosco that contribute to strengthening and nourishing this joy and become the most palpable and intense expression of this joy. I think, for example, of the outings, of the pilgrimages, of the eutrapelia every Friday or Sunday, of the liturgical feasts that help us so much in fervor; I think of the celebrations of the patron saints, of the campfires, of the skits, of music, sports, the floral games, the covivium, and so many other things… which we take for granted, but which not all congregations have, and which become for us a treasure and a most beautiful means of giving glory to God, and for us, on the way, something that makes our crosses more bearable.

Let us not forget that what is ours is “to live with festive joy”; our Constitutions say so in number 41. And it is in this sense that we are encouraged to “know how to celebrate”72 and we are asked not to skimp on the means to “create the atmosphere so that celebration and joy may be lived”73. Even more: we are explicitly told “not to be afraid of spending on food, sweets, prizes that contribute to the celebration”74. This applies not only to the end of the academic year; rather, one must know how to “invest” in cultivating that festive joy in the community.

Because “a fraternity without joy is a fraternity that is dying out. [And] very soon its members will be tempted to seek elsewhere what they cannot find at home”75.

Conclusion

In short, dear sisters, now truly to conclude, I would like to leave you ‘a keepsake’, as Don Bosco calls it, and the same one he left to the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians on August 23, 1885, when ‘after repeated insistence’ he went to give them a little talk.

I make his words my own: “I leave you, then, a keepsake; you will never regret having put it into practice: Do good, do good works. Toil, all of you, work much and always for the Lord with good will. Yes. Do not waste time. Do all the good you can. You will never regret it.

Do you want another? The practice of the holy Rule! Keep your Rule, and I repeat to you that you will never be sorry for it. Note well: our Rules, dear daughters, are infallible and provide us with many advantages; but the most important of all is that they assure us the salvation of the soul. Do not be surprised at the word infallible, because, having been approved by [the church*], which is infallible, each of its articles is also infallible. Read them, meditate on them, try to understand them well and to practice them.

[…] Dear daughters, keep yourselves joyful, healthy and holy, and always be at peace among yourselves”76.

I thank you again for the invitation and for your attention. May God bless you. I wish you this with all my heart.

I commend myself and all the members of the IVE to your prayers. Thank you again.

June 4, 2018
