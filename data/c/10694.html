Homily preached on the occasion of the Feast of the Presentation of the Child Jesus in the Temple

The sacred liturgy on the feast of the Presentation raises us to consider the sufferings of Christ and of Mary. It is the feast of consecration, in which the offering of Christ is made, and it is interesting to see how the offering of Christ takes place by means of suffering. With every step the Savior took, the cross became more evident.

The presentation of the Child Jesus and the prophecy of Simeon open the sorrowful portico of the life of the Most Blessed Virgin.

As soon as Mary presents to God an offering worthy of Him, she immediately receives the reward of an unspeakable sorrow for her whole life, because it is a fixed thought of the Lord that the sufferings of this world should be the roots of the graces of the next. That is why the saints rejoice in suffering, becoming like Christ.

From this moment everything was sorrow for Mary: time, because it shortened the distance to Calvary; the sight of the Child, which reminded her of it; the sight of the fields and the city, which always brought to her mind something related to the Passion. The Most Blessed Virgin had to carry out her tasks always under this affliction. And if she was able to do so, it is because she always had Jesus present.

Thus St. Teresa affirms it, saying: “The Lord said this to me another day: Do you think, daughter, that merit lies in enjoyment? It lies only in working and in suffering and in loving … You see My whole life full of suffering, and only on Mount Tabor will you have heard of My joy. Do not think, when you see My Mother holding Me in her arms, that she enjoyed those consolations without grave torments. From the time Simeon spoke those words to her, My Father gave her clear light to see what I was to suffer …”. There Mary understood better all that she would have to suffer. Perhaps there all the sufferings she had endured at the birth of Christ began to become clear to her.

The immensity of Mary’s sorrow and the promptness with which she accepts her oblation show the holiness of our Mother, for God proportions tribulations to the strength necessary to bear them.

There is something that is always said when speaking of the sufferings of Jesus and Mary, and it is that such is the union between the two that every suffering was increased by the fact that one suffered for the other. And this is so because each suffered for the suffering of the other; more still, each suffered because the other suffered on account of his or her sufferings. St. Bernard said, referring to Mary at the foot of the cross: The same pain that filled the heart of Mary set the heart of Jesus ablaze with bitterness; and he has Our Lady say: I was there seeing Him and He was seeing me, and He suffered more for me than for Himself.

It is interesting, but on this day the Church marks the day of religious life, because it is the day on which the offering of Christ by Mary is celebrated. And if in all the acts of our life we must look to Christ as our model, according to that saying of St. Thomas that the actions of Christ are our example, with much greater reason in this mystery in which the consecration of religious life is represented.

The consecration of Christ and Mary was by means of suffering. Simeon says: My eyes have seen the salvation, but then he adds: a sword will pierce your heart. Salvation and sorrow are intimately united, in such a way that they cannot be separated. If we want to make the same offering as Christ, we must also know how to consecrate ourselves through suffering and sorrow.

Like Jesus and Mary, we consecrate ourselves for salvation, to suffer. This is how salvation is worked. St. Paul tells the Ephesians: Therefore I beg you not to lose heart in your many sufferings, as I suffer for you, for all these tribulations are for your glory.

This suffering is the path that God has chosen for all His saints, even for the greatest persons who ever walked this earth: Jesus and Mary.

St. Ignatius himself in the Spiritual Exercises says: “I will look and consider what Our Lady and St. Joseph are doing, how they set out on the road, how they suffer a thousand trials so that the Lord may be born … and die at last on the cross, and all this for me.” For the dying of Christ was a consequence from the instant He was conceived; the sorrows of Mary are a consequence of that fiat of the Incarnation.

This is the glory of the saints and of the imitators of Christ. Without doubt the reason why Mary suffered was that God willed the Mother and the Son to be alike in everything, and that, since this Virgin was the most perfect of the perfect, she should not fail to share in the greatest glory of the Holy One of holies. And because the greatest glory of this Lord was to have suffered so many sorrows out of obedience to the Father, it was not right that His Most Holy Mother should lack a share in this glory.

On this day let us ask God to have it in our hearts to consecrate ourselves to God by the same means by which Jesus and Mary offered themselves, that is, through sorrow; let us ask to have the longing that St. Bernard had: “But what do we offer, brothers, or what do we give back to Him for all the good things He has done for us? She offered for us the most precious Victim she had, being unable to give a more precious one; let us too do what we can, offering Him the best we have, which is certainly ourselves. He offered Himself; who are you, that you hesitate to offer yourself? Oh, if I had the happiness that so great a Majesty would deign to receive my offering! …It is better for me, and much more useful and glorious, to offer myself to You than to keep myself for myself.”

And if one day the path of suffering seems difficult to us, we must repeat the invocation of St. Bernard: Look to the star, call upon Mary!
