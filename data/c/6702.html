Perfect model of a consecrated woman

[Exordium] Dear Mothers and Sisters, first of all, I would like to thank you for the invitation to celebrate this Eucharist with you on this Saturday traditionally dedicated to the Blessed Virgin Mary. Since we are essentially Marian1, and since our Proper Law has indicated in several of its documents the principal and preponderant role Our Lady has in our spirituality and missionary activity, we cannot fail to consider and go deeper into the example of our Most Holy Mother. In fact, our Proper Law presents her to us as the “perfect model of a consecrated person, whom every religious must always contemplate and imitate”2. That is why today I would like to highlight some aspects, or rather attitudes, of our Mother which, although they challenge every Christian, are –in my view– essential in a Servant.

1. Service

And the first attitude or quality that stands out in Our Lady is precisely that of service. Many times we have read in the Gospel that passage from the account of the Visitation which says: In those days Mary set out for the hill country…3. The words of the Gospel account make us see with the eyes of the heart the young Virgin of Nazareth on her way to the town of Judah where her cousin lived, in order to offer her services. Analogously, each Servant sister, because she is a missionary –which is another essential aspect in us– is or will be sent to another town to offer her services. This implies availability, forgetfulness of self in order to serve others, humility, zeal for souls… However, what strikes us above all in Mary is her attentiveness, full of tenderness,toward her elderly cousin. It is a concrete love, which is not limited to words of understanding, but commits itself personally to genuine assistance. Our Lady does not simply give her cousin something that belongs to her; she gives herself, without asking for anything in return. She has understood perfectly that the gift received from God, rather than a privilege, is a duty that commits her in favor of others with the gratuitousness proper to love.

That is why the quality that must stand out in each Servant sister must be precisely an attitude of service. If a sister does not serve others, she is of no service. It is a contradiction. Because what is proper to every Servant is precisely to serve, with humility, with concrete acts of service toward the members of her own community, toward the poor who come each day to the door of the convent, toward the priests, the seminarians –members of our own Family– and of course toward the souls that have been entrusted to her.

You must be convinced that you have consecrated your lives to the service of the Incarnate Word. That is not something abstract, a vague idea, or something utopian; it becomes concrete “in humble service and generous self-giving, in the gratuitous gift of yourselves through a love to the end”4 in the service of your neighbor. And saying gratuitous gift means without expecting reward, without expecting recognition, without expecting consolations, without demands… It is, moreover, a constant service; it is not something sporadic, but, following the example of Mary Most Holy, it is a self-giving that fills everything and every day.

If a Servant says she wants to be humble, or believes she is humble, but does not serve others, no matter how many resolutions she makes and prayers she raises to heaven, she is beating around the bush. That is why the virtue that, intrinsically associated with service, must most distinguish the Servants is humility.

And from there follows a second quality, which is:

2. Joy

Each day at Vespers we pray the canticle of the Magnificat, which says: my soul proclaims the greatness of the Lord and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior, for he has looked upon the lowliness of his handmaid.

It is precisely from that readiness to serve, from knowing herself to be the ‘servant of the Lord,’ that the joy of the Blessed Virgin springs. Similarly, if each Servant is ready to proclaim her fiat to the will of God –without reluctance, without slowness, without reservations– then she will be able to proclaim her magnificat and live her consecration to the full.

The joy of Our Lady springs from knowing that she is looked upon by God, despite her lowliness5; it springs from the ‘service’ she is able to render, thanks to the wonders the Almighty works in Her.

It is interesting to note that the Magisterium of the Church says that “woman cannot find herself except by giving love to others”6 and therefore she will not find her joy, her fulfillment, except by giving herself. Which I –by my poor lights– understand as giving everything that love asks of us, with total availability, immediately, unconditionally, without asking anything in return, with joy and wishing to be asked for even more.

For this what is needed –among other things, and in my view– is to ask for the grace of maternal wisdom, which instills that wholly provident affection that anticipates and satisfies the unspoken needs of one’s own; which carries charity down to the detail; which makes of its hidden and silent service to one’s own a luminous and joyful sacrifice; which defends one’s own even with a glance; which knows how to spare them pain and toil; which receives with a strong heart the worries and annoyances that cloud their faces; which never keeps a tally of the things it has done for those it loves, because its happiness lies in giving itself “without measure, to the extreme, until it can give no more, to the end.”

Notice that after the Magnificat came those three months of service to her cousin Elizabeth, about which the Gospel says nothing. Or perhaps it tells us the most important thing: good makes no noise; the strength of love is shown in the serene discretion of daily service. And that is a great lesson for all of us who so often seek offices and conspicuous apostolates that attract human glory to us. Our Lady served in silence.

3. Intercession

Finally, a third quality that we should see reproduced in each Servant is that of being an intercessor. We see it in the dialogue between Our Lady and the Incarnate Word at the wedding at Cana. She is sensitive to the needs of others7 and intercedes before Christ. She is the one who asks for graces and favors from heaven for us. St. Louis Marie says that there is no grace of God that does not pass through the hands of Mary. That is why the Blessed Virgin is the Woman who intercedes.

Likewise, each Servant must implore, with her sacrifices and prayers, favors from heaven for all souls, especially for the Institute, for the whole Religious Family and of course for all souls. Our Proper Law tells us so explicitly: “we must have recourse to her merciful intercession”8 and ask for all the graces we need to be faithful to our consecration9, as our formula of profession of vows says, a grace that must be asked not only for ourselves but for all the members of our Institutes.

In this sense “it befits the Servants… to direct everyone toward heaven by the example of their lives and by their intercession”; that is why you must pray for every grace for yourselves, for the Institute, for sinners … and especially now, when these are not easy times for the Church [and for the Institute], is when one must ask the most. The intercession of Mary Most Holy does not fail. Because as a good Mother she is not distant from our worries, our problems or our struggles; rather, with maternal solicitude She seeks to help us, protect us, defend us, give us what She knows is good for us to be happy in time and in eternity…

That is why I invite and encourage you to pray fervently for our own, for our own Religious Family, that it may always remain united and rooted in the precious charism that has been bequeathed to us. And when some cross comes upon you, some sacrifice you must make –God willing– be so good as to offer it –following the example of our heavenly Mother– for the good of our Family.

[Peroration] In short, dear sisters, the exemplary holiness of Our Lady must always move us to lift our eyes toward Her. That is why let us ask in this Holy Mass that the contemplation of the virtues of our Most Holy Mother –especially her readiness to serve, her joy and her dedication to intercessory prayer– may adorn our souls, and that we may reproduce them in concrete acts in daily life. For thus, only thus, will we be behaving as true devoted children of Our Lady and fulfilling our purpose of being essentially Marian.

May Our Lady bless you.
