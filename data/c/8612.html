The appointment of Fr. Teodoro Matsapula on May 8, the feast of Our Lady of Luján, as bishop of the Byzantine-rite Eparchy of Mukachevo in Ukraine, and his upcoming consecration on July 16, is without doubt a milestone in the history of the Institute.

Considering that the Institute of the Incarnate Word was founded barely 40 years ago, and that it has been present in Ukraine for barely 30 years, the appointment of the first IVE bishop is indeed a most singular grace. And this divine gift becomes even more outstanding considering the trying situation the Institute is going through.

This is therefore an event that cannot go unnoticed by us. Divine Providence has chosen us to be witnesses, and in some way sharers, of an immeasurable grace, for there is being fulfilled before our eyes −and with the favor of God− that fervent desire which our Founder left in writing in the Proper Law, namely, that of “being able to help our brothers of the Eastern Churches, who form part ‘of the undivided patrimony of the Universal Church’1”2; a task he considered pressing, particularly for us, who have been called by a special vocation to evangelize the culture3. What a privileged position for this is that of a bishop! And what a great grace that God has chosen one of our own for that noble task.

At the same time, this fact also demonstrates the capacity the Institute has had not to “fall into particularisms, reductionisms, partialities or one-sidedness that undermine the catholicity”4 of the Church; and if we can truly say that “the Catholic Church breathes with two lungs”5, today we can also affirm that the Church herself, by having entrusted to one of our own the shepherding of the souls of an entire territory of the Byzantine Church in Ukraine, recognizes that our Institute also breathes with “two lungs, that is, the Eastern and the Western”6. For that has been “the ‘theological reason’ that has moved us from the beginning to send missionaries to Ukraine”7, since in that nation −and as he had learned from St. John Paul II− Fr. Buela saw “the hope that the Church in Europe may once again breathe with her two lungs, so that the whole continent may experience a new evangelization”8.

Likewise, this simple fact is a tribute to the fully Catholic formation that Fr. Buela wanted for the Institute he founded, according to the will of the Incarnate Word, who wants to gather all into unity9 and desires to form priests “in absolute and unrestricted ecclesial communion”10.

With what pride Fr. Buela would look today upon Fr. Teodoro being anointed by the Holy Spirit “to continue His living mystery on behalf of the Church”11, especially in Ukraine. For with words of praise he said: “it is for us, members of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, a very great and providential grace to be in Ukraine, a historic people, a bridge between East and West”12; “from where the faith and Christian civilization radiated into Eastern Europe”13. And how rewarded our Founder would see his efforts, seeing one of our own consecrated a Successor of the Apostles.

“The state of the bishop,” the Proper Law points out, “is the most perfect of all, even more than the religious state; to him belongs the fullness of the priesthood; to him above all others, the spirit of faith, of wisdom and of fortitude… The bishop knows no enemies of his own; for him there are only children, and the littlest, the humblest, the most unhappy are dearest to him. For all he prays, for all he has words of eternal life, for all he goes up to the altar and offers the Blood of the spotless Lamb that takes away the sins of the world… The bishop is the Good Shepherd, who watches, feeds and evangelizes; who knows how to suffer in silence and knows how to give his life for his sheep”14. That, in brief, is what Fr. Buela longed for in one of our own who might become a bishop, and it is also what we ask today of the Incarnate Word, through the intercession of His Most Holy Mother, for the new bishop, above all: “the spirit of faith, of wisdom and of fortitude.”

What a beautiful occasion this is, then, to celebrate from every corner of the earth not only the consecration of the first IVE bishop, but also the fact that “we are members of one single missionary Religious Family, and above all, we are children of the One, Holy, Catholic and Apostolic Church, which ‘breathes’ with two lungs: the Eastern and the Western”15.

Let us raise our supplication to the Virgin Mary, whom the liturgy of St. Basil praises as most holy, spotless, blessed above all, glorious Lady, Mother of God and ever Virgin, Woman full of grace and joy of the whole universe16, and let us invoke the help of the heavenly patrons St. Basil and St. Josaphat, of St. John Chrysostom and St. Clement of Rome, and of the 27 Ukrainian martyrs, for the new bishop Teodoro Matsapula, so that his ecclesial service may bring “a new apostolic enthusiasm from the patrimony of holiness left by those exemplary disciples of Christ”17 in Ukraine.

May Most Holy Mary, Queen of the Apostles, who knew how to respond to the Angel’s announcement: be it done unto me according to your word18, always bless, protect and help Bishop Teodoro Matsapula, so that he may carry out his mission according to the style of the Incarnate Word. May she shine for him as a sign of sure hope and of consolation in the sacrificial office of pastoral government, which is, as Pope St. Gregory the Great said, “the art of arts.” For these intentions we members of the Institute pledge our prayers.

On this occasion we also congratulate the whole Religious Family in Ukraine: the priests of the IVE on the 30th anniversary of the Institute’s presence in those lands, the mothers and sisters on the 25th anniversary of their mission, and the members of the Third Order on the 15th anniversary of their foundation there. Paraphrasing Pope Leo XIII, we say to them: “When our order flourishes, the Church in Ukraine flourishes.”

Let no one fail to recognize the wonders God works for us and the enormous graces that in His mercy He grants us to see before our eyes, in the midst of so many crosses and persecutions. May the consecration of Fr. Teodoro be celebrated throughout the world!
