Dear brothers:

Today, on this day, on this Sunday, we keep the World Mission Day. The Church therefore invites us today, once again, to consider the missionary vocation of the Catholic Church. This vocation is inscribed in the very moment of her institution: when Christ, after spending the whole night in prayer, chose the first twelve, that budding apostolic college had a single purpose: to send them out to preach. In the Gospels, Christ never tires of repeating that he has been sent to preach the good news, to announce the Kingdom of God, to bear witness to the Father, and so on, and he crowns it all by saying: as the Father has sent me, so I send you. There is no doubt: the Church was born with a missionary vocation; such are the last words of our Lord in the four Gospels, words that must be branded with fire on everyone who has a Catholic heart.

Thus we read in the Gospel of Matthew, at the final farewell on the mount of the Ascension: “Go, teach all nations, … teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you … I will be with you until the end of time.” Mark is more explicit: “Go into all the world and preach the gospel to every creature, … these signs will accompany those who believe: in my name they will cast out demons, they will speak new tongues, they will take up serpents in their hands, and if they drink poison it will not harm them; they will lay their hands on the sick and these will be healed.” In St. Luke he told them that penance for the remission of sins should be preached in his name to all nations, beginning in Jerusalem. The last words that St. John gives us are also words of mission; they are Christ's desire before a heart that loves him: after the threefold confession of love, he says to Peter: “Feed my sheep.” Even the Acts of the Apostles, which narrate the beginnings of the Church, open by pointing out that Jesus ascended into heaven while charging his own: “And you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea, in Samaria and to the ends of the earth.” The Church was born for mission; mission is inscribed in the very essence of the Christian vocation: to be a Christian means to be a missionary; to be a Christian means to have a soul stirred by a longing for mission. This means having a Catholic spirit, a universal spirit: to the ends of the earth.

1. The salvation of souls

To preach Christ, to carry the gospel to the ends of the earth; to make men share in the Life that comes from Him, to make men love God, the glory of God, the salvation of souls: here is the vocation of the Catholic Church. It is for this end that the Church forms priests in her seminaries; it is for this end that the Church sends her most dedicated and virtuous priests to the most inhospitable lands and countries. I think now of the priests and missionaries who suffer hardships, who endure hunger and privation, who are locked up in prisons and often subjected to torture for confessing Christ: “Go into all the world and preach the gospel to the whole creation.” There come to mind those two missionary priests in Sudan, in Khartoum (the capital), who, according to the latest report of the Congregation for the Evangelization of Peoples (the Roman congregation in charge of the missions, to which the collections of all the churches and chapels of all Christendom are sent on this day), are still being tortured so that they will confess to a crime they never committed; the priests who in Arab countries cannot make their names known; those who were imprisoned in Vietnam last month; those who in China are condemned to capital punishment if they do not answer to the national Church; and so many others who spend and wear out their lives, as St. Paul said, so that Christ may reign, so that Christ may conquer and so that Christ may command.

It is for this end that so many men and women, leaving behind the pleasures and riches of this world, have dedicated their lives to the enclosure of monasteries to pray for the mission of the Church: “the salvation of souls” (St. Thérèse of Lisieux, Edith Stein).

How much a soul is worth! When one reads the Gospel texts that lead Christ toward Jerusalem, one can penetrate into the heart of Christ and his love for souls: “I have a baptism of fire with which to be baptized, and how distressed I am until it is accomplished!” Before the Passion, at the moment of the institution of the Eucharist, we read: “having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.” A truly great work:
Pseudo-Dionysius: “it is the most divine of all divine works.”
St. John Chrysostom: “whoever converts a single soul pleases God more than if he were to give away all his goods. There is nothing dearer to God than the salvation of souls.”
Clement of Alexandria: “it is so pleasing to God that one would say He has no greater desire than to see all men saved.”
Our Lord to Fr. Colnado: “work for the conversion of sinners, for nothing is more pleasing to me.”
The martyr St. Carpus said to his executioners: “Strike me again, for again I am ready to die for sinners.”
St. Lawrence Justinian: “if you are concerned for the honor of God, you cannot seek it in any better way than by working for the salvation of souls.”
St. Bernard: “In the eyes of God, one soul is worth more than the whole world.”

This was the obsession of the saints:

For St. Thérèse of Lisieux: “I desire only one thing: to make God loved.”
St. Augustine: “If you love God, sweep everyone along toward divine love.”
St. Alphonsus Maria: “Whoever truly loves God does all he can to move everyone to love Him.”
St. Teresa said: “Whenever we read in the lives of the saints that they converted souls, it stirs in me much more devotion, more tenderness and more envy than all the martyrdoms they suffer; for this is the inclination Our Lord has given me, since it seems to me that He prizes one soul that, through His mercy, we might win for Him by our effort and prayer more than all the services we can render Him in this life.”
Don Orione exclaimed: “Place me, Lord, at the mouth of hell, so that by your mercy I may close it.”

For the saints this was something truly great:
St. John Chrysostom: “I would wish to be regarded as an object of execration, provided that my subjects were converted.”
St. Bonaventure: “I would undergo as many deaths as there are sinners in the world, so that they might be saved.”
Of St. Cajetan it is said that “he died of grief at seeing the souls that were being lost.”

2. We and the missionary work

Dear brothers, before such greatness we certainly cannot remain indifferent. We too, to the extent of our possibilities, must undertake the work of the salvation of souls, “the most divine of divine works,” and labor in our own surroundings so that those who are in contact with us may attain what the Fathers of the Church called “the grace of graces,” that is: “the grace of saving one's soul.” This is a great sign of predestination:
St. Augustine said: “If you have saved a soul, you have predestined your own.”
And St. James, in his letter: “Know that whoever brings back a sinner from the error of his way will save his soul from death and will cover a multitude of sins.”
And may the universal mission of the Church never be indifferent or foreign to us, so that men may know Jesus Christ and the Life that comes from Him. May it not happen to us as to the one who had one talent and was rejected by our Lord because he did not make it bear fruit. According to St. Ambrose: “these are the ones who can work for the salvation of souls and do not do so.” And St. Augustine, to those who say that it is enough to save their own soul, says: “What are you saying? Do you not remember the servant who hid his talent?”
May this day, on which the Church makes us recall our missionary vocation, serve to enkindle in us a thirst for souls. May we not incur the reproach of the angels at the Ascension of the Lord: “Men of Galilee, why do you stand there looking up at heaven…?”
May the Blessed Virgin make us aware of our vocation, and grant us a missionary heart, a Catholic heart.
“Do not speak to me of finis terrae, a magic name that would close the path to every intention. A man of small ambition is he who would shut up his longings in so mean a word. As long as there remains one border of the earth that does not praise the One who came to save us, the earth has no end.”
