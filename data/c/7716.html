The magisterium of St. John Paul II is inherent to our Proper Law

Dear brothers, the celebration of the holy memorial of Pope St. John Paul II, Spiritual Father of our Religious Family, takes on a special significance in this place and at this moment through which our Institute is passing; all the more so since on this occasion, with this Holy Mass, the community of fathers who have been living for some time in this famous abbey is officially established, whose aim is to lay the foundations for the relaunching of the Center for Higher Studies.

Moreover, the fact that we find ourselves in the very place where St. Thomas brought his life, and in a certain way his work, to its consummation makes this a unique and historic celebration.

John Paul II himself called St. Thomas Aquinas the “apostle of truth”1, words that in a certain way summarize the mission that our Center for Higher Studies is to have and that it wants to instill in those who will teach and study in this house in the future. You will remember that a little more than a year ago we also gathered here to make a vow to St. Thomas, asking him to grant us the grace that today we joyfully see fulfilled (the testimony of this offering can be found in the commemorative plaque and the votive lamp located in the Chapel of the passing of the Holy Doctor to the Father’s House).

1. John Paul II, the IVE and its insertion in the Church

I would like, first of all, to recall something that is known to all, and that is important, because in a certain way it defines us and explains why we celebrate this day in such a particular way: the Magisterium of St. John Paul II is absolutely inherent to the proper law of the Institute and “animates the fundamental aspects of the charism”2 (these are the founder’s exact words).

And so it is: we have this stamp that has marked us from our origins, fidelity to the Magisterium of St. John Paul II. And we can say that if it is true for each one of us –as St. John Paul II also taught– that “to belong to this beloved Religious Family also means to make its charism and its spirituality one’s own and to accept it as the framework in which our life offered to God unfolds”3 “for the building up of the Church”4–, it is also true that each one of us, and our Institute as a whole, never considers itself an island among the other ecclesial communities, but rather “devotes itself in a particular way to the work of the Church in the world”5.

Since “Christ Himself is Incarnate in His Body, the Church”6, we are aware that “we would most gravely betray our charism if we did not work to have an authentic ecclesial spirituality, one that incorporates us fully into the Church of the Incarnate Word. That is why our proper law expressly declares that we want to know nothing outside of Her”7.

In such a way that there is fulfilled in us that saying of the one we remember today, our beloved St. John Paul II, who said: “love for the Church has been the origin of the rules and constitutions of religious families, which at times expressly declared the commitment of submission to ecclesial authority”8. Thus it is –as we can see, and as is reflected throughout the length and breadth of our whole proper law on innumerable occasions– that we always recognize in the Supreme Pontiff our first and supreme authority, to whom we profess not only obedience but also fidelity, filial submission, adherence and availability for the service of the universal Church9.

But besides the fundamental documents of the Institute’s proper law, which on each of their pages repeat like a refrain that what is ours is and always has been “to love and serve, and to make Jesus Christ loved and served in His Mystical Body, which is the Church”10, there are also the fundamental elements attached to the charism, through which our Institute shows again and again, under the explicit guidance of the Second Vatican Council and of the magisterium of St. John Paul II, that we understand that “the path followed by the Church through the centuries is the sure path and that, therefore, it cannot be abandoned”11.

As the Council itself wisely affirms: “It is for the very good of the Church that all Institutes have their own character and proper end. Therefore, the spirit and aims of the Founders, as well as sound traditions, must be known and faithfully preserved, for all of this constitutes the patrimony of each of the Institutes”12.

Therefore, “the strictest fidelity to the supreme Magisterium of the Church of all times, the proximate norm of faith”13 is an integral and constitutive part of our charism, of our mission and of our way of sanctification and apostolate; and it is also a mark of honor. Indeed, to act otherwise would not correspond to the spirit of our Institute, which always had and has as its sure criterion sentire Ecclesiam and sentire cum Ecclesia, acting in perfect communion with its pastors14; nor would it fit within our apostolic mission as religious, since this can be nothing other than the building up and extension of the kingdom within the perspective of ecclesial unity15. This is so true that each of the non-negotiable elements attached to the charism not only is set out and developed with magisterial documents (particularly those of Pope John Paul II), but is a clear and explicit response to the requests made by the Council itself, seeking in everything to reflect “the doctrine of the Church as communion, proposed so emphatically by the Second Vatican Council”16, and thereby demonstrating our commitment to apply its teachings as faithfully as possible.

2. John Paul II, the IVE and its fidelity to St. Thomas

In this context of love for Holy Church, the pillar and foundation of the truth17, it is not surprising that it is “our clear intention to follow St. Thomas Aquinas”, whom –in the words of John Paul II– the Second Vatican Council cites, making his doctrine its own18, and whose teachings “not without reason the Supreme Pontiffs and the Code of Canon Law itself19 have taken up and made their own. [Moreover], the Second Vatican Council also prescribes, as we know, the study and teaching of the perennial patrimony of philosophy, a distinguished part of which is constituted by the thought of the Angelic Doctor”20. Thus, for example, the Second Vatican Council points out that “the Church attends with solicitude to schools of higher learning, above all the Universities and Faculties, and those that depend on her she endeavors to organize in such a way that each discipline is cultivated according to its own principles, its own methods and its own freedom of scientific inquiry, so that the understanding attained of it may become deeper each day and, carefully taking into account the most recent research of contemporary progress, it may be perceived more profoundly how faith and reason tend toward the same truth, following in the footsteps of the doctors of the Church, above all of St. Thomas Aquinas”21.And elsewhere it determines that candidates for the priesthood should:Learn… to illustrate the mysteries of salvation as far as they can, and to understand them more deeply and observe their mutual relations by means of speculation, following the teachings of St. Thomas22.Whether some like it or not: to be faithful to the Council clearly means to be Thomists.

Consequently, we could not take pride in being faithful to the Magisterium of the Church and to St. John Paul II, whom we remember and celebrate today with so much affection, if at the same time we did not strive to follow an element as fundamental for the proper formation of our candidates as the perennial doctrine of the Aquinate. Because, let us remember it once more, the “Church has proclaimed that the doctrine of St. Thomas is her own doctrine”23. And as the Council itself reminds us, “the mission of priests is not to teach their own wisdom, but the Word of God, and to invite all insistently to conversion and holiness”24.

It is not possible to list all the reasons that have led the Magisterium to choose St. Thomas Aquinas as a sure guide in the theological and philosophical disciplines; but we can recall today three reasons that have been particularly highlighted in this regard by our beloved St. John Paul II:

– The first is “the sincere and total adherence that [the Aquinate] always maintained to the Magisterium of the Church, to whose judgment he submitted all his works, during his life and at the moment of death”25. For indeed, he himself preferred “to the voice of the Doctors, and to his own voice, that of the universal Church, as if anticipating what Vatican II says: ‘The whole body of the faithful who have received the anointing of the Holy Spirit cannot err in believing’26; ‘When the Roman Pontiff or the Body of bishops together with him define a point of doctrine, they always do so in accordance with Revelation itself, to which all must adhere and conform27”28.

  • Secondly, because of his fidelity to the voice of the Word of God, transmitted by the Church.

  • And, thirdly, without any doubt, and perhaps this is the preponderant reason, because he “laid down the principles of universal value that govern the relationship between reason and faith. Faith contains, says St. John Paul II, in a superior, different and eminent way, the values of human wisdom; for this reason it is impossible for reason to be at odds with faith, and if it is in disagreement, it is necessary to review and reconsider the conclusions of philosophy. In this sense faith itself becomes a precious help for philosophy”29. This is one of the reasons why the idea has arisen of naming our Center for Higher Studies, in this new stage, the Instituto Fides et Ratio, an institute that may be, in this context, a source for an authentic dialogue, from the standpoint of essential Thomism, with contemporary culture; so that, in this way, an authentic, genuine and lasting new evangelization may be carried out.

That is why the same Holy Pontiff, in continuity with his predecessors and following the directives of the Second Vatican Council, named St. Thomas Aquinas “Doctor Humanitatis” –as he himself says– “because of his passionate dedication to the truth and the value of his anthropology and of his metaphysics”, and proposed him as a model of dialogue with the culture of our time30. And he added: “In the cultural conditions of our time it seems very opportune to develop ever more this part of Thomistic doctrine that deals with humanity, since his affirmations on the dignity of the human person and on the use of his reason, in perfect harmony with faith, make St. Thomas a teacher for our time”31.

[Peroration] My dear all, St. John Paul II was a great promoter of the study and teaching of the Aquinate, as is shown by the fact that he was the first member of the International Society of Thomas Aquinas while still a Cardinal. Likewise, throughout his entire pontificate, he took care to keep alive the teaching of St. Thomas, so beautifully described and taught in his encyclical Fides et ratio, as well as in his many addresses, audiences and numerous documents. In fact, this document has always been considered the Magna Carta of our Center for Higher Studies.

St. John Paul II constantly recommended to religious men, to seminarians, to priests, to religious women, the study and deepening of the theological and philosophical work of the Angelic Doctor. And so, for example, on one occasion he spoke these words that suit us so well: “You, more than others, must cultivate familiarity with the thought and writings of this peerless master, and renew and enrich his doctrine”32

In this holy place, which Divine Providence chose as the fitting place for the passing of St. Thomas to his eternal espousal with the Truth, St. John Paul II says again to all of us: “The truth, like Jesus Christ, can be denied, persecuted, fought, wounded, martyred, crucified; but it always comes back to life and rises again, and it can never be torn from the human heart. St. Thomas placed all the strength of his genius at the exclusive service of the truth, behind which he seems to want to disappear, as if for fear of obstructing its brilliance, so that it, and not he, might shine in all its brightness”33.

“I encourage [you] today… to become true disciples of St. Thomas, capable of facing the quaestiones disputatae, and of dialoguing with all who are far from the faith and from the Church…”34.

I end with this sentence of St. John Paul the Great, which I would like to remain impressed on your minds and in your hearts. It says: “The founders knew how to incarnate the Gospel message in their time with courage and holiness. It is necessary that, faithful to the breath of the Spirit, their spiritual children carry on through time that witness, imitating their creativity with a mature fidelity to the charism of the origins, always attentive to the demands of the present moment”35.

May these two outstanding non-negotiable elements of our charism, namely, fidelity to the Magisterium and the following of St. Thomas in his doctrine, shine with particular splendor in this Center for Higher Studies that we are relaunching.

We ask this grace today of the Incarnate Word and of His Most Holy Mother through the intercession of St. John Paul II.
