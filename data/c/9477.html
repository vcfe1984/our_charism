The humanity of Christ – The living monument of God’s love

Recalling the thought of our Founder
During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the seventh day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in the motive of the Incarnation , we call to mind Fr. Buela’s thought on the subject, taken from his books “El Arte del Padre” and “Las Servidoras V.”

The humanity of Christ

The end of the Incarnation was the redemption from sins: «The principal cause of the divine Incarnation is the expiation of sins»; it is «to save sinners» (1 Tim 1:15), it is the «restoration of human nature»; in other words, «He came into the world so that men might exchange the love of earthly things for that of spiritual things». In Lumen Gentium 55 we are taught for what purpose the Son of God took human nature from the Virgin: «in order to free man from sin through the mysteries of His humanity».

[In accordance with which, Fr. Buela declares in Las Servidoras V:]

The living monument of God’s love

Out of love God sent His Son into the world so that He might give His life for us on the Cross: God so loved the world that He gave it His only-begotten Son… (Jn 3:16), in such a way that: The love of God toward us was made manifest in this, that God sent His only-begotten Son into the world… In this is love, not that we have loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son as propitiation for our sins (1 Jn 4:9-10). Love, then, which is manifested in the Incarnation of the Word, and in the Redemption, by dying as propitiation for the sins of all.

A love that goes first, because God takes the lead. He takes the initiative. He has the primacy in love: He loved us first (1 Jn 4:19).

A love that has its origin in Him: … charity comes from God (1 Jn 4:7); He is the inexhaustible source of all true love, and every little spark of love springs from that burning bonfire of charity which is the love of God.

It is a greater love: No one has greater love than this, to give one’s life for one’s friends (Jn 15:13).

It is a love of election: You did not choose me, but I chose you… (Jn 15:16).

It is a fruitful, full, abiding love: …and I have appointed you that you should go and bear fruit, and that your fruit should remain… (ibidem).

Well then, this love of God is not only manifested by the fact that the Word became flesh (Jn 1:14), it is not only manifested by His Passion and Death on the Cross: Father, forgive them… (Lk 23:34), but, in addition, He has left a living, perpetual, efficacious, supreme monument of His love: the Eucharist! Having loved His own who were in the world, He loved them to the end (Jn 13:1); He loved them to the limit of His strength, He loved them to the extreme, He loved them to the point of remaining under the bread and under the wine. He loved us even to the Eucharist!

The great school of Christian love is the Mass. It opens its doors every day, and will open them until the end of the world, until He comes (1 Cor 11:26). For everyone who wants to learn to love as Christ loves, it is a solicitous teacher, who teaches not only with words but, what is much more, with the deed itself.

In the Mass, by learning to love, we show ourselves to be children of God: everyone who loves is born of God (1 Jn 4:7); we come to know Him more: everyone who loves knows God. Whoever does not love does not know God, for God is love (1 Jn 4:7-8); we come to have life through Him: … so that we might live through Him (1 Jn 4:9).

In the Mass, with the Eucharistic bread, God teaches us, in the mill of His heart, to let ourselves be ground like the grain of wheat. Truly, truly I say to you, if the grain of wheat does not fall into the earth and die, it remains alone; but if it dies, it will bear much fruit (Jn 12:24), until He teaches us to love with His own love.

By loving us He teaches us to love, since love is repaid with love.

He teaches us to love God: God is love, and whoever lives in love abides in God, and God in him (1 Jn 4:16), this is the love of God: that we keep His commandments (1 Jn 5:3); and He teaches us to love our neighbor: … let us love one another; if God loved us in this way, we too must love one another… if we love one another, God abides in us and His love is perfected in us… whoever loves God must also love his brother (1 Jn 4:7.11-12.21).

[…] In the Mass, Christ Himself forms us in the school of His love. On the table of the altar He kneads our heart together with His own, made into white wheat flour, and He teaches us, with the delicacy of a Teacher, with the affection of a Father, with the nobility of a King, with the strength of a Lion, with the meekness of a Lamb, with the sureness of the Way, with the lavishness of a Savior, with the sharing of a Companion, with the closeness of a Brother, with the majesty of a Lord, with the confidence of a Friend, that if I do not have love, I am nothing… not having love, nothing profits me… Love is patient and ready to serve. Love does not envy; it does not brag; it is not puffed up; it is not discourteous; it does not seek its own advantage; it does not get irritated; it keeps no account of evil; it does not take delight in injustice. Love delights in the truth. Love excuses everything. Love believes everything. Love hopes everything. Love bears everything. Love will never die (cf. 1 Cor 13:2-8). Having loved His own He loved them to the end, to the point of keeping no secret in His heart, to the point of teaching us to love with the love of His own heart, to the point of making us «living victims for the praise of His glory». […]

The crisis in participation in Sunday Mass, which in some places is growing worse, is due to the general crisis of faith, but above all its cause is the crisis of love in which the contemporary world is struggling, which makes us recall that saying of Jesus: …the charity of many will grow cold (Mt 24:12).

The soul that loves God cannot abandon Holy Mass.

The fact that the Mass is a work of love and that it requires love as a response makes it difficult to teach participation in it by means of rules, as St. Basil the Great says: «The love of God is not something that can be learned by rules and precepts. Just as no one has taught us to enjoy the light, to love life, to love our parents and teachers, so too, and with greater reason, the love of God is not something that can be taught; rather, from the moment this living being we call man begins to exist, a spiritual force is placed in him, in the manner of a seed, which contains within itself the capacity and the tendency to love. This seminal force is diligently cultivated and wisely nourished in the school of the divine precepts, and thus, with God’s help, it reaches its perfection».

[We ask the Blessed Virgin, Mother of the Redeemer and our Mother, for the grace that the virtue of charity may increase in us, by which we immolate ourselves spiritually in each Holy Mass by uniting ourselves to the one sacrifice of the Incarnate Word.]
