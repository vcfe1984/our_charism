Conference at Saint Laurent Sur Sevre – Day of Our Lady of the Most Holy Rosary

As I said in the homily at Holy Mass, today we have the grace of being gathered as a Religious Family only a few steps from the place where the remains of the great St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort rest.

Today is a significant date for us, among other things because today is also the 51st anniversary of the priestly ordination of our Founder, who from the beginning wanted our Institute to be missionary and Marian. And, as Fr. Buela himself once said, it has been “by the inspiration of Our Lady [that] we consecrate ourselves to her with a fourth vow according to the letter and the spirit of St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort”1.

And so, in our Constitutions we read that our calling is to work “in utmost docility to the Holy Spirit and within the imprint of Mary”2—that is a constitutive element of our charism—“in order to prolong the Incarnation in all things, making a fourth vow of Marian slavery according to St. Louis Marie de Montfort”3. That is why Marian devotion is one of the non-negotiable elements attached to the charism, which “cannot be lost without grave harm to our charism”4, that is, without disfiguring our identity as the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word.

On this beautiful day, moreover, to crown our blessings, we have had the joy and the privilege of renewing our consecration to Our Lady. That is why it seemed to me that it might do us all good, before each one goes his way and we return to our houses, to reflect on what it means to live our consecration according to the spirit of St. Louis Marie. And we will explain it by pointing out some specific aspects or points of our Constitutions which—in my view—are key in acquiring that spirit of the saint of Montfort of which Fr. Buela speaks to us.

As you well know, our spirituality, “which wants to be that of the Incarnate Word”5, is “marked, with special prominence”6, by consecration to Mary “‘in maternal slavery of love’ in the manner admirably set forth by St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort”7. We are not going to go deeply here into everything you already know about the fourth vow. Rather, I wanted to focus a little on the life of St. Louis Marie in order to grasp the spirit with which he lived, since our Proper Law implicitly presents him to us as a model of the love of Christ in Mary, pausing on some passages of our Proper Law that seem to me to show clearly the influence of Montfortian spirituality on everything that is ours.

There are four points in this regard that, it seems to me, deserve all our consideration and that are like links in a chain leading us to enter into that spirit.

1. “Specialists in the wisdom of the cross” (Const. 42)

Let us begin by saying “that our spirituality derives from the Person of the Word and of His Mother”8. That is a literal quotation from the Constitutions, which in the following five paragraphs set about unfolding the basic principles that spring from this truth and on which the spiritual life of our Institute is founded9.

The primacy of Jesus Christ, the preexistence of the Person of the Incarnate Word, the mystery of the Incarnate Word as the way to go to the Father, the mystery of His earthly life in which, from His Mother’s womb, He teaches us to depend totally on her10, etc. You can read all this yourselves, with great profit, in the Constitutions, numbers 37 to 41.

But we come to number 42, where, speaking of the Incarnate Word’s departure from this world, and therefore of the “Paschal Mystery of Our Lord [which] is an inexhaustible source of spirituality”11, it tells us that “we must be specialists in the wisdom of the cross, in the love of the cross and in the joy of the cross”12. And here we have one of the many points of contact, or rather influences, of the spirit of St. Louis Marie on our spirituality.

The Royal Spanish Academy defines the word ‘specialist’ as an adjective describing a person who cultivates, practices or masters a particular discipline, subject or activity13. This means that we have to cultivate the wisdom of the cross and put into practice the love of the cross, until we come to master this ‘science,’ which will be when we show joy in the cross.

Many commentators on St. Louis Marie argue that “the ‘Montfortian corpus’ as a whole is a going to and fro toward the cross”14, and thus all his works underline the centrality of the cross in the life of following Christ. And it is very important to grasp this well. Because, as our Proper Law rightly says: “If in the present economy of salvation the Passion of Christ was necessary, our suffering will also be necessary. If there were another way to go to heaven, Jesus Christ would have followed it and, what is more, would have taught it. But it is not so; Christ went by the royal road of the holy Cross and taught us to go by it”15. Therefore, the first reality that must be accepted is that the cross is part of our program, with such an acceptance that it may be an image—we could say—of the acceptance Montfort had. That is, not just any acceptance, but a Montfortian acceptance.

We already said something in this morning’s homily about the sufferings the holy missionary had to endure: from his childhood he suffered the violent temperament of his father, yet he prayed for his conversion. When he was a little older he shows himself to be a person who has compassion on the cross of others, and he is seen giving his clothes to the poor, distributing alms. Then, in the seminary, he lives the sad reality of the not few battles his fellow seminarians waged against him with their mockery and humiliations.

“In the seminary of Paris,” one of his biographers recounts, “Montfort knew many persecutions. His enemies made his life impossible. Even his professors mistreated and slandered him”16. He suffered the abandonment of his friends, as he himself recounts in a letter to Marie-Louise Trichet: “I have no friends but God alone. All those I once had in Paris have abandoned me”17; among them was also his spiritual director, Monsieur Leschasier18. In 1703 he finds himself living alone on the rue Pot-de-Fer in Paris. You can imagine that by this point Monfort was already an impoverished and crucified man. For men, willingly or not, tore his reputation to shreds. “He is a man suspected by everyone”19, says one of the commentators. He was only 30 years old at the time.

In his priestly ministry he will know the same situation. Because of the slanders and injustices poured out on him, he was thrown out of seven dioceses, on the grounds of an untimely zeal in his missions, in spite of his obedience to his bishops. Everywhere they were bothered by the primacy he gave to the spiritual life, because it contrasted sharply with those who were content with a mediocre spirituality. Let the case of the Calvary of Pontchâteau suffice as an example of this situation of humiliation.

And how did St. Louis Marie live all these painful situations? …here is what is important:

With an authentically magnanimous spirit, orienting the soul “to accomplish great works, extraordinary undertakings”20.

His spirit was the spirit of a prince21 of which we read in our Proper Law: he concerned himself with great things… (think only of the Calvary of Pontchâteau: between 400 and 500 people worked on its construction—though some say there were thousands—it was an immense work for the time, and yet he did it); but he also accomplished great works in every virtue. Because he was of a noble spirit, far was he from being a tributary! St. Louis Marie did not subordinate himself unduly to temporal powers, to cultural fashions, to the spirit of the world, as if they were the ultimate end instead of God22. That is why he never wanted to detach the Gospel he preached from the cross, as so many do today in wanting to win followers. Or as so many others do who seek to reduce Christ’s message to a political and social revolution. Montfort favored the spiritual ‘revolution’ that passes through the cross and also leads to the cross, so as to be conformed to eternal Wisdom. That is, what he proposes falls within the strict logic of the imitation of Jesus Christ.

That is why he was capable of chastising himself (of mortifying himself) and of chastising. That is why he felt honor as he felt life, and knew at every moment the things for which one must die. That is why he knew how to give even when not obliged to, and also knew how to abstain from things no one forbade23.

But St. Louis Marie, in a particular way, showed his greatness of soul in tribulations and difficulties, which were many and great, as we illustrated a moment ago. And one sees that he was magnanimous because he did not play the victim. And in a magnificent letter he writes: “Men and devils, in this great city of Paris, are waging a very lovable and sweet war on me. Let them slander me, let them ridicule me, let them tear my reputation to shreds, let them shut me up in prison! What precious gifts! What exquisite delicacies! What alluring grandeurs! [and pay attention to what he says:] They are the baggage and retinue of divine Wisdom, which she brings in with her into the house of those with whom she wishes to dwell”24. Do you see? With that spirit he faced crosses. What is more, he asked others to pray that God would give him crosses, and when God sent him one, he asked them to join in his thanksgiving.

The small-souled man always reckons that he is suffering a great deal and shows himself so before others. That is why our Constitutions, quoting the holy missionary, warn us against this subtle and ingenious but venom-filled deception, which is believing that our crosses are great and that they come to us as proof of our fidelity25. “Be very careful!—he goes on to say—with that delighted turning over and over of one’s own ills, that Luciferian belief that you are of great worth…”26. St. Louis was not one of these. He did not think much of himself. He did not play the victim because he was authentically magnanimous. And because he was authentically magnanimous, he was profoundly humble. In the Letter to the Friends of the Cross he writes: “we are nothing but wet hens and dead dogs”27. Claiming to be great without being humble is totally and essentially different from being great and humble. The difference may perhaps seem minimal to someone, but one’s life depends on this difference.

Montfort lives all the injustices he encounters, all his pastoral failures, with tranquility and in peace. He does not lose heart in the face of difficulties; on the contrary, he lives in the peace of a person conscious of doing God’s will. And this is very important for us to learn and to exercise ourselves in and to try to be specialists in, because—listen to me carefully—our calling is to work, to live, to go on mission, “in the most difficult situations and in the most adverse conditions”28. That is our ‘natural environment.’ That is our program! And that is vital in the spirit of Montfort.

That is why St. Louis Marie says to the friends of the cross, and I repeat it to you: “do not fool yourselves!”29. Necessarily, if we do things as God commands, the cross will be present in our lives: in study, in the mission, in the community, in the life of prayer, in what concerns the situation of the Institute, in our own family, in what concerns our health or physical strength, etc. “If you—says the holy missionary—pride yourselves on being guided by the spirit of Jesus Christ and on living the life of that Head torn by thorns, expect nothing but thistles, scourges, nails, etc., in a word, the Cross”30. There is no philosophy more realistic than that.

So what do we do? Because this sounds like madness, someone might say. And I answer him with what Montfort said to a religious woman who was suffering great crosses: “Have confidence. It is a sure sign that [God] loves you”31. And he adds: “I say sure because the best sign that God loves us is to see ourselves hated by the world and assailed by crosses, such as the deprivation of the most legitimate things, opposition to our holiest initiatives, the most atrocious and stinging insults, persecutions and misinterpretations on the part of the best-intentioned persons and of our best friends, unpleasant illnesses, etc.”32.

Which is exactly what our Proper Law teaches us when it says: “When the world says to us: Look at the madmen! Stones are thrown at them and they kiss the hand that throws them; people laugh at them and mock them and they laugh too, like children who do not understand; they are beaten, persecuted and martyred, but they give thanks to God who found them worthy. When the world says that, it is a sign that we are on the right track. Madness of love! But the madness of the Cross makes one wiser than the wisdom of all men33”34.

How many religious are frightened by the things said about the Institute! Do they perhaps forget that “the greatest grace God can grant our tiny Religious Family is persecution”35?

“Train yourselves, then, in this supereminent science, under the guidance of the Incarnate Word” 36, says St. Louis Marie. “Whoever among you knows best how to carry his cross, even if he is illiterate, is the wisest of all. […] Rejoice, then, you poor ignorant man, and you, humble woman without talent or learning…! If you know how to suffer with joy, you know more than any doctor who does not know how to suffer as well as you do!”37 . Do you see? In knowing how to suffer with patience lies the way to be united to Christ and to attain Wisdom. “Everything lies in knowing how to die! That is the great science!”38, says our Proper Law, and it is the first link in this chain for attaining the wisdom of the cross.

Therefore, far be it from us to behave as enemies of the cross39. And when do we behave as enemies of the cross? The Directory of Spirituality tells us in number 138: when we reject it, when we trim it, when we lower it, when we avoid being persecuted because of the cross of Christ, when we do not preach the whole message of the Gospel. But also when we sacrifice “the truth and one’s own conscience, claiming to maintain a false peace, not to cross a friend, to avoid some problem or, at times, to gain an advantage by silence or by applause”40. That is, when we are worldly.

And how can we identify worldly religious? I answer by paraphrasing some lines of the Letter to the Friends of the Cross, which go like this: “Those religious whom you find everywhere, dressed in the latest fashion, extremely delicate, haughty and conceited to the utmost, are not the true disciples of Jesus crucified. […] God help me! How many caricatures of religious there are out there, who claim to be members of Jesus Christ, when in reality they are His most treacherous persecutors, because while with one hand they make the sign of the cross, in their heart they are its declared enemies!”41. And elsewhere he adds: “They love pleasures too much, they are excessively concerned with their comforts, they value the goods of this world too much, they fear contempt and humiliations too much! Yes, they esteem and praise the cross, but in general, not concretely their own, from which they flee as much as they can, or which they drag along reluctantly, amid grumbling, impatience and complaints”42.

If we, as members of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, want to live according to the spirit of this consecration, then we have to live in the more and in the beyond… where one no longer counts, nor calculates, nor weighs, nor measures43. Not in a utopian way, not when I make perpetual vows or when I complete 50 years of priesthood, but in daily life, now and when we return to our houses and have to deal with the little crosses of every day. “Take advantage of everything. Do not waste the smallest particle of the true cross”44.

If there is anything St. Louis Marie wants to teach us, it is that the cross is the sine qua non way to be united to the Person of the Incarnate Word, to be steeped in His sentiments, to come to be truly like another incarnation of the Word45. We have to realize that the cross has an intrinsic value in itself. For if God sends us the cross (as we usually say), He also sends us Jesus Christ, and therefore: Never the Cross without Jesus and never Jesus without the Cross46.

When St. Louis Marie was persecuted throughout France, expelled from dioceses, thrown out of several hospitals, when they ordered the demolition of the Calvary that had cost him so much to raise, when they had him sleeping under a staircase, when his fellow seminarians mocked him, he did not go to Twitter to tell the world “they are being unjust to me,” nor to tell the world that the Church has to change her doctrine, nor did he start his own Church (although he had a great many people following him); nor did he sit down angrily to write a blog airing his barely mortified sensitivity and saying ‘how badly they treated him.’ He obeyed and became little by little more like Christ. Because the cross, according to the spirit of the saint, is the vehicle by which the divine penetrates the human, and, without confusion, without mixture, without absorption and without destructive opposition, the human or the natural is gradually raised to the supernatural and divine plane.

I believe that if St. Louis Marie were alive he would join the Incarnate Word. If we want to enter into the spirit of the consecration of which Fr. Buela speaks to us, in order to achieve it, it is necessary, then, to look at how St. Louis Marie became a specialist in the Cross.

2. “To suffer with her” (Const. 89)

Some of you have visited or are going to visit the first house and chapel of the Daughters of Wisdom, where the Cross of Poitiers («Croix de Poitiers») is found. This cross “speaks of everything that must be done and lived in order to attain holiness, or to be specialists in the science of the cross.

Nevertheless, in order to be able to put into practice all the teachings found on that cross and all the means Louis Marie preaches for holiness, grace is needed”47. Because, of course, one reads what is written on that cross and says: “this is masochism… it is impossible.” Nevertheless, Montfort is aware that our nature rejects the cross, fears it, avoids it, laments that it is so heavy, and he knows from his own experience that only by overcoming oneself can one accept it. But for that, grace is necessary. Do you follow?

He himself acknowledges it when he says: “To put into practice all these means of salvation and sanctification, you absolutely need grace and divine help. These are undoubtedly granted to all, though in different measure. I say this because, although God is infinitely good, He does not give His grace to all with the same intensity48. But He gives each one sufficient grace. With fidelity to a greater grace, you will accomplish great deeds; to a lesser grace, you will accomplish limited ones. The value and excellence of the grace given by God and welcomed by man determine the value and excellence of our actions. These are incontestable principles”49. And here comes the second link of this chain.

Because to find grace, one must find Mary. That is why, if you notice, at the point where the vertical and horizontal parts of the Cross of Poitiers meet, there is the monogram of Mary: MA. Why? Because Mary is the treasurer50 of the grace that enables man to carry his cross.

And this is the point I want to highlight here and put before all of you for your consideration. If we want to be specialists in the wisdom of the cross, in the love of the cross and in the joy of the cross according to the spirit of this consecration, we need to focus on the fact that we will only be able to achieve it by living Marian slavery in its most relevant aspects. With Mary, in Mary, through Mary and for Mary51, with all the richness those words have. Being specialists in the wisdom of the cross is “truly achieved” according to the spirit of this consecration only with the closeness, company and help of the Mother of God. Much can be said about the fourth vow we make and about all the elements it entails, but this aspect, because of all the meaning it holds, has in my view a particular preponderance, because of all that it contains in reference to the other links of the chain we have been speaking of.

Hence our calling—as the spirit of St. Louis Marie indicates—is not only to carry the cross in following Christ, but to do so under the guidance of the Most Blessed Virgin.

Many times we think that our devotion to Our Lady comes down to wearing Our Lady’s scapular, praying the three Hail Marys, or singing to Our Lady after an evening of eutrapelia. But we seldom remember the preponderant role our Mother in Heaven has when God blesses us with some cross.

Our Lady is not distant but a solicitous Mother. And just as she stood at the foot of the cross when Christ was dying, so she stands at the foot of my cross when I am unjustly accused, when I suffer some illness, when I fail, when I get an insufferable superior, when I get a super difficult mission…

Therefore, we must have recourse to the Most Blessed Virgin “with great simplicity, trust and tenderness”52 at all times, places and circumstances, but especially in the hour of the cross.

That is why our Constitutions command us, in an expression full of content, “to suffer with her”53, and I would say, clinging to her. It is something to keep always in mind… otherwise it does not work… one does not learn, nor live, nor can one be a specialist in the madness, science, wisdom and joy of the Cross.

Because the way of the cross is steep, but with this Kindly Mother one advances more gently and calmly54. So that, in doubts, she may enlighten us; in going astray, she may turn us back to the right path; in temptations, that she may sustain us; in weaknesses, that she may strengthen us; in falls, that she may raise us up; in discouragements, that she may revive us; in scruples, that she may free us from them; in the crosses, toils and setbacks of life, that she may console us55.

For us, the members of the Institute, the Mother of God is our ordinary recourse in all material and spiritual difficulties and in the most difficult and adverse circumstances.

Now I would like to give you a warning here: do not go thinking that because we have just renewed our consecration to Our Lady we are now free of every cross and are going to live a suffering free life. Quite the contrary. Very probably we will have to suffer more than others56. Because those who most want to devote themselves to the service of the Mother of God receive greater favors from heaven, which are precisely crosses.

But beside us is she who, as St. Louis Marie says, “sweetens all crosses”57. And in allotting us more crosses she also gives us grace to carry them with joy and patience. “And if for some time these friends of God must necessarily drink the chalice of bitterness, says the Saint, the consolation and joy they receive from their kindly Mother—after the sadness—encourage them immensely to carry even heavier and more bitter crosses”58.

Therefore, even if circumstances become even more difficult, one must remain at peace and teach oneself to trust in the consolation of our Mother, because, as the holy missionary says: “the protection of the Most Blessed Virgin is too great”59. One must not shrink before crosses; one must “face up to them,” as St. Ignatius says. Let them say what they want! As St. Louis Marie said in the letter we quoted earlier. That is the magnanimous and humble spirit the Saint transmits to us. That is entering into the spirit of this consecration.

Notice that our Constitutions say that the spirit of our Religious Family wants to be none other than the Holy Spirit60, who is precisely the Spirit of Christ61, and immediately afterwards they say that we need the Most Blessed Virgin to be the model, the guide, the form of all our acts62. Why? Simply because the “spirit of Mary is the spirit of God, for she never conducted herself by her own spirit”63. So if we want to reach God, to be united to Him, we have to place ourselves in an attitude of total and absolute availability with respect to Mary and, through her, to Jesus Christ64.

It is “easy” to consecrate oneself to Our Lady and to renew the consecration once a year; it is “easy” to pray the rosary; “what is really difficult is to enter into the spirit of this consecration”65. For the spirit of the consecration is the spirit of Mary, which is the spirit of Christ and which, as St. Louis Marie says, is the spirit of the Cross.

That is why, “how happy—says St. Louis Marie—is the one who finds himself wholly possessed and led by the spirit of Mary! A spirit that is gentle and strong, zealous and prudent, humble and intrepid, pure and fruitful!”66.

Since the spirit of Christ is the Holy Spirit, and that is the spirit of our Institute, it has nothing to do with the “‘spirit of opposition,’ which forms groups or factions in opposition to whatever the Superior orders”67; ours is not a “spirit of reserve, of non-participation, of non-consent with others”68; it is not the “spirit of the bureaucratic civil servant, with multiplied demands on the people, whom he sees as clients, who thinks himself ‘obedient’ because he complies with the formalities but neither burns nor is burned with the fire of the Holy Spirit, and does not know what it is to venture out for Christ, nor is he creative, nor does he carry out unprecedented apostolates”69; it is not the “ghetto spirit, which claims that the Church is exhausted in its parish, city, province or country”70; it is not the “tributary spirit”71.

Nor is it a spirit of slow calculations72. It is a spirit that moves us to act, because “acting is proper to love”73, the Directory of Spirituality affirms. That is why one must follow the clear-sighted prompting of the holy missionary, who says—pay attention—: “being totally consecrated to her service, it is fitting, then, that you not remain idle, but that you act as the good servant and slave. That is, that, relying on the protection of the Virgin Mary, you undertake and accomplish great enterprises for this august Sovereign”74. The Mother of God was not a passive instrument, but a cooperator in human salvation; therefore, “neither must we be passive instruments”75 (this is word for word from the directory). Do you see where this is going?

A great many times our Proper Law says explicitly what this spirit of the Institute consists of for us. I name a few examples here by way of illustration:

  • “It is the spirit of faith and of love”76.
  • It is the “spirit of a prince”77.
  • It is a “spirit of generosity and purity of intention”78.
  • It is a “spirit of service”79.
  • It is a “spirit of observance”80.
  • It is the “Spirit of Truth”81.
  • It is a “spirit of sacrifice”82.
  • A “spirit of communion”83.
  • It is a “spirit of faith, of wisdom and of fortitude”84.
  • It is the “spirit of the beatitudes”85.

Therefore, the “fourth vow according to the letter and the spirit of St. Louis Marie” must be lived by the members of the Institute with these notes or characteristics we have just mentioned. To achieve this, we have perhaps the best example in Father de Montfort.

3. “… to strip oneself for God of all that is not God” (Const. 68)

This is another central point, of capital importance, to keep always present. There is no other way to union than detachment, than the emptying or stripping of all that is not God. An eminent and outstanding doctrine in St. John of the Cross, a doctrine perfectly embodied in the teaching and particularly in the life of Montfort. We are here before a sine qua non condition, before an unavoidable element, which requires conviction and energy to undertake. [We do not have much time, so I will treat it very briefly]

To possess this spirit one must first “strip oneself for God of all that is not God”86, as our Proper Law indicates to us. And in this too we agree entirely with St. Louis Marie, who commands us “to live in authentic interior and exterior poverty, effectively renouncing the goods of the world”87. Similarly our Proper Law tells us: “The evangelical counsel of poverty, in imitation of Christ, implies a life poor in fact and in spirit, strenuously sober and detached from earthly riches”88.

And so in Montfortian teaching, one of the most beautiful crosses is voluntary poverty. Montfort teaches us that one must “follow naked the naked Christ,” using the words of St. Jerome which we also quote in number 61 of the Constitutions.

This is so because “the poverty assumed by Christ increases the freedom of spirit and the spirit of a prince that we must have by our consecration as religious”89. And elsewhere our Proper Law adds: “The religious must be poor in order to attain perfection”90. For it is by being poor in spirit that God comes to satisfy our soul with the riches of His Wisdom. And that is why poverty presents itself as the third link of this chain.

During his life St. Louis Marie gave us a very great example of poverty: since he did not have many things, he enjoyed that freedom of spirit we spoke of, and therefore he was always ready to go off to any place to serve in the building up of the Kingdom with Divine Providence as his only companion. It was inconceivable to him that a true follower of Jesus should not walk in the footsteps of His poverty.

4. “Tireless preachers…” (Const. 231)

There is a fourth element which, in my view, it is essential to make explicit when speaking of entering into the spirit of this consecration; an element lived in an outstanding way by Father de Montfort. St. Louis Marie was not only a mystic (and one of great stature), but, precisely because he was one, he was a man of action, and this was seen in his life, practiced with a passion and energy that is not easy to find—I dare say—even in other saints.

What he lived in his soul led him to action; contemplata aliis tradere is read in the first line of his autobiography; and this he achieved in an admirable and praiseworthy way; and in this too he is our model.

So love of the cross, practiced hand in hand with Our Lady, living without attachments (poor), necessarily leads us to zeal and love for souls. Because the good is diffusive of itself. And therefore this too is an unavoidable element of the spirit of this consecration. Indeed, our Constitutions speak to us of “being tireless preachers […] with a tongue, lips and wisdom that the enemies of the truth cannot resist”91. Therefore, if we are to be specialists in the wisdom of the cross, that must be embodied in love for souls and in apostolic and missionary zeal. “Essentially missionary,” we say, and it is a strong phrase

Accordingly, we see that St. Louis Marie, trusting entirely in Providence, preached 115 missions in France92. That was what filled him with enthusiasm: missions to the poor in the countryside. He worked without rest in several parishes and said that it was in catechism for the poor of the city and the countryside that he felt most at home93.

As you know, St. Louis Marie is one of the models from whom we draw inspiration for preaching popular missions94. And since everyone here is or is preparing to be a missionary, we will briefly relate what the Directory of Popular Missions says about him, and thus you will appreciate how much influence the spirit of the holy missionary has on our way of doing apostolate.

“St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort founded in 1705 the ‘Missionary Fathers of the Company of Mary,’ characterized by their Marian devotion and their apostolic zeal. St. Louis Marie always carried a cross and a small statue of Our Lady, which he set up at Masses, in the confessional, in his room, etc. He made special use of processions and hymns.

The themes of his mission sermons were: the life of grace, sin, death, judgment, hell and heaven. In addition, he spoke of married life, of the Most Blessed Sacrament of the Altar, of the goodness of God; every Saturday of the mission he gave a sermon on Our Lady. Moreover, over the course of six weeks he ordinarily spoke of penance, and each day explained one of the mysteries of the Rosary. To make the people penetrate the truths of the Christian faith, he composed mission hymns. The ceremonies he held during the missions were the commemoration of the dead, adoration of the Most Blessed Sacrament, consecration to the Most Blessed Virgin, the renewal of baptismal promises and the erection of a crucifix.

He also returned after a time to secure the fruits; his “return mission” lasted a week, in which he concentrated everything on preparation for death”95.

Likewise, as you know, preaching is of particular importance for our apostolate96. That is why our Proper Law paternally exhorts us to esteem the teaching of the great masters of sacred preaching97, and in this case, once again, our Founder has chosen St. Louis Marie.

Numbers 105 to 110 of the Directory of Preaching of the Word are devoted to illustrating the style or spirit with which the holy missionary preached. He exhorts us principally not to be worldly, but to preach like the apostles. “Nothing is easier than to preach according to fashion… what a difficult and sublime thing it is to preach like the apostles!”98.

“The preacher must denounce the spirit of the world, and not live according to it: ‘Since the fashionable preacher does not attack the heart, which is the citadel where the tyrant has entrenched himself, the latter is not much disturbed by the commotion outside’”99. “‘But let a preacher filled with the word and spirit of God barely open his mouth, and all hell will sound the alarm and move heaven and earth to defend itself… A preacher of this divine temper will stir up, with the mere words of truth, a whole city and a whole province, through the war that is raised in it”100.

The qualities that a preacher must have according to St. Louis Marie are also listed, and they let shine through that love of the cross we spoke of earlier. That is why he says:

“The apostolic missionary—that is, we—preaches, then, with simplicity, without artifice; with truth, without fables, or lies, or disguises; with intrepidity and authority, without fear or human respect; with charity, without wounding anyone; and with holiness, looking only to God, with no other interest than that of the divine glory, and first practicing himself what he teaches others”101.

And he also warns us of the things preachers must avoid when they preach:

– “taking complacency in what they say and in the fruit they obtain; – begging for applause directly or indirectly after preaching; – envying others on seeing that they are more followed, more moving, etc.; – listening to or promoting gossip against other preachers; – becoming angry, something very easy and natural when the listeners give occasion for it while the preacher is speaking; – apostrophizing a listener directly or indirectly, naming him in a veiled way, pointing him out with a glance or with the hand, or saying things that can only apply to him; – continually, affectedly or exaggeratedly condemning the rich and great of the world, magistrates and officers of justice or officers of justice; – censuring, criticizing or detailing the sins of priests”102.

And finally, he reminds us that Jesus Christ “sends you just as He sent the apostles, like lambs in the midst of wolves103. [And therefore] you must imitate the gentleness, humility, patience and charity of the lamb, so as to transform, by this most divine means, the wolves themselves into lambs”104

*****

In short, it seems to me that in these four points, like the links of a chain, the great influence of Montfortian doctrine on our spirituality and on our way of doing apostolate is condensed. So that we may realize that consecration to Our Lady according to the spirit of St. Louis Marie is not lived only when we consecrate ourselves, or when we pray the rosary, but must exert its influence on our whole life in a profound way. Only thus shall we come “to be other Christs”105, which is our goal, and which will be when we have attained the Wisdom of the Cross.

For entering into the spirit of this consecration means adhering to the spirit possessed by the true disciples of Jesus Christ. And walking in the footsteps of His poverty, humility, contempt for worldly things and evangelical charity, teaching the narrow path of God in pure truth, in accordance with the holy Gospel and not with worldly codes, without being disturbed by anything or showing partiality; without sparing, listening to, or fearing any mortal, however powerful he may be106.

The spirit of consecration to Our Lady makes us specialists in the science of the cross, free men, detached from everything107, men always available108, always ready to run and to suffer everything for the Incarnate Word and for His cause, knowing that combats and persecutions will only serve to make the power of Christ’s grace, the energy of His virtue and the authority of Mary Most Holy shine more brightly109.

This must be understood not only at the individual level, but at the level of the Institute and the Religious Family. SLM was the founder of two religious congregations: the Company of Mary (priests) and the Daughters of Wisdom (women religious), and for both of them he had to suffer great tribulations.

That is why, with him too, let us pray for our Congregation, so that all the crosses we may have to suffer as an Institute, and which may put our fidelity and trust to the test, may serve only to give solidity to the Religious Family, to found it more firmly on the very Wisdom of the cross of Calvary.

That is why, today and always, with St. Louis Marie let us say: Lord Jesus, remember your Congregation.
