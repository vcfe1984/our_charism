[Exordium] “In a special way, we will dedicate ourselves to the preaching of the Word of God in all its forms,” one of them being “the carrying out of popular missions,” says number 16 of our Constitutions. Ours is a vocation particularly destined for the missions.

“God willed that the world should be saved by means of missionaries,” says our Directory of Popular Missions1. And thus, throughout the whole history of the Church, God in His infinite mercy has associated countless men and women with Himself to cultivate His vineyard, “since ‘it is only through the missions that souls attain their eternal salvation’”2. So great is the importance of preaching and of the missions that the Church points out that among the principal duties of the priest is obviously the proclamation of the Gospel, but also, if they are pastors, the organizing of popular missions at certain times3.

The end of the popular mission follows from the missionary mandate4 and is, as you know, the “renewal of Christianity in believers”5 and “the conversion of sinners”6. Now, the paradigm of every mission lies in the first mission of the disciples, narrated in St. Luke in the Gospel we have just heard.

The evangelist says: … the Lord appointed seventy-two others. It is God who appoints and chooses His missionaries. As He did before with the seventy-two disciples, He continues to do so now through His Church. Bishops and religious Superiors choose missionaries in His name and with His authority7. And in case anyone thinks this is something of the past, let me tell you that, by the grace of God, the Institute is today on mission in more than 103 jurisdictions in 45 countries. And at present we are on mission in 16 European countries, in 13 Asian countries, in only 10 countries in the Americas, in 3 countries in Africa and in another 3 in Oceania. What is more, as of today we have at least some 164 requests for foundations –both for monasteries and for parishes– awaiting realization.

I send you

The Gospel goes on to say: … and sent them. The Lord not only chooses, but also sends. A sending that is in the likeness of the sending of the Son by the Father: As the Father has sent Me, so I send you8. A sending that formally constitutes the mission9. But He does not send them in just any way; St. Luke carefully specifies that He sent them two by two… “Our Lord must have had His reasons for acting this way, and in fact many continue to proceed in the same manner. St. Gregory the Great said: ‘He sent them in this way because the precepts of charity are two: love of God and love of neighbor; and with fewer than two there cannot be charity: this indicates to us that whoever does not have charity toward his brothers should not take up the office of preacher’10. And in this sense we read in our Constitutions: ‘it is also very true that, if in mathematics one plus one is two, one man plus another man is two thousand. One man together with another grows in courage and in strength, fear disappears, and he escapes any trap’11”12.

By the grace of God, at present very few of our men are alone, and although from the very beginning the intention was that they should not be alone, sending a companion has not been easy. For example, the priest who is in the Solomon Islands has been waiting a long time for another father to arrive, but certain difficulties with visas at the beginning, and then the pandemic, have made it impossible for the priest assigned there to arrive. Only this month are they going to open the borders, and God willing the other father will be able to go. However, we can say that the immense majority of the foundations made in these last 6 years have always been made in pairs, except in exceptional cases in which it was seen necessary for one to take up the mission first until the other arrived.

St. Luke goes on to say that Christ sent the missionaries ahead of Him to every town and place where He was to go. That is, He sends them to the magnificent task of preparing the way by which He Himself will reach souls. This is the great consolation of the authentic missionary: the certainty of being sent by Him and for Him who is the one who must come to men. Hence the unshakable confidence in the inexhaustible power of the mission for the conversion of men. It is Jesus Christ Himself who wants to come to them13.

Now notice that it says to every town and place… That is why the Proper Law explicitly tells us: “There is no place where there is a soul that is forbidden to the missionary. To the humblest huts, to the highest heights, to the most rugged ravines, to where there are fewer people, where less fruit is expected, where the people are more unruly, where there are more difficulties… there the missionary must go, staff in hand, by car, by plane, on foot or on horseback, by sulky or by boat… because that is his vocation and that is where obedience sends him”14. One of the requests for foundations we have, for example, is to work with the Native Americans in South Dakota in the United States; another is to found in two more cities in Uzbekistan… We, as members of the Institute, cannot reject a place a priori. We have to be ready to go anywhere, to every town and place where He wants to go. We are His ambassadors, as St. Paul said: We are, then, ambassadors for Christ, as if God were exhorting through us15. And wherever we go, our mission is always to bring Christ: “There is no true evangelization as long as the name, the teaching, the life, the promises, the Kingdom, the mystery of Jesus of Nazareth, Son of God, are not proclaimed”16. People are not interested in hearing about politics, about soccer players, about celebrity gossip; what people want to hear from the missionary is precisely about Christ, without cuts, without watering down His teaching, but rather that His truth be preached with all authenticity and vigor. And for that one must prepare, one must study, one must be well formed.

Not long ago I was reading some statistics from the Fides agency17 which said that the number of priests in the world is 414,336, which means there is 1 priest for every 14,853 people in the world. Likewise, the number of major seminarians in the world has decreased and currently stands at 114,058 (68,609 diocesan and 45,449 religious), almost 2,000 fewer seminarians than the previous year. That is why it is not surprising that today too Christ says to us: The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few. Pray, therefore, the Lord of the harvest to send laborers into His harvest. The apostolic work is enormous; that is why we must stop our ears when someone, contradicting the words of the Lord, says there are many vocations. The laborers will always be few and the harvest will always be plentiful. Only thieves and robbers18, only the hireling shepherd19, can be so criminal as to divert, discourage, fail to work for, or oppose vocations. We must always pray, asking God to send laborers into His harvest. But we must also work for missionary vocations, and one of the means we have to promote vocations is precisely the popular mission20. How many of our members came to know the Institute through popular missions!

So far, so good. Christ sends us, we go two by two, we go everywhere… but that does not mean there will be no difficulties. Christ does not deceive us; He is very explicit when He tells us: Go! Behold, I send you as lambs in the midst of wolves. Our Lord insists: Go!, as if saying, do not fail to go, although He warns us of the dangers we will face. We are unarmed prophets (that is, defenseless), weaponless; we have only spiritual weapons. That is why the Proper Law, quoting the great and experienced apostle Paul, gives us the following exhortation: Put on the armor of God so that you may be able to withstand the snares of the devil. For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the principalities, against the powers, against the rulers of this dark world, against the spirits of evil in the heights. Therefore, take up the armor of God, so that you may be able to resist on the evil day and, having overcome everything, to stand firm. Stand, then, with your waist girded with truth and clothed with justice as a breastplate, your feet shod with zeal for the Gospel of peace, always holding up the shield of faith, so that with it you may extinguish all the flaming darts of the Evil One. Take, also, the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God; always in prayer and supplication, praying on every occasion in the Spirit, keeping watch together with perseverance and interceding for all the saints…21.

Pastoral work is a cross

You have heard it many times, and you will have experienced it many times too: “Pastoral work is a cross, difficulties, dangers, sufferings: it is not taking advantage of the milk, the meat or the wool of the sheep. Those who empty out the cross empty out pastoral work. Hence so many strident and resounding pastoral failures. Keep in mind that the sheep will not follow a stranger; rather, they will flee from him, because they do not know the voice of strangers22”23.

Then Christ gives us the following recommendation: Carry no purse, no bag, no sandals. What does this mean? The Proper Law, when it explains this instruction of the Incarnate Word, says that “it does not mean that our Lord wants us to do without all of that, but rather it teaches the spirit of poverty that the missionary must have. That is, we must not be attached to what is unnecessary. We must trust in Providence without limit, while putting the means”24.

Now it is interesting that Christ says: And greet no one on the road. Does He perhaps command us to be unfriendly or impatient? No! What our Lord indicates to us here is that “the task is urgent; we must not waste time, nor opportunities to do good: ‘opportunity is like iron, you have to hammer it while it’s hot’25”26, as the Martín Fierro says. Therefore, we must make the most of all the opportunities God gives us to do apostolate. One day many of you will very probably be in some parish, and you will realize how important it is that the priest be at the door at the end of Mass or at the end of any celebration, greeting the people, listening attentively, without hurry, without anxiety, to whoever approaches him. The immense majority of people go to Mass only on Sundays; if on that day you do not attend to them well, if you do not give them due attention, the people will go looking for help elsewhere or will stop coming, and you will have lost the beautiful opportunity of performing a great act of charity. Pastoral charity is indispensable for carrying out the priestly ministry effectively. Always keep this in mind.

But “nowadays it is not enough to call people to come to the Church. We must go out to meet people!”27. “We must go where people live, to their environments, to their workplaces”28. Jesus Himself visited many houses and reminds us of this apostolate in today’s Gospel: Into whatever house you enter, first say: “Peace to this house.” St. John Paul II gave great importance to this apostolate of visits. That is why it is told that once, when he visited a parish in Rome, he said to them: “I come to your parish to carry out in it the principal apostolic ministry: the ministry of visiting”29.

Let us note, moreover, that Christ establishes peace as the end of the mission, peace in souls, because what the mission seeks is precisely to reconcile souls with God. “That is why it is so important that in the personal visit everyone be invited to be in the grace of God. Hence one must guide the conversation with people, without getting caught up in superficial or less important topics, but aiming at conversion”30.

That visit to homes, so highly recommended by the Proper Law and by Christ Himself, “must not be a ‘doctor’s visit,’” a short one; “nor hurried, as if taking a census”31. At times more importance is given to the number of houses visited than to the spiritual fruits, and it should not be so. One must take all the time necessary to be able to detect the spiritual needs of the family, its difficulties, to be able to deal with their questions, answer their doubts, dismantle the sophisms against the faith, etc.”32

The Gospel goes on to say that the seventy-two returned rejoicing… “Always, inevitably, the missionary returns from the mission with immense joy. Perhaps there is no man more joyful than the authentic missionary. What the Lord promised will always be fulfilled: Blessed are those who work for peace…33”34. Every week, when we watch the little video of ¡Viva la misión!, we have the opportunity to see the joy of our missionaries, even though they are in very adverse places. For “the characteristic of every authentic missionary life is interior joy, which comes from faith”35.

Now, what does it mean when it says: Lord, even the demons are subject to us in your name, and Christ answers them: “I saw Satan fall from heaven like lightning”? Well, there the effect of a mission well done is declared: the fall of the devil, unable to trap souls in sin. And that is the reason for missionary joy. Christ goes on to say: Behold, I have given you the power to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall harm you… That is why, even if all the forces of hell come together, they will have no power over the missionary, since his powers come to him from Christ. Nothing can harm one who is a true disciple of the Lord.

St. Francis Xavier, in one of his letters, speaks of the dangers of the mission, but stresses that in the mission there are dangers greater than poison and violent death, and these are the loss of hope and of trust in God, when one sees oneself threatened, when the apostolate seems to bear no fruit. Then the saint says: “if it is important for His greater service, God will protect us from all the dangers of this life, and without His permission and His authorization, the demons and their ministers can do us no harm. That is why our security lies in the word of the Lord: Whoever loves his life, according to this world, will lose it, and whoever loses it for God will find it36. And in this other similar one: Whoever puts his hand to the plow and looks back is not fit for the Kingdom of God”37.

The Gospel concludes by saying: but do not rejoice that the spirits are subject to you; rejoice that your names are written in heaven. “The greatest reason for the missionary’s joy is to have been chosen by the Lord to preach His Gospel; a choice and a mission that will not go unrewarded: let him know that whoever brings back a sinner from the error of his way will save his soul from death and will cover a multitude of his sins38”39.

[Peroratio] I conclude by making my own the words of Blessed Paolo Manna: “We need a veritable army of superior men, filled with the Spirit of God, capable of founding and organizing new Christian communities and Churches, capable, moreover, of suffering much: not mere soldiers, but leaders; not hirelings or amateurs, but true Pastors of souls in the most sublime sense of the word, who know how to form Jesus Christ in souls from the overflowing of their treasure of grace and virtue”40.

Let us ask the Most Holy Virgin, Queen of the Apostles, in this Holy Mass, for the grace never to diminish, for any reason, our missionary identity, nor to forget the exact purpose of the ministry and of the apostolic service to which we have been called: may we be, today and always, essentially missionary41.
