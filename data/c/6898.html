The devil you do not know
Part I

«All things must be restored, entirely, in Christ. He must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet” (1 Cor 15:25), claiming the whole universe for Christ, recapitulating all things in Christ, things in heaven and things on earth (Eph 1:10)»1.

This paragraph of our Constitutions, while it speaks to us of a specific responsibility to restore all things by making Christ reign, also speaks to us of the inevitable confrontation with his enemies that this task brings with it.

Who are these enemies?

The Word of God teaches us that they are those who deny that the Word has taken human flesh: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God; and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not of God; this is the spirit of the Antichrist, which you have heard is coming, and now is already in the world» (1 Jn 4:2-3).

So it is important not to be naive and to recognize that in this undertaking we live side by side—like the wheat and the weeds2—with the enemy who is already in the world (1 Jn 4:3).

One of the offices of St. John of the Cross, perhaps unknown to many, was that of exorcist. Nevertheless, any of his biographies mentions his arduous and intricate struggle with the enemy of souls. One of the most renowned cases, and one that illustrates what we have just said—every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not of God; this is the spirit of the Antichrist—is that of the nun María de Olivares, an Augustinian nun in Ávila. Briefly: «One fine day she begins to quote and comment on passages of the Bible with incredible brilliance. Her fellow nuns are amazed and her fame spreads from mouth to mouth. And the pilgrimage of people who want to see this marvel begins. It is said that “she speaks all languages, and knows all the sciences,” without having studied. Several eminent theologians examine her and approve that spirit of the nun, who speaks with them about everything divine with incredible self-assurance; and they think it is a case of infused knowledge, of that charismatic knowledge that God infuses into the humble and simple.

But the superiors are not fully convinced, so they contact St. Teresa so that she will send St. John of the Cross. It must be said that the saint is a sworn enemy of miracle-mongering3; he is most reluctant to attribute things to interventions of God, special locutions, etc., as if the Most High had nothing else to do but contravene the laws that He himself has established. He mercilessly disqualifies those who label “everything as from God”4.

The fact is that off goes the holy friar, at 32 years of age (1574). Friar John enters the parlor; on the other side of the grille the nun appears. For an hour the friar speaks, asks, inquires. The nun does not open her mouth; not one word from her who never stopped talking with others who had come to see her. On coming out of the interview, St. John says: “Let your paternities not be deceived, for the spirit this religious has is not the spirit of God but the spirit of the devil, who has her deceived. Gentlemen, this nun is possessed.” In short, the exorcisms begin. As a clear result of the first conjurations, it comes to light, through the confession of the possessed woman herself, that she had given herself to the devil a year after entering the convent, while still very young. And she did so not by word but in writing, recording it in a document written in her own blood. There is someone who declares that St. John of the Cross told them that the possessed woman “had written on all the joints of her body the names of the demons who possessed her.”

One day Friar John says to the nun: translate for me these words of the Gospel of St. John: Verbum caro factum est et habitavit in nobis. And she translates quickly: ‘The Son of God became man and lived with you.’ You lie!—retorts the exorcist—the words do not say ‘with you,’ but ‘with us.’ It is as I say—she retorts—because he did not become man to live with us, but to live with you.’ Years later Friar John will recall with sorrow that the possessed woman wept because there were people who loved God. And this truly is diabolical»5.

So you see how every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God; and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not of God; this is the spirit of the Antichrist, which you have heard is coming, and now is already in the world (1 Jn 4:2-3).

Certainly this is an extreme case, and it is not always easy to discern what is preternatural in these cases, nor does the Church readily condescend to or support the tendency to attribute many events and direct interventions to the devil; but in principle it cannot be denied that Satan has a great eagerness to harm souls and lead them to evil6. On the other hand, there is a common work and an ordinary presence of the devil in our lives. Constantly, whether we are aware of it or not, we face this formidable enemy who—while it is true that he rarely claims a dramatic role in our lives—nevertheless normally intrudes in a harmful and even destructive way into our journey through life. Not for nothing does the Master of the faith say that this enemy of the soul «is the darkest and most difficult to understand»7. That is why we are now going to see what his characteristic traits are (according to St. John of the Cross) and then what his strategies are, in order to forewarn ourselves against his attacks. Because, just as we must be masters of prayer, we must also be masters of discernment.

1. Characteristics of the enemy

Among the most significant traits we can list: envious; liar; deceiver; malicious; cunning; proud; strong.

  • Envious

We have just said it in the case of María de Olivares: the possessed woman «wept because there were people who loved God.» Which is a very clear sign of diabolical envy. Those tears, translated into the pure language of the catechism, meant sorrow at another's good, and what a good! This is precisely what defines the devil, who has «great envy and sorrow at the good and peace of the soul»8. That is why in his Spiritual Canticle St. John of the Cross wrote: «through his great malice, he envies all the good he sees in it (in the soul)»9. And because he envies, he goes into action along the whole spiritual path. And those who walk most resolutely in the spiritual life he attacks the most, and therefore greater vigilance and discretion are needed. What I mean is that his envy is not mere resentment (like a persistent desire); it is not envy of a static kind, but dynamic and aggressive; that is why one of the great enemies that wage war and hinder the soul's journey is the devil10, who strives forcefully to block the passage of this road11 like a true highwayman. In the famous digression St. John of the Cross makes about the three blind guides who could lead the soul off the road, the second is the devil12 , who wants «the soul, since he is blind, to be blind also»13, and he places himself at the passage from sense to spirit14. He is, spiritually speaking, the adversary of the soul15. The more envious, the more an enemy, and the more an enemy, the more envious. This is his mark.

St. Ignatius expresses this obstruction by the devil, saying in the 2nd rule for the discernment of spirits for the 1st Week: «… it is proper to the evil spirit to bite, to sadden and to put obstacles, disquieting with false reasons, so that one may not go forward»16. And we will see further on the various strategies he uses for this.

What is certain is that wherever he can do harm, there he is. Unfortunately, his envy and malice also grow sharper, and wherever he can do the most harm, there he employs his powers in the most subtle and forceful way. Souls especially advanced in the ways of the spirit he tries to bring down by any means. «For he values more hindering this soul of one carat of this its wealth and glorious delight than making many others fall into many other and grave sins, because the others have little or nothing to lose and this one much, because it has gained much, and very precious»17.

At all times St. John of the Cross points out the «great envy of the peace and interior recollection»18 of the soul that the devil has. And he calls them foxes, because these cunning and malicious demons, with countless disturbances and movements, disturb the devotion of holy souls. Elsewhere he refers to Satan as a «disturbing presence»19. All of which corresponds to that 5th rule of discernment that St. Ignatius gives for the second week, which says: «… if it weakens or disquiets or disturbs the soul, taking away its peace, tranquility and quiet which it had before, it is a clear sign that it proceeds from the evil spirit…»20. He gives something very similar in the 1st rule, where he says that «it is proper to the enemy to fight against spiritual joy and consolation, bringing apparent reasons and persistent fallacies»21.

In the face of this ‘characteristic’ of the enemy we must know how to act, guided by the sound principles that St. Ignatius offers us, which are always valid. And to apply them not only to ourselves, but to know how to make use of them for the guidance of souls.

This first principle:

[329] 1st rule. The first: it is proper to God and his angels in their motions to give true spiritual gladness and joy, taking away all sadness and disturbance that the enemy induces.

As a second principle: to impress upon the soul the rule that says: [330] 2nd rule: it belongs to God our Lord alone to give consolation to the soul without preceding cause; for it is proper to the Creator to enter, to leave, to cause motion in it, drawing it wholly into love of his divine majesty.

  • Liar-deceiver

The very name devil means: to cause destruction, to divide, to slander, to deceive. That is why the Apostle St. John said: do not trust every spirit (1 Jn 4:1). Because there is an earthly, animal and diabolical wisdom, opposed to the wisdom from above (Jas 3:15).

«According to Sacred Scripture, and especially the New Testament, the dominion and influence of Satan and of the other evil spirits extends to the whole world […]. The action of Satan consists above all in tempting men to evil, influencing their imagination and their higher faculties in order to be able to turn them in the direction contrary to the law of God»22.

Thus, for example, in the face of or against the interventions of God, who manifests himself to his prophets through images and representations23, «the devil tries with his own, apparently good ones, to deceive the soul, as can be seen in the (book) of Kings, when he deceived all the prophets of Ahab, representing to them in their imagination the horns with which he said he would destroy the Assyrians, and it was a lie24»25.

This capacity to lie and deceive is increased indirectly when God, by his just judgments, permits it to him more abundantly, God thus becoming the cause of all these harms—a privative cause, specifies John of the Cross, «which consists in his withdrawing his light and his favor; so withdrawn that they necessarily fall into error»26. With the withdrawal of heaven's light and favor «God gives the devil leave to blind and deceive many, their sins and boldness deserving it. And the devil can do so and gets his way, they believing him and taking him for a good spirit. So much so that, even if they are strongly persuaded that he is not, there is no way of undeceiving them, inasmuch as they already have, by God's permission, ingrained in them the spirit of understanding backwards»27.

On various occasions throughout his works the saint refers to the power of suggestion that the devil has, by which he goes about sowing falsehoods. For example, in the case of ‘interior words,’ in which «the devil meddles a great deal, especially with those who have some inclination or fondness for them»28, and he says that the evil one employs this way of communicating «with those who have made some pact with him, tacit or express, and as he communicates with some heretics, especially with some heresiarchs, informing their intellect with very subtle, false and erroneous concepts and reasons»29. His power of suggestion is also manifested with respect to knowledge and understandings about creatures; lies that are not inconsequential or irrelevant, but concerning «the sins of others, and bad consciences, and bad souls, falsely and with much light, all in order to defame and with the desire that it be uncovered, so that sins may be committed, placing zeal in the soul as if it were in order to commend them to God»30. This reminds me of that letter of St. John of Ávila, which he wrote to a religious preacher who was greatly persecuted: «They (the servants of God) suffer for God and because they drew near to God; and the persecution is against God. If the persecutors think otherwise, perhaps they diminish their guilt somewhat, but not our crown; and if they, deceived, think they are serving God, we, undeceived, know that we are serving God»31. How often all those media attacks are an explicit manifestation of precisely this: it is all in order to defame, so that sins may be committed, making the soul believe that it is acting well.

That is why St. Ignatius warns that it is very important to be guided by the «synderesis of reason»32, that is, by right judgment. God will always move us through reason illuminated by faith. The devil, on the other hand, works by suggestion, that is, through things that of themselves do not enter the sanctuary of the soul, which are the intellect and the will; but rather the senses, the memories of what has been apprehended by the senses, the emotions, etc.

At this point it is key to apply that rule which St. Ignatius gives and which says: [333] 5th rule. The fifth:

we must pay close attention to the course of our thoughts; and if the beginning, middle and end are all good, inclined to all good, it is a sign of the good angel; but if in the course of the thoughts he brings, it ends in something bad or distracting, or less good than what the soul had previously proposed to do, or it weakens or disquiets or disturbs the soul, taking away its peace, tranquility and quiet which it had before, it is a clear sign that it proceeds from the evil spirit, enemy of our progress and eternal salvation.

Let it be clear: the only sure means to discover the deceits of the enemy is an examination that attends solicitously to all the movements of our spirit, that is illuminated by supernatural grace and is guided and directed by sound direction. This is a constant struggle; that is why St. Ignatius says that «we must pay close attention,» that is, to the complete succession of our resolutions: if it ends in something bad, distracting, or weakens the soul, it is clearly not from God.

Treating of the imperfections of proficients who have already passed through the passive night of sense, the Mystical Doctor writes one of the harshest pages of all his books, warning spiritual persons who enjoy in abundance so many spiritual communications and apprehensions, and where he lets us see many of the effects by which something is recognized to be the work of the devil. In these circumstances the devil, says the saint, «with great ease enchants and deceives» the spiritual person, «she not having the caution to resign herself and defend herself strongly in faith from all these visions and feelings»33. The devil's organizational chart is perfect: «For here the devil makes many believe vain visions and false prophecies; here in this position he tries to make them presume that God and the saints are speaking with them, and they often believe their own fancy; here the devil is wont to fill them with presumption and pride, and, drawn by vanity and arrogance, they allow themselves to be seen in exterior acts that appear holy, such as raptures and other appearances. They thus become bold with God, losing holy fear, which is the key and guardian of all the virtues. And so many falsehoods and deceits are wont to multiply in some of these, and they grow so old in them, that their return to the pure path of virtue and true spirit is very doubtful»34. That series of “here”s shows how the devil is a realist in his affairs and knows very well how to take advantage of concrete occasions.

In his Precautions the saint says that one of the devil's most ordinary wiles for deceiving spiritual persons «is to deceive them under the appearance of good and not under the appearance of evil; because he knows that evil, when recognized, they will hardly take»35. Then he says: «The devil can represent in the memory and fancy many false notions and forms that seem true and good, impressing them on the spirit and sense with great efficacy and certainty by suggestion, in such a way that it seems to the soul that there is nothing else, but that it is just as it appears to it, because, as he transfigures himself into an angel of light, it seems light to the soul»36.

You already notice the similarity it has with the 4th rule of discernment that St. Ignatius gives in [332] of the book of the Exercises: it is proper to the evil angel, who takes the form sub angelo lucis, to enter with the devout soul and come out with himself; that is to say, to bring good and holy thoughts in keeping with such a just soul, and afterwards, little by little, he tries to come out drawing the soul into his hidden deceits and perverse intentions.

Notice that he says «he enters with the devout soul,» that is, he brings good and holy thoughts, but afterwards, little by little, he tries to get his own way, «drawing the soul into his hidden deceits and perverse intentions.» The devil does not mind waiting; he knows how to take many long detours, and above all he likes to feign great devotion, great observance, at least external. In fact, it seems something good, one becomes disordered in it, and then we have fallen into the trap. Then we fail in charity under the cloak of piety; or we are excessively rigorous under the cloak of observance. St. Thomas teaches that: «Justice without mercy is cruelty»37, and the Constitutions say this very thing explicitly. The devil consents to our practicing some good works, even to our doing penance, to our praying… but he waits crouching to spring and seize his prey.

That is why «one of the means by which the devil easily catches incautious souls and blocks their way to the truth of the spirit is through supernatural and extraordinary things, which he displays through images, now in the material and corporeal ones that the Church uses, now in those that he is wont to fix in the fancy under the guise of such and such a saint or his image, transfiguring himself into an angel of light in order to deceive (2 Cor 11:14)»38.

Even more, using that same deceptive tactic of seeking to obstruct through the very means of sanctification, St. John of the Cross says that the devil is the second cause of the carnal rebellions that some persons suffer when they set themselves to prayer or when they are «exercising the Sacraments of Penance and the Eucharist»39. With this he wants to intimidate and frighten souls so that they abandon these spiritual exercises, given that kind of conditioned reflex to which they find themselves subject. It is like someone who goes to adoration or to celebrate Mass and has such frightful impure thoughts that he then feels tempted not to make the adoration or not to celebrate Mass.

  • Malicious – cunning

John of the Cross has already told us how the devil, through his great malice, is extremely envious40. The greatness of his envy will be revealed to us as gigantic when we see the breadth and magnitude of his malice.

Notice that diabolical malice, which sometimes disguises itself as goodness, is not something superficial, but something that inspires and governs his steps in the sad business of seeking the perdition of men.

It is his malice that drives him to harm souls, so that his family may thus increase. Let us say that the devil possesses an envious malice and at the same time a malicious envy, which does not give up its task of harming souls.

The whole world lies under the evil one (1 Jn 5:19), says the apostle St. John. And so we see how with cunning they go «penetrating into the world and into human existence in order to exercise their power through them, but at the same time they hide themselves in this world and in human existence. Or, if you will: while they and their power are manifested in and through men, the elements and institutions, at the same time they withdraw into themselves, remaining concealed. And this concealing of their presence belongs essentially to their nature»41. These principalities and powers, moreover, speak the language of the masters of the culture of death, of temptation, of sin and of the lie, and present themselves as accusers of the brethren.

God, on the contrary, when he speaks to good souls does so with a sweet, light, gentle touch, like «a drop of water entering a sponge»42. That is, he speaks to them in the language of consolation, which is a dwelling familiarly with God in the manner of two friends and companions, or of two members of the same family who live in the same house. There is a sensible increase of the theological virtues; the soul finds itself inflamed with love for God and experiences great desires to fulfill the will of God perfectly and to suffer for God.

Now, when God grants favors or graces to the soul «through the good angel, God ordinarily permits the adversary to know of them; for one thing, so that he may do against them what he can according to the proportion of justice, and thus the devil cannot allege his right, saying that he is not given the opportunity to conquer the soul, as he did in Job (1:9-11; 2:4-8). Which would be the case if God did not allow there to be a certain parity between the two warriors, namely, the good angel and the bad, regarding the soul, and thus the victory of either may be more esteemed, and the soul victorious and faithful in temptation may be more rewarded»43.

Although the malice of Satan and his followers is so great, so widespread and deep, John of the Cross is the man of hope and optimism. His Sayings of Light and Love open with this perspective, even while recognizing the abundance and power of diabolical and human malice: «The Lord has always revealed the treasures of his wisdom and spirit to mortals; but now that malice is more and more showing its face, he reveals them much (more)»44.

Our saint is right when, characterizing the three enemies of the soul, he says of the second: «The devil is the darkest to understand»45, and his temptations and wiles the most difficult to understand46; that is why he advises the Discalced nuns of Beas: «It is very necessary, my daughters, to know how to withdraw the body of the spirit from the devil and from our sensuality»47.

  • Proud

Writing about the harms that follow for the soul from placing the joy of the will in natural goods, St. John of the Cross speaks of the fallen angels and of the harm it was for those angels to rejoice and take delight in their beauty and natural goods, for because of this they fell into the hideous abysses48. Then he applies this to a concrete case: «Whoever trusts in himself is worse than the devil»49. More clearly and with more emphasis: «and some (spiritual persons) become so proud that they are worse than the devil»50. The saint deals this sledgehammer blow when speaking of the foolish presumption and vanity that certain persons have, full of «secret self-esteem and pride, and they never quite understand that they are steeped in it up to their eyes»51.

Our Constitutions, with great realism, express how this pride is a root of tribulations and discontent for us. Which happens «when a member closes in on himself, selfishly seeks his own interests and falls into one of the four roots of self-love, namely, one's own judgment, one's own will, one's own honor and one's own pleasure.» Then it gives us an example: «They are the ones who throw stones and hide their hands, who see only the speck in another's eye and do not see the beam in their own»52.

That refined pride from which they suffer, and which in certain cases seems superior to the diabolical, they have undoubtedly also attained through the fault of the devil, whom John of the Cross never loses sight of. Proud, he wants to make others proud. And so he attributes no small part of this responsibility to him, saying: «and in these (beginners) the devil often increases the fervor and the desire to do these and other works so that their pride and presumption may keep growing, because the devil knows very well that all these works and virtues they perform not only are of no value to them, but rather turn into vice for them»53.

The devil himself, through the forms, notions and reasonings that he can add into the life of the soul, can affect it «with pride, avarice, anger, envy, etc., and instill unjust hatred, vain love, and deceive in many ways»54.

When it fell to St. John of the Cross to give his judgment on the unclear spirit of a Discalced Carmelite nun, the saint found five defects «for judging it to be a true spirit.» The fourth and principal one was precisely that «in this way she follows there do not appear effects of humility, which, when the favors are true, as she thinks, are never ordinarily communicated to the soul without first undoing and annihilating it in an interior abasement of humility.»

As a final remedy for making the evil spirit disappear, he prescribes the following: «[…] test her in the exercise of the virtues, plain and simple […] and the tests must be good ones, for there is no devil who will not suffer something for the sake of his honor»55.

  • Strong

The Mystical Doctor assures us that the malice with which the devil is clothed is pure weakness56. Nevertheless, he does not fail to affirm and warn about the strength, the power of the devil.

This enemy of the soul is strong, and with great force he tries to block the soul's way on its path of sanctification; the more the soul strives to advance, the stronger his temptations and wiles become, and the harder to overcome and the more difficult to understand than those of the world and the flesh; and to make matters worse, he makes those enemies, the world and the flesh, grow stronger as well57.

It is fitting here to call to mind once again that rule of St. Ignatius which says:

[325] 12th rule. The twelfth: the enemy acts like a woman in being weak against vigor and strong when given his way; for, just as it is proper to a woman, when she quarrels with a man, to lose heart and take flight when the man shows her a bold face; and on the contrary, if the man begins to flee, losing heart, the anger, vengeance and ferocity of the woman grows very great and beyond all measure; in the same way it is proper to the enemy to weaken and lose heart, his temptations taking flight, when the person who is exercising himself in spiritual things shows a bold face against the temptations of the enemy, doing the diametrical opposite; and on the contrary, if the person who is exercising himself begins to be afraid and to lose heart in suffering the temptations, there is no beast so fierce on the face of the earth as the enemy of human nature in pursuing his damnable intention with such great malice.

In that showing ‘a bold face’ and doing ‘the oposito per diametrum’ lies the key.

But in some cases his ‘strength’ reaches such a point that «no human power can be compared with his,—says St. John of the Cross—and so only divine power suffices to be able to overcome him, and only divine light to be able to understand his schemes»58. If divine power is the only thing capable of overcoming so strong an enemy, we will have to take hold of that power, we will have to clothe ourselves with it. The advice of John of the Cross, founded this time more on Scripture, runs thus: «For which reason the soul that is to overcome his strength will not be able to do so without prayer, nor will it be able to understand his deceits without mortification and without humility»59. The watchword is perfect, bearing in mind that deceits, lies and traps form part of the strength he displays against poor mortals. «That is why St. Paul, warning the faithful, says these words: put on the armor of God so that you may resist the wiles of the enemy, for this struggle is not as against flesh and blood (Eph 6:11-12); understanding by blood the world, and by the armor of God, prayer and the cross of Christ, in which are humility and mortification»60.

Only the Cross of Christ has this power, inasmuch as the Crucified and Risen One was the only one capable of freeing us from so strong an enemy, God having, in his Son made man, «disarmed the Principalities and Powers, exhibited them publicly, incorporating them into his triumphal procession»61. Faith assures us that the last word is the triumph of God.
