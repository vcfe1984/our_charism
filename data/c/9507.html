The Virgin Mary Is the Mother of God

Recalling the thought of our Founder
During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the eighth day of the novena, in which we pray specifically for our faith in Mary as Mother of the Incarnate Word, we recall Fr. Buela's thought on this subject, taken from his book “El Arte del Padre”.

The Virgin Mary Is the Mother of God

If someone is called the son of such-and-such a mother, it is because he has taken his body from her, even though his soul does not come to him from his mother, but from outside. But the body of that man was taken from the Virgin Mother; and we have also seen that the body of that man was the body of the natural Son of God, that is, of the Word of God. Therefore it is fittingly said that the blessed Virgin is mother of the Word of God, and also of God, even though the divinity is not taken from the mother, for it is not necessary that the son take from the mother everything that belongs to his substance, but only the body.

Moreover, the Apostle says: “God sent his Son, made of a woman” (Gal 4:4), by which words it is shown how the mission of the Son of God is to be understood, since he is said to be sent inasmuch as he is made of a woman. And this could not be true if the Son of God had not existed before being made of a woman, since what is sent to something necessarily exists before being in that to which it is sent. But that man, the adoptive son, according to Nestorius, did not exist before being born of a woman. Therefore the saying “God sent his Son” cannot be understood of the adoptive Son, but must be understood of the natural Son, that is, of God, the Word of God. But if one is made of a woman, he is called the son of a woman. Therefore God, the Word of God, is the son of a woman.

But perhaps someone will say that the Apostle's expression should not be understood as if the Son of God had been sent in order to be made of a woman, but as if the Son of God, who was made of a woman and under the law, had been sent to redeem “those who were under the law” (Gal 4:5). And according to this, the statement “his Son” would not need to be understood of the natural son, but of that man who is a son by adoption. But such a sense is rejected by the very words of the Apostle. For only he who is above the law, who is the author of the law, can free from the law. Now, the law was laid down by God. Therefore it belongs to God to free from the law. And yet the Apostle attributes this to the Son of God of whom he speaks. Therefore the Son of God of whom he speaks is the natural Son. Consequently, it is true to say that the natural Son of God, that is, God, the Word of God, was made of a woman.

This is also made evident by the fact that in the Psalms the redemption of the human race is attributed to God himself: “You have ransomed me, O Lord, God of truth” (Ps 31:6). The adoption of sons of God is brought about by the Holy Spirit, according to what is said: “You have received the spirit of adoption as sons” (Rom 8:15). But the Holy Spirit is not a gift of man, but of God. Therefore the adoption of sons is not caused by man, but by God. It is caused, then, by the Son of God, sent from God and made of a woman; which seems clear from what the Apostle adds: “So that we might receive the adoption of sons” (Rom 8:15). Consequently, the Apostle's word must be understood as said of the natural Son of God. Therefore God, the Word of God, was made of a woman, that is, of the Virgin Mother.

St. John says: “The Word became flesh.” Now, he cannot have flesh except from a woman. Therefore the Word was made of a woman, that is, of the Virgin Mother. The Virgin, then, is mother of the Son of God.

The Apostle also says: “Christ comes from the patriarchs according to the flesh, he who is above all things, God blessed forever” (Rom 9:5). Now, he does not come from the patriarchs except through the Virgin. Therefore God, who is above all things, comes from the Virgin according to the flesh. Consequently, the Virgin is Mother of God according to the flesh.

[In accordance with this, Fr. Buela said in a good-night talk1:]

To be ignorant of this is to be ignorant of the most elementary things, and the worst of it is that it sows confusion, even in consecrated souls, because afterwards I had the case of a seminarian who came to ask me the same question: [“Can God really have a mother?”], exactly the same question. When such outrageous notions enter a consecrated soul, that soul will not remain consecrated for long, because if it does not know that Jesus Christ is true God and true man, and is One alone because both natures are united in the One Person of the Word, what sense does religious life have, what sense does virginity have, what sense does poverty have, what sense does obedience have, what sense do the sacraments have? If the one we see on the cross does not have a human nature hypostatically united to a divine Person, no one paid for our sins, because he would be a mere man; neither would baptism wash you of your sins, nor confession, nor would the Eucharist be the Body and Blood of the Lord, together with his soul and his divinity.

All the sacraments fall, the Church falls. That is why we are as we are, that is why there are so many priests who have devoted themselves to temporal matters, and some seem to have stopped believing in the eternal truths. That is why we must always go deeper into these truths of faith, because it is these truths of faith that are capable of moving our heart to imitate the Lord. And to know them in such a way as to be able to respond when someone comes out with something like that. Because, as John Paul I said: “Of the faith we only have what we are capable of defending.” If there is something of the faith that we are not capable of defending, it is because we do not have faith, because we do not have the faith we ought to have. That is why tonight let us ask the Blessed Virgin, to whom we are going to sing in a few minutes, for the grace to defend with the greatest possible strength the truth of the Word who became flesh.
