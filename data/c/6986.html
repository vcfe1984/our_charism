[Exordium] Today God grants us the grace of meeting in this place to celebrate the august memorial of St. Gianna Beretta Molla. A celebration made even more beautiful and moving by the presence of her daughter Gianna Emanuela. It is not every day that one has the opportunity to celebrate Mass in the company of the saint’s immediate family; that is why this is a special grace.

In a general audience a few years ago, the Holy Father said: “being a mother does not only mean bringing a child into the world, but it is also a choice of life”1. And “to give one’s life,” the Pope goes on to say, is “to have a spirit of martyrdom; it is to give it in duty, in silence, in prayer, in the honest fulfillment of duty; in that silence of daily life, […] it is to bear witness always, even in the worst moments, to tenderness, to self-giving, to moral strength.”

If this is said of every mother, how much more can these words be applied to St. Gianna.

A heroic and exemplary choice

To this woman, wife and mother who made a monumental choice, a heroic and exemplary option, and who will bear witness down through the centuries –as John Paul the Great said– “that only the one who has the courage to give himself totally to God and to his brothers fulfills himself.”

An option that speaks of many previous options, perhaps smaller, but definitely laden with true love and therefore sacrificial. I am pleased to quote at this point her own husband, Pietro Molla, who, speaking of the choice made by his beloved wife, said: “hers is certainly a heroic witness of maternal love. Her choice of love was extremely difficult considering that she was a mother who loved her other three children intensely and who loved life itself. Her choice cannot be properly understood and appreciated except in the light of Gianna’s firm convictions, of her conscience as a mother, of her unshakable trust in Providence. […] It was certainly a dramatic choice […] a tremendous sacrifice, and one that makes manifest her trust in Providence”2.

Our options

In the same way, each one of us here present is called to make our options daily, small and great, some more dramatic than others, but always faithful to our convictions, to the faith we profess; making of our lives “an unceasing worship of divine Providence”3. Certain that God, who sees in secret, will reward us4. For it is from these small fidelities that great holiness is born5, which will spur many others to follow the Incarnate Word carrying their own cross. St. Gianna said: “The secret of happiness is to live moment by moment and to thank God for everything that he, in his goodness, sends us day after day.” That is trusting in Providence!

That is why, for me, in my modest opinion, the magnificent example of St. Gianna clearly teaches us a great truth of life, namely that the option for the good –the true exercise of our freedom– so very often brings with it suffering and the cross. Suffering that must be borne with serene acceptance, with trust in the Providence that knows how to draw good out of evil. And a cross that must be carried every day, whatever our vocation or our occupation in life may be. Because, as the life of St. Gianna shows, it is in the oblation of oneself that one becomes fruitful, that one bears fruit.

Hence the heroic example of love and fidelity of St. Gianna speaks eloquently to every person living in our tumultuous and confused age. As a daughter and sister in a family, she was a model of service and diligence in study. When she was young, she was kind, cheerful and sociable, but entirely devout and very active in the Catholic faith that she professed with such clarity. As a successful doctor, she showed how medical science serves the dignity of human life. As a wife and mother, she made Jesus the heart of the home, the strength of the family, the goal they were to reach. She also knew how to balance her busy schedule as a working mother while maintaining an ardent life of prayer, especially through daily Mass and Communion and the praying of her Rosary, also daily. In short, St. Gianna tells us: “When one fulfills one’s own duty, one must not worry, because God’s help will not be lacking.”

The luminous example of this wife and mother reminds us that “love is a light –in the end, the only one– that constantly illuminates a darkened world and gives us the strength to live and act”6. Let us always keep it in mind in our choices.

[Peroratio] St. Gianna was the last saint canonized by St. John Paul II. At the end of his canonization homily the Holy Father asked: “Let not your hearts be troubled or afraid7. The earthly vicissitudes through which the saint passed spur us to persevere on our way, trusting in God’s help and in the maternal protection of Mary”8. A request that we renew today before her mortal remains. We ask her to watch over each one of us from heaven and to sustain us with her powerful intercession so that we may always and everywhere choose the good, love, the Supreme Good.

A happy feast of St. Gianna Beretta Molla to all!
