Vow to St. Thomas Aquinas
Offering of a Votive Lamp at Fossanova

Make vows to the Lord your God, and fulfill them1, says the psalm.

That is why, on this magnificent and solemn occasion of the 744th anniversary of the death of the Angelic Doctor, we gather to make a promise under vow to St. Thomas Aquinas.

He himself taught us that “by a promise, [men] ordain what they are to do for the good of others”2. For “a promise proceeds from the purpose of doing something. And purpose requires prior deliberation, since it is an act of the deliberate will”3.

And so, today we wish to offer this votive lamp to the one who was acclaimed “Light of the Church and of the whole world”4, that it may hang above the altar of this chapel, built precisely on the spot that divine Providence chose for the Saint's passage to true life, and as a vow for the realization and the fruits of our Center of Higher Studies.

May this votive lamp remind us all, and serve as a testimony before others, that it is our purpose to work with untiring effort and in perfect harmony so that this Center of Higher Studies may be a place in which “each discipline is cultivated according to its own principles, its own methods and the freedom proper to scientific research, in such a way that the understanding of these disciplines may grow deeper each day, and that, by considering with all attention the problems and discoveries of recent times, it may be seen more exactly how faith and reason move harmoniously toward the truth, which is one, following the teachings of the doctors of the Church, but above all through the scientific study of the Angelic Doctor”5. For it is our deliberate intention “to face the challenges of the modern world in the light of essential Thomism” and thus to offer “a positive and effective answer to the great challenges, or even dramas, of man”6 today.

For we are convinced that the Gospel which the Incarnate Word came to bring us “was not meant to be kept hidden. [Rather,] it must be set on the lampstand, so that people may see its light and praise our heavenly Father (cf. Mt 5:15-16)”7. We ourselves are to work “not like the hireling who wants his wages after each day of work, nor like a servant who works for pay for a while in order later to secure an independent position, but serving Christ and the cause of the Gospel without pledges, without days off, without days of rest, without consolation and without glory. Like the candle that loves and is consumed before the Sacred Host without noise and without interruption, leaving behind not even ashes”8.

Thomas died here to become a greater light for the world, so that Fossanova might thus be a lampstand, a place renowned for this burning lamp that is not hidden. From now on may it be said that here St. Thomas dwells as in his own home9.

That is why today we light this votive lamp: so that the light of the Sun of Aquino10, who enlightened the Church of God more than any other doctor11, may radiate all its splendor from our house of studies; that it may make the light of the truth that the Incarnate Word came to teach us shine in souls (for He Himself told us: teach them to observe all that I have commanded you12) and may kindle in hearts the spark of ardent apostolic zeal (so necessary for the new evangelization), so that those who are formed here may go forth with the impetus of apostolic charity to carry the torch of the good news into every sphere of humanity and, by its influence, transform humanity itself from within13.

It is our fervent desire that, from now on, all the priests of the Institute of the Incarnate Word who offer the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass on this altar and in the light of this votive lamp do so for this twofold intention: First, in thanksgiving for the immense grace of Thomism, which was bequeathed in its integrity, and as an integral part of the charism of the Institute, to all the members of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word. Likewise, we desire that our priests, in celebrating Holy Mass, ask, through the intercession of St. Thomas Aquinas, who loved the Incarnate Word in the Eucharist so exquisitely, that Our Lord may be pleased to protect, accompany and bring to a successful conclusion all the efforts that are being made, and that thanks to his intercession will continue to be made, by the future members of the Institute, so that what is today the project of the Center of Higher Studies may soon become reality, be consolidated, grow and expand, for the glory of God and the good of souls.

Furthermore, since, as our Proper Law says, “after Jesus Christ no one does as much for the evangelization of culture as our Mother in heaven”14, from whom we trust to obtain the “indispensable help to prolong the Incarnation in all things”15, we commit ourselves to offer to the main Church of the Abbey an image of the Blessed Virgin Mary. And another image, that of the master of thought16 and doctor St. Thomas Aquinas, who loved the Truth disinterestedly, sought it wherever it might be manifested, and highlighted its universality17, allowing us to reach Him who perfects all things18. So that, interposing before God the most efficacious patronage of the Mother of the Incarnate Word and of the apostle of truth19, both may guard and keep unharmed our fidelity and loving veneration for the Supreme Truth, which is God Himself. For the world needs the God who became incarnate in the most pure womb of Mary and whom St. Thomas knew, loved and adored20.

May the Eternal Lord of all things accept this offering which, under vow, we His children of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, with His favor and help, before His infinite goodness and before our glorious Mother, and through the intercession of the august doctor St. Thomas Aquinas, present today as a pledge of our unending commitment to work with esprit de corps so that the Center of Higher Studies may be distinguished by solidity and thoroughness in the “scientific study of St. Thomas Aquinas, as theologian, philosopher and exegete, without forgetting or neglecting all the legitimate developments contributed by later theologians, philosophers and exegetes and, especially, the developments contributed with great clear-sightedness by the Roman See”21, remaining constantly “open to every particle of truth wherever it may be found”22 and always with the purpose of making “the Gospel penetrate the fabric of society, transforming people's hearts and the structures of society, in order to create a civilization of true justice and love”23 and making Christ reign24. For, as the Aquinate affirms: “He Himself is the whole good of the Church, and there is no greater good than He, nor are all together greater than He alone”25.

May the Incarnate Word Himself, who is at once the inspiration, motive and model of so colossal an undertaking, present our vow before the Eternal Father and help us to be faithful in the work that He Himself has begun.

Through Christ our Lord.

R./Amen.
