Our badge of honor: our belonging to the Church

Cf. Directory of Spirituality, 227

In one of their first paragraphs our Constitutions declare that “we want to be founded on Jesus Christ… because the rock is Christ1 and no one can lay any other foundation2. We want to love and serve Jesus Christ, and to make Him loved and served: His Body and His Spirit. Both the physical Body of Christ in the Eucharist, and the Mystical Body of Christ, which is the Church, formed by ourselves who, through holiness of life, must become ‘other Christs’, and by all men in whom we see Christ Himself, especially the poor, sinners and enemies. We want to be ‘like another humanity of His’3, we want to be chalices filled with Christ that pour out His superabundance upon others, we want to show with our lives that Christ lives. And the Spirit of Christ, because He is the soul of the Church and because if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Christ4”5. With this succession of “we want,” they make clear what the foundation of our Religious Family of the Incarnate Word is.

In full agreement and continuity with the above, the Directory of Spirituality states without hesitation that “we want to know nothing outside of Her”6 , that is, nothing outside the Church, since “Christ Himself is Incarnate in His Body, the Church”7. And we fervently desire that “our belonging to Her, through faith and Baptism, may always be our badge of honor”8.

In this month of May, in which the liturgy providentially sets before us the celebration of the Christ of the Quebrada, a date so significant for us since it is the day of the foundational grace, and a few weeks later the Solemnity of Pentecost, it seems to us that it may be of spiritual benefit for all the members of the Institute to contemplate once again the mystical Life of the Incarnate Word, that is, “the marvel of the Church, Body of Christ, nourished by the Word of God, One, Holy, Catholic –missionary and ecumenical–, Apostolic, enriched by and resting upon the three white things”9. A Church which, moreover, is the one who “by her authority, accepts and approves”10 the charism of an Institute precisely “for the building up of the Body of Christ”11.

It is our desire that the reading of these considerations may help to deepen and increase our faith in the mystical Life of the Incarnate Word, as we asked in the novena in preparation for the Annunciation of the Lord; and at the same time, that it may serve to delve more and more deeply into the beautiful texts that our law contains on this subject.

1. Christ, Head of the Church

As could not be otherwise, already in the first lines of our Constitutions we confess “Christ who ‘remains in the Catholic Church governed by the successor of Peter and the Bishops in communion with him’”12. Likewise “we confess the preeminence of Christ, even as man, over all creation. A primacy that Christ has over the souls and over the bodies of the members of His Mystical Body and also over all men of all times –He is Head of all– even of those not predestined, who will cease to be potential members of the Body of Christ only when they leave this world”13.

And without delay, the Directory of Spirituality goes on to say: “We confess that Christ is Head of the Church14 and of all men15, and that He has over all a threefold primacy: of order, of perfection and of power. He has priority of order, since by His nearness to God His grace is the highest and the first, though not in time; for all who receive grace receive it in relation to His: Those whom He foreknew He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, so that He might be the firstborn among many brothers16. He has priority of perfection, because He possesses the fullness of all graces: We have seen Him… full of grace and truth17. He has priority in power, since He has all power to communicate grace and glory to all the members of His Body: From His fullness we have all received, grace upon grace18”19. According to this last point, we could say that Christ also has primacy in interior influence. It is a physical, intimate, vital, mystical influence, analogically comparable to the influence of the vine upon its branches.

From which it follows that “absolutely the whole humanity of Christ, His body and His soul, has an influence on all the men and women of the world and on their bodies and souls, and in all times. All, because they belong to the Mystical Body, in fact (in act) or as a possibility (in potency), even the unbaptized, or pagans, or sinners, or… All! receive the influence of Christ, by means of actual graces. Neither the devil nor the Antichrist is the head of the wicked, properly speaking, since they can in no way exercise an interior influence. They exercise only an exterior influence: by means of temptations, bad examples, or infestations, or suggestions or possessions. Insofar as man freely wills to turn away from God through sin, he submits himself to the power of the devil. And only in this improper sense can the devil be called the head of the wicked”20.

And because of this threefold primacy of the Incarnate Word as Head of the Church, which is His Body, “the Church founded upon the rock cannot be destroyed”21. His Mystical Body will suffer wounds; there will be ‘scandals,’ just as He was a stone of stumbling, because if the human nature of Christ suffered rejection and ‘apparent defeat,’ why expect His Mystical Body to be exempt from it? Archbishop Fulton Sheen wrote in one of his books: “If He allowed thirst, pain and a death sentence to affect His physical Body, why would He not allow mystical and moral weaknesses such as loss of faith, sin, scandals, heresies, schisms and sacrileges to affect His Mystical Body? When all these things happen, it does not mean that the Mystical Body of the Church is not Divine in its innermost nature, any more than the Crucifixion of our Lord meant that Christ was not of divine nature”22. “The presence of the Holy Spirit in the Church means that she, although marked by the sin of her members, is preserved from defection”23.

St. John Paul II explained that the words of our Lord: Upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it24 “attest to Jesus’ will to build His Church, with an essential reference to the specific mission and power that He, in due time, will confer on Simon.” “The word rock –the Pope went on to say– expresses a permanent, subsisting being; consequently, it applies to the person [of Peter], rather than to an act of his, which is necessarily passing. The duration of the Church is bound to the rock. The Peter-Church relationship repeats in itself the bond between the Church and Christ. Jesus, in fact, says: ‘My Church.’ This means that the Church will always be the Church of Christ, the Church that belongs to Christ. She does not become the Church of Peter, but, as the Church of Christ, she is built upon Peter, who is Kephas in the name and by virtue of Christ”25.

In the face of the ups and downs of time and circumstances, we must always keep in mind that “our Lord is the Founder, the Head, the Sustainer and the Savior of this Mystical Body”26. Knowing how to give glory to God by trusting without limits in His Providence, which disposes all things for the good of those who love God27, and believing with unshakable firmness that even the most adverse events, those most opposed to our natural outlook, are ordered by God for our good, even if we do not understand His designs and do not know the end to which He wishes to lead us28.

Our proper law devotes many pages to developing the very profound theological theme of the mystical Life of the Incarnate Word, since from it spring principles essential to our spirituality. It is not our intention now to transcribe all that is said there –although we do warmly recommend reading it29– but rather we would like to point out some of those elements rooted in faith in the mystery of the mystical Life of Christ which have to do with our consecrated life and particular mission; since it was the Holy Spirit who granted a singular grace to our Founder so that through him our Religious Family might contribute to the building up of the Church according to its own particular way of living the religious life and the apostolate30.

2. Badge of honor

All the members of the Institute must be very clear that “just as the Word assumed a human nature to fulfill the plan of salvation, so, to prolong that plan throughout the ages, He chooses other human natures so that salvation may reach all men of all times. The Church is Jesus Christ continued, spread abroad and communicated; she is like the prolongation of the redemptive Incarnation, continuing the threefold office: prophetic, priestly and royal”31. Together with this, it is imperative to remember that our belonging to the Church, not only through Baptism but also through faith, must be our badge of honor. That is to say, we must stand out for our filial love and obedience to our Holy Mother the Church.

For it could not be otherwise, since faith in the Church is unfailingly united to faith in the mystery of the Incarnate Word. “For one cannot separate the Church from Christ, nor Christ from the Church”32.

We know well that the Holy Spirit, after the Resurrection and Ascension of Jesus into heaven, was sent to the Church33 precisely on the day of Pentecost. “Therefore the Holy Spirit is a gift given by God to the Church: you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit34, a gift that at the same time gives her life […] and gives her life to such a degree that He is rightly called ‘the Soul of the Church’”35. The Holy Spirit, then, plays a fundamental, essential, constitutive role in the Church. He is the source of the Church’s unity and the source of holiness; He is the pledge of eternal life: the Spirit of the promise, who is the pledge of our inheritance36; He is the one who helps us, since the Spirit comes to the aid of our weakness, for we do not know how to pray as we ought, but the Spirit Himself intercedes for us with inexpressible groanings37; He is the one who enriches the Church with the diversity of His gifts: all these things are worked by one and the same Spirit, who distributes to each one as He wills38. Note that “the Holy Spirit dwells in the Church, not as a guest who remains, in any case, a stranger, but as the soul that transforms the community into a holy temple of God39 and continually assimilates it to Himself by means of His specific gift, which is charity40”41.

For this reason, our proper law paternally exhorts us to ask “the Lord always to grant us His Holy Spirit, and docility to Him, so that we may belong more and more to Christ, and hence to His Mystical Body, since if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Christ42”43.

“The hierarchical and at the same time mystical, visible and spiritual, earthly and heavenly, canonical and charismatic, human and divine reality [of the Church], by a profound analogy ‘is compared to the Mystery of the Incarnate Word’44, since ‘Christ Himself is Incarnate in His Body, the Church’45. Therefore, we who are honored to call ourselves religious ‘of the Incarnate Word,’ would most gravely betray our charism if we did not work to have an authentic ecclesial spirituality, one that incorporates us fully into the Church of the Incarnate Word”46.

Therefore, we not only confess our faith in the Church as the manifestation of the Mystical Life of the Incarnate Word in time, but we seek to stand out for our belonging to Her with our whole life.

To look no further, our very consecration cannot be understood except in the Church, and for this reason “we desire to live in a state that ‘more closely imitates and perpetually represents in the Church that form of life which the Son of God chose when He came into the world…’”47. Thus “moved by charity… we want to live more and more for Christ and His Body, which is the Church”48.

The existence of our Institute and what guides its action is precisely its vital relationship with the Church, as our proper law clearly expresses throughout all its documents. Here we shall only point out some of the elements that make this evident:

▪ We are convinced that “only in the most absolute fidelity to the Holy Spirit can one skillfully wield the sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God49. Our poor breath is fruitful and irresistible only if it is in communication with the wind of Pentecost”50. And for this reason we cannot conceive of the reading of Sacred Scripture except as done ‘in the Church’51, for which we consider absolutely necessary the strictest fidelity to the supreme Magisterium of the Church of all times, the proximate norm of faith52. Indeed, for us “the Word of God must be ‘deepened in the Church’53, that is, with the same Spirit in which it was written54; only when understood in this way does the Word of God become the strength of our Institute55. Hence the formation we impart to those in formation “‘is based and built above all on the study of sacred doctrine and theology’56, which proceeds from faith and seeks to lead to it, as the Magisterium of the Church and Catholic theologians have always taught”57. Moreover, “the teachings of the Pastoral Constitution on the Church in the Modern World Gaudium et spes of the Second Vatican Council, the Apostolic Exhortations Evangelii nuntiandi and Catechesi tradendae, the address of Pope St. John Paul II to UNESCO and others on the same subject, the Puebla Document, the Encyclical Letter Slavorum Apostoli, the Encyclical Letter Redemptoris missio, the Post-Synodal Apostolic Exhortation Pastores dabo vobis, n. 55, c; and all the future directives, guidelines and teachings of the ordinary Magisterium of the Church that may be given concerning the specific end of our little Religious Family”58 constitute our rule of work in order to make a real contribution to the mission entrusted to us. For this reason we do not merely say it, but we take care and make it our concern “to be men docile to the great discipline of the Church, expressed in the Code of Canon Law, in all the other ecclesial norms and laws, and docile to the particular discipline of our Institute”59.

▪ In turn, our Constitutions point out that “the spirit of our Religious Family wants to be none other than the Holy Spirit, and if it degenerates into another, from now on and from wherever we may be, we pledge our supplication that the Lord may blot it out from the face of the Church”60.

All the members of the Institute –whether brothers, seminarians, priests, monks, novices– form in the Church one and the same Congregation, regardless of their place of origin or the place where they are on mission, of how many years they have been in religious life, of the academic degrees they may or may not have, of the offices they hold, etc. We all have the same rule, the same charism, the same spiritual patrimony; we all have the same mission, that is, “to work, in utmost docility to the Holy Spirit and within the imprint of Mary, in order to claim for Jesus Christ all that is authentically human, even in the most difficult situations and in the most adverse conditions”61. For this reason an intense fraternal love must unite us, today and always, in our vocation.

The Holy Spirit, “Soul of the Church,” is a spirit of charity, and our proper law sets out countless provisions so that we may live according to that spirit as an integral and essential part of our fidelity to the charism and to the very vocation received within the Church. For example, we are told, and here we would like to emphasize it, that “we must strive, by every means, that ‘no one be disturbed or saddened in the house of God’62. For this it is absolutely indispensable to live fraternal charity: ‘That is: let them consider others more worthy63; let them bear with boundless patience one another’s weaknesses, both of body and of spirit; let them strive with all their effort to obey one another; let all seek the good of others rather than their own; let them put into practice a sincere fraternal love; let them live always in the fear and love of God; let them love their Abbot [Superior] with sincere and humble charity; let them prefer absolutely nothing to Christ, and may He bring us all together to eternal life’64”65.

Without charity toward our brothers, whether they be peers, subjects or superiors, our confession of faith in the mystical Life of the Incarnate Word, which is prolonged in every soul in grace, is of no use whatsoever. However much such a person may have read all the documents of the Magisterium, however many years he may spend as a missionary in distant countries or sacrifice himself in the silence of the cloister, however much he may call himself a most obedient son of the Church, however much he may call himself very faithful to the charism of the Institute and appear outwardly to follow all its rules… Because whoever says he loves God and does not love his brothers is a liar66. What is more, such a person “even if he is with us in body, does not belong to our spiritual family”67, since he betrays or departs gravely from the spirit of our Religious Family, which is the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of Charity.

▪ Being “essentially missionaries”68, we members of the Institute, as faithful sons of the Church, are dedicated by vocation to the spreading of the Gospel. The Holy Spirit is the one who guides our discernment of reality so as to interpret the signs of the times and know how to respond with the zeal of the saints69 to the missionary needs of the Church today.

What is more, “we will carry out all our missionary undertakings in communion with the Church, aware that it belongs to the missionary Dicastery to direct and coordinate throughout the world the work of the Evangelization of peoples and missionary cooperation”70.

In view of this, our major Seminaries prepare future priests by the Church and for the Church71: therefore, the pastoral formation of the candidates strives to make them know and live the ecclesial dimensions72, inviting them to open their minds and hearts, and to make themselves available for every opportunity offered them to proclaim the Gospel73. This formation also seeks to ensure that all our candidates are ready to be sent to preach the Gospel in any part of the world74 and to “take an interest ‘not only in the particular Church… but also in the universal Church’”75. Because we are “convinced that the missionary dimension of ecclesial life is not something that depends simply on personal generosity, but belongs to the very nature of the Church76 and of the ministerial priesthood”77, we give great importance to our seminarians being filled “with so Catholic a spirit that they grow accustomed to going beyond the limits of their own diocese or nation or rite and to helping meet the needs of the whole Church, ready to preach the Gospel everywhere”78.

Proof of this is that our missionaries dedicate all their strength to the service of the great work of establishing and extending the kingdom of Christ in the world, and are today found in 44 countries carrying out a great variety of apostolic works. And since “the love of God, of the Church and of souls imposes on us the apostolic work of vocations”79, because the matter of vocations “affects the whole Church in one of her fundamental marks, which is her apostolicity”80, at present we can give thanks to God that, in the course of our short history as a religious and missionary Institute, He has blessed us with numerous native vocations from the places where we are on mission and even from countries where we are not yet present. In fact, our numerous vocations in formation come from 45 different countries. For as St. John Paul II rightly said: “Vocations to the priesthood and to consecrated life are a sure sign of the vitality of a Church”81. Deo gratias!

▪ The mystical Life of the Incarnate Word is enriched by and rests upon the three white things82: the Eucharist, the Blessed Virgin Mary and the Pope. “The Church is the Body of Christ. This means that the Eucharist, in which the Lord gives His Body and transforms us into one Body83, is the place where the Church permanently expresses herself in her most essential form”84. In this sense, our proper law points out that “the oneness and indivisibility of the Eucharistic Body of the Lord implies the oneness and indivisibility of His Mystical Body. From each Eucharist arises the self-giving to the Lord by being inserted into His one and undivided Body”85.

Consequently, by the spirit of faith that animates us and that has been handed down to us, we have as our motto “with Peter and under Peter”86. For “the unity of the Eucharist and the unity of the Episcopate… are not independent roots of the unity of the Church because, by institution of Christ Himself, Eucharist and Episcopate are essentially linked realities87”88.

This being so, we always find the deepest foundation of our unity as a Religious Family in the Eucharist89. The Eucharist is one of our great loves; it is the principal thing, the most important thing we must do each day90. A sign of this is the great importance and the great care we put into the celebration of Holy Mass in our religious and missionary communities, since wherever we go the Sunday Eucharist especially is central91. From there flows our pastoral ministry of holiness as the source and means for establishing a truly Christian culture.

▪ “Because the Church is the ‘universal Sacrament of salvation’92, she is open to the missionary and ecumenical dynamic, and not turned in on herself, since she has been sent to proclaim and bear witness to, to make present and to extend the mystery of communion that constitutes her; to gather all people and all things in Christ93. For this reason, in the image of the Church, our little Religious Family never wants to be turned in on itself, but open like the arms of Christ on the Cross, whose arms were wrenched out of joint from opening them so wide in love”94. Consequently, all the members of the Institute make their own the ecumenical cause of the Church, for it is our determination “to work with all our strength to build up our life in union with the legitimate Pastors, and most especially with a heartfelt adherence to the Bishop of Rome, thus showing forth the one Church:

– So that all Christians may come to perfect unity, that the promise and prophecy of the Lord may be fulfilled: There will be one flock and one Shepherd (Jn 10:16), and that His prayer may bear fruit: Father, that they all may be one (Jn 17:21). This is the ecumenical work.

– So that all men may confess the adorable Name of the Lord Jesus, fulfilling His commandment: Go into all the world and preach the Gospel to every creature (Mk 16:15). This is the missionary work. Not forgetting that: ‘it is truth, more than anything, that builds unity: the communion of minds easily becomes a union of hearts…’95”96.

It is our mission, therefore, to remain “open to every particle of truth wherever it may be found”97 in order to discover “the seeds of the Word,” with joy and respect, so as to seek to awaken in men a more ardent desire for truth and charity98.

Without forgetting that there are countless men and women who have not yet heard the Name of Jesus and to whom the immense gift of salvation has not yet been offered, each day we must devote ourselves to the explicit proclamation of Jesus as Lord, without which there can be no true evangelization. At the same time, inculturation and interreligious dialogue play an important role in several places where we are present; there our missionaries, in full communion with the Church of Christ, bring the truth of the redemption that God accomplished in Jesus. Let us add here that a serious and open dialogue with cultures and religions should not be considered as opposed to the mission ad gentes, but as “part of the evangelizing mission of the Church”99. This requires on the part of our missionaries serious personal preparation, mature gifts of discernment, and fidelity to the indispensable criteria of doctrinal orthodoxy, moral integrity and ecclesial communion100. “It must never be forgotten that dialogue can deepen and purify the Catholic faith, but it cannot change it”101, since “the unity that ecumenism seeks not only cannot do without the truth, but is unity in the very truth”102.

▪ Our Constitutions state that the Mystical Body of Christ, which is the Church, [is] formed by ourselves who, through holiness of life, must become ‘other Christs’103. It is therefore natural that our proper law should exhort us to be formed in virtue according to “the doctrine of the great masters of the spiritual life… and of all the saints of all times whom the Church proposes as exemplars so that we may imitate their virtues. In such a way that, by following the Pope in doctrine and the saints in life, we shall never go wrong, since the Pope cannot err in the teachings of faith and morals, nor did the saints err in the practice of the virtues”104. Moreover, the Blessed Virgin, most eminent member of the Mystical Body of Christ, is another of our great loves because of “her union with Christ and with the Church. Because she begot us, the members, together with the Head. Because she was given to us as Mother”105. For “one cannot speak of the Church unless Mary is present… The Marian dimension is essential to the one Church of Christ, just as the Eucharistic dimension and the Petrine dimension are essential to it”106. Indeed, Mary is for us the model of ecclesial communion and the image and beginning of the Church107, and we firmly believe that, just as she was in the midst of the Apostles, so she is in our midst and in the midst of the Church of all times108.

Likewise, in our task of proclaiming the Gospel, from the very beginnings of the Institute we have been taught always to do so “with the fervor and enthusiasm of the saints, even in moments of difficulty and persecution in a de-Christianized and atheistic world”109. Because “the saints are a most eloquent sign of the vitality of the Church; they have always transformed the world. They have been the true reformers of the world and of the Church. They are the best members of the Mystical Body of Christ. They are the greatest and most complete fruit of the Incarnation and the Redemption. The saints have been the outstanding witnesses and protectors of the divine Tradition of the Church, that is, they recall and transmit with their lives the very breath of the Church. They have known how to overcome all the obstacles that stood against Evangelization, and therefore they are models to follow and to whom we must turn in the work of Evangelization”110.

▪ “Our third great love [is and] must always be the white figure of the Pope: ‘Where Peter is, there is the Church’” 111. For we understand that “the idea of a Body of Churches calls for the existence of a Head of the Churches, which is precisely the Church of Rome”112. Thus, then, we members of the Institute, wherever we may be, strive to give “witness of a firm and convinced communion in filial relationship with the Pope, perpetual and visible center of unity of the universal Church, and with the bishop, ‘visible principle and foundation of unity’ in the particular Church, and in the ‘mutual esteem among all forms of apostolate in the Church’”113. Consequently, “we make our own the teaching of St. Ignatius of Loyola: ‘To be right in everything, we must always hold that the white I see, I will believe to be black, if the hierarchical Church so determines’114”115. Because “to be with the Pope in everything means to be with God in everything; to love Jesus Christ and to love the Pope is the same love”116, since “… to love the Pope, to love the Church, is to love Jesus Christ”117.

Persuaded that “the Pope must be loved on the cross; and whoever does not love him on the cross does not truly love him”118, our proper law makes its own the expression of St. Luigi Orione, who says: “We must annihilate ourselves at the feet of the Church and of the Superiors and obey out of love for Christ and be like doormats… let no one ever surpass us in filial obedience, in deference and love for the Pope and the Bishops, whom the Holy Spirit has placed to govern the Church of God”119.

3. To enrich His one Church

From Pentecost to our own day, the Holy Spirit pours out His gifts in a great multiplicity of forms in order to enrich with them His one Church, which, in her varied beauty, unfolds in history the unsearchable riches of Christ120. In any case, it is always a divine gift, fundamentally one, even within the multiplicity and variety of the spiritual gifts, or charisms, granted to persons and communities121.

Among the immensity of gifts with which God is pleased to adorn His Church are the charisms granted to men and women destined to found ecclesial works and especially religious institutes, which receive their character from the charisms of their founders, live and act under their influence and, to the extent of their fidelity, receive new gifts122, as St. John Paul II explained. In fact, the Council observes that “the Church willingly received and approved with her authority” the religious families123; for it belongs to her above all not to quench the Spirit, but to test everything and hold fast to what is good124. That “is the reason why the Church defends and supports the proper character of the various religious institutes”125 .

Hence “a true religious keeps faith with and shows great love not only for the gift of religious life but also for his own institute, which includes in a particular way “the mind and purpose of the Founder”126 and the Constitutions”127, since ultimately it is a matter of being faithful to the gift of God and to His Church, which has raised up our Religious Family for a specific ecclesial service: the evangelization of culture.

Since the Church canonically erected our Institute and approved its Constitutions on that May 8, 2004, there is a particular reciprocal bond between the two, for from that moment our Institute became part of the spiritual and apostolic patrimony of the Church128.

For this reason, as we draw very near to celebrating the day of the foundational grace, and within the ecclesial context we have been discussing, it seems fitting to us to quote once again that paternal warning which the Spiritual Father of our Institute gave us, saying: “at times one finds today a prejudice according to which the ‘differences’ that characterize and distinguish religious institutes from one another should be eliminated. Each institute must take care to maintain its own ‘physiognomy,’ the specific character of its own reason for being, which has exercised an attraction, which has given rise to vocations and particular attitudes, giving a public witness worthy of esteem. It is naive and presumptuous to believe, in the end, that each institute must be the same as all the others by practicing a general love of God and neighbor. Whoever thought this way would forget an essential aspect of the Mystical Body: the heterogeneity of its constitution, the pluralism of models in which the vitality of the spirit that animates it is manifested, the transcendent human and divine perfection of Christ, its Head, which can only be imitated according to the innumerable resources of the soul animated by grace”129.

Aware of the great importance of our specific vocation, both for our Religious Family and for the whole Church, let us come to know, deepen, live with conviction and transmit the proper spirituality of the Institute to the whole Church.

*****

As we said at the beginning, this month of May that we begin today is strewn with dates very significant for our Institute: May 3, the day of the foundational grace; May 8, the solemnity of Our Lady of Luján –Queen of the Institute–; and May 23, the solemnity of Pentecost, the day on which the Church was born. All of them invite us to pause and celebrate the gift that the Holy Spirit has granted to our Institute in relation to the growing needs of the Church and of the world. Let us know how to recognize in the magnificent charism and spiritual patrimony of our Institute one of the clearest signs of divine generosity, which has inspired and continues to spur the generosity of so many souls. We must truly rejoice at this fact, since it shows that among men the sense of service to the kingdom of God and to the growth of the Church is broadening and deepening.

Let us have confidence in the One who has called us, the Incarnate Word Himself, Head of the Church. He has entrusted to us the wonderful gift of this special vocation for the sake of the whole Church, so that we may go and bear fruit, fruit that will last130. Let us have confidence in God who is able to make us abound copiously, far more than we ask or think, by the power at work within us; to Him be glory in the Church and in Christ Jesus, in all generations, for ever and ever. Amen131.

May Mary Most Holy, Mother of the Church, whom we will celebrate with great solemnity this coming May 8, always bless and protect the life of our Institute. Through her intercession may the Lord grant us His Holy Spirit, and docility to Him, so that we may belong more and more to Christ132, and hence to His Mystical Body, and may we “sing forever the mercies of God”133.
