[Exordium] In 1982 the spiritual father of our Religious Family visited Spain, and there, before more than a thousand religious of different congregations, he said to them: “The religious is not called to work as an isolated person or on his own. Today more than ever it is necessary to live and work united, first within each religious family and then collaborating with other consecrated persons and members of the Church”1.

Since today I have the joy of celebrating this Mass together with all the members of the Religious Family in this beloved country, it seems important to me –above all because of the special circumstances we are going through– that those words of St. John Paul II find an echo in the depths of our souls: Today more than ever it is necessary to live and work united,first –says the Holy Father– within each Religious Family.

Working united

This, although it is a watchword common to all religious, is something fundamental for us. Because we have been conceived in the Church and for the Church as a Religious Family. And how is it manifested that we form a Religious Family? The Constitutions say it very clearly: in that “we profess public vows and live fraternal life in common”2; that is at the level of each Institute. But in turn, both the Institute of the Servants and the Institute of the Incarnate Word form one and the same Religious Family within the Church3. And therefore, although our Institutes are juridically independent, they have the same spirit, the same charism, the same Constitutions, the same founder, the same mission in the Church. That is why they are “indissolubly united”4, as the Constitutions themselves express very well. Moreover, this includes the lay people of the Third Order who live according to the genuine spirit of our Religious Family.

Hence anyone who seeks to sow division –whether from outside or from within– and in whatever way, attacks one of the fundamental marks of our Institutes and, consequently, blows up all the potential, or at least seriously hinders the whole evangelizing force that could be had by working united. Because we were conceived to function as a Family. God took us out of our families, but He planted us in another Family, and that Family is made up of the priests of the Incarnate Word and the Servant Sisters. It is what the Pope said: no one has been called to work as an isolated person or on his own. And what is said of a religious, individually speaking, applies analogously to our Institutes.

That is why, if division is a great obstacle to evangelization, working in unity gives our mission a strength, a vitality, an effectiveness it would not otherwise have. In my visits to the various missions I have verified that, even though adequate means may be lacking, even though progress may come in very small steps, even though all the work may be very sacrificial, even though the place where one is assigned to do missionary work may be very adverse… if one works in unity, according to the spirit of the Institute, that already bears incalculable fruit, and one can say with complete certainty that a great work of evangelization is being done, even if perhaps the fruits are not as palpably noticeable as we would like. But certainly the Gospel of Christ is being preached to others –and with great eloquence– even without preaching. And that is what I have been able to see here.

When we accepted our Lord’s call and decided to consecrate ourselves in our Institutes, we not only took the charism and the spirituality as our own, but we accepted our Religious Family as the framework in which our life offered to God and our mission would unfold. It is part of the deal we made when we took our vows, so to speak.

We are children of this beloved Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, and founded on Him we form “a single whole, a compact bloc”5, as our proper law expresses very well. And it is necessary to maintain that unity, to foster it, to defend it and to know how to make the most of it.

We will fulfill our mission much better if we are imbued with this filial spirit toward the Institute and this fraternal spirit among ourselves.

Fraternal charity, the distinctive mark of our identity

In the world in which we live, afflicted by indifference, by divisions, by conflicts in families, unity among brothers, rooted and founded in charity, is an eloquent example for all, which silently preaches the charity of Christ. What attracts many people to our Religious Family is precisely that: the family spirit.

“To live fraternal charity is an imperative of the Incarnate Word; He it was who raised fraternal love to a sublime degree when He left us the commandment of love as His testament: I give you a new commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you6; this is my precept, that you love one another as I have loved you7”8. If one makes the effort to accept one another and strives to overcome the typical difficulties of heterogeneous communities, by that very fact one is showing the transcendence of the motive that has brought us together, that is, God. “In the community –in the mission– we are together not because we have chosen one another, but because we have been chosen by Christ”9.

This commandment of charity that Christ left us, although it is common to all Christians, is very important for us as religious of the Incarnate Word, and even for the laity: “it is on this point that one’s identity as a member of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word is at stake in a most particular way, because only through the effective practice of charity can they be called disciples of Him who said: by this they will know that you are my disciples: if you have love for one another10”11.

“Without charity we are nothing; we may build buildings, schools, colleges, orphanages, we may even form great groups and movements, but if the genuine spirit of Christian charity is lacking in these works, all of them would be in vain, and in time they would end up collapsing like a house of cards, and they would be worth nothing”12, because charity is the life of the soul, and therefore we are dead if we are not led by the love of God, which is identified with love for our brothers. And that is why, where there is charity, even if one is lost in an insignificant little town, even if apparently no one notices our good works, even if one barely manages to get support, even if everything is rowing against the current and in time weariness begins to make itself felt, everything bears much fruit, very much fruit. “For there is no better witness than that of Christian charity. In fact, a fraternal life lived as it should be necessarily attracts well-disposed people, and it is very likely that God will bless that witness with even unexpected fruits”13. And that shows. It shows in the kind way people treat you; it shows in the fact that people support the initiatives of the Institute as far as they are able; it shows in that they turn to the religious to tell them their hardships; it shows also in that the apostolate keeps growing, and there is more joy in the religious and more fervor in everyone… and there is a longing to do more and better. And all of this is very good, and I congratulate you. Perhaps here you do not realize it, you take it for granted, but this does not happen everywhere, and it is beautiful to see how over these years the mission of the Religious Family has been growing more solid here in Chile, because there is growth in charity, in fidelity to the charism of the Institute, and so vocations are arising, and families and young people are drawing near… truly a grace of God.

Notice that the Constitutions and the Directory of Spirituality name some 5 things that are indispensable for us: the help of Our Lady for evangelization; to unite oneself to the adorable Person of Christ; that for persecution to be blessed it has to be for Christ’s sake, and what is said about us must be false; but also two of those 5 things refer to charity: thus, when speaking of joy, one reads in the Constitutions: “it is absolutely indispensable to live fraternal charity”14, and when speaking of the apostolate it says: “charity is indispensable to evangelize the culture”15.

This charity, it is understood, is not only that which is practiced by the fathers who work in the home, nor is it limited only to when food is distributed, but, as our proper law says in one of its documents: “True charity [is that] which seeks the good of the other even at the cost of one’s own harm, which sees to it that others are well before one’s own good, which tries to make others happy more than oneself, which has its eyes set more on one’s neighbor, in whom it contemplates the presence of God, than on personal comforts”16. And that is something we can all practice, every day.

[Peroration] That is why I encourage you to continue always working united, taking special care of charity among yourselves, which is a treasure. May your communities always be an oasis of peace and welcome, without anyone being marginalized, generously accepting the daily renunciations, helping one another to carry your burdens. Which means, as St. John Paul II said, “to accept with benevolence the real or apparent defects of others, even when they are annoying, and to accept gladly all the sacrifices imposed by living with those whose mentality and temperament do not fully agree with our own way of seeing or judging”17. All that charity, practiced in silence, firmly founded on charity toward Christ, before which nothing is to be preferred, is then translated into great strength for the apostolate, into a defense for the very vocation of each one of you, and into a great consolation for the Heart of Christ.

Let us ask the Incarnate Word in this Holy Mass to grant these three graces to the Religious Family:

  • That of always living and working united;

  • That of conducting ourselves with esprit de corps, that is, as “a single whole, a compact bloc”;

  • The increase in us of the charity that makes us give ourselves more each day in the apostolate.

We ask this through the intercession of the Most Blessed Virgin of Mount Carmel, patroness of the Chilean people.
