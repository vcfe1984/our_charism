Christmas Eve Mass, December 24, 2018

Lk 2:1-14 – Preface of Christmas

In the first reading we have just heard, the Prophet Isaiah speaks to us of a people that walked in darkness … [that bore a] heavy yoke, and whose shoulders were wounded by a rod1. And that people, with all its darkness and its pain, on a night like this one, saw a great light and its joy was made great2.

So it is: to a depressed, despairing and distrustful world God came3. And he came to order men and the world according to God, to order not only their hearts and their souls but also their business dealings, their family life, their governments and everything.

How did he do it? God solved the social, political, family and economic problems of the world not by promulgating a new economic system; not by showing us a mountain of papers with the results of his research revealing new aspects of the problem; he did not do it with wars. He saved the world from its evils by being born as a Child in the insignificant village of Bethlehem.

That apparently trivial incident, so common and so ordinary, in which –as we heard in the Gospel– the owners of the inn turned the Holy Family away, simply telling them that there was no room for them in the inn, is the event that turned the order of the world upside down and the solution that gave it its peace.

The Evangelist tells us that his Mother sought refuge in a manger and laid the newborn Child on the ground of this world. And this Child –whose birth we celebrate today– like another Samson shook the pillars of the world from its very roots, pulled down the edifice that was already collapsing and built the Temple of the living God, where men will once again be able to sing because they have found their God.

But someone may ask: what does the birth of God as a Child have to do with the social, political and economic conditions of his time and of our time? What relationship can there be between the Child lying on the straw of a manger and Caesar on his golden throne? The answer is this: the birth of the Son of God in the flesh was the introduction into the historical world order of a new life; it was the proclamation to the world that the rebuilding of society has to do with its spiritual regeneration; that nations can be saved only when men are reborn to God as God is born tonight to men.

By becoming man, God enters the created order, becomes part of its history, draws close to us and gives us a strength that comes from on high. Our Proper Law cites in this regard a phrase of St. John Paul II that says: “God was never so close to man -and man was never so close to God- as precisely at that moment”4. Put in other words, “The Savior… willed to become Son of Man so that we might become children of God”5. That is why Christ is born as a Child: to teach us that liberation from all the economic, social and political chaos can be achieved only by a birth.

Humanity was mentally weary and spiritually exhausted; for four thousand years it had been conducting the experiment of humanism, and it found itself like a sick man who cannot cure himself. It was in a state like that of our world, which since the days of the Renaissance has tried to build a civilization on the self-sufficiency of man without God.

Let us realize that humanity, left thus to itself, sinks and becomes little more than a beast. We have sufficient proof that people of an advanced culture can end up as a savage culture… (if not, watch the news every night).

Without supernatural help, society goes from bad to worse until its deterioration becomes universal. Not evolution, but regression is the law of man without God, just as it is the law of the sunflower without the sun.

Humanity left to itself cannot even tie its own shoelaces. With all the knowledge man has today about chemistry, about genetics… we cannot make a human life in a laboratory, because we lack the life-giving principle that is the soul, which comes only from God. Life is not a push from below, but a gift from above. It is not the result of man’s necessary ascent, but of God’s descent. It is not ‘progress”; it is the fruit of the Incarnation.

Therefore, like that world into which Christ was born, the world of today also needs not a mixture of old ideas touched up, not a new economic system, not a new monetary system –it needs a New Birth. It needs the entry into its order of a new life and a new spirit, which only God can give. We cannot give ourselves that New Birth, just as none of us can be born again naturally. If we are to be reborn to this new life, the principle of regeneration must come from heaven, and that is precisely the meaning of the Incarnation: the introduction into the world, at the level of human life, of the Life of God, who came not to judge the world but to save it. And that is why I say that the Incarnate Word solved our problems by being born as a Child, because the regeneration of society has to do with being born, being born to the life of grace and growing according to it.

What God did to his individual human nature –which he took from the Blessed Virgin Mary– is what he wishes to do, in no lesser degree, to every human nature in the world; that is, to make us partakers of the divine life. The Incarnate Word, who is from all eternity, willed to be born in Bethlehem on a night like tonight; he wants us, and all the men and women out there, who were born of our earthly parents, to be born to the eternity of our Heavenly Father, made new creatures, possessed by the new life of his grace and members of his Kingdom.

The same divine life that came into the world two thousand eighteen years ago must return once again into the course of the world. And unless the world is reborn to the new life, it will perish.

And hence the most important and leading role of our monastic branch, of the monks of the Institute of the Incarnate Word: to implore heaven that there may descend upon souls –as the dew descends every morning upon the field– that divine life which makes souls fruitful.

That is why the monastic rule says that our contemplatives: “Will pray and offer penances for the souls in Purgatory, for ecumenism, for the life of the Church, for human promotion, and for other problems that pertain to the realization of the temporal order according to God and to the establishment of the Kingdom of God in souls”6. This is the great need.

“At present there is a great famine in the land, a famine not of bread, because we have already had too much of that and our luxury has made us forget God; a famine not of gold, because the glitter of so much of it has blinded us to the meaning of the twinkling of the stars; but a much more serious famine, and one that threatens almost every country in the world: the famine of truly great men. In other words, the world of today is suffering the terrible nemesis of mediocrity. We are dying of commonness; we are perishing through our pettiness.

The greatest need of the world is for great men, someone who understands that there is no greater conquest than victory over oneself; someone who realizes that real worth is attained not so much through activity as through silence; someone who seeks the Kingdom of God and his justice, and puts into practice the law that only by dying to the life of the body do we live for the life of the spirit; someone who will defy the mockery of Good Friday in order to win the joy of Easter Sunday; who, like a flash of lightning, will burn through the bonds of those feeble interests that tie up the energies of the world; who, with a fearless voice, like another John the Baptist, will rouse our weakened nature from that elegant sleep of cowardly repose; the need of someone who will win victories not by coming down from the Cross and compromising with the world, but who will suffer in order to conquer the world”7.

This Christmas, in a word, what we need are holy (monks), because saints are the truly great men the world needs to obtain for us the precious divine gift of his grace and mercy. Monks who are –as today’s second reading says– fervently devoted to doing good8. Monks who show us that grace which has been manifested to the world and which is Jesus himself, born of the Virgin Mary, true God and true man; who has come into our history to free us from darkness and give us light9. Monks who, in short, make us experience that great joy which the shepherds experienced on having found Jesus, who is Love made flesh.

We ask this grace of the Mother of the Incarnate Word, who contemplated the Messiah in her Son wrapped in swaddling clothes.
