Talk to the Seminarians of the Mother Mary of the Incarnate Word Seminary

[Exordium] Dear all: Since this talk is addressed especially to you, seminarians, I want to tell you that I am very happy to be among you. It gives me great joy to see how Christ’s call bears fruit in your souls. And for that reason I cannot –we cannot– fail to thank the Incarnate Word for the gift of your vocation for our beloved Institute. How can one not rejoice, having before one the promise of future priests, of future missionaries with whom God blesses us? That, first of all.

Now then, for this meeting here with you, I thought it would be very profitable to reflect on the importance of our intellectual formation as part of that ‘configuration with Christ’ to which we are called and to which we solemnly committed ourselves on the day of our religious profession.

The secret of our formation lies in configuration with Christ. The Venerable Servant of God, Archbishop Fulton Sheen, used to say: “seminarians usually say: ‘I am studying for the priesthood.’ But how often does the seminarian say, or even think: ‘I am studying to be a Priest-Victim’?… And yet, that is precisely what we do. That is what you are doing here in these years of Seminary.

And within this process of configuration with Christ, the seminary offers an irreplaceable help, because it is here that the foundations of the future ministry are laid.

It is necessary that you, and all those who are in the houses of formation preparing to carry out great works for the glory of God in the missions to which they will be sent, have a very clear grasp of the doctrine of Our Lord, the Truth planted in the mind and in the heart, so as not to be deceived by vain fantasies.

The Church and the people expect missionary priests to possess a fervent devotion to the truth1. A truth that must be transmitted even at the cost of renunciations and sacrifices, bearing witness to the Truth with one’s own life. Our beloved John Paul II wrote in his book “Rise, Let Us Be on Our Way!”: “One cannot turn one’s back on the truth, stop proclaiming it, hide it […] One must bear witness to the truth, even at the price of being persecuted, even at the cost of one’s blood”2.

1. The importance of good formation

That is why it seems to me that it is always worthwhile to reflect on the importance of our formation; for it is a reality, a fact, that many aspects of what we will do in the future as religious and missionaries, in the various posts where God calls us to serve Him –whether in the cloister or in mission lands–, will be marked in some way by the seriousness, the integrity, the solidity and the depth of the formation imparted in our classrooms. Because our intellectual formation is imparted with a marked pastoral perspective and “with the intention of disposing them –you, seminarians– to carry out in the future, and competently, the ministries proper to a pastor: preaching, catechesis, teaching, counsel, spiritual direction, the wise discernment of God’s will in the lives of men, etc. This pastoral dimension of study certainly requires particular attention to the problems of today’s world. For the priest must be sensitive to all that happens around him, to the cultural movements of his age, to the currents of thought. Only thus will he be able to illuminate, from Christian revelation, the problems that concern man, bringing the truth that comes from Jesus Christ”3.

That is why on this occasion I would like briefly to highlight the great importance for our times, because of the heavenly fruits it produces, of the study of the doctrine of St. Thomas, which in the words of Pope Urban V is “true and Catholic”4 and which “has over all others –added Innocent VI– propriety of words, order in its matters, truth in its judgments, in such a way that those who follow it will never be seen to stray from the path of truth, and whoever attacks it will always be suspected of error”5. A doctrine that becomes key when it comes to evangelizing the modern world, and that appears more necessary and timely than ever for the times in which we are given to live.

2. The world we are called to evangelize

The world in which we live… The Venerable Servant of God, Archbishop Fulton Sheen, in the prologue of his book “Religion Without God” describes with clairvoyance the religious panorama we face today: the religious panorama of the contemporary world. He says there, for example, that in today’s world “the word ‘God’ is still used by certain thinkers, but it is emptied of all its content and becomes diluted and diffuse so that it may be adapted to signify any idea, however volatile, or any kind of insubstantial fantasy”. He characterizes modern thought as a thought in which “God has been dethroned, the heavens have been abandoned and man has been put in the place of God[…]”.

Thus the Venerable Archbishop formulated the very thing that our Cornelio Fabro would later denounce systematically and brilliantly in his studies, in his courses and in his books: that is, the primacy of immanence and its baneful consequence, atheism, a world without God.

Msgr. Fulton Sheen continues his “description” of our world, saying that: “the problems that once centered on God are now solved in terms of man, and those that once referred to man are now merged with the whole universe. […] God is humanized, and man naturalized. Physical science […] comes to tell us what God is and what man is.” And he goes on to say: “Man now not only imagines himself to be God, but really believes he is beginning to become so. And thus man, raised to this state they call ‘supernatural’, has no need of a redeemer; just as in the natural state he has no need of God. As a result of this philosophy of self-sufficiency there arises the bizarre phenomenon proper to our times, of a religion without God and a Christianity without Jesus Christ”. The general principle implicit in all this is ultimately the denial of the transcendent and the affirmation of the immanent6.

From which follow the nefarious errors we must live alongside. And thus –says Fr. Alfredo Sáenz– preeminence is given “instead of to God, to man… instead of to the love of God, to love of neighbor… instead of a message of salvation, [one speaks of] a social message… instead of the Cross, of openness to the world… instead of absolute truth, of the truth of the times”7, “faith is dissolved into the rational, the sacred becomes profane”8, and so there is an attempt to accommodate the Church to the modern world, to group Catholicism with Protestantism; in the end theology and philosophy are confused with sociology and psychology9, and there is no longer talk of the slavery of sin, of the reality of hell, etc., all of which is consistent with immanence, since thus, under these presuppositions, as Fr. Cornelio Fabro said, “without hell, existence becomes a picnic”10.

3. Our preparation to evangelize the world in which we live

Our Proper Law says that it is to this world that God sends us as lambs in the midst of wolves in order to transform “the wolves themselves into lambs”11; to seek and to know how to identify the “seeds of the Word” through which the Holy Spirit continues to act in the heart of man12. To be the salt and light of the world13.

This magnificent undertaking requires of us –who are essentially missionaries– a competent philosophical preparation, which above all gives us “a solid and coherent knowledge of man, of the world and of God”14, for it represents “an indispensable help for deepening the understanding of the faith and communicating the truth of the Gospel to all those who do not yet know it”15. It is in this sense that our Constitutions stress the urgency of its study16, since it is sound philosophy, the metaphysics of being, that gives us a “certainty of the truth”, and “without it there is no basis for a total personal self-giving to Jesus and to the Church, since ‘if one is not sure of the truth, how can we stake our own life and have the strength to challenge seriously the lives of others?’”17

In our intellectual formation we must imbue ourselves with the Truth, which is nothing other than conforming our mind to the mind, to the criteria of judgment, of the Incarnate Word Himself, and which is a most important part of our ‘configuration with Christ’. From there, “we must proclaim the Gospel with the fervor and enthusiasm of the saints, even in moments of difficulty and persecution in a de-Christianized and atheistic world”18, says the Directory of Missions Ad Gentes. And here we see how our intellectual formation has the very greatest importance as preparation for being a Priest-Victim.

Therefore, let us strive to acquire what our Proper Law –using an expression of Fr. Cornelio Fabro– calls “essential Thomism”. In such a way that, well imbued with the doctrine of the Angelic Doctor, we may be capable of thinking from him; and, “from him, enter into dialogue and debate with contemporary problems and thinkers” –as our specific end asks of us and our Proper Law commands us–. Only thus will we be able to discern what corresponds to the Gospel, so as to accept it, and what does not, so as to reject it. This obviously entails an active judgment on human thought and on Thomism itself in relation to modern thought19.

It is not enough to know ‘something’, just like that, superficially. “The lack of an authentically metaphysical intelligence renders pastors of souls incapable of knowing reality, of making precise diagnoses and applying the appropriate remedies. And from there are born formalistic or essentialist ‘spiritualities’ and ‘pastoral approaches’, without grip and without biting into reality. […] To avoid that, a metaphysics with grip is needed, one that bites into reality”20.

That is why today we are asked that, taking into account the philosophical investigations of modern times, especially those that most influence the life of nations and the progress of the sciences, we come to know well the character of the age in which we are given to live, so as to be prepared for dialogue with our contemporaries21. And in this way to seek the solution of human problems in the light of Revelation; and to know how to apply eternal truths to the changing condition of human affairs, and of course, to communicate them in an appropriate way to the souls entrusted to us22.

That is why today, in what is the Mother House of our beloved Congregation, I would like, by way of conclusion, to quote the wise counsel given in our Directory of Intellectual Formation: “Without the discipline and habit of study, the future priest will not be able to be the wise man according to the Gospel who, in season and out of season, exhorts with the word of God, convinces with the truth and frees from error. The priest is called to be a teacher of the Christian faith and, therefore, must be capable of giving a reason for the faith he preaches and teaches. Dedication to study must be undertaken with this pastoral perspective”23.

That is why I told you at the beginning that many aspects of all that we do in the future depend -with the help of God’s grace- on the seriousness, solidity and depth of our formation.

The philosophy of St. Thomas is the philosophy of being, that is, of the ‘actus essendi’, whose transcendental value is the most direct path for rising to the knowledge of subsistent Being and Pure Act, which is God. And only by rediscovering in its fullness being and the first principles of being and of thinking can man validly ascend to Subsistent Being Itself, his beginning and end, and the supreme guarantor of his inalienable freedom. Let us remember that each one of us is a disciple of Him who one day presented Himself to the disciples saying I am24. So we too must be priests, religious and missionaries of ‘being’.

This implies: “being pioneers, ready to take the first steps for Christ, to commit oneself totally to God, trusting in His Word, in His power and in His mercy. It also implies being men of transcendence, not confined or trapped in the temporal by the principle of immanence, but rather knowing how to rise through created things, so as to reach also beyond the limited horizons of this world”25. It implies launching oneself –with a genuinely Catholic spirit– to help in the needs of the whole Church with a spirit ready to kindle the light of the Gospel everywhere.26

Let us then implore on this day the Mother of the Incarnate Word, Patroness of this beloved Seminary, for the grace to know how to make the most, with resolute spirit and great fidelity, of all the time we dedicate to our formation.

May the Mother of God, who is Teacher by the holiness of her example, educate us with maternal wisdom to configure our lives to that of the Incarnate Word.

Thank you very much!

May God bless you all.
