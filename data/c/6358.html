Homily on the occasion of the priestly jubilee of some members of the IVE

[Exordium] On the occasion of our Priestly Jubilee, we have the grace of making a pilgrimage in this Land, which is Holy because the Incarnate Word dwelt in it. And particularly today, to us, missionary priests of the Incarnate Word –come from the four corners of the earth– God gives the grace of beginning this pilgrimage precisely here, in Nazareth, in the very town where the angel appeared to the Most Holy Virgin to ask her consent so that the Word might take from her body and her blood to become man; and when She pronounced her fiat, in this place the Word of God clothed himself with our flesh in her most pure womb. Et verbum caro factum est.

And so, in this town, so small and so sublimely simple, the Incarnation of the Word took place; that mystery which identifies us and which is central in the mystery of God. It is that mystery which was present in God from all eternity and which becomes, in time, an event greater than the creation of the world, one that can never be surpassed by any other1.

The grace of knowing the fact of the Incarnation and the grace of faith in the Incarnation is overwhelming. This is the event that sums up the very history of humanity and at the same time the history of each man and woman in particular (ours too)… this is the mystery that not only divides history in two, but also answers man’s great questions, being, as it were, his metaphysical center. Here, we could say, and only before this mystery, the human spirit finds rest… here everything becomes clear, everything is understood and everything turns into light… And the word was the light of men

Here the Word became flesh, and that is why it is in this place that all the aspirations of the human being and of all creation are resolved: all things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made of all that has been made, says St. John, underscoring the centrality of the event.

We priests have an altogether special share in that mystery. For in the Incarnation of the Word there “took place the sublime priestly consecration of Jesus Christ, since He was marked and anointed forever as High and Eternal Priest, sole Mediator between heaven and earth”2. And we, already 25 years ago by the grace of God, were ontologically configured to Christ, Teacher, Sanctifier and Shepherd of his People3, and this, too, forever. That is why being here makes us return, in some way, to the origins not only of the history of salvation but also to the very origin of the Catholic priesthood and of our priesthood. That is also why here, in this place, lies the mystery of our lives, of our own lives.

It is here that we can understand more fully the mystery we carry in our hands and which we must live each day. For it is the place where the event occurred that has defined our existence and our earthly experience.

How, then, are we to respond before such sublime realities?

It is here, too, that we find the answer: the humility, the joyful docility, the genuine and serene obedience of the Virgin of Nazareth4, who, by pronouncing her fiat, gave the Word from her own human nature, through which He taught, governed and sanctified; these become the paradigm and the greatest model for our fidelity to that mystery of which we are continuators.

Analogously, one day we too, like the Virgin at the Annunciation, were asked that question of entering into the central mystery of history: Would you give God your human nature? And by the great mercy of God we too pronounced our fiat, and God granted us the immense treasure of holy orders, which is the same as saying that God was pleased to clothe us with Christ5.

This reality is ineffable for each one of us: our lives have been indelibly intertwined with the High and Eternal Priest, which inserts us specifically into the Trinitarian mystery6. God has chosen us to be priests of the Incarnate Word, for the sublime office of “prolonging the Incarnation in every man, in the whole man and in all the manifestations of man”7. That is our end, that is our charism, that is and must always be our commitment8. For this we were born and for this we live. That is to say, the same Word who one day, in this very place, assumed a human nature to fulfill the design of salvation, is the same incarnate God who has chosen our poor “human natures so that salvation may reach all men”9, making us his priests and thus continuing what He did: teaching, governing and sanctifying, by delegating to us “his threefold prophetic, priestly and royal function”10. That is why we can say that, analogously, the Incarnation continues to take place, and that it took place particularly in us when we were anointed as priests and became “living instruments of the Eternal Priest so as to be able to carry on, through time, his admirable work… Since every priest –as the Magisterium teaches– represents in his own way the person of Christ himself”11.

That is why here today we can and must renew our commitment to the threefold priestly office of Christ that has been entrusted to us, so that we may be faithful and always true continuators of the Incarnation of the Word in time and in this portion of history in which Divine Providence has given us to live.

1. In such a way that our priesthood cannot be understood unless we teach Christ with our word and with our very life: He and his mysteries are always, directly or indirectly, the subject matter of our preaching and of our earthly existence. And just as the Virgin told the servants at the Wedding of Cana: Do whatever He tells you, so we too must proclaim the Gospel in such a way that those who hear us are able to “translate those truths into concrete life, into witness”12, into criteria for living. Therefore it falls to us to be thoroughly imbued with Christ, with his Truth. So that, the fire of his Truth burning first in us, we may radiate it to others. This takes concrete form in the pursuit of a deep unity of life that leads us to try to be, to live and to serve as another Christ in all the circumstances of life13.

2. Moreover, as priests we exercise the authority of Christ and must rule and feed his flock: Our Constitutions say it clearly and beautifully: our “destiny is not command or honors, but total self-giving to the service of God and to the pastoral ministry”14. And we will not find a better example of humility and service than that of the Virgin. “The Virgin gave her ‘yes,’ here in Nazareth, as a handmaid, and God looked upon the lowliness of his handmaid15, and it was then that the Word took the form of a slave, becoming like men16 in her most pure womb”17.

3. Finally, as priests we must sanctify, and to do so we must be Victims with Christ: The Word of God became incarnate in the immaculate womb of the Virgin of Nazareth precisely in order to be able to be a Victim, to be able to immolate his humanity. And therefore the ministerial priesthood is a sacrificial priesthood. This is an inescapable condition. That is why there is usually much suffering in priestly life. This is our vocation: “sacrifice,” with all its consequences. The priest must spend his life letting himself be consumed, like Jesus in the Eucharist. In other words, “the vocation to the priesthood is a vocation to the Cross. It is a vocation to mystical dying, to spiritual dying, day by day… [to live] as men condemned to death… This is the priestly program!”18.

[Peroratio] That is why, of the Most Holy Virgin, who joined herself with a maternal heart to the priestly sacrifice of her Son, lovingly consenting to the immolation of the Victim begotten in her womb, as a consequence of that fiat pronounced here in Nazareth, let us ask the grace that she take us into her hands and, in the shelter of her Mother’s Heart, conform our priesthood to the Priesthood of her Son, and give us the grace to persevere until death, proclaiming with our whole life that the Word became Flesh and dwelt among us. So be it.
