October 1

The Saint

Memorial of Saint Teresa of the Child Jesus and of the Holy Face, virgin and Doctor of the Church, who entered the monastery of the Discalced Carmelites at Lisieux while still very young. Through her innocence and simplicity she became a teacher of holiness in Christ. She taught the way of Christian perfection through spiritual childhood, showing a mystical concern for the good of souls and the growth of the Church. She died on September 30, 1897, aged twenty-four.1

The Institute's Proper Law

Directory of Spirituality

For us, Christ is mysteriously identified with every person, and each person and all people can be understood only in Christ Jesus. […] In every person, “Jesus is hidden in the depths of his soul.”2

And Saint Thérèse of the Child Jesus likewise says: “This is all Our Lord claims from us. He has need of our love—He has no need of our works.”3

The Founder

“That is why we must say with Saint Thérèse of the Child Jesus: ‘God will tire of trying me before I cease to trust in him’; and above all: ‘I have reached a point where I can no longer suffer, because all suffering is become so sweet.’4 Let us never forget that ‘the priest bears within himself the image of Christ, in whose person and by whose power he pronounces the words of consecration. Thus, in Holy Mass, the priest and the victim are in a certain way one and the same reality.’”

Excerpt from Sacerdotes para siempre. The surrounding Founder excerpt and the first attributed Thérèse saying remain working translations; the second Thérèse saying uses the published English edition below.
