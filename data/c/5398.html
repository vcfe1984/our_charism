“The self-destruction of the Church”

St. Paul VI1

G. K. Chesterton once said: “There is one thing in the world that never makes progress, and that is the idea of progress.” By this he meant that unless we have a fixed concept of what progress really is, we will never know whether we are making any advance. Unfortunately, there are many who, instead of working to attain an ideal, change it, and call that progress. In that way, one could never know whether one is progressing from Buenos Aires toward Rome if one identifies Rome with New York. Only when the goal is fixed and definitive can we say that we have a target to aim at and the energy needed to shoot our arrow toward that target.

It often happens that man loses sight of the goal and concentrates on what is merely transitory and earthly, and tries to derive some pleasure from it. We see it in daily life. They are the ones who delight in turning the pages of a book but never finish reading the story; the ones who take brushes in hand but never finish the painting; the ones who travel the seas but never reach a port. And this can also happen to religious, to priests, and of course it can also happen at the ecclesial level, which very often translates into Christian progressivism.

That is why, when in 1980 St. John Paul II addressed the French Bishops’ Conference, he warned them: “It is not at all strange that in this ‘post-conciliar’ stage there have also developed, with considerable intensity, certain interpretations of Vatican II that do not correspond to its authentic Magisterium. I am referring to the two well-known tendencies: ‘progressivism’ and ‘integrism.’ The former are always impatient to adapt even the content of the faith, Christian ethics, the liturgy and ecclesial organization to changes in mentality, to the demands of the ‘world,’ without taking sufficiently into account not only the common sense of the faithful, who feel disoriented, but also what is essential in the faith already defined: the roots of the Church, her centuries-old experience, the norms necessary for her fidelity, her unity, her universality. They are obsessed with ‘moving forward,’ but toward what ‘progress,’ in the end? The others—pointing out certain abuses which we are evidently the first to reprove and correct—harden their position, stopping at a particular period of the Church, at a particular level of theological formulation or liturgical expression which they regard as absolute, without penetrating sufficiently into its deep meaning, without considering the totality of history and its legitimate development, frightened by new questions, without admitting, in the end, that the Spirit of God continues to act today in the Church, with her Pastors united to the Successor of Peter”2.

Pope St. Pius X had already warned of this same thing in 1907 in his Encyclical Pascendi, in order to rebuke and unmask the doctrine of what was later called modernism and, more recently, progressivism. Wishing to expose this most dangerous and tangled doctrine and present it to the Church, he called it, quite simply, “the synthesis of all heresies”3, or, as it is also said, “the sewer of all heresies.” As such, it does not let itself be easily understood—nor does it wish to be—and it attacks the Church and our faith from very different flanks, seeking to fill her with confusion by its doctrine, as perverse as it is slippery, because “more than a system, it is a confusion”4, even though all who raise the progressive banner “without exception, wish to be and to pass for doctors in the Church”5, as St. Pius X said.

Founded on an immanentist philosophy, progressivism presents itself as a continuation of the Gnosticism of the first years of the Church, which in recent times has gathered more and more strength, as the great Pope St. John Paul II said: “It must be admitted realistically, and with deep and anguished sensitivity, that Christians today, in large part, feel lost, confused, perplexed and even disillusioned; ideas contrary to the truth revealed and always taught have been scattered by the handful; true and proper heresies have been spread in the dogmatic and moral field, creating doubts, confusions, rebellions; even the liturgy has been manipulated; immersed in intellectual and moral ‘relativism,’ and therefore in permissiveness, Christians are tempted by atheism, by agnosticism, by a vaguely moralistic illuminism, by a sociological Christianity without defined dogmas and without objective morality”6.

Fr. Alfredo Sáenz, for his part, stated: “in recent times, especially from the end of the last century, something unheard-of occurred, namely that this ‘worldly’ world penetrated with its criteria into the very interior of the Church, mainly through modernism and the subsequent progressivism, to the point that [St.] Paul VI could speak in dramatic terms of a ‘self-demolition of the Church’”7.

Indeed, progressivism remains very much latent in the Church today, since its promoters “hide in the very bosom of the Church, being enemies all the more harmful the less openly declared they are”8, seeking to distort her nature and to confuse the faithful. That is why our duty is to know it more deeply in order to unmask it better, and thus to defend the Mystical Body of Christ and protect souls, so as not to be unfaithful to the most sacred of our duties, which is to be servants of the Truth9.

St. Paul VI said, with brilliant analysis: “Everyone knows how the Church is immersed in humanity, forms part of it; from it come her members, from it she draws precious treasures of culture, and how she suffers its historical vicissitudes and also contributes to its successes. Now, everyone likewise knows that humanity in this time is on the path of great transformations, upheavals and advances, which profoundly change not only its outward forms of life but also its ways of thinking. Its thought, its culture, its spirit are being intimately modified, both by scientific, technical and social progress and by the currents of philosophical and political thought that invade and pervade it. All of this, like the waves of a sea, engulfs and shakes the Church herself: the minds of the men who entrust themselves to her are strongly influenced by the climate of the temporal world, so much so that a danger, as it were, of vertigo, of bewilderment, of aberration, can shake her very solidity and induce many to follow the strangest ideas, imagining as though the Church ought to deny herself and embrace entirely new and unthought-of forms of life. Thus, for example, the modernist phenomenon—which still surfaces in various attempts at expressions foreign to the authentic reality of the Catholic religion—was it not precisely a similar episode of the predominance of psychological-cultural tendencies, proper to the profane world, over the faithful and genuine expression of the doctrine and norm of the Church of Christ? Now, we believe that in order to immunize ourselves against such a danger, ever imminent and manifold, coming from many sides, a good and obvious remedy is to deepen the Church’s awareness of what she truly is, according to the mind of Christ contained in Scripture and in Tradition, and interpreted and developed by genuine ecclesiastical teaching, which is, as we know, enlightened and guided by the Holy Spirit, who is always ready, when we ask Him and when we listen to Him, to give unfailing fulfillment to the promise of Christ: The Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you all things and will remind you of all that I have said to you (Jn 14:26)”10.

1. The great storm

Historically, and as Fr. Julio Meinvielle demonstrates well and at length in one of his masterworks11, progressivism is a continuation of what is known as the Jewish Kabbalah12, which is nothing other than the culpable perversion of the primordial tradition originating with our first parents, and of the revelation given to the patriarchs, owing to the chosen people’s continual contact with idolatrous peoples. Our Lord alludes to this perverted tradition or secret gnosis when He says to the Pharisees and scribes: You nullify the commandment of God in order to keep your tradition […] making void the word of God through this tradition that you hand on; and you do many other such things13.

After the Incarnation of the Word of God, the Redemption and the founding of the Church, this Kabbalah also penetrates nascent Christianity, thus giving rise to Christian Gnosticism, which affirms a fundamental ontological monism that ends up flowing into absolute pantheism. It gives evil a positive consistency and a dynamic force, and places within man the very origin of his salvation, in such a way that he needs no savior and no grace.

This Christian Gnosticism took on different names and survived thanks to many philosophers, theologians and writers who, with heterodox doctrines, opposed what the Magisterium of the Church taught and authentic Christian philosophy and theology, represented most perfectly in St. Thomas Aquinas.

Now, at the end of the nineteenth century, this Christian Gnosticism appears more vigorously under the name of modernism, carried forward by a throng of intellectuals who were consumed with the intention of ruining the Church14, seeking to change her doctrine from within and by means of confusion.

Modernism, against which St. Pius X struck forcefully, could be summed up as the effort to accommodate the Catholic faith to the requirements of the world; that is, to make the Church of Jesus Christ bend and conform, in complete submission, to the wishes and whims of the course of history, on the sole argument that history is the manifestation of God. “The subjectivist, immanentist and evolutionist currents that infected the modern mentality were infiltrating Catholic intellectual circles, giving rise in exegesis, in the history of dogma and of the Church, in philosophy and theology, to a new interpretation of Christianity which, in actual fact, altered it fundamentally and thereby destroyed it”15. Modernism wanted to destroy the divine nature of the Church and turn her into a merely human entity at the service of modern history. That is why we do not fear to affirm that “at bottom, the ‘modernists’ of yesterday and today have in common the method presupposed by philosophical immanentism; but, as Lortz says, modernism is not so much a system of heretical doctrine as a heretical way of thinking16”17. Thus it was that men like Félicité Lamennais devised strange ways and arguments to accommodate themselves to the world, betraying the Church and their own consciences. Nevertheless, as we have rightly been taught: “if modern civilization involves the absolute autonomy of man before God, it is abundantly clear that the Church cannot be reconciled with it”18.

It is fitting to note that St. Pius X in a certain way foresaw the later development of modernism, which today has arrived at nihilism on the theoretical level (insofar as such a thing is possible) and at ‘absolute’ relativism on the practical level. Whichever form of immanentism (empiricist, rationalist, materialist, historicist, sentimentalist, scientistic, progressive in the sense of the Enlightenment) is taken as the method and as the basic philosophy for Theology, it will have the same conclusion: the elimination of the very possibility of supernatural Revelation and the denial of mystery. And that, for the Catholic Church, would mean the elimination of the revealed deposit and, therefore, death through her reduction to the sphere of the world19.

Lately, thanks to many authors of the Nouvelle Théologie20, and with greater tenacity after the Second Vatican Council, this Christian Gnosticism presents itself under the name of progressivism and strives eagerly to subject the Church both to Marxist ideology and communism and to liberalism, denying a Christian social order21. Today, it is this progressivism that is the great storm the Church must face for the salvation of souls.

2. Confused mixture

Being the gnosis that it is, progressivism presents itself as a confused mixture: “a concoction of supernatural and natural, a fusion of faith and reason, a spurious combination of Christ and Caesar, a confusion of theology with philosophy or sociology or psychology”22. What is more, contemporary progressivism, having taken on the essence of Marxism—which is dialectics—has no qualms about affirming and denying diametrically opposed things, or contradicting itself, because it does not seek the truth but rather to accommodate itself to the age. Pius XII had already underlined this characteristic: “These new opinions are not always proposed in the same order, with the same clarity or in the same terms, nor always with full unanimity of views among themselves; and in fact, what some teach today more covertly, with certain precautions and distinctions, others more daring will spread tomorrow openly and without limits”23.

Progressivism also presents itself as an inversion, which is the fruit of that mania for making dialectics out of everything. “Instead of God, man… instead of love of God, love of neighbor… instead of a message of salvation, a social message… instead of the Cross, openness to the world… instead of absolute truth, the truth of the times”24. They do not know how to harmonize things that are apparently opposed, because they have never meditated on the mystery of the Incarnate Word. They subvert reality and turn it upside down, because they never manage to have a correct appraisal of things. “It is seeking first the things that are added and then, if there is time and desire left, the Kingdom of God”25.

Progressivism is also characterized by dissolution. Since its strategy is confusion, it seeks to dissolve the truths of the faith and the Catholic religion itself, stripping them of their philosophical and historical foundations and always calling them into question, arrogating to itself the right to doubt the dogmas and the faith handed down by the Apostles and protected by the Magisterium. Thus they present false ‘salvations’ which, although they bear the name Christian, are nothing but disguised ways of allying oneself with the spirit of the world. They dissolve, in turn, the whole supernatural order into the natural, stripping it of its value and justification. They postulate an “absolute independence between the spiritual and the temporal,” “in such a way that it authorizes acting in the temporal sphere without concerns of a spiritual kind”26.

Likewise, the Holy Pope declared with all his force: “They go about crying out that the government of the Church must be reformed in all its aspects, but chiefly in the disciplinary and dogmatic, and therefore that it must be harmonized inwardly and outwardly with what they call the modern conscience, which tends entirely toward democracy; for which reason, the lower clergy and the laity themselves must be granted a certain share in government, and authority, which is too concentrated and centralized, must be distributed. […] They likewise claim that the influence of ecclesiastical government in political and social affairs must be modified, so that, while separating itself from civil ordinances, it nevertheless adapts itself to them in order to imbue them with its spirit. […] In morals, that the active virtues must be placed before the passive ones, and that the former must be practiced in preference to the latter. […] There are, finally, some who, gladly following their Protestant masters, desire that sacred celibacy be abolished in the priesthood.

What, then, remains intact in the Church that must not be reformed by them and according to their opinions?”27.

3. Inevitable consequences and immediate causes

Dr. Alberto Caturelli, in an article prepared on the occasion of the 100th anniversary of the Encyclical Pascendi, points out that “philosophical, scientific and historical immanentism, which they arbitrarily postulate as having a knowable object, in contrast to faith, whose subject they deem unknowable, is the source of all errors and all rebellions”28.

St. Pius X had already pointed out the inevitable consequences of this erroneous way of thinking, which leads to atheism and to the denial of all religion as the result of pure symbolism.

“The proximate and immediate cause [of this evil] is, without doubt, the perversion of the intellect”29. If we ask about the motivations or personal causes that lead someone to think in that way, the Holy Father pointed out two fundamental ones: pride (“puffed up with the haughty name of science”30) and, simultaneously, the vice opposed to the virtue of studiousness, which is curiosity in the sense of a vicious pursuit of the new (“under the impulse of a bold and unbridled love of novelties”31), not of objective truth. In the case of Theology both vices are aggravated, because man sets himself up as the supreme norm32.

If we ask “about the immediate causes, two emerge with invincible evidence: a) ignorance: this is not a common ignorance but a profound one (if it can be so described), an ignorance that can affect many ‘learned men’ who teach in Seminaries and Ecclesiastical Universities without real adherence to the Magisterium and with contempt for metaphysical realism; St. Pius X says: ‘All the moderns, without exception, who wish to be and to pass for doctors in the Church, although they extol modern philosophy with grandiloquent words and despise scholasticism, did not embrace the former (…) except because their complete ignorance of the latter deprived them of the arguments necessary to distinguish confusion of ideas and to refute sophisms’33.

b) The other cause is contained in the first, for it is none other than hatred of the scholastic method. These self-appointed ‘holders’ of a ‘parallel magisterium’ reject ‘the scholastic method of philosophizing, the authority and tradition of the Fathers, the ecclesiastical magisterium’ (…) ‘They generally ridicule and despise scholastic philosophy and theology’34. In 1907 [as in 2022] all those who defend orthodoxy and fight for the Church are victims of ‘the conspiracy of silence’35; this way of proceeding against Catholics, says St. Pius X, ‘is all the more odious because at the same time they exalt without any moderation, with perpetual praise, all those who agree with them; the books of these men, the Pontiff concludes, full of novelties throughout, they receive with great admiration and applause…’36”37. Moreover, “if the Church condemns one of them, not only do they unite to praise him in public and by every means, but they go so far as to pay him almost the veneration due to a martyr for the truth”38.

“Had St. Pius X lived today, he could have verified that what he denounced then has now attained a certain absoluteness. The windows of Catholic bookstores are full of books by Boff, Küng, Gutiérrez, Rahner, Moltmann and many others; precisely because they have been ‘censured’ by the Holy See they are good business… they sell… they sell a lot. In Pascendi, the Pope denounced the permeation of seminaries and congresses, journals, newspapers and religious orders”39.

4. Its attacks

As has been said, progressivism is a conglomerate of all heresies40, and therefore there is no aspect or truth of our faith that it does not attack or seek to destroy.

Beginning by destroying the preambles of faith, and in league with the philosophy of immanence, it takes away from reason its capacity to know reality objectively. “The essence of progressivism lies in its considering reality to be one-dimensional; that is, despite words to the contrary, progressivism pushes toward a monism, and full progressivism is an absolute monism, which, in rejecting transcendence, affirms absolute immanence and entails a false concept of God”41. And as a consequence of this, progressivism denies the capacity of human reason to transcend creation and know its Creator, because for them God is nothing more than the world, and there is no distinction between being by essence and being by participation. Such is the real situation of the world, and they want to make us believe that today a ‘Theology without God’ is needed because that is the ‘maturity’ of the Christian. And if we want the world to listen to the Christian message—the progressives maintain—we must start from that ‘maturity’ (which takes on the situation of the atheistic world) and inaugurate a new exegesis…

That is why progressivism is also a bitter enemy of the historicity of Sacred Scripture, often considering it a mere myth or a fanciful creation of certain communities, thus stripping Divine Revelation and the Person of Our Lord of all historical value, and calling into question the truthfulness of His very words contained in the Gospels. “It sinks the revealed datum into history and only into history. Archaeology and other disciplines placed at the service of the new exegesis acquire here an inordinate importance”42. What is more, for progressivism, Christ is not God as the Church has understood Him and as the Holy Fathers and the Holy Doctors have so aptly explained, among whom St. Thomas Aquinas stands out: the Ipsum Esse Subsistens, transcendent and purely spiritual, personal, free, provident and creator. Christ is nothing more than a human person in whom the presence of God was found in a more particular and evident way, thus denying the hypostatic union of the human and divine natures in the Second Person, the Son, a union that is by assumption. Consequently, Christ is not the Savior of the human race, but simply an example or motivation. Man saves himself by acting according to his nature in the total decision about himself. Thus the theologian Karl Rahner affirmed in his doctrine of anonymous Christianity. Once this outlandish assertion is accepted, all the sacraments collapse, as does the Church herself, her saving mission and her task of baptizing all nations. If man does not need the sacraments to be saved, then the Church becomes no less than a simple humanitarian organization and not the ark of salvation, being left completely secularized.

From here, the progressive mind reinterprets everything, from the Eucharist, as a mere symbolic and commemorative celebration—speaking of transignification or transfinalization instead of transubstantiation—that has nothing actual or sacrificial about it, to the spiritual life of man and his search for God, even denying the existence of Heaven and Hell, or gratuitously asserting that at the end of time all men will be saved, because, according to them: “God is good and punishes no one”43.

Traditional morality is replaced by a situation ethics, which ends up endorsing the most outrageous aberrations and sins, even intrinsically evil acts, such as the homosexual act. They even go so far as to speak of homosexuality as licit and willed by God. But at the same time, many trivialities are branded as sinful, such as a simple neglect of nature or indifference toward supposed climate change. They are champions of a severe justice and at the same time preachers of a false mercy: “In the temporal order they want justice without mercy, and in the supernatural order mercy without justice, thus exposing themselves to attaining neither justice on earth nor mercy in heaven”44.

They speak of the priesthood as if it were not a sacrament instituted by Jesus Christ and constitutive of the hierarchy of the Church, but rather they assert that all Christians are priests in the same way. Thus they want to introduce female priesthood, and with the excuse of not falling into the Scylla of clericalism they fall into the Charybdis of the trivialization of Holy Orders. They detest vocations to the priesthood and to consecrated life, promote almost exclusively the lay vocation, and are appalled that some congregations have many vocations—as if that were something bad!

The liturgy is also a victim of progressivism, for countless stupidities, abuses and deformations are introduced into it under the excuse of making it more entertaining or accessible to the people, or they turn it into an instrument and propaganda for anti-Christian ideologies.

The false principle of “vital immanence” can go much further. This vital immanence postulates that human reason is rigorously enclosed within the circle of “phenomena,” and it closes off all natural access to God and all supernatural access to external revelation, thus leading to agnosticism. It makes faith depend on some phenomenon, even if this lies in the field of science and history. That is why the phenomenon itself is disfigured, being attributed a reality it does not have45.

For the progressives “it is enough,” Dr. Caturelli states, “to accept or postulate the non-existence of propositions with a content of truth in order to conclude in the most radical relativism that invades the world today. It is not only a matter of academic circles; relativism (each one has ‘his’ truth and no one has ‘the’ truth) has penetrated not only science and ‘Theology’ (encyclicals are only the Pope’s ‘opinion’ and are debatable) but social life, all public manifestations, family life and politics”46.

5. The remedies

St. Pius X recognized with great clarity that the principle of immanence was the common nerve of the errors he denounced at that time. We can say that it continues to be so for the problems that afflict us today. It has been said since ancient times that there is no “new” heresy. That is true. In progressivism there is a stubborn effort to “return” to the old man, transferring salvation to the world; but the only thing new is Christ, the Incarnate Word; therefore all errors and heresies are old, even if they wear a mask of “novelty.”

What remedies did the Holy Father propose in 1907? Among others that the Pope sets out in the encyclical, we mention the following:

  • “In the first place, then, with regard to studies, we will and definitively command that scholastic philosophy be made the foundation of sacred studies”47. So that no doubts remain, he adds: “The main thing to note is that, when we prescribe that scholastic philosophy be followed, we understand chiefly that which St. Thomas Aquinas taught,” and he warns: “departing from the Doctor of Aquino, especially in metaphysical questions, will never fail to be of great harm”48 . “With this foundation of philosophy thus laid, let the theological edifice be built with great diligence”49.
  • On the other hand, he exhorts the promotion of “the study of Theology, so that clerics may leave the seminaries filled with great esteem and love for it and may always regard it as their favorite study”50. He also advocates the study of secular disciplines, provided it is not to the detriment of sacred studies.
  • Finally, he mentions some practical measures regarding the choice of “the rectors and teachers of seminaries or of Catholic Universities,” saying that if “they are in any way imbued with modernism [progressivism], they are to be removed without any consideration from the office, whether of governing or of teaching, and if they already exercise it, they are to be dismissed”51.

* * *

Progressivism, in short, in its many manifestations and facets, seeks quite simply to destroy the transcendent God, the God of the Gospels and of our Catholic faith. “Progressivism—liberal or Marxist, bourgeois or revolutionary—does not work so that the public and social life of peoples may be subordinated to God as to its ultimate end, because ‘its’ god is no longer transcendent; if every vulgarity is possible in ‘its’ liturgies, it is because ‘its’ god is not majesty itself; if they have questioned every truth of the faith and doubted every miracle or prophecy in Scripture, it is because ‘their’ god is not almighty; if they are fruitful in doubts and in sterile certainties, it is because ‘their’ god is not infinite truth”52. And having removed God from their midst, they have put man in His place, making gods of themselves, believing themselves to be the center of history, of theology and of salvation. Hence one speaks only of the rights of man, and little or nothing of the rights of God. They do not want Him present in society, whether communist, capitalist or of any kind whatever. Nor do they speak of making Him known in non-Christian countries, and they do not want to preach Him anew in the nations of ancient Christian tradition.

Because of this progressivism, this reigning atheism, many today regard the modern world as ‘without hope.’ In truth, at times it seems like a long Good Friday in which everything divine seems to have failed. Perhaps never as now has the future seemed so unpredictable. People seem to suffer from an anguished sense of desolation.

However, there is neither need nor room for such hopelessness. The world seemed as desolate before, when it crucified Christ, as it is now, and yet it rose again to the freshness of a new Christian civilization. The same can happen now, as it has already happened at least a dozen times since the coming of Christ. Yet one must realize that the modern ideal is not exactly goodness, but success. And such an ideal makes penance incredibly difficult. It is true that paganism before Christ also sinned, but the sin of modern paganism has the serious aggravating circumstance of having betrayed an ideal that the pagans did not have, namely, Christ. And that is why “progressivism” cannot be considered progress, because it has replaced the eternal Ideal with the temporal world and its maxims. Therefore, humanity will not rise again to peace and happiness through political and economic remedies, even though these too are necessary, but fundamentally through the spiritual regeneration of hearts and souls, through conversion—or return—to Christ, the only peace and happiness of man. That is why St. Pius X said on assuming the See of Peter: “If we are asked for a motto that expresses Our will, we will always present this one alone: to restore all things in Christ53”54.

This is the task that falls particularly to us as members of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, who have been called to “assume” cultures, purifying them and elevating them on the basis of Christ and His Gospel, understood “in the Church.” Therefore, analogously to the mystery of the Incarnation, it must be an assumption; that is, there must be an action and a passion that express the becoming and not only the final result, and that refer distinctly to each of the terms, that of departure and that of arrival. A mere union is not enough for us, nor is merely putting a nominalist label on reality so that it may truly be ruled by Christ. “Being in the world” only makes sense for us when it depends on “not being of the world.” Only thus can one truly be salt of the earth and light of the world55. Otherwise we would become salt that has lost its savor and a light under the bushel basket56. “Not being tributaries”57 continues to be our watchword.
