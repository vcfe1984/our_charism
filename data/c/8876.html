Sermon preached by Fr. Carlos Miguel Buela on June 20, 1998, in the Parish of Our Lady of Sorrows, on the occasion of the clothing of the novices of the Institute of the Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará

We are celebrating the feast of the Immaculate Heart of Mary. It is a particularly dear feast for us because our congregation began its public life on the day on which the Pope and all the bishops of the world consecrated the world −that is, all of us and all things− to the Immaculate Heart of Mary. That was on March 25, 1984.

I always remember that here in San Rafael, on the day we were beginning with the diocesan seminary, the consecration was made by Bishop León Kruk in front of the parish of St. Joseph, whose pastor was Fr. Ortego, the youngest of all the priests of the diocese with his youthful 80 years. Mass was then celebrated on the sidewalk in front of his parish, and during the Mass the consecration to the Immaculate Heart of Mary was made.

Just as She kept the things of her Son in her heart (cf. Lk 2:19), so we on this day, in remembering her, wish to keep her always in our heart.

This week I was able to finish reading a book which is not recent, but is indeed very interesting, about St. James in Spain. In it I found a phrase that seemed to me very beautiful for the sermon of this day. The author says: “On the wits and the energy of the unresigned will depend the course of the world”1.

It seems to me an appropriate occasion to develop what this means. I will begin with the final part of that phrase.

“Will depend the course of the future”

We men of our time forget −frequently, unfortunately− something that is very important: it is in our hands to be able to steer the course of the future.

In the face of this, there are three differing positions: according to the Hebrews, works are enough; according to the Protestants, faith alone; the position of Catholics embraces both aspects: faith and works.

The first position is the one held by the Jewish people by reason of their culture, their history and their way of seeing things. They are convinced that by acting, they bring about change. In fact they have shown it: nineteen hundred years after having lost their homeland, through tenacity, through acting, through working, they recovered it; another example is what happened with their language: the Hebrew language, which had been dead for nineteen hundred years, became −in what constitutes a unique case in the history of the world up to now− a living, spoken language; it is a language heard on radio and television; it is read in the newspapers and spoken by people in the streets.

The other position is that of Protestantism. For them, as Luther said, faith alone is enough. It is with faith, without the need for works, that the future is changed.

The Catholic position, which is neither the one nor the other, rises like a mountain above two valleys. Catholicism teaches: faith comes first, but works must accompany faith.

That is to say, it is by reason of faith and by reason of works that the course of the future can −and must− be changed. Just as the Blessed Virgin did: she had faith, she believed in the Word she already bore in her Heart, she said “Yes” (cf. Lk 1:38) and began to bear her Word not only in her heart but also in her most holy womb, by accepting in faith and acting accordingly. She changed the course of the future! That woman who kept all the things of her Son (cf. Lk 2:29) is the greatest woman who has ever existed among all women. That is why her kinswoman Elizabeth greets her: Blessed are you among all women! (Lk 1:42). And as time goes on, the Blessed Virgin will be ever more exalted, as St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort says so well.

And looking at that woman, seeking to imitate that woman who knew how to change the course of the future, we have had in this century a group of unresigned ones, who are the martyrs, the confessors of the faith, as were, for example, Cardinal Midzensty, Stépinac −who is about to be canonized2−; like the Chinese Cardinal Ignatius Kung Pin Mei; as John Paul II was and is. They did not resign themselves. They did not believe that nefarious political slogan −which was raised even within the ranks of the Church− that said: “better red than dead.” And they changed the course of history! The theorists spoke of the course of history inexorably marked out by ideology. It seemed something inevitable. And yet that group of unresigned ones changed the future.

The course of history can be changed…

We know that the course of history can be changed, first of all because we have faith, and because we know that God is the only one capable of moving the wills of men, and He moves them. In order to be able to change the future we must keep in mind when and where it is changed: that future is changed in the present, it is changed in the here and now; it is in the present that the future is changed. I always like to recall the triumph of General Wellington over Napoleon. Years after that resounding victory, he returned to the place where he had studied and, before all the cadets who admired him as a hero, he said these wise words: “Waterloo was won here.” In the classrooms where he studied and prepared himself.

In the same way these young women will be able to say tomorrow that, like the Virgin, today they say their “Yes,” because it is a yes that springs from faith, and if it is accompanied by the works proper to faith it will certainly help to change the course of the future.

“On the wits and the energy”

The author names two elements: wits (or intelligence), and energy.

Wits, that is, intelligence, that which distinguishes us men and women from irrational creatures. We are capable of thinking; animals do not think. But it is not enough merely to think; one must think and act. One must have energy of will, energy of character, in order to live in accordance with the truth the intellect knows. The future is changed precisely by the correct use of the two principal faculties of the soul: intellect and will. It is similar to what Solsyenitzin, one of the great men of this century, proposed. He spoke of “lucidity and courage.”

To change the course of history it is necessary to have lucidity, that is, to have a lucid intellect capable of detecting problems and of finding adequate solutions; and one must have energy of will so as not to give in before those who would want other things. It is necessary to fight, and to fight is a grace.

“Of the unresigned”

Who are the unresigned?

  • They are those men and women who do not hand themselves over by placing themselves, improperly, in the hands of another.
  • They are those who do not yield to public opinion when it is against the Gospel, because truth does not depend on the majority. When Pilate asks the majority: What do you want me to do with the one you call king of the Jews? (Mk 15:12), the majority shouted: Crucify him! Crucify him! (Mk 15:13).
  • They are those who do not submit to the spirit of the world, because it is contrary to the Holy Spirit, to the Spirit of Truth, because the spirit of the world runs along other tracks, takes paths contrary to those of the Holy Spirit.

I want to give some examples of what the ways of the world and its opinions are, and what the ways of the Holy Spirit are, which is the way of the unresigned:

  • Today the majority fall into what has come to be called TV addiction; people live as slaves of television −called in Spain the idiot box. If one is not resigned, one asks oneself, “Why should I be a slave? I must be free.” By the grace of God, still up to now, we are not slaves of television; we do not habitually watch TV.
  • Today many women live as slaves of the latest fashion craze; these Sisters of ours do not care at all about fashion; today they begin to wear a habit that will probably not change for years. That is to be unresigned.
  • Today people live in a pansexualist culture; these Sisters are going to take a vow of chastity.
  • Today people live preoccupied with money, they live as slaves of consumerism; these Sisters take a vow of poverty.
  • Today people exalt freedom to the point of licentiousness; these Sisters will take a vow of obedience, which is not resignation, it is non-resignation, because it does not mean placing oneself in the hands of another human person, but is the gift of oneself, placing oneself totally in the hands of God, because it is wanting to do the will of God. It is exactly the opposite.

We must form unresigned men and women

We must seek to form unresigned men and women.

We hear it said ad nauseam: “there are no vocations”; “young people today are busy with other things”; “in the past families were large and that is why there were many children who wanted to consecrate themselves, not now”; “priests are not needed”; “the place of the priest is taken by the laity…”. But if one is not resigned, one knows that God raises up vocations, that God does not let Himself be outdone in generosity by anyone, and that God sends vocations. And here we see it proven!

“Many missionaries never went out from our country…”. And why should they not go out now? We must not resign ourselves! By the grace of God we have sent missionaries to the five continents, and by the grace of God I can repeat a phrase I have already used on another occasion: “We are bleeding ourselves dry in missionaries.”

It is also said:

  • “Young people should not be asked for much sacrifice”; we propose sacrifice to them.
  • “No, young people will be driven away by the cross of Christ…”. And it is the cross of Christ that calls young people! It is exactly the other way around!
  • “High goals drive young people away…”; and it is high goals that call young people: we have young men studying extremely difficult languages: Chinese, Arabic, Russian, Quechua; the languages of tribes: the Chipibos, the Kachipos…
  • “Young people do not know how to have fun…”. We have fun.
  • “The sense of feast has been lost…”. Let us reconquer the sense of feast. The most famous phrase of the Constitutions is one of St. Athanasius: “From feast to feast we go toward the great Feast”3 (It is the one the seminarians like most…!). That is fine; “from feast to feast” is our pilgrimage to the “great Feast” of Heaven.

This position is not arbitrary, and it has a deep foundation: we are rebels against the world because we follow Jesus Christ, and that is why we are a sign of contradiction!

We do not want young people who have crashed into the stars, but young people with a star, who know where to go, who follow the star!

Not young people with a saddle, but young people with spurs, who are capable of having lucidity and courage!

We aim to form men and women who are free with the freedom of the children of God!

It is possible to change the course of history

It is possible to change the course of history, but only with unresigned men and women, who freely follow the way of the Holy Spirit, which is the way that will lead them to bear witness to God before those who do not know Him.

If these Sisters accompany today’s “yes” with works, they will certainly contribute to changing the course of history, and the history of many souls.

On how many of these Sisters so many souls in so many places will depend!

How great is the good they are called to do, how important is the courageous witness of regarding God as an Absolute in their lives!

How many men and women will feel edified by the witness of these Sisters!

We want men and women capable of changing the course of history!
