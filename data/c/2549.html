This Word is truly the Truth1

Directory of Spirituality, 54

“Social networks exponentially amplify gossip and idle talk, to the point that we do not know what is true, no matter what the subject may be”2. This sentence, which belongs to Tristan Harris3, gives us in some way a glimpse of the dictatorship of relativism reigning in modern culture.

Harris argues that “technology is degrading our capacity for attention, our relationships, our courtesy, our community, our habits, our differences, our critical thinking […] our mental health, our creativity, our intimacy, our self-esteem, our productivity, our common ground, our shared truth, our attention, our systems of government, nations, our values. The great problem with technology −he continues− is that it is degrading human beings”4.

This problem has not escaped the Pontiffs of our time, including Pope Francis himself, who in his latest encyclical Fratelli tutti speaks of “a degradation: a ‘leveling down’ by way of a superficial and negotiated consensus”5, due, among other things, to “a feverish exchange of opinions on social networks, often directed by media information that is not always reliable. They are merely monologues that proceed in parallel, perhaps imposing themselves on the attention of others by their loud or aggressive tones. But monologues commit no one, to the point that their contents are frequently opportunistic and contradictory”6 and they become mere “spreaders of confusion and lies”7.

St. John Paul II already warned us of the same thing two decades ago: “Media culture has been progressively penetrated by a typically postmodern sense in which the only absolute truth admitted is the nonexistence of absolute truth or, if it did exist, it would be inaccessible to human reason and therefore irrelevant. With such a perspective, what matters is not the truth but ‘the story’; if something is newsworthy or entertaining, the temptation to set aside considerations of truth becomes almost always irresistible”8.

Likewise, Pope Francis speaks of “the illusion of communication” through social media, in which “everything is to be shown, and each individual [or Institution] becomes the object of gazes that pry, strip bare and spread abroad, frequently anonymously. [Where] respect for the other is shattered”9 under the false excuse of “a suitable form of group care”10 or under the cloak of a “false community mystique”11, when in reality these are associations against someone whom they consider an enemy12.

That is why, now that we are close to the celebration of the Incarnation of the Word −Way, Truth and Life13−, recognizing that we are called to an absolute and total love of the truth, to work so that the principles of the Gospel may effectively influence the life of men, and to combat error with all our strength, in the midst of a world that believes that error has the same rights among men as the truth, it has seemed fitting to us to devote these pages to developing (even if briefly) the fact that the noble office of being “servants of the Truth”14 most often brings with it the opposition of the world; for have we perhaps forgotten that our Lord called Satan, the prince of this world, the father of lies15? As everyone is aware, many institutions and members of the Church who work actively in spreading the Gospel, among them our Institute, are constantly and unjustly slandered, defamed and misrepresented on today’s online services by cowardly minds who unscrupulously write without knowing what they are doing16, and speak and pronounce on what they do not know. But it is precisely full adherence to the truth that the Word became flesh that makes man free, and its proclamation from the housetops of this world17 is the cause for which it is worth suffering every difficulty in order to faithfully fulfill the mission that has been entrusted to us.

1. Modern totalitarianism

Let us begin by quoting here a “memorable” text of St. John Paul II: “If there is no transcendent truth, in obedience to which man achieves his full identity, then there is no sure principle for guaranteeing just relations between people […] If one does not acknowledge transcendent truth, then the force of power takes over, and each person tends to make full use of the means at his disposal in order to impose his own interests or his own opinion, with no regard for the rights of others. […] The root of modern totalitarianism, therefore, is to be found in the denial of the transcendent dignity of the human person, who as the visible image of the invisible God”18.

That is why, when “the logic of being is replaced by the ‘logic of power’ and science and technology are withdrawn from the service of man (through leveling) and subordinated to ever-increasing productive efficiency, spiritual autonomies are no longer admitted”19 and totalitarianism is established. This is what is currently happening because of technocracy, for which “greatness” is miserable opulence and its end is well-being; it is, then, a satanic inversion of the very concept of well-being, which makes men slaves of the “standard of living” and feeds everything that is vanity. And since in such a world the understanding of being is an unbearable scandal, it hates the intellect, as all tyrannies do.

“What is happening to us today” −points out Pope Francis− “and dragging us into a perverse and empty logic, is that ethics and politics are being assimilated to physics. Good and evil no longer exist in themselves; there is only a calculus of benefits and burdens. As a result of the displacement of moral reasoning, the law is no longer seen as reflecting a fundamental notion of justice but as mirroring the dominant ideas. Here we enter into a degradation: a ‘leveling down’ by way of a superficial and negotiated consensus. In the end, the logic of force prevails”20.

In this context, and so that we may better grasp the reality of modern totalitarianism and realize that it is not a mere philosophical disquisition, it seems worthwhile to us to bring up here the opinion of those very people who were involved from within in the social networks that the whole world uses today. Tristan Harris himself, the former Google employee we quoted at the beginning, admits: “The classic saying is: ‘If you’re not paying for the product, then you are the product.’ A lot of people think, you know, ‘Oh, well, Google is just a search box and Facebook is just a place to see what my friends are doing and look at their photos.’ But what they don’t realize is that they [Google and Facebook] are competing for your attention. […] You know, Facebook, Snapchat, Twitter, Instagram, YouTube, companies like these have a business model that consists in keeping people hooked on the screen. […] It is a for-profit business model of disinformation. You make money to the extent that you allow messages to reach anyone at the best price without any kind of regulation”21. Sandy Parakilas, a former Facebook employee, for his part maintains: “We have created a system that leans toward false information. Not because we want to, but because false information makes companies more money than the truth. The truth is boring”22.

People from this milieu also speak of AI (artificial intelligence) as what is currently driving the world. By the term ‘artificial intelligence’ they mean computers (machines) that imitate cognitive functions such as ‘learning’ and ‘problem-solving ability’. “In these companies like Google there are immense rooms, some of them underground, some of them underwater, just of computers. Tons and tons of computers, as far as the eye can see. They are all deeply interconnected with one another and run extremely complicated programs, sending information back and forth to one another all the time. They run many different programs, for many different products, on those same machines. Some of those things could be described as simple algorithms, while others could be described as algorithms so complicated that they could be called intelligence”23, says Justin Rosenstein24.

Such mega-computers operate with algorithms according to some definition of success. If a company wants to sell something (a product, a form of government, a religion, an idea, a cultural model, etc.), it builds the algorithm according to that definition of success (which generally has a commercial or political interest), which in the vast majority of cases will result in a gain (of money). Every day the computer itself ‘learns’ how to sell more and better: which ads to show, in what order, which video to recommend, etc. (and it does this differently with each person) so that the person spends more and more time entertained with that product (or idea) until he finally buys it or consents to it. To do this, AI needs a great deal of information about us, and it certainly takes care to collect it. Thus, for example, a person who reads a newspaper online, or receives the news in his email, or watches some program on YouTube may come to believe that everyone thinks the same as he does, simply because the opinions he reads and the videos YouTube recommends to him are precisely similar to the ones he looks for, the news is generally of the kind he usually reads, etc.; and so the temptation grows to accept something he does not even know to be true (because the distinction between truth and illusion is distorted), and critical thinking is nullified, as he thinks something is true simply because there is a ‘manufactured consensus’ given by the number of views of a video, or the number of comments, etc., with the result, not infrequently, that something completely false goes ‘viral’. Not long ago a politician in the United States said: “We are a nation of people who have isolated ourselves so as to watch only the channels that tell us we are right”25. And when one has reached that point, one is easily manipulated, because in reality one is choosing to see only the options that YouTube, Google, etc. show one. Another example: Facebook tells people ‘choose your friends’, but people do not realize that Facebook has already chosen them for them, since it presents to them only those whom the computer ‘thinks’ might interest them. All this has serious cultural, psychological, political, sociological consequences, etc., as is to be expected26.

A former Facebook investor said: “If everyone is entitled to their own facts, there is really no need for mutual agreement, no need for people to come together. In fact, there is no need for people to interact. [However,] We need… to share some of the reality we understand”27.

That is why in 2009 Pope Benedict XVI had warned: “Life is not a mere succession of facts and experiences; rather it is a search for truth, goodness and beauty. Our decisions and the exercise of our freedom are directed toward these ends, and in them −truth, goodness and beauty− we find happiness and joy. We must not let ourselves be deceived by those who merely seek consumers in a market of undifferentiated possibilities, where choice itself is presented as the good, novelty is confused with beauty, and subjective experience supplants truth”28.

For her part, Cathy O’Neil29 maintains that unfortunately “we are allowing the technologists to frame this as a problem they are prepared to solve… That is a lie. People talk about AI (artificial intelligence) as if it knew the truth. AI will not solve these problems. AI cannot solve the problem of false news (fake news). Google (she is referring to its computers) does not have the option of saying, ‘Oh, is this a conspiracy? Is this true?’ Because they do not know what the truth is… They have no indicator of truth that is better than a click”30.

These few examples serve to show how “technocracy does not admit the criticisms of the intellect nor critical thinking (replaced by ‘team research’); nor, at bottom, does it admit any dialogue at all, because it admits it only in one direction (with those who are inside the ‘system’); for the true opponent there is no room except aggressive silence, typical of dogmatic intransigence ‘against all knowledge that does not have external experience as its origin and calculation based on data as its result’”31. “Reading (which makes one think) is replaced by the ‘civilization’ of the image, by that stupefying hammering of images that admits ‘dialogue’ in only one direction. This tyranny is in truth ashamed of the tradition of the West and wages an offensive against six essential factors:

  1. Against authority, in whose empty place it sets up an insidious form of authoritarianism (at the same time as it ‘speaks’ against it);
  2. Against the environment;
  3. Against feelings;
  4. Against every moral principle;
  5. Against imagination; and
  6. Against political ideologies themselves, in pursuit of union-industrial power.

This reduction of everything to itself leads technocracy, by means of ‘rational planning’, to the destruction of the landscape and of the sense of nature at the hands of ‘technicians’ and ‘experts’32. They are precisely the ones who first spoke of the problem of ‘pollution’ (caused by themselves) without noticing what Sciacca calls the ‘pollution of consciences’, which is its main and true cause”33.

Sadly, this leads not only to the destruction of the natural habitat but also of the urban habitat (“all together and each one shut up in his isolation”) as well as of the political-social habitat; to “de-theologization,” which runs parallel to “depoliticization”: a politics without politicians who think or thinkers who think about politics, whose “democratic character” works like this: first, the plans are foreseen, drawn up and carried out according to technocratic criteria, the only one with the power of decision; then, the working and consuming “masses” are persuaded by propaganda and advertising; finally, once the persuasion has been carried out, the plans are submitted to the “democratic consultation that gives ‘consensus’”34. Thus everything is integrated into the system: “democratized” education (the learning machine is already foreseen), the confusion of language as a sign of the loss of the exercise of thinking35 and, finally, the worldwide technological “organization,” the great totalitarian tyranny, above communism, liberalism and the Church conceived as a Christianity without faith or sense of the supernatural. […] Cultural impiety is, therefore, the most terrible totalitarianism, as an expression of the nihilation of Being.

It would be naive to think that this technocratic totalitarianism exists only in the so-called “people’s democracies” or communist ones, for, as is evident given the massive reach of the media, it also exists in the so-called “Western democracies,” where men are instruments of production for consumption. “The true ‘product’ of this process is the uncultured man, though ‘armed with the most developed technology’ and at the same time capable, out of hatred, of destroying the whole world of culture; it is thus a sort of uneducated ‘uncivil’ man who ‘has only the appearances of civilization, but barbarous customs and laws: civilization falls into incivility’; the right to be well informed is violated daily by the so-called ‘social communication media’ and true anti-democracy slips into populism, because ‘populism is the great enemy of the education of the people, if to educate oneself is to mean to raise oneself up’ (to perfect oneself as a man); there does not and cannot exist a ‘collective’ culture (which is the opposite of culture) nor truly cultural tasks in ‘teams’ or ‘cooperatives’ of ‘thinkers’; and so, through the abuse of these media (cinema, television, social networks, websites, apps), a culture of the image, a substitute for the book… murders the word and thought,’ condemning children (and future men) to ‘chronic infantilism, which is what the civilization of well-being and technology, the anti-culture, looks toward”36.

That is why a contemporary author said that we are heading “toward a dark age with so many interplanetary voyages and other marvels of measurement, all rational calculations without intellect –whose sign is limit– all technical elaboration and manipulation of empirical and verifiable data for social well-being, but without love, which is the sign of the sacred; an age that will taste to the very bottom all the uncivilized misery of man without truth and without God, extreme spiritual destitution”37.

Technocratic cultural impiety also presupposes, as we have just noted, religious impiety, because that “organization” is considered the end of all religions; at bottom, it is considered the “earthly fulfillment of the messianic promises”38. In such an immanent and horizontal society there is room only for a worldly, “new” and, above all, de-Hellenized “Christianity,” since the existence of a transcendent God is not a Christian problem but a Greek one; God died with Christ and, therefore, Christianity “frees itself from God”39; what is more, this vertical fall of the Church includes the de-Romanization of Christianity (the death of the “Church”-institution), and thus the Church can turn to the building of Babylon, the impious society40. In the first case, “a ‘de-Hellenized’ Christianity is a Christianity that has renounced the logos or the natural light of truth and, with it, being, and therefore it is impossible for it to receive Christ as supernatural Logos”41. Without a metaphysics of being (without logos) it is not possible to elaborate dogmatic theology, nor even positive theology, and biblical theology is denatured42. Such, then, is the first picture of religious impiety, which presupposes the loss of being and, therefore, the loss of respect (piety) for everything; in other words, impiety is “the absence of respect for every thing, institution and value”43. Thus the spirit of the world as world imposes itself.

“Hence this immense technocratic totalitarianism, in relation to the Church, advocates, first, the integration of the Church into technocratic society within the shared horizon of a welfare society, thus perfectly accomplishing ‘the Enlightenment and neo-Enlightenment continuity between liberalism, communism and modernism”; secondly, the distortion of the Second Vatican Council so as ‘to prevent the awakening of faith within the welfare society’44”45.

Pope Benedict XVI denounced a few years ago: “Darkness truly threatens man because, yes, he can see and examine tangible, material things, but not where the world is going and where it comes from. Where our own life is going. What is good and what is evil. The darkness concerning God and his values is the real threat to our existence and to the world in general. If God and values, the difference between good and evil, remain in darkness, then all the other illuminations that give us such incredible power are not only progress, but at the same time also threats that put us, and the world, in danger. […] In material things we know and can do so much, but what goes beyond this, God and the good, we no longer manage to identify”46.

The question then arises: What is to be done? “We must get used to unmasking the various ways in which the truth is manipulated, distorted and concealed in public and private spheres. What we call ‘truth’ is not only the spreading of facts carried out by journalism. It is above all the search for the most solid foundations that lie behind our choices and also behind our laws. This presupposes accepting that human intelligence can go beyond the conveniences of the moment and grasp some truths that do not change, that were true before us and will always be so”47. “That is why faith, which shows us the light of God, is the true illumination; it is an irruption of God’s light into our world, an opening of our eyes to the true light”48.

In this sense, it seems essential to us to recall the role of the missions ad gentes carried out also through media of every kind, since these can play an essential role in the work of evangelization and in making available to people the truths and values on which human dignity rests and by which it is perfected, together with the values of transcendence. The presence of the Church in the media is, in fact, an important aspect of the inculturation of the Gospel required by the new evangelization to which the Holy Spirit is calling the Church throughout the world49. And we are convinced that Christ, our Divine Redeemer, is the Good News, ever perennial, which can and must be preached through the various means of communication so as to be heard by the whole world.

2. I am the light of the world50

Let us place ourselves, then, in that Gospel passage which sets us in the city of Jerusalem, during the feast of Tabernacles, and let us contemplate the Incarnate Word. As soon as Jesus appears on the scene, the crowds look for him, though not before muttering a great deal about Him (the evangelist points out to us), since some said ‘he is a good man’ while others said ‘No! he leads the people astray’51. Nevertheless, having gone up to the Temple, Jesus declares to his hearers that his doctrine is the doctrine of God who sent him and of whom He is the Son. In the court of the Temple, there were probably, very near Him, some sumptuous hangings from which hung two lamps that spread their light over the Temple. Our Lord sat there, between those immense lamps that lit up the faces of friends and enemies alike, and at the threshold of the Holy of Holies, He who is thrice Holy proclaimed that the Light of God had come to enlighten men who walked in darkness: I am the Light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life52.

There was no error in his words. He did not say that he was like the light; he did not say that he was like those lamps that lit up the darkness; he did not say that he was the light of some people and not of others; but that he was the very Light itself, which is identified with the Truth and which enlightens every man who comes into this world. To say such a thing, Jesus had to know all things. Yet his audience did not remember having seen him in any of the great schools of Jerusalem, nor having seen him seated at the feet of Gamaliel. Then those who heard him −common people, Pharisees, Levites− looked at one another until they ended up asking him: Who are you? They were astonished to hear him say that his Truth was the Light of the world and that he possessed it from all eternity: You are from below, I am from above. You are of this world, I am not of this world. […] If you remain faithful to my word, you will truly be my disciples: you will know the truth and the truth will make you free53. His hearers, not grasping the great truth that the Incarnate Word was the Light of the world, asked: Are you perhaps greater than our father Abraham, who died?54. Our Lord’s answer was nothing other than an affirmation of his eternity: Abraham, your father, thrilled with joy, hoping to see my Day: he saw it and was filled with gladness. The Jews said to him: ‘You are not yet fifty years old, and you have seen Abraham?’ Jesus answered: ‘I assure you, before Abraham was born, I Am’55.

In saying “I Am,” Yahweh, Jesus identifies himself with the Light, equal to the Truth; that is, he identifies himself with God. That was his battle cry to an erring world, an alarm that rang out for the slaves who were to be set free in the name of the one Truth that makes man free. But just as the noonday sun is too strong for weak eyes, so the Light of the world was too bright for those minds accustomed only to the light of a candle. That is why, in their fury against Him who claimed to be the only Truth of this world, the Light of Life, uncreated Wisdom, the people took up stones to stone him56. But at the very moment when the arms of those who sought to stone him drew back for the swing of the sling, Christ hid himself57proving once more that He was the Truth, for the Truth hides itself from those who seek to kill it and do not seek it with simplicity and humility of heart.

Of them the Holy Father said: “There are people […] who cannot live in the light because they are accustomed to darkness. The light dazzles them; they cannot see. They are human bats: they only know how to move about at night. […] But the worst thing is that the eyes, the eyes of the soul, from living so much in darkness grow so used to it that they end up not knowing what light is. They lose the sense of light because they grow more accustomed to darkness”58.

“What scandalizes people about Jesus is his nature as God incarnate. And as with Him, so with us too ‘they set traps for us in life’”59. And just as Christ suffered opposition, rejection, and even death for bearing witness to the Truth, so must his followers suffer. The Incarnate Word does not deceive us; he told us clearly: They will hand you over to the sanhedrins, and you will be scourged in the synagogues, and you will appear before governors and kings because of Me, to bear witness before them… you will be hated because of my name60. That is why, with wise insight, Ven. Archbishop Fulton Sheen said: “To be tolerated [or accepted] is sometimes a sign of weakness; and to be persecuted, a compliment. The mediocre survive. The person who is persecuted shows that his belief is taken seriously and that the cause he defends must be eliminated if evil is to triumph. It is true that the wicked are persecuted, but they do not fall within the beatitude, for as St. Paul says: if I hand over my body to be burned, but have not charity, it profits me nothing61. A martyr must die for his faith, not for his property, not for his good name, not for his party. Numerous are the martyrs who are the architects of their own martyrdom, but they have no place in the ranks of those to whom the Kingdom of heaven was promised for carrying the cross on their shoulders”62. What we mean by this is that, although many –even “religious people”63− are bent on destroying us, and attack us by becoming “part of networks of verbal violence through the internet and the various forums or spaces of digital exchange, even in Catholic media where defamation and slander tend to be treated as normal, where all ethics and respect for the good name of others seems to be left aside64, our task continues to be to bear witness −in a luminous and singular way65− to the inescapable truth that the Word became flesh… full of grace and truth66.

Now then, consider any other ‘master,’ philosopher, spiritual guru, great thinker, moralist or preacher who has walked upon this earth, no matter which. None identified himself with the truth. They may have said ‘I will show you the way,’ but none said as our Lord did: I am the Way. They may have said: ‘I will tell you how to possess the truth or how to discover the light,’ but none said as our Lord did: I am the Truth; I am the Light of the world. Many may have promised a life that dies no more, but none said as our Lord did: I am the Life. All pointed to an ideal outside themselves. In the case of our Lord, He is the Ideal with a capital letter. There is no system outside Him. His most adorable Person is the system. There is no way outside his Way, no truth outside his Truth, no life outside his Life. There is nothing outside or beyond the Incarnate Word, for in Him all the paths and truths scattered throughout the world find their center and their source, to such a degree that Christ could affirm with all authority: Without me you can do nothing67. Which is as much as to say: ‘you will be able to buy and sell without Me, you will be able to enlarge your buildings and increase your possessions without Me, you will be able to build ships and create incredibly powerful technologies, but you will not be able to take a single step toward the Ideal, which is the divine Life, without Me, because I am the Life. You cannot even go to the Father without Me, because the Father and I are One68.

“The Incarnate Word governs the whole law of relations of the supernatural and the natural order, but He also governs what, within the latter, must be ordered, without unhealthy dialectic and against every false monism”69. That is why the Constitutions tell us very clearly: “Our Lord, [is the] Way we must follow and [the] example we must imitate”70 and the truth we must proclaim. Even when they contradict us or seek to undermine what we have barely managed to raise up with so much sacrifice, even in the midst of the harshest poverty and in spite of our great miseries. Christ, true God and true Man, is and must always be the platform from which we launch ourselves boldly to restore all things71. That is our noble mission, for God wants all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth72, recognizing that Christ himself, who sends us on mission, at the same time promises us his divine help: I will be with you always, until the end of the world73.

To act otherwise would be not only to live far from reality but to betray the very purpose for which God has raised up the Institute. Without the Incarnation of the Word the foundation of our faith is lacking, and without faith, our life collapses.

It is true that, as St. John Paul II pointed out, “the world of the media can sometimes seem an environment as unfavorable to evangelization as the pagan world was in the time of the Apostles. But just as the first witnesses of the Good News did not withdraw when they met hostility, neither must the followers of Christ do so today. The cry of St. Paul still resounds among us: Woe to me if I do not proclaim the Gospel!74”75.

“In a particular way −the Constitutions point out, and we have grown up in religious life hearing it− it is urgent to exercise the apostolate in the so-called ‘modern areopagi.’ Among these new environments is, first of all, the world of communication, on which the work of evangelization of modern culture largely depends, since ‘it is unifying humanity and turning it into a ‘global village’… that is why [the media] are the chief means of information and formation… The new generations, above all, are growing up in a world conditioned by these media”76.

Social networks belong to those modern areopagi of which our Proper Law speaks. And although, as we said at the beginning, many use these platforms to spread lies and sow confusion, they are nonetheless a valid means for proclaiming the truth. In this regard Pope Benedict XVI affirmed: “These technologies are a true gift to humanity and therefore we must ensure that their benefits are placed at the service of all human beings and all communities”77, and after speaking of the “extraordinary potential of the new technologies”78 he exhorted Catholics “to bring the witness of their faith to the digital world”79. For just as “in the early days of the Church, the Apostles and their disciples brought the Good News of Jesus to the Greco-Roman world. Just as then evangelization, in order to bear fruit, needed an attentive understanding of the culture and customs of those pagan peoples, so as to touch their minds and hearts, so also now the proclamation of Christ in the world of the new technologies requires a profound knowledge of them in order to use them appropriately. To you […] belongs in a particular way the task of evangelizing this ‘digital continent.’ Take up with enthusiasm the proclamation of the Gospel to your contemporaries. […] The most valuable gift you can offer them is to share with them the ‘good news’ of a God who became man, suffered, died and rose to save humanity”80.

We would not like to go on without mentioning that, by the grace of God, the Institute uses these media in various parts of the world to promote the cause of Christ and its work of evangelization. Nevertheless, in our view, there is much still left for us to conquer in terms of quality in the presentation of the message and of pedagogy when presenting topics that are in themselves very profound.

* * * * *

Jesus, the Incarnate Word, is the Light of God that “makes encounter possible. It makes communication possible. It makes knowledge possible, access to reality, to truth”81. Therefore, for us it will always be a duty and a privilege to proclaim the truth, the glorious truth about human life and human destiny revealed in the Word made flesh. That is why we religious of the Incarnate Word must affirm not only with our lips, but with the witness of our life and with our works of apostolate, that the “Word is truly the Truth”82. For “everything in Him is transparency, authenticity, sincerity, coherence, truth: I am… the Truth83 […] In Him there is nothing empty, hollow, or not hypostatically assumed. There is nothing of varnish or shell. Nothing feigned or camouflaged. Nothing of lies, falsehood, insecurity, concealment or hypocrisy. He is One Alone84 –the Word– in two distinct natures, both perfect, whole and hypostatically united”85.

Today and always let us raise high the standard of “Our One Master and Lord Jesus Christ86, in whom is contained a reference to all creatures. [For] Christ is the point of union and meeting of the comprehensor and the wayfarer; of the Master87 and the slave88; of the century and the second; of the universe and the atom; of the from now on of salvation and the until now of the promises; of the not yet of what we hope for and the already accomplished; he is the point of union and meeting of God and man; of the eschatological and the incarnate; of sidereal distances and the millimeter. That is why nothing escapes Christ: neither space travel, nor the world of electronics and computers, of the most current science or the most perfected technology, nor recent discoveries, nor the family, work, culture, the economic-political sphere, etc.”89.

Let us ourselves learn, and teach others, to follow the wise counsel of St. Ignatius of Antioch: “Stop your ears when you hear anyone speak of anything that does not have Christ Jesus as its foundation…”90 and let us not cease “to attempt everything so that the love of Christ may have supreme primacy in the Church and in society”91.

Yes, it is true, the preaching of the Word made flesh is a scandal that brings with it the persecution of the world. But it is well to remember: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God; and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not of God; this is the spirit of the Antichrist, which you have heard is coming, and now is already in the world92.

Let us not be deaf to the anguished appeal that the Incarnate Word makes to us from the Cross:

“I am the Light, and you do not look at me.

I am the Way, and you do not follow me.

I am the Truth, and you do not believe me.

I am the Life, and you do not seek me.

I am the Master, and you do not listen to me.

I am the Lord, and you do not obey me.

I am your best Friend, and you do not love me.

If you are unhappy, do not blame me”93.

Let us quench the thirst for God and relieve the spiritual destitution of men with the clear and courageous preaching that the Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us… full of grace and truth94.
