Solemnity of St. Joseph, Spouse of Mary March 19, 2019

Mt 1:16. 18-21. 24

[Exordium] Dear brothers and sisters, today we contemplate the figure, so dear and close to the heart of all of us and of the Church herself, of St. Joseph, spouse of the Virgin, protector of the Incarnate Word and true servant of God. Inspired by the words of our dear St. John Paul II, who said that reflecting on the participation of the Spouse of Mary in the divine mystery will help us to find our identity within the redemptive plan1, it seemed to me that it may be useful for each Servant sister, but also for the Institute of the Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará as a whole, to reflect on three attributes of St. Joseph: as spouse, as father and as servant, since each of them contains a cluster of virtues which, exercised as he did in the domestic setting of our religious life, can help us -and a great deal- to attain perfection and, in the end, bring us to “our home,” which is heaven.

1. Spouse

St. Joseph was in all truth the spouse of Mary. In fact, it seems as though the evangelists delight in referring to him as the spouse of the Virgin of Nazareth. We heard it a moment ago in the Gospel of St. Matthew: Jacob begot Joseph, the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ2. St. Luke for his part speaks of Mary as a virgin betrothed to a man named Joseph3. And the angel Gabriel himself says to this just man: Do not be afraid to take Mary your wife into your home, for what has been conceived in her is the work of the Holy Spirit. She will give birth to a son, whom you shall name Jesus, because he will save the people from their sins4.

These last words of the angel to Joseph are what is known as the ‘annunciation’ of St. Joseph, in which he hears not only the divine truth about the ineffable vocation of his spouse, but also the truth about his own vocation5. The Gospel goes on to say that St. Joseph did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him6.

And I would like to dwell on that, because such must also be the attitude of each Servant sister: “the attitude proper to the ‘third class of persons,’”7 which gives itself entirely to God and to his plan –as our Directory of Spirituality says, and these are words that must be weighed– without diminutions or retractions, without reservations or conditions, without subterfuges or delays, without retreats or sluggishness8.

The Magisterium of the Church teaches that “the just man of Nazareth possessed above all the characteristics proper to a husband”9 and, as the evangelist says, he was a good husband10, with all that this entails, which I would like to sum up with that saying of St. John of the Cross: “Whoever is truly in love at once lets himself lose everything else in order to gain more in what he loves”11. For at the cost of no small personal sacrifices St. Joseph knew how to be the pillar of his family, the defender of the greatest treasures a man could possess on this earth, absolutely devoted to his family, for there was nothing that could distract him from the eminent and prior place that his family held in his mind and in his heart. And St. Joseph did all this, as the Mystical Doctor of Fontiveros says, “without seeking gain or reward, but only to lose everything and himself in his will for love of them”12.

St. Joseph made –and I want to emphasize this– a complete oblation of himself, of his life, of his work, of his heart, of all his talents, in order to place them at the service of his family. And so he let himself lose everything else in order to gain in what he loved most: the Incarnate Word and his Most Holy Mother. Because –we know it– love, if it is true, has nothing to do with calculations.

How instructive, then, is the example of St. Joseph for all the members of our Religious Family, called like him to “live always through Jesus and through Mary, with Jesus and with Mary, in Jesus and in Mary, for Jesus and for Mary”13.

Certainly St. Joseph was the first to bow before the Virgin of Nazareth when She became the Mother of God and the Queen of this world. But it is no less true that St. Joseph exercised over the Blessed Virgin the sweet and gentle authority of a husband solicitous for her well-being and happiness, and therefore his Most Sweet Spouse was not only subject to him in everything, but with attentive and provident love took care of him (what is more, we can say that She was the first devotee of St. Joseph).

In the intimacy of the house of Nazareth, she who is –in the words of St. Louis Marie– “the reflection of eternal light, the spotless mirror of the majesty of God and the image of his goodness”14, with great affection was always ready to serve him with all the deference and delicacy of a wife, to attend to his needs whatever they might be, to listen to him attentively, to respect him in his role as head of the family, to follow his directions. Though She was the Mother of God, it never occurred to her to have a plan different from that of her husband, or to go another way, or as we would say today, ‘to have a different agenda.” Simply because the Virgin recognized in her husband “the one whom the heavenly Father willed to make, on earth, the man of his confidence”15, her protector and the “governor of Incarnate Wisdom”16. Both knew themselves to be united, though in different roles, to the same and single plan of redemption.

That is why an author says that the Most Holy Spouse of St. Joseph, with her ineffable sweetness, was the one who inflamed the heart of her most chaste spouse with holy ardor, who enlightened his mind and gave strength to his arm. For his part, St. Joseph also knew that he could always go to Her in search of relief for his soul, in search of genuine and enthusiastic help for his labor and his struggles, of refreshment on his pilgrimage, of fortitude and courage in adversity, and of someone with whom to share his joy in his achievements. All with great confidence, knowing themselves to be members of one same family with a common project.

How can we fail to see in the example of the Blessed Virgin and St. Joseph the great models to imitate in the joint work of our Institutes as parts of “one same Family with an identical specific end”17, which is none other than “to love and serve, and to make Jesus Christ loved and served”18, precisely as they did. Because our union, dear sisters, is not reduced to the Constitutions and the Directories: we have a common plan, a mission entrusted by God to both Institutes as members of one single Religious Family within his Church. It follows, therefore, that our union must be complete, constant and unalterable if we truly want to be faithful and to render Him some service effectively; knowing how to immolate ourselves in a real way each day19 in order to attain the ideal of our Religious Family, which is none other than that He reign20. And this brings me to the second attribute of St. Joseph.

2. Guardian of the Incarnate Word

“The Incarnate Word remained hidden for 30 years: he hid himself in the shadow of Joseph”21.

Truly St. Joseph consecrated his life to caring, with the deep affection of a father, for the Son of God born of Mary. And to caring for him in every sense: education, food, clothing, in his piety, teaching him a trade, forming him in the virtues. But St. Joseph was also his guardian, his staunchest defender, and that is why he did not fear to leave behind everything familiar and journey to an unknown country to protect him. For that reason St. Bernardine of Siena goes so far as to call St. Joseph “savior of our Savior,” “teacher of Incarnate Wisdom”22 .

In other words, what I mean is that St. Joseph knew how to place the exercise of his fatherhood at the service and in the guardianship of the person and mission of the Incarnate Word.

And are we not called to do the same when we are invited, as missionaries, to forge that love of a father and of a mother that devotes itself without reserve and without looking back to the service of Jesus Christ23?

We may even think that St. Joseph will be very pleased because on this day we want to ask the Lord for all the Servants and, to tell the truth, for every member of our Religious Family, the grace of that maternal wisdom that instills that wholly provident affection which goes ahead to meet the unspoken needs of one’s own (and when I say one’s own I also mean the priests of the IVE); which carries charity into the smallest detail; which makes its hidden and silent service to one’s own a luminous and joyful sacrifice; which defends one’s own even with a look; which knows how to spare them sorrow and work by putting itself at their disposal; which receives with a strong heart the worries and annoyances that cloud their faces; which never keeps a tally of the things it did for those it loves, because the happiness of every Servant must lie in giving herself “without measure, to the extreme, until she can give no more, to the end”24.

Because seeking the limelight, thinking ourselves self-sufficient, multiplying demands, lack of docility, pretending that attacks on our Institutes do not affect oneself, not taking a genuine and effective interest in the needs of each Institute, that is, of our one Religious Family — none of this has anything to do with the example of St. Joseph, much less with the spirit of our Religious Family.

Always keep in mind that each one of you, like St. Joseph, is called to “live for the Incarnate Word and to let yourselves be guided by Him”25. Therefore, says our Proper Law, “you must be zealous for the honor and glory of the Word”26. That is the humble and mature way in which the Servants serve; that is how they “participate” in the economy of salvation, precisely as the adoptive father of Jesus did.

3. Servant of the Incarnate Word

Lastly, the third attribute with which the Church honors St. Joseph is that of having been the faithful and prudent servant of the very Son of God.

Indeed, St. Joseph possessed an “absolute availability to serve faithfully the saving will of God”27 and in all truth “gave himself entirely to serving the Incarnate Word”28 with heroic fidelity to the commitments assumed before God.

In a few minutes we will say the Prayer over the Offerings, which asks precisely that we be granted the same love and purity of heart that St. Joseph had in serving Jesus. And what prayer could be more fitting to pronounce here with all fervor together with you, who bear the honorable name of “Servants of the Lord.”

If a true Servant must love and give herself to the point of the heroism of self-giving without reserve29, then what is proper to the Servants is to give all of themselves, and their very life with it, for the greater glory and service of the Incarnate Word and, according to the order of charity, to those of the Religious Family in the first place. This requires on your part “not a halfway self-giving; not a self-giving only in some places or in some things; not a self-giving at some moments; but a total and full self-giving, in whatever place and time it may be”30, just as Joseph of Nazareth did.

Since, then, “the oblation of oneself”31 is the characteristic that must stand out most in the Servants, how the exhortation you have received from the very beginning must resound in you!: “None of so much selfishness, so much pettiness, so much calculation! We must go for total self-giving!”32. In that spirit must live, and in that spirit must be formed, the many vocations that, through the intercession of the glorious Patriarch, we trust will come to be part of the Institute of the Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará.

And yet, how often we ourselves obstruct this perfect love for the Incarnate Word, fearful that in having Him we will be left with nothing else. We hesitate to venture out and risk everything for God; we fear losing something if we follow his plan, without realizing that with Him we have everything! That is not the example of the spouse of Mary.

Giving oneself entirely and out of love to the Family will never be to the detriment either of oneself or of one’s own Institute. On the contrary! It will increase in us forgetfulness of self, the spirit of faith, detachment from our own judgment and our own plans, even detachment from our own goods, now placed at the service of the common project; all of which must redound in fidelity to the intention of the Founder, in joy and deep peace, because then we will be doing what God asks of us, and in the way He asks it of us. All of this will contribute to the fullness and fruitfulness of our religious life, and I believe it will be the greatest contribution we can make to the cause of the evangelization of culture.

And at this point I want to take the opportunity to thank all the Sisters who, throughout the length and breadth of this world, silently, simply, render us countless services. Which, let me tell you, in many cases represents an irreplaceable support for many of us priests.

Like St. Joseph and like the First Servant, always hold high the magnificent title of Servants. Being aware that to be a Servant implies living in the “more” and in the “above and beyond”33. Do not be afraid when love becomes demanding. Do not be afraid when love asks for sacrifices. Do not be afraid of the cross of Christ. Because the cross is the source of all joy and peace34 and the only way to reach the “most high embrace”35 of union with God, which is given, as St. John of the Cross says, “to those already exercised and tested in the service of the Bridegroom”36.

Take this thought home with you today: All the wealth of a Servant consists in giving herself to the Word37, and that is and must always be your “particular style of sanctification and apostolate”38.

[Peroratio] So then, through the intercession of St. Joseph, today I invite you to join me in asking the Lord for the whole Institute of the Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará, in addition to the graces I have already mentioned, these three graces:

1) the grace to excel in “humble service and generous self-giving, that is, in the gratuitous gift of yourselves through a love to the extreme”39 on behalf of the ideals of the Religious Family.

2) The fatherly and provident protection of the Foster Father of the Incarnate Word for all the Sisters in all their missions.

3) The grace of many vocations –active and contemplative– who, in imitation of the First Servant and of St. Joseph, want to live and die in the joyful and solicitous service of the Incarnate Word.

We ask Jesus to grant them to us through the glorious Patriarch St. Joseph and his Most Holy Spouse and our Mother, the Virgin of Luján.
