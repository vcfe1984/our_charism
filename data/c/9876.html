[Exordium] In yesterday’s homily we considered and contemplated that profound and ineffable gift that Christ has given to every consecrated soul, particularly to women religious, which is that of having called them to the dignity of brides, He Himself becoming, in the fullest sense, the Divine Bridegroom of that soul. Already in this life, thus anticipating −by an ineffable choice of love− the mystical espousals of eternal heaven. Christ, bridegroom of virgins. Let us now look, in today’s homily, at this same truth but from the other angle, from the perspective of the bride. More concretely, at what this ineffable reality entails for every soul that has been chosen to travel through this life as bride of the Word, … intimately united to Him, precisely in spousal love.

1. THE RELIGIOUS AS BRIDE

What we considered yesterday is certainly to be admired: what Christ does for that soul whom He chooses to become, by a special title, His divine bride. We have seen His love of election, by which He chose her among so many (this is a great truth); His love of self-giving, by which in some way He gave Himself up for her, giving Himself in a mystical surrender of love; His exclusive love, since there is no greater love than this, spousal love. Let us pause for a moment to consider what this means for each of the religious souls whom Our Lord has wished to honor with so sublime a title.

To become a bride −even on the natural level, how much more on the supernatural− requires a whole disposition of the heart. While it presupposes a choice on the part of the lover, it also implies, on the part of the beloved, a correspondence of love in self-giving. Hence the bride, so to speak, moved by the gift of love that has been shown, must dispose herself to return love for love. If God has given Himself to her and has declared Himself to her in everything, it is also necessary, for there to be an authentic espousal, that the bride too give herself totally to that love: St. John Chrysostom reminded virgins: “consider that He has given Himself to you without reserve”; do the same yourselves.

In the first place, I consider that there are three characteristics with which the bride must correspond to the love of the bridegroom:

A. It must be a love that is one, which implies:

In the first place, to be souls of a single love. Every religious must know that, by that divine union of love, she must cancel all other loves in order to live for Him alone (as wives do). And since His love was sublime, they must dispose themselves to live with a sublime love, alone for Him. They must live as if only their Divine Bridegroom existed. She must be for Him alone, as good wives are with their husbands. There is nothing else that should matter, there is nothing else she should think about, and even less −if the love is true− is there any other love that should be put before Him. For Him alone; that is why St. John of Ávila said that it belongs to the religious, by virtue of her divine espousal, to sigh for the Bridegroom: “To be the bride of Jesus Christ she must be a dove. The bride of this Lord must also be a turtledove. Why a turtledove? Because its song is a moan. The song and laughter of the nun must be weeping for her betrothed, Christ. In the choir, in the garden, in the refectory, in the cell and everywhere, she must go about sighing for her bridegroom Jesus Christ, and this must be her office and this must be her song, and when she is singing in her choir with the sweetest melody, her heart must be sighing with an interior and very deep sigh for her most beloved Bridegroom, whose memory and desire must never depart from her heart. With this she must go to sleep, and of this she must dream while sleeping, and this must be the first thing that comes to her memory on waking; her heart always melted in His love, and her memory occupied with nothing other than her beloved Bridegroom”1.

It is the same St. John of Ávila who says that only one thing belongs to the bride, and that is to look after the honor of the Bridegroom; he writes it in a very beautiful dialogue. “See also that you must take great care, as good brides, of the honor of your bridegroom. Do you know what is the first thing the bridegroom says to his bride, especially if he is jealous? He says to her: ‘My bride, see that my honor is in your hands, and that all my honor depends on you. For the sake of the one God, I ask you to look after me (these are words that each one of us must feel addressed to himself).’” What will the bride say to this, if she is good? “I will look after your honor; I will never do wrong to your honor.” Especially if the bridegroom were to say to her: “My bride, see that the one who is pursuing you does not do so out of love he has for you, but out of hatred he has for me, so that by dishonoring you he may dishonor me.” What will the bride answer when someone wishes to deceive her with some other love: “Away, traitor, for you are not pursuing me because you love me, but to dishonor my bridegroom … if I looked only at myself, perhaps I would consent to the temptation, but by this you want to dishonor my bridegroom, and that is why you will take nothing from here, but you will go away defeated by the favor of my bridegroom, because where you thought to dishonor him and me, you are the one left dishonored, and my bridegroom is left with the honor, for I owe it to him.”

Very beautifully said: it belongs to the religious to look after the honor of her Divine Bridegroom. Her love must never be soiled by another love; it must be unique. St. Francis of Assisi said that the

bride should say: “You are my honor, my fortune and my delights: You are everything to me.” St. Paul declared it: “The virgin consecrated to God thinks of nothing but God and of being wholly God’s” (1 Car 7:34-35).

In the second place, a love that is one also implies being souls who are united by love, who take on each other’s likeness through love. This too is required of every bride, and in a particular way of the bride of the Word. Spousal love necessarily demands unity, and intimate unity: they are no longer two but one flesh, says Genesis. Bridegroom and bride are made one in love; they are one in their love. That is why the same John of Ávila teaches that the bride and the bridegroom must be of the same condition; this is her obligation, to become like the bridegroom: “What more is there to do, ladies, brides of Jesus Christ, than that you have the qualities of your Bridegroom, so that you may be one with Him.” This is an imperative of spousal love; we see it in the world too… a marriage is stable when they are one, or, as is commonly said, when one is for the other, or when they are a match in being one. For St. John of Ávila says: “There is nothing more foolish than for two people of very different qualities to be joined into one.” So then, ladies, to be what you ought to be, since you are brides of Jesus Christ, you must imitate Him in His qualities, so that you may be conformed to Him, as it is right that the bride be to the bridegroom, and to such a bridegroom as the one you have, ladies.” That is why there is so much insistence that the life of the religious must be like that of Christ; it must be like another humanity of His, in which no division can be found. Just as one cannot conceive of a wife who does not unite herself with her husband, one cannot conceive of a bride who does not have a love affair with Jesus Christ. Why? Because the love is spousal; that is why they must be one with Him… like wives … otherwise they are not brides.

B. It must be an indissoluble love …

Like that of spouses. Such must be the unity forged between the spouses that there can be nothing that wounds the love or shows any symptom of dissolubility. If there is dissolubility, it is because the love is cold, it is wounded. That is why in the spousal love of the soul with God −as Sacred Scripture recalls so many times− there must necessarily be involved a resolute hatred of sin, of disordered affections, of false loves, in a word, of everything that could threaten the dissolution of love.

This is very beautiful, and it is very consoling to think about. He is the most faithful of all bridegrooms; if −God forbid− there were a dissolution, that dissolution would be because of our fidelity. If we are faithful, then it is a love that can never be broken. The Bridegroom is the most faithful of all; if the love between a bride of Christ breaks down, it will always be because of the infidelity of the bride, because in some way she is betraying that love, and worse still, she is despising that love. The dissolubility of marriage implies an infidelity to love; this occurs on the part of one of the spouses, but it can never occur on the part of Jesus Christ, since He is faithful. St. Paul emphasizes this truth forcefully when he writes: This statement is certain: if we have died with Him, we shall also live with Him; if we remain firm, we shall also reign with Him; if we deny Him, He too will deny us; if we are unfaithful, He remains faithful, for He cannot deny Himself (2 Tim 2:11-13). The religious bride must therefore dispose herself −like every bride− to give herself in a love of fidelity, as faithful as is the love the bridegroom offers her. That is why the letter to the Hebrews rightly recommends: Of the one who is called, what is asked of him is that he be faithful.

C. It must be a love for ever:

God chose you for an eternal love, not only indissoluble but also irrevocable. Vocations, like the gifts of God, are irrevocable, the letter to the Hebrews also teaches. “If He chose you, be sure that it is neither a false choice nor a fragile choice; it is an eternal choice.” That is why women religious are very rightly called brides of the new and eternal covenant. And this name is full of meaning, even though we cannot develop it here in its full extent. To be brides of the new covenant means to be brides forever. Your vocation is eternal, says the prophet Hosea. Such is the religious vocation; it has, then, resonances of eternity. That is why the brides of the Word, because they are brides of the New Testament (not like those of the Old), are brides forever; this is their vocation, a vocation guaranteed by the most stable fidelity. The spousal union between God and His people in the Old Testament (in the old covenant) proved fragile; the unfaithful people destroyed it and broke the covenant, as the prophet Jeremiah declares (they destroyed my covenant). In the New Testament it is not so; the covenant between God and His people was made decisively with the precious seal of the Blood of Christ; it is solid, stable and therefore eternal. Women religious, brides of the Word, are religious of this covenant. “A covenant full of love, since it was achieved thanks to the fact that the Divine Bridegroom penetrated so deeply into the interior of the bride that He changed her heart so that she would be His forever.” To be brides of the new covenant means to be brides of the berit olam, that is, brides forever. I found the one whom my soul loves; mine he is and I will not let him go (Song 3:4).

2. THE PECULIARITIES OF BEING A BRIDE OF THE WORD

In the second place, I also want to say that in the spousal love between each religious and the Incarnate Word there are certain sublime peculiarities that cannot be left aside. Rather, it is necessary to know them very well, to keep them very much in mind, in order to realize all the purity this love signifies, to know all that it implies, and also to come to know all the delights it holds. Of all these peculiarities I want to set forth only four, which characterize very well the greatness of the bridegroom’s love:

A. The religious is a bride through faith and in faith. The mystical espousal is made through faith and in faith in the Son of God. The Lord says it through the prophet Hosea: I will make you my bride through faith (Hos 2:10). This implies very sublime and profound considerations. The spousal knowledge between the bridegroom and the bride will be constituted by the sublime knowledge of faith, the highest of all, the most spiritual of all, the purest, the deepest and the most complete, the most solid and secure, since it rests on the very knowledge that God has of Himself. To this He calls you. In some way, by this spousal union and by this choice of love, the bride is invited to know more of the depths of God than any other soul. Her union is mystical and spiritual, not banal and carnal. The deepest of unions, the union of intentions, of soul and of heart, is the union that nothing and no one will be able to break. Such is the union, such is the love. That is why every religious, however alone she may sometimes be, however far away she may be, however afflicted or tormented she may be in martyrdom, must have an imperturbable security and peace. Why? Because her Bridegroom will always be beside her. This guarantees and seals the union in a deep, stable and lasting way. It is the purest love because it is spiritual, not conditioned by anything carnal; it is the deepest and most profound purity of the notion of love; and that is also why its joys are ineffable. For this it is enough to read the writings of any of the great women mystics when they speak of the delights of the spiritual marriage: St. Catherine of Siena, St. Teresa of Jesus or Sister Elizabeth of the Trinity.

Hence the imperative requirement to grow in the life of prayer, which is precisely where the union of love in this divine marriage takes place. It is there that the delights with the Bridegroom must be found; it is there that they must experience the joys of this sublime love. Pope John Paul II said it in that well-known Wednesday audience: “Among all the values present in feminine consecrated life, first place must always be given to prayer. It is the principal form of carrying out and expressing intimacy with the Divine Bridegroom. All women religious are called to be women of prayer, women of piety, women of interior life, of ‘a life of prayer.’

B. Fecundity through the cross: The world thinks that when a virgin consecrates herself, she renounces being a mother, but it is very mistake To accept being a bride of the Incarnate Word is to dispose oneself for the very opposite; it is to dispose oneself to be the most fruitful of all mothers. Her fruitfulness will be all the greater −as with every bride− the greater the unity she keeps with her Divine Bridegroom, He

who accomplished the most fruitful work the world has ever beheld, since He made the redemption of men fruitful through the tree of the cross. “Hence appears the dimension of fruitfulness proper to consecrated virginity, inasmuch as there is a deeper participation in the sacrifice of Christ for the redemption of the world.” That is why the unity of fruitfulness with the Divine Bridegroom must be realized by embracing Him on the cross. The bridegroom and the bride must be of the same condition in order to be fruitful; that is why she must love the cross as Christ loved it; on it she must meet Him and on it she must rejoice with Him. Fr. Buela rightly said in one of his homilies that women religious are brides of blood. The cross is the bed of love, for it is on the cross and in the fruitfulness of the cross that they were espoused with a passionate love of God for being a soul, a passionate love of Jesus for His virgin brides.

That is why St. Alphonsus says that: “although it is true that Jesus Christ died for all, He died especially for virgins, His brides.” And that is why St. Magdalene de’ Pazzi rightly says that, the religious having been called to be the Bride of the Crucified, she must look at nothing in her whole life and in all her actions except Jesus Crucified, and must occupy herself with nothing except considering the great love that her Divine Bridegroom had for her. And St. John of Ávila: “The life of the bride must be like that of her Bridegroom. What was His life? You know it well: labors, tears, poverty, humility and, finally, so great a love for His bride that for love of her He shed His blood, to make beautiful His bride, who had been disfigured, and He died on the cross to give her life, because she was dead… Look, ladies, at who your Bridegroom is and how great is the love He has for you and with which He loves you. Has there ever been a bridegroom in the world who drew blood from his veins to make perfume for his bride? It has never been seen. Well, see it here. You have a Bridegroom, ladies, who broke open His veins, who opened His side, to make a perfume to beautify you and a medicine with which you might be healed of your illnesses and wounds. Do you see here the life of your Bridegroom and the love He has for you? Well then, your life, ladies, must be like this: tears, poverty, humility, contempt, obedience, and the more of this you have, the more like your Bridegroom you will be, and likewise the more beloved of His you will be, because you are more like Him; and you must love Him so much that you would shed your Blood for Him, if need be, and lay down your life for whatever concerns His honor”… and he continues: “blood you must shed, ladies, for love of your Bridegroom” […]. And so it is fitting that, since your Bridegroom went by this road, she would not be a good bride who did not go by it.” How well St. Alphonsus put it when he said: “The crucified bridegroom wants crucified brides.”

C. Spiritual motherhood. If fruitful, then mothers. For women religious, together with the Divine Bridegroom, beget through their sorrows and their sufferings, through their intimate union with the Bridegroom by means of prayer and of the cross with Him, souls for eternal life, souls for the life of grace. As motherhood goes, there is no motherhood greater or more sublime than this: to beget for eternal life. Physical motherhood, which begets children for death, bears not even a shadow of comparison; spiritual motherhood, on the other hand, begets children for eternity. That is why women religious are made like, in a most particular way, to the Blessed Virgin, who was mother of grace, she who begot us to a life much greater and more perfect and beautiful than the one to which our natural mothers begot us. Not to realize this is not to understand the greatness of consecrated virginity, which, far from renouncing fruitfulness, entails a disposition to become mothers in a much fuller and more perfect sen se… like the Blessed Virgin Mary. The Pope said it: “Virginity entails a renunciation of physical motherhood, but in order to be translated, according to the divine plan, into a motherhood of a higher order, upon which shines the light of the motherhood of the Virgin Mary. All consecrated virginity is destined to receive from the Lord a gift that reproduces in a certain measure the characteristics of the universality and spiritual fruitfulness of Mary’s motherhood. I suppose you know this, but do you realize what it means? The world does not understand; it comes to think that it means a failure … it is not to under stand at all what it means for a soul to be a bride of the Word.”

D. Finally, a peculiarity of the bride of the Word must be her joy. From all that has been said there follows, then, the source of joy that the mystical espousals of the cros signify. Here too there is much to think about and much to say, but we cannot say it here. How poor is the joy that a married woman in this world can attain compared with that sublime joy, of a higher and spiritual order, which belongs to the one who is called to be a bride of Jesus Christ. To the bride of the Word −says St. Alphonsus− belong the greatest delights and the greatest joys. “Oh, my ladies, brides of Jesus Christ, who could tell you of the endearments and conversations, so gentle and loving, that pass between the bride who thus leaves everything to occupy herself with her bridegroom alone! Who could tell you of the gentle music that such a bride hears from the cross of her beloved, the embraces so loving, the gifts so truly those of a bridegroom that pass between these two beloved ones! You, ladies, as people through whom this will have passed and will pass each day, could better inform those of us who do not know of this matter. Yours is this office, and to you it belongs to know this; and whoever does not know this does not yet fully know how to be a true bride …. Do you remember, ladies, what I said here on another occasion. That God did not give the sweet food of manna to the people of Israel until the flour of Egypt had run out for them? So, then, your Bridegroom will not give you the manna of His consolation and delight if first the flour of the delight that we ourselves seek does not run out for us, and if first the consolation of Egypt, the consolation we had on earth, does not run out.” Only by giving yourselves to Him “will you be good brides and appear beautiful before His eyes.” In short, there is no bridegroom who can make his bride happier, with the most sublime of his joys, than the Divine Bridegroom.

[Peroratio] Finally, to conclude for today, each religious must feel in her heart the question that Christ asked Peter and the other disciples at the Last Supper after having washed their feet: ¿Scitis quid facerim vobis? “Do you understand what I have done to you?” We can say the same now, and −I continue with St. John of Ávila− “your beloved bridegroom Jesus Christ can say to you: do you understand what I have done to you? Do you understand the favors the Lord has done you in setting you apart from the world and choosing you as His brides? Do you understand it, ladies? Do you comprehend what He has done with you? Then have it well understood. That before you were born, or even conceived, and long before −what am I saying, long!−, infinitely before the world was made, God already had all of you who are here inscribed in His breast to be His brides… God had chosen all of you from among so many thousands of people, not only so that you might know Him by faith, like other Christians, but precisely from among the other Christians He chose you for a vocation as sublime as that of being His brides.” Understand it well… Then have it well understood.
