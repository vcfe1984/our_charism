Cross in Lithuania

“And I, once lifted up from the earth, will draw all to Myself”
(Jn 12:32)

After Mass we go in procession with hymns to the place where the Cross of the IVE is planted on the Hill of Crosses.

On arriving in front of the cross, the celebrant says:

[We have gathered here, on the Hill of Crosses, a mystical place of Christendom in Lithuania, to proclaim to the world that]

The Cross of our Lord —the means chosen by infinite wisdom “to give life to many and in this way to transfigure the world”1— is also for us what it was for Christ: “the greatest proof of love”2. For the Cross “was the chair from which [the Incarnate Word] taught, the altar on which he was immolated, the temple of his prayer, the arena where he fought, and as it were the forge from which so many wonders came forth”3. And therefore, from the beginnings of our beloved Institute, the Cross that the Incarnate Word chose for Himself has been the noble emblem that identifies us and the solemn proclamation that, for our members, the exceedingly great and sacred task of the evangelization of culture is a cross; and that the cross is our program and the royal road we have chosen in order to follow the Incarnate Word “amid the persecutions that are never lacking to the Church”4, and that it is in this very cross that we find all joy and all hope fulfilled on this earth.

That is why this Cross, which today we are to plant in this soil, is an image of the perennial and fervent desire that our little Religious Family be “never folded in upon itself, but open like the arms of Christ on the Cross, whose arms were dislocated from opening them so wide in love”5, so that its present and future members, “taking up their cross [courageously] and sharing in the sufferings of Christ”6, may cooperate in “the most divine of divine works”7, which is the eternal salvation of souls, and that in this way our world may be transfigured.

May this Cross, which bears at its center the coat of arms of our Institute, be a sign of our commitment to bear witness before the world that in the Cross of Christ is found all Truth and the very Wisdom that must be preached. May our Religious Family, in the shadow of this Cross —which “is the mighty weapon of Christ, the staff of the Good Shepherd, with which the divine David goes out to fight the infernal Goliath and with which he knocks with authority at the door of heaven and it is opened to him”8— find all its strength in weakness, all its riches in its most austere poverty, and the most consoling joy in the midst of arduous trials.

“O triumphant Cross of Christ,

inspire us to continue the task of evangelization!

O glorious Cross of Christ,

strengthen us to proclaim

and to live the Gospel of salvation!

O victorious Cross of Christ,

our only hope,

lead us to the joy and peace

of the Resurrection and eternal life!”9

Prayer of blessing

When the homily is over, the celebrant, standing before the cross with hands extended, says the prayer of blessing (which may be preceded by a hymn):

We bless you, Lord, holy Father, who in the excess of your love obtained for us the remedy of salvation and of life on the tree from which the first man had drawn ruin and death. For when the hour of his Passover came, Jesus the Lord, priest, teacher and king, freely ascended the tree of the cross and made it the throne of his glory, the altar of his sacrifice, the chair of truth. There, lifted up above the earth, he conquered the ancient enemy and, clothed in the purple of his blood, drew all men to himself, full of love; there, with arms outstretched, he made to you, Father, the offering of his life and poured saving power into the sacraments of the New Covenant; there he taught by his death what he had earlier announced by word: that the grain of wheat, when it dies, bears abundant fruit. Therefore we beseech you, Lord, that your faithful, in venerating this sign of salvation, may receive the fruits of redemption that Christ Jesus merited by his Passion; that on the cross they may put their sins to death and that, by the power of this cross, they may overcome pride and strengthen their weakness; that in it they may find consolation in their afflictions and security in their dangers; and that, protected by its power, they may travel unharmed along the roads of this world, until you, Father, receive them into the heavenly Homeland. Through Jesus Christ our Lord.

R. Amen.

The cross is then sprinkled with holy water and incensed while a hymn is sung.

X

Prayer after the blessing of the Cross:

Eternal Father, Lord of history,

who in your designs of infinite mercy

willed that your Son become man to redeem man

by his death on the Cross.

Incarnate Word, “who died and rose again,

who suffered misunderstanding, persecution and condemnation at the hands of the powerful;

who, after passing among us doing good to all by word and deed,

healing the sick and feeding the hungry,

allowed yourself to be nailed to the Cross”10.

Holy Spirit, who strengthen with every virtue11 the followers of the Crucified according to the might of his glory, for all patience and long-suffering with joy12.

O Most Holy Trinity, receive this Cross which today rises up to your throne imploring the grace of “the folly of the cross, which makes one wiser than the wisdom of all men”13 for all the members, present and future, of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word.

Incarnate Word, to whom God the Father granted the Name above every name, and in whose Name we are gathered, grant us perseverance to the end in the honorable office of serving you “even in the most difficult situations and in the most adverse conditions”14.

Christ Crucified, who said when I am lifted up from the earth I will draw all to Myself15, grant us the grace that, through the witness and preaching of your Triumph on the Cross, “men and women, and particularly the young, may be supernaturally drawn to follow your way of life solely by the glory of the Cross, the wisdom and power of God”16.

Sweet Christ Crucified, grant your missionaries to fight with confidence in the power of your cross and to raise aloft throughout the whole world the precious standard of your Cross.

May the light of your cross, O Lord,

illumine and give meaning to all our crosses.

This we implore of you, we your children of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, through the loving intercession of your Mother and our Mother, the steadfast Witness at the foot of your Cross.

Today and always, Lord, may the Triumph of the Cross be our triumph.

Amen.

Final blessing of the Celebration
