Homily preached to the monks of the Incarnate Word on the occasion of the feast of the Holy Family

Homily in Getafe – St. Mother Maravillas December 30, 2018

Lk 2:41-52

Today we celebrate the beautiful feast of the Holy Family, which presents to us the sorrowful episode of the loss of the Child Jesus in the temple, which was, according to several authors, the greatest sorrow of the heart of the Mother of the Incarnate Word.

The Gospel narrative allows us to imagine the life of the Holy Family in Nazareth, interrupted only by the obligations of religion, as we have just read: they used to go to Jerusalem every year for the feast of Passover.

Jesus, Son of Mary, who at that time was surely 12 years old, walked at his Mother’s side. Let us think with how much tenderness the Virgin would contemplate her Son, and how each minute he would seem to her more beautiful than the moment before. After arriving at the gates of the city, the Holy Family went to the Temple for the celebration of the Passover, and there St. Joseph and the Virgin knelt on the steps of the temple with that Child before whom every knee must bend…

When the feast was over and the crowds were returning to their homes, the men through one gate, the women through another, all gathered in one place to rest for the night, and it was then that Mary and Joseph realized that the Child Jesus was not with them.

Imagine how terrible the grief of the Immaculate Heart of Mary was. The Virgin-Mother herself declares her affliction when she says: we have been looking for you, full of anguish. There were many sorrows in the world that night, but none like Hers. That is why an author says poetically: “If the stars had had a heart, they would not have shone that night.” How much anguish in the heart of that Mother!

Our Lady seemed to be in darkness, as if she did not understand what the Lord was doing with Her. Yet she did not cease to seek Jesus, true God and true Man, who had gone away from Her. Perhaps never before had our Most Holy Mother realized how united her Heart was to that of her precious Son Jesus.

Today, in this beautiful season of Christmas, we find ourselves, by the merciful design of God, celebrating this Holy Mass before the tomb of St. Mother Maravillas of Jesus. She too was the spiritual mother of countless souls, and she too had her heart fixed on the Most Sacred Heart of Jesus and on that of his Holy Mother, the Virgin Mary.

Everyone knows of Mother Maravillas’ immense love for the Heart of Jesus. To look no further, the reason for the existence of the Carmel on the Cerro de los Ángeles was to satisfy the request that the Divine Heart made to her “with loud cries” to have a Carmel that would keep Him loving company.

But great and tender too was Mother Maravillas’ devotion to the Mother of God, from whom she seems to have learned to love the Heart of Jesus so faithfully and so perfectly. For, like Most Holy Mary, she too made the will of God the axis of her life and lived totally abandoned to whatever his designs disposed for her. This is undoubtedly the fruit of her deep Marian devotion.

She herself confessed: “I have taken the Most Holy Virgin as Mother in a most special way, and She is also the one in charge of preparing me, covering me and protecting me” (56: C-3193).

In her letters, in her exhortations, in her conversations, she always repeated that the only important thing and the source of true happiness was to grow in the love of God, to please Him and to live attentive to his will, after the example of Mary: “How happy we are, truly wanting what He wants, and concerning ourselves with nothing but loving Him and saying yes to Him in everything!”; “She who most consoles our Christ,” she would tell her nuns, “will be the happiest”; “The greatest happiness on earth, which nothing can take away from us, consists in uniting oneself to God and fulfilling his will, loving Him and serving Him.”

This love of God, which grew to gigantic proportions in the soul of Mother Maravillas as the years went by, can be said to have been the natural consequence of the deep and true love she professed for the Blessed Virgin, which led her silently and so quickly to the feet of the Heart of Jesus. She always liked to recall how great her joy was on learning that Carmel belongs entirely to Mary. Indeed, very frequently in her letters she called her convents “the houses of Our Lady” and said: “How can we live in her house, and please the Lord together with Her, without imitating her, as the Holy Mother desired? […]. It seemed to me that, since the Lord has me here now, I must strive for it, not only for myself, but so that this house may truly be the house of Our Lady: to be truly poor, sacrificed, humble, nothing” (28: C-101). You will also remember that episode when, in view of the difficulty she experienced in praying the fifteen mysteries of the rosary every day, she bound herself to it by a vow.

This devotion to the Virgin Mary, so simple yet so deeply rooted in the heart of Mother Maravillas, is what –it seems to me– led her to love the Son of God so dauntlessly. That is why we, the religious of the Incarnate Word, are asked to have Mary as “the model, the guide and the form of all our acts”1. We are further bound to this by the most sweet duty acquired through the fourth vow that we one day professed to the Mother of God.

Now, if devotion to Our Lady is common to every Christian, and therefore common to all religious, it must nevertheless be distinctive of all the monks of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, as the rule clearly specifies: “Every monk of the Institute of the Incarnate Word… must have a particular devotion to the Blessed Virgin”2, striving “to live to the full the ‘interior and true devotion’ of the slaves of Mary”3.

And the Directory adds: “Because of his special configuration with Christ the Victim, the monk must seek strength in her in moments of trial, because she remains at the foot of the cross of each one of her children”4.

I would like then, if you will allow me, to point out briefly three lessons that it seems to me we can learn from the sorrow of the Mother of God, which made her so close to us in our search for Him, using in this case some passages from the writings of Mother Maravillas.

1. To pray with reality. True prayer is born from the heart, from the present moment we are living, because prayer is precisely being honest and transparent before God. Each one of us occasionally passes through moments of darkness like the Blessed Virgin in the search for her Son, which often requires of us that ‘entering into patience’ of which the saints speak. Taught, certainly, by the Mother of God, St. Mother Maravillas wrote in a letter: “when the Lord does not wish to lift his hand, the consolation that creatures can give lasts but little, and there is nothing to do but, closing one’s eyes and always trusting and hoping against all hope, to throw oneself into the arms of the cross, where the arms of Christ are”5.

2. The sorrow of the Mother of the Incarnate Word also teaches us that the loss of Jesus, no matter how brief, is the greatest of evils. The magnitude of the Virgin’s grief is for us a visible way of grasping the magnitude of evil and of sin. Convinced of this, Mother Maravillas wrote: “To see souls, which have cost Him so much, being lost, is horrifying, and what a thing it is to give one’s life for them!” (34: C436). “Let us pray much that souls may return to God, and let us console Him for so much ingratitude, giving ourselves more and more to Him.”

3. And lastly, we see how in this event the Mother of the Redeemer, being physically separated from her Son, experienced our apparent separation from God when we pass through the nights of the soul. All that longing for God, all that spiritual melancholy for heaven, the Most Holy Virgin felt as her own because she did not have her little Redeemer.

Therefore, an author says: She was suffering in reparation for all the souls who once had faith and lost it; for all those souls who once loved God and forgot Him; for all those hearts that once loved Him and today have abandoned Him. That is why we say to her: pray for us sinners.

Hence St. Mother Maravillas said: “I ask the Most Holy Virgin to help me see things truly in her presence, so as to accuse myself of them and, purified and washed by her mercy, to begin what little life I have left, a new life in Him, with Him and through Him” (64: C-727).

Lastly, and with this I conclude, from the Most Holy Virgin Mother Maravillas learned –and we must do the same– to say her fiat unconditionally. That is why she firmly affirmed: “Holiness is nothing other than our will united to God’s” (58: C-4403). “We shall see what the Lord wants. The truth is that we are happy. If the Lord were to ask us about this and about everything: about the moment of death, about which illness we would prefer to die of, about how we would like to be, etc., etc., we could only say to Him: ‘Lord, when You will, as You will, what You will; it is the only thing we want and desire,’ so all our desires are most completely fulfilled, for they are none other than his will” (59: C-2297).

To the intercession of St. Mother Maravillas of Jesus we entrust the growth in us of that ‘attunement’ with the Mother of God, so that we may advance by leaps and bounds in holiness and one day rest in the Most Sacred Heart of Jesus, whom she loved so much. For this intention we continue praying in the Holy Mass.
