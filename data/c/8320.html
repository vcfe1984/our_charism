Homily preached on May 30, 2023, at La Finca on the occasion of the burial of Fr. Buela

[Exordium] Dear brothers and sisters, just as one day missionaries set out from San Rafael to the whole world to conquer souls for the kingdom of Christ, so today, from the four corners of the earth, those missionaries return, and many others come for the first time, to pay tribute to their father… to our beloved father and founder, Fr. Carlos Miguel Buela.

In just a few hours, this soil of San Rafael will keep forever in its bosom the mortal remains of one who, like a seed cast into the furrow, knew how “to die to himself in everything so that his children might live”1.

The death of a father –at least naturally speaking– leaves us all with a great sense of loss.

But we grieve with hope; with the firm conviction that He who wept at the death of His friend Lazarus is the one who said: those who are like Me in suffering and contempt will also be like Me in glory2; yes, you will have sorrows and labors in preaching My law, in doing all that I have commanded you, because great temptations and persecutions will rise up against you, but all this will be for you an occasion of great joy, which will make you forget all your past sorrows and sadness. And we know that the promises of the Lord are true and that God is not mocked.

Joy and hope

What is more, on this occasion, as you will already have seen during the funeral in Genoa and as we have experienced these days here in San Rafael, the joy is also irrepressible, and this allows us to verify, even in a tangible way, that the “surprising fruit of sufferings for the cause of the Lord is joy, in perfect harmony with the beatitudes”3, as the father wrote in “El Señor es mi pastor.” And in this case, we rejoice because the father, after so many vicissitudes, persevered to the end, as a priest and as a religious of our religious family, and we can think that God in His infinite mercy has granted him the grace of infinite joy, of the treasure that does not corrode, which God Himself promised to those who fight the good fight. The father has already entered into “the Great Feast”…

We rejoice, in short, because we trust in God that there has fallen to Fr. Buela’s lot that which he loved4 and to which he dedicated his whole life: the Incarnate Word Himself, whom he contemplates from the arms of Most Holy Mary.

Given the circumstances that God in His providence disposed, the father, who in his life was a great formator, has also left us great lessons with his death, and the first that leaps to the eye is −in my opinion− that, as he himself told us: “we must be men of invincible hope; have an unrestricted trust in the power of Jesus Christ, who said: heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away. [We, Fr. Buela would say,] We must be men and women of invincible hope!”5.

Many of us can testify that when the storms raged so fiercely that we could say with the psalmist: from where will my help come?6, we always found the father serene, with an attitude of imperturbable trust, and it seemed to us as if the trials we were going through, which we considered ‘tremendous,’ made no dent in him. But with that same attitude of trust in God he seemed to answer us and say to us with the psalmist: my help comes from the Lord, who made heaven and earth and He will not let my foot slip7.

Later, leafing through the last book he wrote, we find the reason for this, for there he writes in his characteristic style: “in the face of the threats to dissolve us, not to carry out the priestly ordinations, etc.: I never worried about that. Never. I defined myself as ‘undaunted.’ That is, I suffered nothing. They were just poor men…”8. And I believe this is so because, as St. John of the Cross says: “the one whose spirit is detached is more content and joyful in want, because he has placed his all in nothing whatsoever, and finds in everything breadth of heart”9.

So convinced was he that all this was the work of God alone that he said that this was the “reason why even in the greatest trials and in the most unjust diatribes (insults) I have consoled myself simply by shrugging my shoulders”10.

That was Fr. Buela: with his gaze and his heart firmly grounded in the Incarnate Word. He always sought “to separate himself from the ‘pattern of this world,’ in order to insert himself into the will of God, entering into what pleased God, into what is good, into what is perfect”11.

And elsewhere he writes: “I never took it upon myself to give God advice, nor to ask Him why He acted that way. He is God and He does what He wants”12.

So it is that today he seems to want to teach us that in the face of trials and difficulties, which are never lacking, nor does God want them to be lacking −speaking individually and as an Institute−, we must never be troubled; we must not fear nor lose confidence, much less become discouraged or look back. On the contrary, we must be men and women of stout spirit in the midst of the hardships we have to suffer, because, as Fr. Buela has taught us by his example throughout his whole life and now by his death, “the Providence of God [is] the only one that guides and governs history”13.

That is why the great word that sums up today the message I want to convey is: trust!

Unlimited trust and abandonment in our Lord, first of all; filial trust in our Blessed Mother, who is solicitous to help us; trust even in the greatest trials because, as we have seen in the life of our Founder, God sends them to us for our benefit. Trust in the immense treasure that God has granted us through the person of Fr. Buela, which is the legacy we must guard and make fruitful.

2. Trust!

Only time will show us the historical moment we are living, with all its weight and all its influence on the future. But what we can affirm today is that it was a religious priest with the spirit of a prince who, docile to the grace of God, raised up this which today we call the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, which is made up of religious who must be of princely spirit and noble soul14, for it is they who, with the favor of God and the indispensable help of Our Lady of Luján, will carry our beloved Institute forward.

If we remain united and the ideal bequeathed to us by Fr. Buela beats fervently in our hearts, there is no mountain we cannot climb −even though there is much vertigo and much danger15−; there is no sea that the little boat of our Institute will not sail to accomplish great works for the inculturation of the Gospel16, even though the salt water of reproach and injustice splashes our faces.

Because our place is and always will be “out into the deep!, far from the shore and from dry land, far from merely human, calculating and cold thoughts”17. Let us never forget where our heart is, and where it must always be… our way of life and our natural habitat: out there in the deep, “Duc in altum! It is an invitation to accomplish great works, extraordinary undertakings, where there is much of adventure, of vertigo, of danger, where the waves toss the boat, the salt water splashes the face, the prow cuts its way for the first time, where there are no tracks and the only points of reference are the stars, where the keel is shaken by clashing whirlpools, the unfurled sails receive the fury of the wind, the masts creak… and the soul trembles… Out into the deep!, … where the water churns, the heart beats fast, where the soul knows heavenly inebriations and fascinating joys”18.

Because ours is and will always be “to take seriously, thoroughly, the demands of the Gospel: go, sell all that you have…19”20, and that is the longing that must beat in the heart of all those who are called to follow the Incarnate Word, the Supreme Captain, in our Institute, of all the members of our beloved Third Order and of our Sisters. Because ours is always, unfailingly, to give everything for God21, as our Founder has taught us so well.

And just as in times past legions of men united under one standard to overcome enemies and win triumphs for their king, so we must remain united under the standard that bears the emblem of the Incarnation of the Word, holding fast to the patrimony that has been bequeathed to us, and work without reserve for the reign of Jesus Christ according to the specific charism of our Religious Family, which is nothing other than “to bring to fullness the consequences of the Incarnation of the Word”22, and which so often implies the self-emptying of Calvary23.

If we remain united, committed to missionary work, and faithful to the spirit that has been bequeathed to us, to the spirit of the Institute, under the mantle of Our Lady,

  • we will not bow before the spirit of the world;
  • we will not break by distrusting God;
  • we will not yield before the sharpest threats;
  • we will not give up, even though the struggle be fierce;
  • we will not surrender before the giant tyrants of the world;
  • we will not draw back from our endeavor;

rather, we will be honoring in truth, and not only in word, the memory of Fr. Buela and, most importantly, we will not disappoint our Lord.

*****

[Peroratio] That is why today, paraphrasing St. Francis de Sales, we can hear Fr. Buela tell us with his usual candor and firmness: “Be firm in trust in the Providence of God, which, if it prepares crosses for us, will give us courage to bear them. […] Do not run ahead of the painful events of this life; arm yourselves with a perfect hope that, as they come, God, to whom they belong, will deliver you from them. He has protected you until now; hold fast to the hand of His Providence and He will assist you on every occasion and, if you cannot walk, He will carry you. What do you fear, since you all belong to God, who has assured us that all things will work for the good of those who love Him? Do not think about what will happen tomorrow, because the same Eternal Father who takes care of you today will do so tomorrow and always: He will give you no evil, and if He does, He will give you invincible courage to bear it”24. “Forward, always forward! Ave Maria and forward!”25.

And let us never forget the “tremendous” words of Master John of Avila, which we have written in our spirit, in our constitutions, and which are part of our DNA; and which point out to us the treasure that has been given to us in exchange for the gift of our lives: it is the Ipsum Esse who says it to us: “I (am) your Father, because I am God; I your firstborn brother, because I am man. I am your payment and ransom: why do you fear debts, if you, through penance and Confession, ask to be released from them? I am your reconciliation: why do you fear wrath? I am the bond of your friendship: why do you fear God’s anger? I am your defender: why do you fear adversaries? I am your friend: why do you fear that you will lack anything I have, if you do not depart from Me? Yours is My Body and My Blood: why do you fear hunger? Yours is My heart: why do you fear being forgotten? Yours is My divinity: why do you fear miseries? And besides, yours are My angels to defend you; yours My saints to pray for you; yours My blessed Mother to be for you a caring and tender Mother; yours the earth, so that in it you may serve Me; yours heaven, because you will come to it; yours the demons and the hells, because you will trample them as slaves and as a prison; yours is life, because with it you win the life that never ends; yours the good pleasures, because you refer them to Me; yours the sufferings, because you suffer them for love of Me and for your own profit; yours the temptations, because they are merit and the cause of your eternal crown; yours is death, because it will be for you the nearest passage to life. And all this you have in Me and through Me; because I did not win it for Myself alone, nor do I wish to enjoy it alone; for when I took fellowship with you in the flesh, I took it so as to make you sharers in what I labored, fasted, ate, sweated and wept, and in My sorrows and deaths, if it is not prevented on your part. You are not poor, you who have so much wealth, unless you, by your bad life, knowingly choose to lose it!”26.

To the Pure and Clean Conception of Luján we commend the soul of her beloved son, our dear Father, Carlos Miguel Buela. Amen.
