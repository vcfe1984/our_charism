Common, proper and specific end

To evangelize the culture in order to extend the Incarnation of the Word

Like every Institute of consecrated life, whether religious or secular2, we have a universal and common end –usually called vocation– by which we seek to follow Christ more closely under the action of the Holy Spirit, dedicating ourselves totally to God as our supreme love, so that, given over by a new and special title to His glory, to the building up of the Church and to the salvation of the world, we may attain the perfection of charity, and, through the charity to which the profession of the evangelical counsels of chastity, poverty and obedience leads, be united in a special way to the Church and to her mystery3.

Likewise, we tend toward the proper end of every Institute of religious life, which is none other than the total consecration of our person, manifesting the wonderful espousal established by God in the Church, a sign of the life of Heaven. Thus we will consummate the full gift of ourselves as a sacrifice offered to God, by which our whole existence becomes a continual worship of God in charity.

This is manifested in the fact that we form a family4, take public vows and live fraternal life in common; and the public witness we must give entails a separation from the world5. In order to live according to the Holy Spirit, one must necessarily put away from oneself the spirit of the world: The Spirit of truth… the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him (Jn 14:17).

Finally, as our specific and singular end, we wish to dedicate ourselves to the evangelization of culture, that is, to work “to transform by the power of the Gospel

-the criteria of judgment,
-the determining values,
-the points of interest,
-the lines of thought,
-the sources of inspiration,
-the models of life of mankind”6;


“so that they may be imbued with the power of the Gospel
-the ways of thinking,
-the criteria of judgment,
-the norms of action”7,

for we cannot forget that the Second Vatican Council has pointed out that: “The split between the faith which many profess and their daily lives deserves to be counted among the more serious errors of our age”8 and this is due in great measure to the fact that the world “has, in recent centuries, been drawing away and separating itself from the Christian trunk of its civilization”9, which has led to the de-Christianization of culture.

We consider that some of the most important means for attaining the established end is to work on the turning points of culture, namely: families, education –especially seminary, university and tertiary education–, the social communications media, and men of thought or “intellectuals,” with regard to the initiation and calling, development, discernment, formation, consolidation, accompaniment and subsequent exercise of the vocation to the intellectual apostolate.

That is why we commit all our strength so as not to shy away from the missionary adventure, in order to prolong the Incarnation of the Word “in every man, in the whole man and in all the manifestations of man”10, assuming all that is authentically human, so as to be like another humanity of Christ, in order to carry out with greater perfection the service of God and of men11.
