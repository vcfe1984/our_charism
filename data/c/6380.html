Dialogue for the 1st Day of Families in San Rafael

San Rafael, 1997

Part I

Dear brothers and sisters:

In this procession we wish to hold a dialogue on the rights of the family, for, as the motto of this Day convoked by His Holiness John Paul II states, the family is a gift and a hope-filled commitment for humanity. Therefore, in order to become aware of the challenges facing the Family in the Third Millennium, we wish to follow the text of the “Charter of the Rights of the Family” presented by the Holy See, the central and supreme governing body of the Catholic Church, to all persons, institutions and authorities concerned with the mission of the family in the contemporary world.

The Bishops gathered in Synod in Rome in 1980 asked for this charter. Pope John Paul II confirmed this request1 to present a formulation –as complete and orderly as possible– of the fundamental rights of that natural and universal society which is the family. These rights ultimately derive from the law inscribed by the Creator in the heart of every human being. All the Episcopal Conferences of the world and the leading experts in the field were consulted. Thus, respect for these rights must be promoted and their effective recognition and observance ensured.

Preamble of the Charter.

The Preamble of the Charter sets out the foundations of the rights of the family.

  1. Do the rights of the human person have a social dimension?

The rights of the person, even though expressed as rights of the individual, have a fundamentally social dimension which finds an innate and vital expression in the family2.

  1. Is the family founded on marriage?

The family is founded on marriage, that intimate union of life, a complement between a man and a woman, which is constituted by the indissoluble bond of marriage, freely contracted, publicly affirmed, and which is open to the transmission of life3.

  1. What is the only institution that transmits life?

Marriage is the natural institution to which the mission of transmitting life is exclusively entrusted4.

  1. Does the family exist before the State?

The family, a natural society, exists prior to the State or any other community, and possesses inherent rights which are inalienable5.

  1. What is most important about the family?

The family constitutes, much more than a mere juridical, social and economic unit, a community of love and solidarity, which is irreplaceable for the teaching and transmission of the cultural, ethical, social, spiritual and religious values essential for the development and well-being of its own members and of society6.

  1. Is the family a place of encounter and of help?

The family is the place where different generations come together and help one another to grow in human wisdom and to harmonize individual rights with the other demands of social life7.

  1. Do the family and society complement each other?

The family and society, which are mutually linked by vital and organic bonds, have a complementary function in the defense and promotion of the good of humanity and of every person8.

  1. Must society defend the family?

The experience of different cultures throughout history has shown the need for society to recognize and defend the institution of the family9.

  1. How must society help the family?

Society, and in a particular manner the State and International Organizations, must protect the family through measures of a political, economic, social and juridical character, which aim at consolidating the unity and stability of the family so that it can exercise its specific function10.

  1. Is the family under attack?

The rights, the fundamental needs, the well-being and the values of the family, even though they are progressively being safeguarded in many cases, are often ignored and not rarely undermined by laws, institutions and socio-economic programs11.

  1. Does poverty threaten the dignity of the family?

Many families are forced to live in situations of poverty which prevent them from carrying out their own mission with dignity12.

  1. Why does the Church defend the family?

The Catholic Church, aware that the good of the person, of society and of the Church herself passes by way of the family, has always held it part of her mission to proclaim to all the plan of God intrinsic to human nature concerning marriage and the family, to promote these two institutions and to defend them against every attack directed against them13.

Part II

Article 1

Are all persons free to choose their state of life?

All persons have the right to the free choice of their state of life and thus the right to marry and establish a family or to remain single14.

a) Is there a right to marry and establish a family?

Every man and every woman, having reached marriageable age and having the necessary capacity, has the right to marry and establish a family without any discrimination whatsoever; legal restrictions on the exercise of this right, whether permanent or temporary in nature, can be introduced only when they are required by grave and objective demands of the institution of marriage itself and of its social and public character; they must respect in all cases the dignity and the fundamental rights of the person15.

b) What conditions must society provide?

All those who wish to marry and establish a family have the right to expect from society the moral, educational, social and economic conditions which will enable them to exercise their right to marry in all maturity and responsibility16.

c) Must public authorities recognize the institutional value of marriage?

The institutional value of marriage must be recognized by the public authorities; the situation of unmarried couples must not be placed on the same level as marriage duly contracted17.

Article 2

Is free and full consent necessary for marriage?

Marriage cannot be contracted except by the free and full consent of the spouses duly expressed18.

a) Must families avoid exerting undue pressure on the choice?

With due respect for the traditional role of the families in certain cultures in guiding the decision of their children, all pressure which would impede the choice of a specific person as spouse is to be avoided19.

b) Must the religious freedom of the spouses be respected?

The future spouses have the right to have their religious liberty respected. Therefore, to impose as a prior condition for marriage a denial of faith or a profession of faith which is contrary to conscience constitutes a violation of this right20.

c) Do man and woman have the same dignity and equal rights?

The spouses, in the natural complementarity which exists between man and woman, enjoy the same dignity and equal rights regarding marriage21.

Part III

Article 3

Do the spouses have the right to decide responsibly on the number of children to have?

The spouses have the inalienable right to found a family and to decide on the spacing of births and the number of children to be born, taking into full consideration their duties towards themselves, their children already born, the family and society, in a just hierarchy of values and in accordance with the objective moral order which excludes recourse to contraception, sterilization and abortion22.

a) Is interference by public authority regarding the number of children to have a grave offense against human dignity and justice?

The activities of public authorities or private organizations which attempt in any way to limit the freedom of couples in deciding about their children constitute a grave offense against human dignity and justice23.

b) Is it licit to make economic aid conditional on limiting the number of children?

In international relations, economic aid for the advancement of peoples must not be conditioned on acceptance of programs of contraception, sterilization or abortion24.

c) Does the family have the right to be helped by society?

The family has a right to assistance by society in the bearing and rearing of children. Married couples with large families have a right to adequate aid and should not be subjected to discrimination25.

Article 4

From what moment must human life be respected?

Human life must be respected and protected absolutely from the moment of conception26.

a) Does abortion violate human rights?

Abortion is a direct violation of the fundamental right to life of the human being27.

b) Is it licit to manipulate the human embryo?

Respect for the dignity of the human being excludes all experimental manipulation or exploitation of the human embryo28.

c) Is any intervention on a person's genetic heritage licit?

All interventions on the genetic heritage of the human person that are not aimed at correcting anomalies constitute a violation of the right to bodily integrity and contradict the good of the family.

d) Do children have rights?

Children, both before and after birth, have the right to special protection and assistance, as do their mothers during pregnancy and for a reasonable period of time after childbirth29.

e) Do all children have equal rights?

All children, whether born in or out of wedlock, enjoy the same right to social protection with a view to their integral personal development30.

f) Must orphans have special protection from society?

Orphans and children who are deprived of the assistance of their parents or guardians must receive special protection on the part of society. With regard to foster care or adoption, the State must provide legislation which assists suitable families to welcome into their homes children who are in need of temporary or permanent care and which at the same time respects the natural rights of the parents31.

g) And children with disabilities?

Handicapped children have the right to find in the home and the school an environment suitable to their human development32.

Part IV

Article 5

Do parents have the right to educate their children?

Since they have conferred life on their children, parents have the original, primary and inalienable right to educate them; hence they must be acknowledged as the first and foremost educators of their children33.

a) Must they educate them according to their convictions?

Parents have the right to educate their children in conformity with their moral and religious convictions, taking into account the cultural traditions of the family which favor the good and the dignity of the child; they should also receive from society the necessary aid and assistance to perform their educational role properly34.

b) Do parents have the right to choose the schools for their children?

Parents have the right to choose freely schools or other means necessary to educate their children in keeping with their convictions. Public authorities must ensure that public subsidies are allocated in such a way that parents are truly free to exercise this right without incurring unjust burdens. Parents should not have to sustain, directly or indirectly, extra charges which would deny or unjustly limit the exercise of this freedom35.

c) Do they have the right that their children not be compelled to attend classes they do not agree with?

Parents have the right to ensure that their children are not compelled to attend classes which are not in agreement with their own moral and religious convictions. In particular, sex education –which is a basic right of the parents– must always be carried out under their close supervision, whether at home or in educational centers chosen and controlled by them36.

d) Is it licit for the State to impose a compulsory system of education that excludes all religious formation?

The rights of parents are violated when a compulsory system of education is imposed by the State from which all religious formation is excluded37.

e) Must parents take part in the educational work of schools?

The primary right of parents to educate their children must be upheld in all forms of collaboration between parents, teachers and school authorities, and particularly in forms of participation designed to give citizens a voice in the functioning of schools and in the formulation and implementation of educational policies38.

f) Does the family have the right to be helped, and not attacked, by the media?

The family has the right to expect that the means of social communication will be positive instruments for the building up of society, and that they will reinforce the fundamental values of the family. At the same time the family has the right to be adequately protected, especially with regard to its youngest members, from the negative effects and misuse of the mass media39.

Article 6

Does the family have the right to exist and to progress?

The family has the right to exist and to progress as a family40.

a) Must public authority respect families?

Public authorities must respect and foster the dignity, lawful independence, privacy, integrity and stability of every family41.

b) Does divorce threaten the family?

Divorce attacks the very institution of marriage and of the family42.

c) Must the extended family be held in esteem?

The extended family system, where it exists, should be held in esteem and helped to carry out better its traditional role of solidarity and mutual assistance, while at the same time respecting the rights of the nuclear family and the personal dignity of each member.

Part V

Article 7

Does the family have the right to live its own religious life?

Every family has the right to live freely its own domestic religious life under the guidance of the parents, as well as the right to profess publicly and to propagate the faith, to take part in public worship and in freely chosen programs of religious instruction, without suffering any discrimination43.

Article 8

Does the family have a right in the building up of society?

The family has the right to exercise its social and political function in the construction of society44.

a) Do families have the right to form associations?

Families have the right to form associations with other families and institutions, in order to fulfill the family's role suitably and effectively, as well as to protect the rights, foster the good and represent the interests of the family45.

b) Must the role of families be recognized?

On the economic, social, juridical and cultural levels, the rightful role of families and family associations must be recognized in the planning and development of programs which touch on family life46.

Article 9

Do families have the right to an adequate family policy?

Families have the right to be able to rely on an adequate family policy on the part of public authorities in the juridical, economic, social and fiscal domains, without any discrimination whatsoever47.

a) Do families have the right to dignified economic conditions?

Families have the right to economic conditions which assure them a standard of living appropriate to their dignity and full development. They should not be impeded from acquiring and maintaining private possessions which would favor stable family life; and the laws concerning inheritance or transmission of property must respect the needs and rights of family members48.

b) Do families have the right to social security?

Families have the right to measures in the social domain which take into account their needs, especially in the event of the premature death of one or both parents, of the abandonment of one of the spouses, of accident, sickness or invalidity, in the case of unemployment, or whenever the family has to bear extra burdens on behalf of its members for reasons of old age, physical or mental handicaps, or the education of children49.

c) Do elderly persons have rights?

The elderly have the right to find within their own family or, when this is not possible, in suitable institutions, an environment which will enable them to live their later years of life in serenity while pursuing those activities which are compatible with their age and which enable them to participate in social life50.

d) Do the families of prisoners have rights?

The rights and needs of the family, and especially the value of family unity, must be taken into consideration in penal legislation and policy, in such a way that a detainee remains in contact with his or her family and that the family is adequately sustained during the period of detention.

Part VI

Article 10

Do families have the right to live together?

Families have a right to a social and economic order in which the organization of work permits the members to live together, and does not hinder the unity, well-being, health and stability of the family, while offering also the possibility of wholesome recreation51.

a) Must remuneration for work be sufficient?

Remuneration for work must be sufficient for establishing and maintaining a family with dignity, either through a suitable salary, called a “family wage,” or through other social measures such as family allowances or the remuneration of the work in the home of one of the parents; it should be such that mothers will not be obliged to work outside the home to the detriment of family life and especially of the education of the children.

b) Must the work of the homemaker be recognized?

The work of the mother in the home must be recognized and respected because of its value for the family and for society52.

Article 11

Does the family have the right to decent housing?

The family has the right to decent housing, fitting for family life and commensurate to the number of the members, in a physical environment that provides the basic services for the life of the family and the community53.

Article 12

Do the families of immigrants have the same rights?

The families of migrants have the right to the same protection as that accorded other families54.

a) Do they have a right to their culture?

The families of immigrants have the right to respect for their own culture and to receive support and assistance towards their integration into the community to whose well-being they contribute.

b) Do immigrants have the right to live united with their family?

Emigrant workers have the right to see their family united as soon as possible.

c) Do refugees have the right to be united with their family?

Refugees have the right to the assistance of public authorities and international organizations in facilitating the reunion of their families.

Conclusion

Today the Christian family, founded on marriage, which is an image and a sharing in the covenant of love between Christ and the Church, will make manifest to all the living presence of the Savior in the world and the genuine nature of the Church, both by the love, generous fruitfulness, unity and fidelity of the spouses, and by the loving cooperation of all its members55. “Go into the whole world and teach all families the gospel of eternal salvation, which is the “gospel of families”56.
