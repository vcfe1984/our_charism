Holy Mass at the first Minor Seminary of the Institute

[Exordium]

Dear Fathers, prefects, and dearest minor seminarians:

I am extremely pleased, and it is truly a great joy and a beautiful consolation to have come here, to the first Minor Seminary of the Institute, which this year is celebrating its 30th anniversary. And even more, to come and meet these [#] souls who have decided to follow our Lord, dedicating to him from now on the best years of their life! The flower of life! Giving God the best! I thank you very much for the joy you have given me and for the possibility of celebrating this Holy Mass with you.

In the first reading, taken from the Prophet Nehemiah, we have heard how three times Scripture says: Do not be sad1.

That is why I wanted, in this Holy Mass and now that we are gathered around him who is the very Fountain of Joy, to reflect together with you on this characteristic so proper to the Minor Seminary, which is joy2, and which goes hand in hand with the family atmosphere that must reign in this house3.

Everyone here has perhaps heard or read how St. John Bosco greeted his boys, saying to them: “Be joyful.”

And why?

Because this attitude of joy is rooted above all in a profound sense of faith in which the presence of the Lord as someone who loves and saves always dominates and stands out4. Hence our joy is founded on the mystery of the Incarnation5. Rejoice, the archangel St. Gabriel said to Most Holy Mary at the Annunciation. And when the Child had been born, the angels also said to the shepherds: I announce to you a great joy. Christ is our Friend, who wants only our good and, in fact, the greatest good possible. How can we not be filled with joy!

This attitude of joy is also rooted in the fact that God, being our Father, takes care of all our affairs, and that is why, as Christians, we must “rejoice always and in everything”6. And know how to flee, as from a serpentine temptation, from everything that threatens joy: discouragement because of difficulties –whatever they may be: in study, in prayer, in living together with the other seminarians–, entertaining thoughts about what we have left behind, worries about family, and anything else that makes you less happy, because that is not from God. On the other hand, also avoid being oneself the cause of sadness to others: ‘let no one be disturbed or saddened in the house of God’”7, said St. Benedict. I say the same to you.

Also know how to open your soul to your spiritual director with the greatest confidence when any of these or other things trouble your soul. Because that is how it is: we all need help in the struggle…

But even more, cultivate and promote joy with endless initiatives. And for that, here in the Minor Seminary, you have a thousand opportunities!

In recreation, in games, in competitions, in the floral games, in the conviviums, the feasts of the saints… all, all of that must not only be done, but must fire us up with joy. In such a way that the soul is soaked in that joy, which is ultimately a gift of God, and that this very joy may serve as our motivation to face the struggle with a magnanimous spirit and generosity.

You will know the life of the martyr José Sánchez del Río, who is the patron of our Minor Seminary in the United States. When they took him prisoner, knowing that they were going to kill him, he wrote a letter to his mother which reads as follows:

“My dear mom:

I was taken prisoner in combat today. I believe that at this moment I am going to die, but it does not matter at all, mom. Resign yourself to the will of God; I die very happy, because I die in the ranks of our God. Do not worry about my death, which is what troubles me.

Rather, tell my two other brothers to follow the example of their youngest brother, and you do the will of God. Have courage and send me your blessing together with my father's. Greet everyone for me for the last time, and finally receive the heart of your son, who loves you so much and wished to see you before dying.

José Sánchez del Río.”

Note that supernatural and spiritual joy of which we spoke at the beginning in the life of this martyr (he was barely 14 years old). I die very happy…

With that same joy we should all face the crosses that God sends us. Because Christ not only helps us with his grace, but with his Resurrection he has already won the victory for us. Always keep that very much in mind, especially in moments of trial, of difficulty. Nothing whatsoever should make you lose your joy.

The very Patron of this Seminary, James the Less, wrote in his letter: rejoice deeply when you find yourselves subjected to any kind of trials8. Because God is above everything! So he is above everything that makes me suffer. And thus truth is above falsehood, good above evil, beauty above ugliness, love above hatred, peace above war, mercy above vengeance, life above death, grace above sin; in short, being above nothingness, Our Lady above Satan, Christ above the Antichrist, God above all9. Because “God is infinite joy”10, as St. Teresa of the Andes said.

That is why we must celebrate, since the reason for celebration is joy. The Prophet Nehemiah goes on to say: Go and eat splendidly, drink sweet drinks, and send something to those who have nothing11.

In the Minor Seminary, too, there must be celebration. Mainly on Sunday –which is the prolongation of the Paschal Mystery– and at the same time a sign and foretaste of the true Feast with a capital F, which will be Paradise12. And the best way to do this is with the celebration of Holy Mass, with active participation, ever more conscious and more fruitful13. And also, of course, with the other feasts…

They say that St. Mary Mazzarello was so interested, and so continually, in the joy of the girls that, in imitation of Don Bosco, she always asked them: “Are you happy?” For she considered the joy of her daughters to be the principal proof of their holiness14. If her daughters were happy, then she was doing things well.

This joy is also asked of those whose principal apostolate is the work of forming the Minor Seminarians. Note that our Proper Law tells us: “Everything must contribute to the joy ‘which must be the atmosphere of every center of education’”15. It is necessary, then, to know how to create the atmosphere in which celebration and joy are lived, without fear of spending on everything that contributes to the festivities16. May the boys always be happy! That will also be our joy.

Let us walk, then, on the pilgrimage of this life always rejoicing17, as St. Paul says, since, as we heard in the first reading: joy in the Lord is our strength18.

[Peroration]

To the parents of these children and young people who are here, and to those who for various reasons cannot be here today: my most sincere gratitude and my best wishes. I know that God will not leave you without reward in this life and in the next for the great gift of your sons to the Church. May you know how to appreciate the predilection he has shown you in calling one or several of your sons to so high a vocation.

For you who are here, and for the parents who are farther away, especially those who are sick or going through some difficulty, we pray in this Holy Mass.

To Most Holy Mary, who is the Cause of our joy, I entrust you all. May she always take care of you and be pleased to see you always joyful. To her we also give thanks for all the priestly, contemplative and missionary vocations that have come from this blessed St. John the Apostle Seminary. For many, many more vocations we also ask her in this Holy Mass.

God bless you all.

In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.
