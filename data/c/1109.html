First of all, I would like to tell you that it is a great joy for me to be among you, to see so many faces that are familiar to me and, in short, to be able to thank you in person for everything you do for us. Even if my words fall short, know that everything you do is a great help and an extremely important contribution to our missionary work. And without a doubt it is a very edifying example of charity that you always give us

St. John Paul II said that the “believer widens the borders of his charity, showing concern for those who are far away as well as for those who are near; praying for the missions and for missionary vocations, following their activities with interest and, when they return, welcoming them with that same joy with which the first Christian communities heard from the apostles the marvels that God had worked through their preaching”1. Well, know that many… very many of our religious bear witness that this is precisely what the members of the Third Order in Argentina do. And in their name, and personally as well, truly, I thank you infinitely.

And a ‘double thank you’ to those who have ‘made a greater offering’ and have offered their children to God to work for the spread of his Kingdom. I see here present many parents and relatives of our religious. It is truly a great joy to have you here. One feels at home with family, because that is in reality what we are, isn’t it? As the Directory of the Third Order says: “a single family, united by the same faith, the same ends, the same mission, the same charism, the same character and the same spirit”2.

Introduction

Today, if you will allow me, I would like to speak to you about the special dignity and mission entrusted to you, lay people of the Third Order of the Incarnate Word, in the Church and in our religious family. These are perhaps very well-known and very elementary points, but, after all, it is always good to recall them, since they are also, in the end, very profound realities.

St. Peter says that Christians are a royal priesthood, a holy nation (1 Pet 2:9). All Christians, incorporated into Christ and into his Church through baptism, are consecrated to God. They are called to profess the faith they have received. Moreover, as all of you know, through the sacrament of confirmation you have also been endowed by the Holy Spirit with a special strength to be witnesses of Christ and sharers in his saving mission. Therefore each one of you is, consequently, an extraordinary work of the grace of God and is called to the highest summits of holiness. At times it can happen that lay people do not fully appreciate the dignity and vocation that is proper to them as lay people. Each one of you, members of the Third Order of the Institute, is called to become holy and to sanctify the whole world from within your own lay condition3. This implies that you are called to play a very important role in the evangelization of the world.

Yes, you, the lay people of the Third Order of the Incarnate Word, are a chosen race, a holy priesthood, called also to be the salt of the earth and the light of the world. Your specific vocation and mission consists in manifesting the Gospel in your lives and, therefore, in introducing the Gospel, like leaven, into the reality of the world in which you live and work. That is why the Proper Law says that our tertiaries must seek “to order temporal affairs according to God4, restoring all things in Jesus Christ5, making Christ manifest to others, primarily through the witness of life, the radiance of faith, hope and charity, illuminating the temporal realities with which it is closely bound up”6. The great forces that shape the world (politics, the media, science, technology, culture, education, industry) are precisely the areas in which you are especially competent to exercise your mission. You know well that if these forces are led by people who are true disciples of Christ and, at the same time, fully competent in secular knowledge and science, then the world will certainly be transformed from within by the redeeming power of Christ7.

In this sense, it is good that you become aware that your role in the mission is of such importance that the mission could never be properly carried out without you.

Inculturating the Gospel

“We must bring it about that Christ reigns in the whole world, in all cultures and in the hearts of men”8, –that is the commitment you have taken on as Tertiaries: to work with all your strength to inculturate the Gospel9–.

So it is good to remember that “in order not to go astray in this task so proper to us, one must know, study and go deeper into the Social Doctrine of the Church,” and that is why it is “indispensable that this doctrine be assimilated and brought into social reality in the forms and to the extent that circumstances permit or demand,” whether in the education of one’s children, or at work, in whatever environment one moves in (and if one no longer works, or is sick, or whatever it may be, well, then to pray and to know how to offer one’s sufferings and daily routine so that the kingdom of Christ may spread).

I know that there are many among you who are very committed to the decidedly Christian task of permeating society with the leaven of the Gospel. In this sense, the Directory of the Third Order says explicitly: “We must never forget that we must transform reality from within, evangelizing the culture and not culturizing the Gospel; everything that is redeemable must be used for good”10.

Means

In this regard, what does this ‘from within’ mean?

Mainly it means through an “authentic witness of Christian life,” which unfailingly implies a life of grace. It implies that our tertiaries “speak and act by example, that they be known by others for the effective practice of the Christian virtues, [simply] because they carry Jesus and Mary in their blood, and know that words move, but examples draw”11. That is to say, there must be consistency of life between the faith one professes and the way one lives. It is not enough to have a son or daughter who is a religious; each one of you has to pray and become holy and go to confession.

You cannot imagine the strength and encouragement toward holiness that the example you give represents for others, even for the religious themselves. How many of ours find in the silent sacrifice of their parents, in the supernatural outlook of their mother, in the self-denial of their sister or brother, a concrete encouragement to practice virtue, to persevere in hard times, to dispose themselves magnanimously to carry out a work simply because they learned it from their father; to be compassionate before the needs of others because that was the example they received from their parents… Always keep that in mind. What you do has effects that go beyond the limits of the home, of work, of the little room you clean or in which you study… they are examples that have an impact on the lives of many.

If not, think of the example of St. Thérèse’s parents. Two years ago, in October 2015, the Pope canonized them. In her autobiography the saint –today Patroness of the Missions, Doctor of the Church, and the greatest saint of modern times12– wrote: “As I had nothing but good examples around me, I naturally wanted to follow them.” St. Thérèse’s father was a watchmaker and her mother a lacemaker. So that you may see the magnitude and the lasting effects of the authentic witness of Christian life.

And on the other hand, there are also the concrete works of apostolate, in which I know so many of you are involved and for which we will never thank you enough… I think of those who help so much in the hogarcitos, during the youth days, the camps, the spiritual exercises; those who, according to their various abilities, give invaluable collaboration to our Religious Family; the many of you who support these works with your prayers and sacrifices and material donations too… All these things, concrete apostolates carried out in union with God, while giving at every moment, whatever environment one finds oneself in, a consistent witness of Christian life, are a contribution, an effective collaboration in the cause of Christ, which is to illuminate every man, the whole man and all human realities with the light of the Gospel in one’s own environment.

To achieve this end, I would like to point out to you… really, more than anything, to remind you in a specific and concrete way of some means in which the members of our Third Order should distinguish themselves and which they should make use of. There are nine, and all are taken from the Magisterium of the Second Vatican Council: (And, if you allow me, I would like to develop these points very briefly following the Magisterium of the Church, interspersing it with some texts from our Directory of the Third Order).

1. The laity do not exercise hierarchical religious activities, but they participate directly in the liturgy of the Church

«Holy Mother Church earnestly desires that all the faithful be led to that full, conscious and active participation in liturgical celebrations which is demanded by the very nature of the liturgy, and to which the Christian people, a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a purchased people (1 Pet 2:9; 2:45), have a right and an obligation by reason of their baptism»13. Therefore, participation in Holy Mass in an ever more conscious and fruitful way is essential, «because it is the primary and indispensable source from which the faithful are to derive the true Christian spirit»14.

2. The laity, through their sanctification, impress a Christian character on all the activities of their life

If each one of you sanctifies his life as belonging to the Church, the Mystical Body of Christ, requires of him, he also sanctifies all the activities of his life, and life itself, private and public15, said Pope Pius XII.

3. Each one of you, by virtue of being a lay person, can and must exercise the common apostolate

As we already mentioned before: «The apostolate of the laity is a participation in the saving mission of the Church itself, an apostolate to which all are commissioned by the Lord himself through baptism and confirmation. And the sacraments, especially the Holy Eucharist, communicate and nourish that love toward God and toward men which is the soul of the entire apostolate»16. That is why the Directory says: “the laity, ‘moved by the charity that comes from Christ, do good’17.” Because love of Christ cannot be separated from love of neighbor.

«The laity are especially called to make the Church present and operative in those places and circumstances where only through them can she become the salt of the earth. Thus every lay person, by virtue of the gifts bestowed upon him, is at once a witness and a living instrument of the mission of the Church herself, according to the measure of Christ’s gift (Eph 4:7)»18.

Now, it is important to keep in mind that “within the love of God, and also as the basis of all apostolic action, the tertiary must stand out for the faithful and perfect fulfillment of the duties of his state. It is impossible to build any apostolic work unless it is founded on one’s first obligations, faithfully and legitimately fulfilled”19.

4. The laity, by virtue of the apostolate, can exercise the mandated apostolate of Catholic Action

«Besides this apostolate, which pertains to absolutely all Christians, the laity can also be called in various ways to a more immediate cooperation with the apostolate of the Hierarchy, like those men and women who helped the apostle Paul in evangelization, laboring much in the Lord (cf. Phil 4:3; Rom 16:3ff). Furthermore, they have the capacity to be appointed by the Hierarchy to certain ecclesiastical offices. Which they are to carry out for a spiritual purpose»20.

5. The laity must sanctify or Christianize, immediately and directly, married and family life

«In it –in married and family life– the apostolate of the laity finds an opportunity for exercise and an excellent school if the Christian religion permeates the whole organization of life and transforms it more each day. Here the spouses have their own vocation: to be witnesses of faith and of Christ’s love to one another and to their children. The Christian family loudly proclaims both the present virtues of the kingdom of God and the hope of a blessed life. Thus by its example and its witness it convicts the world of sin and enlightens those who seek the truth»21.

The Directory of the Third Order abounds in examples and guidelines that can serve very well as a guide as to how to Christianize married and family life. I will simply mention a few examples: forming oneself in a Christian way, knowing, loving and proclaiming the doctrine of the Church22; forming families that are true apostolic nuclei, that foster works of charity, that help other married couples in need23, for example; seeking a solid Catholic education for one’s children, and many other examples that you yourselves can add…

6. The laity must sanctify and Christianize, directly and immediately, economic life in its various aspects

a) the use of private property; b) the management of small, medium and large enterprises in the agricultural, commercial and industrial sectors; c) efficiency and the meaning of work; d) the exercise of the various professions

Vatican II strongly points out the need for the lay person to fully assume his responsibility as one baptized, that is to say, his condition as a sharer «in the priestly, prophetic and kingly office of Jesus Christ», so that, just as through Jesus Christ, the great Prophet, the Kingdom of the Father was proclaimed, so too, «through the laity», «the power of the Gospel may shine forth in daily, family and social life»24.

Each one must do with zeal what he has to do in his chosen profession, “in order afterwards to face, with competence and in a spirit of evangelical detachment, the burden and the joys of the social responsibilities toward which Providence may direct them”25, for which there is undoubtedly need of a serious spiritual life, which makes them see what they do as a collaboration for the kingdom of Christ, so as to discern what the Will of God is, etc.

7. The laity must sanctify and Christianize, directly and immediately, the different manifestations of culture

a) letters; b) education; c) the arts; d) the experimental sciences; e) technology; f) the sciences of the spirit; g) philosophy

Lumen Gentium says: «Even in secular occupations they must help one another toward a holier life, in such a way that the world may be permeated with the spirit of Christ and may more effectively attain its end in justice, in charity and in peace. In the fulfillment of this universal duty, the laity hold the most prominent place. Therefore, by their competence in secular affairs and by their activity, elevated from within by the grace of Christ, let them contribute effectively so that created goods, in accordance with the design of the Creator and the enlightenment of His Word, may be promoted by human labor, technology and civil culture for the benefit of all men without exception; may be more fittingly distributed among them; and may in their own way lead to universal progress in human and Christian freedom. Thus Christ, through the members of the Church, will increasingly illumine the whole of human society with His saving light»26.

«Likewise, let the laity coordinate their forces to remedy the structures and conditions of the world when they incite to sin, so that all these things may be conformed to the norms of justice and may favor rather than hinder the practice of the virtues. By acting in this way, they will imbue culture and human works with moral value. By this manner of acting, the field of the world is at the same time better prepared for the sowing of the divine word, and the doors through which to bring the message of peace into the world are opened wider to the Church»27.

I know there are many among you who already do this. Know that you are a source of pride for us, and I encourage you to keep doing it.

8. The laity must sanctify and Christianize, directly and immediately, civic and political activities, at the municipal, civic, national and international levels

Our Directory, quoting St. John Paul II, says: “the lay faithful can in no way abdicate their participation in politics; that is, in the many different economic, social, legislative, administrative and cultural activities which are intended to promote the common good organically and institutionally… the lay faithful must promote a widespread educational effort, aimed at overcoming the prevailing culture of selfishness, hatred, revenge and enmity, and at developing the culture of solidarity at every level”28.

9. The laity must have as the direct and immediate mission of their lay state the Consecration of the World to Jesus Christ

«For those whom He intimately joins to His life and mission, He also makes sharers in His priestly office, so that they may offer spiritual worship for the glory of God and the salvation of men. Therefore the laity, as consecrated to Christ and anointed by the Holy Spirit, are marvelously called and equipped so that ever more abundant fruits of the Spirit may be produced in them. For all their works, their prayers and apostolic initiatives, their married and family life, their daily work, their rest of soul and body, if they are done in the Spirit, and even the very trials of life if they are patiently borne, become spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ (cf. 1 Pet 2:5), which in the celebration of the Eucharist are most devoutly offered to the Father together with the oblation of the Lord’s body. In this way the laity too, as worshippers acting in a holy manner everywhere, consecrate the world itself to God»29.

And to conclude and to clarify the depth of this consecration of the world to God, nothing is more fitting than the words of Paul VI on August 18, 196530: «The development of modern culture has recognized the legitimate and just distinction between the various fields of human activity, giving to each of them a relative autonomy, demanded by the constitutive principles and ends of each field, so that each science, profession and art has its relative independence, which separates it from the properly religious sphere and confers on it a certain “laicism” which, rightly understood, the Christian is the first to respect, without confusing, as they say, the sacred with the profane. But wherever this field of activity concerns man, considered in his integrity, that is, in accordance with his supreme end, all can and must honor and be honored by the religious light that illumines that supreme end and makes its attainment possible. So that where activity becomes moral, it must be referred to the central pole of life, which is God, and whom Christ reveals to us and guides us to attain. And all of life, even when profane, provided it is upright, can be Christian. Does not St. Paul teach us to refer everything to the Lord: “Whether you eat, drink, or do anything else, do all for the glory of God?” (1 Cor 10:31)».

Manner: evangelizing the culture and not culturalizing the Gospel

Now then, the Directory specifically clarifies that this inculturation of the Gospel must be done—and it uses this expression—by evangelizing the culture and not by culturalizing the Gospel. What does this mean? What must we keep in mind in this regard? What must we guard against?

Well then:

1. From the very fact that Jesus Christ became man without ceasing to be God, we must learn to be in the world31, «without being of the world»32. We must go to the world to convert it and not to blend into it. We must go to the culture and to the cultures of man not to be converted into them, but to heal them and elevate them with the power of the Gospel, doing, analogously, what Christ did: «He suppressed what was diabolical, assumed what was human and communicated to it what was divine»33.

2. It also means that, just as Christ became like us in all things except sin (Heb 4:15), sin, error, and all their derivatives cannot be assumed. That is to say, without conversion reconciliation is impossible; without renouncing evil there is no redemption. There can be no unity at the expense of truth. There is no holiness without purity of soul: «holiness means purity»34.

3. On the other hand, only what has dignity or necessity can be assumed. Neither the inhuman, nor the anti-human, nor the subhuman can be assumed. The irrational, the absurd and all their derivatives cannot be assumed.

4. But nothing that is authentically human should be rejected, since Christ assumed an integral human nature. We must assume everything human—that is, all the elements of truth and goodness present in cultures—since «what was not assumed was not redeemed either»35, and the human that is not assumed «becomes a new idol with an old malice»36.

5. And this assuming of the human must not be merely apparent, but real. This assumption is only real when it truly transforms the human in Christ, elevating it, dignifying it, perfecting it. What is left only at the human level has only apparently been assumed.

6. In a particular way, what has been said applies to the evangelization of culture, where we must always be firm in our Catholic identity and know how to discern and dialogue with those whom we have to evangelize. Without ever forgetting that «true inculturation is from within: and it consists, ultimately, in a renewal of life under the influence of grace»37. Indeed, the Lord Jesus sent His disciples to proclaim the Gospel to the whole world and to baptize all nations (cf. Mk 16:15-16; Mt 28:18-20; also Lk 24:46-48; Jn 17:18; 20:21; Acts 1:8).

7. Furthermore, the Directory of the Third Order says: “With a disinterested love of God, in a specific way, the lay members of the Incarnate Word will distinguish themselves by their help to priests and to men and women religious, who are the representatives of God on earth, those placed by God to direct the Church, being ever ready to serve and collaborate, so that they may always find in these lay people hands ready to collaborate in the great enterprise of evangelization”38. That is to say, this is one of the channels through which you too can evangelize the culture.

Farewell

Well, that is more or less what I wanted to tell you. And to conclude, I wanted to come back to something I told you at the beginning: that we are “a single family, united not only by the same faith, but by the same ends, the same mission, the same charism, the same character and the same spirit”.

The spiritual union among us, between the religious and our dear members of the Third Order, rightly understood, is very deep and bears unforeseeable fruits for the life of our institutes and of the Church.

You are for us a great potential, a treasure! A very great help, in every sense. Let us always remain united, founded on the Incarnate Word and always faithful to Him. Above all, let us have immense confidence in the merits of our Lord Jesus Christ and in the power of His death and resurrection. And let us always remain joyful and always at peace, in the Incarnate Word, because truth prevails over lies, good over evil, peace over war, mercy over vengeance, and the Most Holy Virgin of Luján, the Woman Clothed with the Sun, radiates her grace over our nation, over each one of our families, over each one of her missionary children.

To Her I entrust all of you, and I also commend myself to your prayers.

Thank you very much for coming! May God bless you!
