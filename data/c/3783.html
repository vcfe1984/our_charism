Today, the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word wants to say [to the world]: Rege O Maria!

  • Because our spirituality is that of the “Hail Mary” and of the “Angelus”1;
  • Because our hymn is that of the kenosis2 and the “Gloria” resounds in our souls when we call her “Mother”;
  • Because in her fiat and in her magnificat our religious life finds the model for the self-giving of our own life3:

Rege O Maria!

  • Because our identity is to be “essentially missionary and Marian”4 and Mary, “the first Missionary after her Son Jesus Christ”5, is the eminent model to follow in our task of inculturating the Gospel6;
  • Because the Mother of God, like a tower girded with strong defenses, is the safeguard of our faith and strengthens the bonds of communion among her children;
  • Because She was, by divine election, the ally in the struggle against sin and its consequences7 and her irreconcilable enmity with the evil enemy will have no end:

Rege O Maria!

  • Because no greater consolation has been given us in this valley of tears than to be able to say “that the treasure of God, in which He has placed what is most precious to Him, is also ours”8;
  • Because we want to thank God for His countless benefits and to obtain new ones through the Suppliant Omnipotence of Mary;
  • Because the Mother of the Incarnate Word and our Mother is the image and fulfillment of the Kingdom of God, and she calls us to it and awaits us in it;

For all this and much more, it springs from us as a natural impulse of the soul to say:

Rege O Maria!

And just as in times past legions of men and women united under one same standard to defeat enemies and win triumphs for their king, today we, the members of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, from the four corners of the earth, have united under the sky-blue and white standard of the Pure and Immaculate Conception of Luján, represented in the sign of the scapular we have just received, in order to manifest our irrevocable belonging to the Most Blessed Virgin Mary and our most absolute and radical commitment to work without reserve for the reign of Jesus Christ through His most holy Mother, according to the mission of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, which is none other than “to bring to fulfillment the consequences of the Incarnation of the Word”9.

The scapular that from today we wear on our breast is the sign of our “covenant” and reciprocal communion with the Mother of God. And just as the great ones of the world consider it an honor that other men should wear their insignia, so we trust that Most Holy Mary will look with pleasure upon us, her devoted children, wearing her scapular as a clear and courageous testimony that we are consecrated to her service. That is our badge of honor, and it is in this sublime office that we wish to stand out.

But the scapular is not only the sign of our covenant; it is also the mold in which we want to learn the most absolute availability to the will of the Father, so as to be transformed into what God wants us to be: other Christs10; and it is also a shield and bulwark while “we go on pilgrimage amid the persecutions of the world and the consolations of God”11, according to those words of St. Augustine: “Oh! How powerful and strong we are before Jesus Christ when we are armed with the merits and intercession of the worthy Mother of God, who lovingly overcame the Almighty!”12.

So then, if the scapular of Our Lady of Luján is our standard, our mold, our shield and bulwark, “to marianize life”13 is our program of life.

To marianize is something more than invoking Our Lady; it is to copy the style of Most Holy Mary, her constant yes, her sentiments; it is to incorporate into our existence all that the spirit of Most Holy Mary is and contains.

“All for Jesus and for Mary;

with Jesus and with Mary;

in Jesus and in Mary;

for the sake of Jesus and of Mary”14.

That is the “fundamental code” of our spirituality. It is the precious pearl that we must treasure, guard, know how to transmit to the peoples and bequeath to those who will come after us. Let us realize that “the congregation will always live within the Immaculate Heart of Mary if each one of us is capable of making others see that not only by our words but by our deeds we belong to Her”15.

Let us rejoice, then, and celebrate! For that is the fitting response of a lover to whom what he loves has fallen by lot16. And to us God has granted the honorable privilege of being slaves of Mary.

Listen well, all of you: May wearing the scapular close to our heart enliven our hope, because in the same way that God “looked upon” our Mother the Virgin Mary and did “great things” in Her, displaying His power, we trust that He will do the same with us, His children of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, who give everything through the hands of His Holy Mother; and that is why we can hope with certain hope that our Lord will scatter the proud, cast down the mighty, fill the hungry, exalt the lowly and protect His servants, forever17.

Therefore: “Oh! you, whoever you are [and wherever you may find yourself; with all your limitations and miseries, with all your successes and failures, with all your triumphs and battles, with all your virtues and sins] …look to the Star, call upon Mary.

[…] May her name never depart from your lips, never leave your heart; and to obtain the help of her intercession, do not neglect the examples of her life. Following her, you will not go astray; praying to her, you will not despair; thinking of Her, you will avoid all error. If She upholds you, you will not fall; if She protects you, you will have nothing to fear; if She guides you, you will not grow weary; if She is favorable to you, you will reach the goal. And thus you will verify, by your own experience, how rightly it was said: “And the name of the Virgin was Mary”18.

Come on! Take heart! For we belong to Our Lady and Our Lady is ours.

Today and always, may our cry be: Rege o Maria!

Long live the Virgin!
