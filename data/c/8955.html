Memorial

Missal

From the Common of Martyrs: for several martyrs.

Collect

God, our Father,
who led Saints Pedro and Inocencio, priests,
and companions, martyrs,
with the help of the Mother of God,
to the imitation of Christ
even to the shedding of their blood,
grant us, through their example and intercession,
to confess the faith with fortitude, in word and in deed.
Through our Lord Jesus Christ.

Liturgy of the Hours

Saints Pedro, a diocesan priest and founder of the Teresian Association, and Inocencio of the Immaculate, a Passionist religious, head the multitude of saints and blesseds – bishops, priests, consecrated persons and laity – who gave to Christ the supreme testimony of love, martyred in hatred of the faith in Spain between 1931 and 1939, during the religious persecution against the Church.

Office of Readings

Second Reading

From the sermons of St. Augustine, bishop.

(Sermon 335, 1-2: PL 38, 1470)

The meaning of martyrdom

Since this is the feast of the holy martyrs, what better can we speak of than their glory? May the Lord of the martyrs help us, since he is their crown. A short while ago we heard the blessed apostle Paul proclaiming the cry of the martyrs themselves: Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Such is the cry of the martyrs. Tribulation? Distress? Persecution? Hunger? Nakedness? Dangers? The sword? For it is written: “For your sake we are put to death all the day long and counted as sheep for the slaughter.” But in all these things we conquer through him who loved us.

This is the cry of the martyrs: to bear everything, not to presume on themselves, and to love the One who is glorified in his own, so that whoever boasts may boast in the Lord. They also knew what we sang a short while ago: Rejoice in the Lord and exult, you just. If the just rejoice in the Lord, the unjust know how to rejoice only in the world.

But this is the first army to be overcome: first pleasure must be overcome, and then pain. How can someone overcome the cruelty of the world who is unable to overcome its flatteries? This world flatters by promising honors, riches, pleasure; this world threatens by making use of pain, poverty and humiliation. Whoever does not despise what it promises, how can he overcome its threats? Riches bring their own delight; who does not know it? But justice has a greater delight still.

The Apostle certainly passed over all the flatteries of the world, and he wanted you, the one flattered by the world, to call them to mind. Why? Because he was announcing in advance the combats of the martyrs: those combats in which they overcame persecution, hunger, thirst, want, dishonor and, finally, the fear of death and the cruelest of enemies.

But consider, brothers, that all of it is the work of Christ’s art. The Apostle invites us to prefer the love of Christ to the love of the world. How many hardships must those endure who wish to steal what belongs to others? Persecution? Not even persecution breaks them. The miser says in his heart what perhaps he does not dare to say with his tongue: Who separates us from the craving for gold? Tribulation? Distress? Persecution? The misers too can say to gold: “For your sake we are led to death day by day.”

Rightly, then, do the holy martyrs say in the psalm: Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from that of wicked people. Distinguish, he said, my tribulation, for the misers also suffer tribulations. Distinguish my distress, for the misers also suffer it. Distinguish my persecutions, for the misers also suffer them. Distinguish my hunger, for, provided they acquire gold, the misers also suffer it. Distinguish my nakedness, for the misers too let themselves be stripped for gold. Distinguish my death, for the misers too die for gold.

What does it mean: Distinguish my cause? For your sake we are led to death day by day. They suffer all that for gold; we, for you. The punishment is the same, but the cause is different. If the cause is different, the victory is assured. Therefore, if we look to their cause, we will love these feasts of the martyrs. Let us love in them not their sufferings but the cause of those sufferings; for if we love only their sufferings, we will find many who suffer worse things for evil causes.

But let us look at the cause; look at the cross of Christ: there was Christ, and there were the thieves. The punishment was the same, but the cause was different. One thief believed, the other blasphemed. The Lord, as at a tribunal, acted as judge for both: the one who blasphemed he sent to hell; the other he took with him to paradise. Why this? Because, although the punishment was the same, the cause of each was different. Choose, then, the causes of the martyrs if you want to attain the palm of the martyrs.
