For the minor seminarians

[Exordium] Dear Fathers, prefects, and my dear minor seminarians, it gives me great joy to be able to be with you on the day we celebrate the Immaculate Heart of Mary. This gives us the occasion to ponder that phrase of the Directory of Minor Seminaries which says: “The Minor Seminary must be a school of filial and deep love for the Most Holy Virgin Mary”1.

All of us here, by the simple fact of being Christians, have the Virgin Mary as our Mother. Because “just as in the natural order every child must have a father and a mother, so in the order of grace every true child of the Church must have God as Father and Mary as Mother”2. But even more, being members of the Institute, we belong to the Virgin with an irrevocable belonging: we profess ourselves to be essentially Marian. And therefore our houses, and especially the minor seminary, where the figure of a mother is so necessary, are houses of the Virgin. The maternal presence of the Mother of God is what makes our ‘houses’ a true home, where there is familiarity3, where there is trust, where there is security because the Mother who watches over us is there, where there is joy because one feels loved4; in a word, she is the one who allows us to live in a warm family atmosphere5.

Filial and deep love

Hence, then, the seminary must be a school of filial and deep love for the Virgin, who has a Heart so very, very great that, as St. Louis Marie says, the Virgin “loves her children with tenderness, but with a tenderness greater than that of all mothers put together. Gather together, if you can, –says the saint– all the natural love that all the mothers of the world have for their children into the heart of a single mother for her only son: certainly this mother would love that son very much. Mary, however, truly loves her children more tenderly than this mother would love hers”6.

That is why, right from the start of the day, the minor seminarian must begin by praying the three Hail Marys, which will allow him to greet her –as a son gives his mother a kiss on getting up– and to begin the day in honor of the Virgin7. And at night the same: those Hail Marys are like a good-night kiss to the Mother.

Throughout the whole day there are many moments in which one can show affection to the Virgin, whether by praying the Angelus8, or praying the rosary9, renewing one’s consecration to her after Communion, kissing her mantle during a visit to the Blessed Sacrament, and of course invoking her help in the face of any difficulty, any danger, any temptation; whatever the situation, we must always be quick to have recourse to this tender and kind Mother.

Many of you, I believe all of you, wear the scapular of Our Lady of Luján. In your fears, or if you ever experience loneliness, feel on your chest the embrace of the Virgin who comforts, who heals, who accompanies you night and day through this valley of tears. And whenever you can, at least once a year, try to read some book about the Mother of God that will help you deepen your devotion to the Virgin. St. Louis Marie, as he himself says, read all the books that dealt with devotion to the Most Holy Virgin10

Angello Giuseppe Roncalli

That is why today I wanted to propose to you as an example Angello Giuseppe Roncalli, who later became Pope and is today St. John XXIII. He, as you will know, entered the Seminary of Bergamo when he was only 11 years old. There, at 14, he began to write some spiritual notes that accompanied him, in one form or another, throughout his life, and that were gathered in what is known today as the “Journal of a Soul.” There one reads of the spiritual struggles Angello went through in his life as a minor seminarian: his distractions in prayer11, his failures to keep his resolutions12, his efforts to guard purity13, his struggles to master his tongue and not be so quick to answer impatiently14, even a toothache that would not let him pray15, etc.

However, Angelo, far from being discouraged by the difficulties or by thinking that he was not advancing enough in virtue, would say: “I entrust this to the Virgin, and onward!”16, “with her help I am sure I will be able to get through”17. His great and tender devotion to the Virgin kept growing over the years. And he himself recommended it and resolved to teach it to everyone: “I am going to do the best I can on my part to teach others this truth: that the most direct way to go to Jesus is Mary. In a word, I will be all for Mary in order to be all Jesus’s”18. And that is the motto I would like you to treasure in your hearts. Love our Heavenly Mother filially, that is, with the greatest trust, and deeply. May you be able to repeat in the depths of your souls each day as it begins: I will be all for Mary in order to be all Jesus’s.

When Angello was 18 and was already finishing minor seminary, he wrote: “O Immaculate Mary, you know my needs, my faults, my great need to remain in the background, to humble myself, to be despised; and with all these faults you also know my desire to love you and to become a saint. So you must humble me, you must watch over me, you must sanctify me”19. The struggles, the falls, the difficulties always continued, even as a priest and even as Pope, but the maternal help of the Most Holy Virgin never left his side. For Angello had one day asked the Virgin: “O Virgin of the Rosary, bind me with your rosary to Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament”20. And so it was.

As a major seminarian, at the beginning of the month of May, one reads the following entry in his Journal: “The faithful begin the month of May to honor the Incarnate Word. I too come to your feet with love, like a child, to offer you my life and my acts and to ask you for the grace to love Jesus more ardently each day.” And so that we may be persuaded that the saints did not become saints overnight, but that acts of virtue cost them and they made efforts and, of course, asked the Virgin for help so that she might obtain grace for them and they might come out victorious, Angelo wrote: “A few more words to keep your name in my heart, to offer you flowers, praises and my good deeds, to please you and to ask your help. What will please you most this month will be for me to make continual efforts, though without strain, to fulfill my duty in a perfect, serene and cheerful way, without getting angry and without reluctance. Mary! you who have begotten me, make my soul, my thoughts and my acts resemble yours.” Do you see? He knows that on his own he will not be able to offer the Virgin anything that pleases her; that is why he asks her for help.

When he was made a bishop, the future Pontiff took up the custom of praying the rosary in the chapel after supper with the staff of his house. And later, as Successor of Peter, he published an encyclical, Grata Recordatio, on the rosary, written with filial ardor for his Mother, the Virgin Mary.

He died on the evening of June 3, 1963, in a profound spirit of abandonment to Jesus, longing for his embrace.

[Peroratio] My dear Minor Seminarians: it is my fervent prayer for each one of you on this day of the Immaculate Heart that, in imitation of St. John XXIII, you may always want to be all Most Holy Mary’s in order to be all Jesus’s. I repeat to you again: love the Virgin very much, constantly, tenderly, always striving to give her joy. Do not fail to entrust yourselves to her by praying the Rosary and the three Hail Marys. And take heart! because, as the Directory of Minor Seminaries says: “holiness is attainable because it is, above all, the work of God”21 and of the Virgin.

May Our Lady of Luján bless you and protect you always.
