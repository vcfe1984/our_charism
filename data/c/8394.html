20th ANNIVERSARY OF THE C. FABRO PROJECT

[Exordium] As we celebrate today the 20th anniversary of the commendable work of those who began the Cornelio Fabro Cultural Project and of those who carry it on today, this occasion has dimensions that are in a certain way unprecedented, given the cultural, social and ecclesial context of the moment in which we live, and in some way it marks a milestone in the history of this small project, which began with the aim of making known the thought and person of Fr. Fabro (as the most profound and scientific connoisseur of St. Thomas Aquinas) and has become today the guardian of a great philosophical patrimony.

That is why, in all honesty, with admiration and gratitude, I would like to congratulate all of you who in one way or another, over these years and in different countries, have been involved in such a significant and commendable apostolate!

I would like, then, to devote this homily to highlighting the importance of the intellectual activity of our members as an ecclesial service.

1. Intellectual activity oriented to pastoral work

The first thing that must be said is that pastoral activity and intellectual activity can never be set against each other as if they were two antagonistic things (unfortunately this is one of the great tragedies of the present moment in the Church of Jesus Christ). Both are complementary apostolic activities. What is more, both are necessary. One of the principal causes of the crisis in which the Church finds herself at present is precisely the lack of intellectual formation in the clergy and the false dialectic between pastoral work and study.

The priest is formed intellectually, as that incomparable missionary St. Paul said, in order to bring every mind into homage to Christ9, and this is impossible to achieve without an adequate, strong intellectual and academic formation, capable of dialoguing with sound grounding and competence with contemporary culture.

Without being imbued with the truth, without being solidly grounded in perennial philosophy, we might well ask ourselves: how will the priest of today be able to banish error and enlighten minds in a world which, in spite of countless signs of rejection of God, seeks Him by unexpected paths and painfully feels its need of Him10? St. Paul VI asked this in Evangelii Nuntiandi.

The world expects of us, yes, simplicity of life, a spirit of prayer, charity toward all… but it also expects, indeed demands, in a fundamental and preponderant way, the proclamation of the truth, clearly, challengingly and illuminatingly.

And not only the world; for in the particular times of salvation history in which we are given to live, there is also in the Church a true need for the truth… for what is controversial to be clarified, for old errors swarming about under new labels to be unmasked, for the truth to be explicitly proclaimed, for authentic and traditional values to be safeguarded as is fitting, with the necessary renewal and relevance. Hence the importance of the Project whose twentieth anniversary we are celebrating.

It is the intellectual apostolate, rightly understood, exercised through intellectual activities and among intellectuals (thinkers, men of science, researchers, etc.), that of itself exercises a sociocultural influence and an ecclesial service that is not only necessary but incomparable, irreplaceable… indispensable.

In this sense, our Institute, born to evangelize the culture, has before it a field of action and a responsibility as great as the wide world. And although this is done in many ways, there must always be included, in a fundamental and primary way, the task of taking up and illuminating with the truth11 even the most difficult or front-line fields, where there have been or are confrontations: at the crossroads of ideologies and in the social trenches, between the demands of man and the Christian message12; it is there that our evangelizing action must reach.

Hence we should all be aware of the place that this apostolate (of the Cornelio Fabro Cultural Project) must occupy within the whole of our commitments.

Then the objection arises –widespread even among many members of the Church– that if we work in the intellectual field we run the risk of distancing ourselves from the real world and undervaluing the preference for the “urgent” apostolate among the poor, immigrants, care for the environment, etc., and for an active solidarity with the “voiceless.” Indeed, we will hardly be able to serve men (among them also the poor) if we have no contact with them and if we lack a minimum of experience of their life, or of the “smell of the sheep.”

But let us not deceive ourselves: it is no less true that, precisely in order to promote justice and serve the poor, we must also direct our activity toward those who have responsibility or influence in the structures, or in what the Proper Law calls “the turning points of culture”13, among whom men of thought or ‘intellectuals’ have great social influence. We will be good servants in the social apostolate if we have a solid formation in the intellectual field; otherwise it will be very easy for us to go astray. We have seen this, and not just a few times.

It is the men of thought, as you well know, who are “the principal ‘creators’ of culture, for they have an incisive influence on the behavior and cultural models of men and of societies. It is they, above all, who present to society ‘the criteria of judgment, the determining values, the points of interest, the lines of thought, the sources of inspiration and the models of life of humanity’14”15. Our Proper Law says this, and the Holy Pope Paul VI also said it.

That is why the Spiritual Father of our Institute, St. John Paul the Great, said back in 1988: “Our time, marked by great and important sociocultural changes, urgently requires an evangelization of minds at all levels of knowledge. Therefore –the Pope goes on to say– without letting yourselves be influenced by any doubt, you must remain rigorously faithful to your charism”16 (and I add) as well as to that bold sense of giving a convincing answer “starting from the metaphysics of being to the challenges that come from the various philosophical currents”17.

The loneliness of modern man and the longing for an answer that gives meaning to existence must be a stimulus toward an ever more ardent and effective pastoral zeal. While today the greatest possible intellectual seriousness is required for any apostolic activity (without everything being reduced, evidently, to that), great pastoral sensitivity is also demanded.

That is also why it is necessary that our intellectual activity reach “technological man, who places all his trust and all his interest in science and technology in order to attain the maximum well-being, and then finds himself empty”18; that it root out the relativism that assaults consciences19; that it reach the man who flaunts his unbelief; and that it also reach, why not, the churchman who strays from the purity of our faith, carried away by strange doctrines20, as the Apostle says.

The intellectual apostolate can never and must never be confined within the walls of a library, but must be disseminated, must spread, since, as the Directory of Spirituality says: “the field of our action has no limits on its horizons”21, and that, I believe, is the great challenge for the future of the Fabro Project. So to speak, to “make it reach” … further and further… In a word, we must make the world a sharer in the great treasure that has been bequeathed to us.

For so it is… the intellectual apostolate, rightly understood, is an apostolate with a clear and authentic ecclesial mission. Hence the urgency of having members dedicated to working on and spreading this project, which was designated at the last General Chapter as a “definitely priority” project22.

And truly, in my humble understanding, I believe that the intellectual dimension inherent in our preferential apostolic options would not be respected if a significant number of members were not dedicated to working in an explicitly intellectual apostolate –speaking in general terms (as is being done at this time here in Fossanova)– and specifically in this project, as in any other project that promotes essential Thomism. Since that –as we know– is implied in one of the non-negotiable elements attached to our charism.

I do not mean by this that the Institute should turn completely toward the intellectual, but it is undeniable that this occupies for us a relevant place when it comes to evangelizing the culture. From this follows the unconditional support that this type of apostolate deserves from us and from all the members of our Religious Family (and we must never tire of insisting on this, even if we meet with misunderstanding, even if we have to defend it and impose it); and the accompaniment that the religious employed in this task require.

For it is understood and clear that those who dedicate themselves exclusively to the investigation of truth, to teaching and to dispelling the darkness of ignorance through the spreading of truth, are fulfilling a strictly pastoral office, and of the most qualified kind.

2. Learning, a requirement

That is why our Proper Law says that this is not only the task of specialists or only of the members dedicated to working in the Fabro Project. It is something that concerns us all.

All of us, as priests, having received from Christ the office and mission of teaching the Truth –teach all nations23– need “to be very attentive to the ‘signs of the times’ in order to enlighten souls at the time and moment they need it”24 (this is a quotation from the Proper Law); that is why it is necessary to be formed not only sporadically, but permanently; what is more, the priest should be in a certain way a thinker, in order to discern the human vicissitudes that Providence disposes or permits, so as then to be able to preach well.

Pope Pius XI in one of his encyclicals emphasized this very thing, saying that “the image of the Catholic priest would remain incomplete […] if we did not highlight another most important requirement that the Church demands of him: learning”25.

Nowadays we see how “without solid preparation, not a few priests are devoured by the world; and pastoral work itself ends up in shreds”26.

That is why the Magisterium of the Church emphasizes that “it is necessary […] that the priest, even when already immersed in the overwhelming occupations of his holy ministry, and with his sights set on it, continue in the serious and deep study of theological subjects, increasing from day to day the sufficient store of learning acquired in the seminary with new treasures of sacred erudition that equip him more and more for preaching and for the direction of souls. He must, moreover, for the dignity of the ministry he carries out, and to win, as is fitting, the trust and esteem of the people, which serve so much for the greater fruitfulness of his pastoral labor, possess that store of knowledge, not precisely sacred, which is the common patrimony of the cultured persons of the time; that is, he must be a modern man, in the good sense of the word, as is the Church, who extends to all times, to all countries, and adapts herself to all of them; who blesses and encourages all sound initiatives and does not fear the advances, not even the boldest, of science, provided it is true science”27.

“To evangelize the culture means to ‘make’ Christian culture, and this presupposes having culture”28. Without study, without knowledge of modern culture, dialogue with the world is impossible. That is why we have seen how, throughout history, the Church herself, after having preserved and saved the treasures of ancient culture, has shown us in her most illustrious Doctors how all human knowledge can contribute to the clarification and defense of the Catholic faith; among them stands out “the unsurpassed master Thomas Aquinas”29, whom Fr. Cornelio Fabro knew how to interpret so masterfully.

That is why Fr. Fabro himself –priest and religious– presents himself to us on this anniversary as exemplary for his distinguished work in this privileged field on behalf of the Church.

[Peroratio] That is why I would like to encourage all of you, then, to continue with increased missionary zeal the magnificent work already being done. I believe that of all the apostolates the Institute carries out, perhaps the one that produces the most, tangibly and quantitatively speaking, is the Cornelio Fabro Cultural Project. For it is the apostolate that, under the guidance of the towering philosopher and theologian from Flumignano, engages like no other in an authentic dialogue with contemporary culture.

“Christ, said Paul VI in a masterful phrase, made Himself contemporary to some men and spoke their language. Fidelity to Him requires that this contemporaneity continue”30. This, as I see it, is the great task carried out by the Cornelio Fabro Cultural Project, and it is, at the same time, the great challenge that lies before it.

That is why, once again, we not only congratulate you, but we, the other members, are very grateful to you, and we wish you a future full of blessings and achievements for the cause of living Thomism.

May Christ, through all of us and especially through the work of the Cornelio Fabro Cultural Project, become ever more contemporary to all men.

May the Most Holy Virgin grant us this grace.
