Solemnity of the Exaltation of the Cross

[Exordium] Dear Mothers and Sisters, it is a grace for me, and certainly a great joy, to celebrate this Mass with you, which I gladly offer together with the priests present here in thanksgiving for all the Servants who throughout the world live to the full “the radicality of the Cross”1; and asking at the same time for the grace that “we may never lack the Cross” , but that it may be the “most beautiful program of life that [all] those who take pride in bearing the name of Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará strive to attain”2.

From the very beginning the Servants have been associated with the cross. In fact, they were born from the cross and they carry everywhere the emblem of the Cross of Matará that distinguishes them, and it is precisely the cross that is the platform from which they must always launch themselves “boldly to restore all things in Christ”3. It is as if a Servant cannot be understood without the cross and, I do not think I am mistaken when I say, that it is precisely the cross that draws them to itself.

That is why, if you allow me, I would like to point out some elements that show how the Servants have been –and must always continue to be– bound to the cross.

1. Name

One of the elements that clearly shows us the association of the Servants with the cross is found in the very founding act of the Institute of the Servants, where it is explained how the sisters had wanted to be called “slaves,” and how some thought they should be called “daughters,” but that it was the Bishop, Msgr. León Kruk, who decided that they should be called “Servants,” according to the account of St. Luke which speaks of some women… Mary Magdalene, Joanna, the wife of Chuza, Susanna and many others, who served the Lord while He went through cities and villages, preaching4.

Later, our Founder would add that they are called Servants because the sisters must resemble those holy women who stood by the cross, because of what the evangelists say: there were many women there […] among whom were…5 , by the Cross of Jesus…: Mary, his Mother6; Mary Magdalene, Mary Salome, Mary of Clopas, and many others7. And so these women, having gone up with Him to Jerusalem, contemplated the sacrifice of the cross8. That is why these words are explicitly addressed to the Servants: “You must be among them. The holy women are the first servants there have been in the history of the Church, and from them you receive your name”9.

Therefore it is a most special vocation of the Servants to imitate the Incarnate Word in his self-emptying, as we heard in the second reading. A vocation to self-emptying that implies the cross: the cross of becoming a servant10, of humiliation11, of obedience even to accepting death12 for the love of God.

What is more, the Directory of Consecrated Life states very clearly that it is the task of the Servants to give special witness to the Incarnate Word through “humility, in disinterested service and, in particular, in merciful love”13.

For “one of the characteristics of woman is to make herself a gift. Precisely in ‘the following of Christ, expressed in service’14, [a service which] often implies sacrifice. For to make oneself a gift is to give one’s own life to the other, so that the other may decide what he wants to do with the giver. It is a total service accompanied by a high degree of forgetfulness of self, in order thus to be totally free in the service of the other”15. You must always keep this in mind, since to act otherwise would be to betray the very reason for the existence of the Servants.

2. Cross of Matará

Another element that very especially manifests the indissoluble union between our dear Sisters and the Cross is found in the fact that they are called “Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará” because they identify themselves with the Cross of Matará, “on which are carved the central mysteries of our spirituality: the Incarnation, the Passion, the Eucharist, etc.”16, and we can add: “Because the Lord is crucified there and we want to know nothing but Jesus Christ and Jesus Christ crucified17, through whom the world is crucified to me and I to the world, as St. Paul says”18. And because at the foot of the cross stood the most compassionate First Servant, who must always remind them “not only of why [they are so called], but of the place, the role and the mission of the Servants.”

Likewise we must say that the Cross of Matará, being the oldest in America, had a clear catechetical purpose, becoming for us “a most beautiful example of the evangelization of culture, which is precisely the specific end of our Institutes”19, and it is its doctrine, that is, the doctrine of the cross, that we must go about preaching throughout the world: Jesus Christ crucified, a scandal to the Jews and folly to the Greeks20. And to do this without timidity, but boldly, as we said at the beginning, because our trust must always be in Him who by the power of his cross conquered the world.

3. The Habit and the Cross of Matará

Another element that plainly reveals the firm association of the Servants with the cross is that from the very beginning the religious habit our sisters wore (perhaps you have seen it in photos) bore as part of the tunic the tau cross in blue, a symbol of the religious sister’s espousal with the cross of our Lord.

Today, the habit our sisters wear bears as an integral part and distinctive sign the Cross of Matará, which shines on the blue background of the scapular to announce to the world that they are not of the world21and that they have been “espoused to Christ under the sign of the cross”22. Even more: the cross they always wear on their breast reminds them and proclaims to the world that the sisters hold as their most sublime office “to live with Him the demands of God’s plan and to set out courageously on the way of the cross”23, because “Jesus is loved and served on the Cross and crucified with Him, and in no other way”24, and only thus do they understand that they are to be fruitful, begetting many spiritual children for God.

Therefore, a true Servant of Christ “must never be turned in on herself, but must be open like the arms of Christ on the Cross, whose arms were wrenched out of joint from opening them so wide out of love.”25

4. Feast of the Exaltation

As a consequence of all we have been saying, and without going any further, from the very beginning this same feast of the Exaltation of the Cross which we celebrate today has been and is the proper and principal feast of the Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará; “because of the most lofty significance of the Cross,” says the founding act. So much so that among us this feast has become synonymous with the feast of the Servants.

For in it we are reminded –as we heard in today’s Gospel– that God so loved the world that he gave his only Son, so that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but may have eternal life26. That is, it speaks to us of the redeeming love of Christ, who called them to religious life precisely “only to deal in love with Jesus Christ”27.

But we must also know how to read in this marvelous feast the longing we must have to participate actively in the redemptive action of Christ and to bring that saving love to all peoples, even to the most difficult places (those where no one wants to go)28 and even in the most adverse situations29, striving to conform our life to that of Christ Crucified through “humble service and generous dedication, in the free gift of oneself through a love to the very end”30, as our Proper Law asks.

That is why this feast gives us the occasion to celebrate and give thanks together for the vocation and mission of our dear Sister Servants, which is none other than to raise high the Cross of Christ throughout the length and breadth of this world (as in fact they already do very well in the 35 countries where they are present, and very particularly here, in this beloved country of Ecuador).

[Peroratio] Finally, my dear Mothers and Sisters: you know well that what is proper to us is “neither Jesus without the Cross, nor the Cross without Jesus”31, and that it is one of the healthy traditions of our beloved Religious Family to place at the foot of the cross a sign that says: “This is how one loves.”

Because, as we have heard so many times: “The cross makes fruitful whatever it touches […] The cross is the pulse of love, and to know how to suffer, one must know how to love”32.

That is why on this day, on which with great joy we celebrate the Exaltation of the Cross, which is the same as saying the exaltation of God’s love for men, I invite you to know how to respond to that love with the only possible measure, that is, with radicality.

Far be it from you to become discouraged or troubled before the cross. Far be it from you to make excuses or complain about the cross. Rather, overcoming the fears so proper to our nature and pride, go forward with a firm spirit to embrace the cross with generosity and joy.

“The Cross is God’s most beautiful gift.” And so Jesus made it known to St. Gemma: “See, my daughter, the greatest gift I can grant to a soul loved by me is the gift of suffering”33.

That is why we ask in this Holy Mass: “that you may always know how to embrace, love and never come down from the Cross,” “that you may truly follow Christ Crucified and want to be crucified with Him,” because that is “to attain holiness and to spread the holiness of Christ throughout the whole world”34.

I raise this same prayer today for each and every one of you, invoking the intercession of Most Holy Mary, who is the Model of love to the end.

May Our Lady at the foot of the Cross bless and protect you always.
