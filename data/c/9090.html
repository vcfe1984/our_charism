The most excellent prayer, which is Holy Mass

One of the great loves of every member of the Institute is the Eucharist1. In accordance with this, we read in our Constitutions: The principal thing, the most important thing we must do each day, is to participate in the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass2 and, in our case, to celebrate it. “We must be characterized by the importance that must be given to the celebration of Holy Mass, as well as by the reverent way of celebrating it”3, as one reads in one of the acts of the General Chapter of 2007, which points to this very thing as a non-negotiable element attached to the charism.

That is why one can never insist enough on the fact that it falls to us “to be masters of the ars celebrandi, and to our major seminarians, our brothers, etc., to strive on their part to live the ars participandi in the most perfect way”4.

“It must be borne in mind that the celebration of the Mass is a thermometer of priestly life”5. To such a point that it can be said that “a priest is worth as much as his Eucharistic life is worth; his Mass above all. A Mass without love, a sterile priest; a fervent Mass, a priest who conquers souls. A neglected Eucharistic devotion without love, a slack priesthood, even more, one in danger”6.

The Eucharist, St. Leonardo Murialdo recalled, is not a rite to be performed but a mystery to be lived7.

And if this is a mystery to be lived, then we must not forget that our “style of liturgical celebrations as part of our charism [consists of] celebrations in which the Word becomes incarnate and in which he appears –sacramentally– Incarnate, in which the principal presence and action of the principal Priest8 is always highlighted, in which it is perceived that the essential attitude of the secondary priest is the prayerful attitude –proper to one who knows himself to be a mere instrument, and a deficient instrument, subordinate to the principal cause and subject to its ends–, in which all the visible elements contribute to the splendorous knowledge of the Invisible9.

Concretely: “our liturgical celebrations must be exemplary: ‘in their rites, in their spiritual and pastoral tone, and in the fidelity due both to the prescriptions and texts of the liturgical books and to the norms issued by the Holy See and the Episcopal Conferences’”10.

St. John of the Cross, with a bit of humor, will tell those who want to impose special rules on the priest for the Eucharistic celebration to leave “the mode and manner of saying Mass to the priest whom the Church has there in her place, for he has instruction from her as to how he must do it. And let them not want to use new means, as if they knew more than the Holy Spirit and his Church”11.

Therefore, all of us priests of the Institute must strive so that the liturgy of our Masses may be cathedral-like without formalism, beautiful without affectation, solemn without pomposity, austere yet full, faithful to the rubrics yet creative, with the maximum participation and developing to the maximum all the possibilities the liturgy itself offers, particularly in the chants and sacred music12.

We must celebrate Mass “with unction without affectation, with solemnity without bombast, with fidelity without lacking healthy creativity, with piety without pietism, with the awareness that the one who does the principal work is Jesus Christ –the principal Priest–, being transparent of him and not an opaque membrane that prevents people from transcending to him”13. How many there are who think they are the principal actors of the Mass! “What supernatural vision does the priest have who turns the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass into a kind of show in which he is the protagonist?”14

I find the following statement of the Proper Law supremely true: liturgical celebrations will generally be what the celebrant wants them to be and what he himself is. That is why it falls to him to take care of all the details so that the celebration may be as it should15.

That is why I would like to recall what the Proper Law says, for example, about some details regarding words and gestures in the Mass. It reads:

“The visible elements have great importance in the liturgy. The word must have its rhythm, neither fast nor slow, neither a grating voice nor a lifeless one; it must be spoken with expression, without theatricality, with sincerity, clarity, precision, originality, unction”16. The same applies to the liturgical proclamation of the Word of God, which must first be venerated and interiorized by us so that it may then reach the faithful as a true light and a strength for the present moment17.

– Liturgical gestures must be full, not unnecessarily repetitive, orderly, human, not those of automatons. They must not be omitted. The quality of our celebrations must be cared for down to the details: careless, slack, unprepared, hasty, verbose, anodyne18, insipid celebrations… weaken the faith of the participants19”20.

– Likewise some particular elements are specified, such as:

a) giving room to contemplative silence: “… silence occupies an indispensable place in the liturgy, when it is well prepared; it allows each one to develop in his heart the spiritual dialogue with the Lord21;

b) that the signs speak for themselves. Silence is favored when fire is fire and not an electric lamp; when the candle can melt and drip instead of being a wick fed by a flammable liquid; flowers should give off perfume, open and wither, and that is better than their being artificially eternal; that the liturgical space allow for the dynamism of the liturgical action; that the ambo be the worthy place in which to set the Word of God; that the chair show forth the one who acts in the person of Christ the Priest; that the altar speak for itself and that everything be directed toward it, because the sacrifice has been the price of our souls; that the vestments be worthy; that they make it possible to distinguish the priest from the people and even the principal celebrant from the concelebrants22.

c) Finally, proximate and remote preparation is absolutely necessary for the liturgical celebration in general and especially for our daily Mass. In fact, the day should be divided in two: one part of preparation, the other of thanksgiving. There is nothing more important in our Christian life. When one celebrates or participates in the Mass, the day is already won23.

– Moreover, it is always characteristic of our Masses to give Communion with hosts consecrated at that same Mass and to offer Communion under both species whenever possible and permitted24.

– To take care of the singing, because it too is a “necessary or integral part of the solemn liturgy”25.

I mean, all these little things are details, and much of all this is perhaps already being done, but they are details that always help us to improve and to go deeper into the mystery we celebrate, to pay more attention to it, to do it with more unction, so as not to fall into monotony either, so as not to lose the sense of the mystery we celebrate. And I also believe they shed light when it comes to making concrete resolutions for an ever better and more fruitful celebration of Holy Mass.
