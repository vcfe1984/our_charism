1. In our spirituality

It is remarkable how our Constitutions speak of the virtue of eutrapelia, which shows that the practice of this virtue is not incidental for us. Fr. Buela uses very strong words when he refers to participation in the moments of rest for the soul: “the rest of the soul is delight, so this must be the remedy against weariness of the soul, interrupting the tension of the spirit. Hence it is necessary to procure this rest for the soul through games and festivities, the moderation of which belongs to the virtue of eutrapelia, practiced in the moments of recreation”1.

But not only is it recommended in this way; even much more, in number 214 of the Directory of Spirituality we read: “Whoever does not practice the virtue of eutrapelia gladly will hardly persevere,” and it even warns us: “one must be very wary of those who, under the cloak of virtue, do not want to practice the virtue of eutrapelia; they are generally driven by their selfishness, fail in charity toward their neighbor, and are harsh in judgment. The worst thing is that they go against the truth of man, who, because he is intelligent, has to know how to be playful.”

When community life is spoken of, in the part on discipline, the first point dealt with is that of recreation: “In this order, the time dedicated to recreation or eutrapelia deserves special mention… Above all in the smaller communities no one should be excused from participating in it,” and it says: “generally, if one of our members does not participate cordially in recreation, after correcting him, if he does not change, let him be told charitably that he does not have the spirit for our Institute, and let him be separated”2.

2. Joy in the Christian life

Joy is essential in the Christian life, since it is a necessary fruit of the virtue of charity, that is, of love of God and neighbor lived in fullness. As charity grows, joy will necessarily grow. On the contrary, faults against joy are in turn faults against the virtue of charity.

It can be said that if one lives the Christian life well, he must necessarily live the peace and joy that spring from the paschal mystery of Jesus Christ. “Joy, which is the gigantic secret of the Christian, is spiritual and supernatural and is born from considering the mystery of the Incarnate Word: Rejoice, be glad, the angel Gabriel said to Mary; She would later say My spirit exults with joy (Lk 1:47), having moments before testified to Elizabeth the child in my womb leapt for joy (Lk 1:44); and then the angel to the shepherds: I announce to you a great joy, which is for all the people (Lk 2:10); and it is born from acknowledging the mystery of the resurrection of the Lord: filled… with great joy (Mt 28:8); like the disciples of Emmaus: they still did not believe because of their joy and amazement (Lk 24:41), they returned… with great joy (Lk 24:52); the disciples rejoiced on seeing the Lord (Jn 20:20). That is why St. Paul insists: Rejoice, I say it to you again, rejoice (Phil 4:4)”3.

And this is the foundation of our joy; joy presupposes a foundation, something to rejoice about. In our Constitutions Fr. Buela quotes a phrase of Pieper taken from the book Una teoría de la fiesta which says that to rejoice is “the response of a lover to whom what he loves has fallen to his lot”4. Someone rejoices because he possesses the good that is suitable to him, either in reality or in hope. And this is our case: since we possess Jesus Christ and follow Him in the most perfect way that a man can follow Him on this earth, “what we love has fallen to our lot.” The loss of this good is precisely sadness.

That is why, from the Incarnation of the Word to the fulfillment of the paschal mystery, the Gospel is

the great reason for joy and festivity for all Christians. After the triumph of Christ on Easter

Sunday, there is no room for sadness or for pessimism. Because we know well that not only has He conquered, but He has made us sharers in His victory. That is why a spiritual author said: “all our difficulties, all our tribulations, all our desolations and spiritual dramas, lapse before

this proclamation: Christ my hope is risen.”

That is why we must always be joyful, in everything: in the virtues, in sufferings, in ourselves and

in others, in our community and in the Church.

3. Temptations against joy

That is why we must be very careful that the devil does not draw us out of this natural state of Christianity: the kingdom of God is joy, love and peace in the spirit, says the letter to the Romans. And therefore, if we lose this joy, love and peace, we lose something essential in the Christian life, and with much greater reason in religious life. Moreover, it is an Achilles’ heel in the soul that wants to make progress; if the devil does not leave us in peace, if we live sad and weighed down, we know well that we cannot work on our spiritual life that way.

– That is why we must flee from all sadness or melancholy as from the plague: “That is why the Christian must rejoice always and in everything: the Kingdom of God… is joy in the Holy Spirit (Rom 14:17); may the God of hope fill you with complete joy (Rom 15:13); coming to you with joy (Rom 15:32); rejoice (2 Cor 13:11); rejoice in the Lord (Phil 3:1); be always joyful (1 Thess 5:16)”5.

Be careful, then: when there is sadness in the soul, one must ask oneself why and rationally seek the remedy. Don Bosco, when he saw a sad boy in the oratory, would immediately call him and ask him: “what is wrong?” A sad Christianity is inconceivable. If we play sadness’s game, the only one who wins is the devil.

– One must know how to carry on through desolations: which can and must come to the soul. And not

be distressed by that. We seek the God of consolations and not the consolations of God. Even more, to the

soul that wants to progress in the spiritual life, it matters little whether it is in consolation or desolation; the

important thing is to keep going. Quindi, one must know how to act correctly in these moments: The fifth rule of St. Ignatius: “In time of desolation never make a change, but remain firm and constant in the resolutions and determination in which one was on the day preceding such desolation, or in the determination in which one was in the preceding consolation. Because, just as in consolation the good spirit guides and counsels us more, so in desolation the evil spirit does, with whose counsels we cannot take a path to get things right.”

Do not do as so many do… In such times one cannot make decisions…

– Let neither our tribulations be a cause of desolation, nor our sins, nor our failings, nor our failures: “He must also rejoice in sufferings: I overflow with joy in all our tribulations (2 Cor 7:4); as sorrowful yet always rejoicing (2 Cor 6:10); I rejoice in my sufferings (Col 1:24)”6. That is why, even when something fails, act rationally, correct any error there may be on our part, and keep going. And on the contrary, the more there are, try to be all the happier, like St. John

Bosco.

– Nor must we lose our joy over the goods of others. This is a typical temptation of the devil, against which one must positively fight, striving to consider others as our superiors and to admire God who has given them such gifts: “He must also rejoice in the community of the followers of the Risen One: I rejoice in you (Rom 16:19); my joy is also yours (2 Cor 2:3); we want to contribute to your joy through your firmness in the faith (2 Cor 1:24); always, in all my prayers, I pray with joy for you (Phil 1:4); Brothers… my joy and my crown (Phil 4:1); you are our glory and our joy (1 Thess 2:20)”7.

– We must be very careful that crosses do not become a reason for taking away our joy. On the contrary, we should consider ourselves happy, like the apostles, to be worthy of suffering something for Jesus Christ. Any cross, however heavy, must not take away one gram of our joy, but on the contrary must fill us with gladness. Whatever it may be and however hard it may be, embrace it and kiss it as a preferential gift of God and rejoice in it. It is a temptation against which one must positively practice agere contra. The cross cannot take away our spiritual joy. “Those who relish the cross of Christ our Lord find rest living in these labors, and die when they flee from them or find themselves outside them,” said St. Francis Xavier.

Just because we were scolded, we must not think that everything is falling apart. No! Correct and keep going. Just because we were humiliated, we must not give up; on the contrary, we must take advantage of the grace to grow. “whoever gives −St. Paul says to the Corinthians− let him do it with joy”. It is the only characteristic that is mentioned. That is why we must rejoice in all our spiritual efforts: “He must rejoice in the virtues: whoever practices mercy, let him do it with joy (Rom 12:8); joyful in hope (Rom 12:12); strength­ened in every virtue according to the might of His glory, in all patience and long­suffering, with joy (Col 1:11)”8.

4. Essential remedies

For all these reasons, whenever we have the temptation of sadness, or of not wanting to do something as it should be done, it is necessary to seek an urgent remedy; we know well that we cannot go on like this and that, therefore, we are going backward in the spiritual life.

Three remedies are suggested, then:

– To be rational, not sentimental or melancholic. To act with the strength of reason, looking for where the error lies. As a matter of principle we have no reasons to be sad, and quindi it is a temptation of the devil. Therefore, rationally seek the appropriate remedy. If we have been humiliated, accept it with humility. If we have offended someone, ask for forgiveness. If crosses overwhelm us, embrace the cross of Christ with love. If it is a temptation, reject it with all energy and without hesitation.

Let us remember that there are three causes of desolation: 1. it is because we are tepid, lazy or negligent in our spiritual exercises, and so through our faults spiritual consolation withdraws from us; 2. to test us, to see what we are like and how far we extend ourselves in His service and praise without so much payment of consolations and increased graces; 3. to give us true knowledge and understanding so that we may interiorly feel that it is not ours to bring about or to hold such increased devotion.

– The agere contra, when there is no cause: the sixth rule of St. Ignatius: “Given that in desolation we must not change our former resolutions, it helps greatly to change ourselves intensely against the desolation itself, such as by insisting more on prayer, meditation, on much examination, and on extending ourselves in some suitable way of doing penance,” and the seventh: “let the one who is in desolation consider how the Lord has left him to be tested in his natural powers, so that he may resist the various agitations and temptations of the enemy; for he can do so with the divine help, which always remains with him, even though he does not clearly feel it. because the Lord has withdrawn from him his great fervor, increased love and intense grace, leaving him. nevertheless, sufficient grace for eternal salvation.”

– The affective and effective practice of the virtue of charity: St. John Chrysostom said: “Where charity rejoices, there is festivity”9. Our Constitutions say that “joy is a fruit of the Holy Spirit and an effect of charity; one must try by every means that ‘no one be disturbed or saddened in the house of God’10”11. And they add: “For this it is absolutely indispensable to live fraternal charity:

‘That is:

  • let them hold others as more worthy (Rom 12:10);
  • let them bear with boundless patience their weaknesses, both bodily and spiritual;
  • let them put all their effort into obeying one another;
  • let all seek the good of others rather than their own;
  • let them put into practice a sincere fraternal love;
  • let them live always in the fear and love of God;
  • let them love their Abbot [Superior] with a sincere and humble charity;
  • let them prefer absolutely nothing to Christ, and may He bring us all together to eternal life’12”13.

May Our Lady grant us the grace to live and preserve the joy that she lived.
