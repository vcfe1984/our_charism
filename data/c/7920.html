[Exordium] Good afternoon, everyone! Thank you very much for the invitation to visit the school and to celebrate this holy Mass with all of you on this very special day.

Who knows which saint we are celebrating today? Yes, excellent! St. Thomas Aquinas, an Italian saint, and truly a giant for the whole Church.

Some time ago I visited the tomb of this great saint and doctor of the Church in Toulouse, in the church of the Jacobins. There, on a side altar, in a golden urn, are the mortal remains of this hero. I spent a good while praying at the foot of the tomb of St. Thomas, thanking him for having guided me since I was a kid. For when I was about 14 years old and in secondary school, like several of you here, I had the privilege of hearing from a priest the arguments that prove the existence of God, which St. Thomas had formulated in the 13th century. I don’t quite know why, but listening to those very rational proofs of the existence of God, something like a fire was kindled within me. They made me grasp the reality of God much better, and in some way that was where my desire to serve God, to order my life radically toward Him, was awakened.

While I was praying at the tomb of this great “teacher of humanity” –as St. John Paul II called him– I was thinking about the importance St. Thomas Aquinas has for our time. And you might ask yourselves: what can a 13th-century Dominican teacher teach us? Several things…

Truth comes from God

First, Thomas Aquinas saw with total clarity that, since truth comes from God, there can never be a conflict between the information the sciences give us and the facts of revelation, that is, with what the Bible says. In St. Thomas’s time there were some defenders of the “double truth theory”, who said that ‘the truths’ of philosophy and science were in one category and ‘the truths’ of faith were in another. Therefore, according to this interpretation, one could hold two completely opposite truths as long as one was aware that they were two positions located in different compartments of the mind. According to this theory, one could say that God created man male and female and at the same time accept that there are more genders of persons, or even that they have no gender at all.

Well, St. Thomas realized that this theory did not make much sense and that there cannot be two opposite truths about the same question, and he said so. The apparent conflict between science and religion (to use current terms) arises either from bad science or from bad religion, and it should motivate us to think more deeply. Consequently, we must not be afraid to approach the sciences: physics, mathematics, chemistry, biology, etc.; everything that is learned in the sciences should lead us to the contemplation of God’s work in the world.

On the other hand, this thinking more deeply does not mean that each one must develop an autonomous vision of himself or of the world, different from that of others, where each one has his ‘own truth’, which is what unfortunately often happens, and then we hear people say: ‘that is your truth, I have mine’, and everything is called into question. And the worst thing is that these people think that by stating ‘their truth’ they are authentic, when in reality they repeat ad nauseam what they have read on social media or seen on YouTube, or what has gone viral on TikTok, which has nothing original about it and is not necessarily true.

The only final and satisfactory explanation for the existence of things

Second, St. Thomas knew that the Creator God of the Bible is the only final and satisfactory explanation for the existence of the created things of this world; he called them ‘contingent things’. He was struck by the existence of all those things that do not have their reason for being in themselves. Clouds, trees, plants, animals, human beings, buildings like this one, the benches where you are sitting, planets, stars certainly exist, but they do not have to exist necessarily. This means that the being of these things can only be explained by the fact that there is a higher reality that gives them being. That Supreme Being is what St. Thomas called God. How important this is in the times we live in! You know that there are people who do not believe in God; they are called atheists, and they think that believing in God prevents people from acquiring scientific knowledge. In fact, some have gone so far as to say that God does not exist because He cannot be found in the material world, because, according to atheists, the material world is the only thing that exists. So they think that God is like a superstition, and in fact they ridicule people who are believers. And they live as if there were no life after death. The truth is that God is the reason why there is something rather than nothing, why there are rivers, animals, angels, mountains… everything.

It was St. Thomas who, in his demonstration of the existence of God by the five ways, said that God is the first cause of everything that exists, and His creation is full of secondary causes that work every day; for example, the law of gravity, which keeps us from floating in space. God is the first cause and gravity is a secondary cause, which God designed for the order and stability of the universe. However, some atheist scientists say that the autonomy of nature (that plants grow by themselves in the forest, for example) implies that God does not have all power; moreover, it implies that God does not exist, they say. But that is not true. God does not cancel the activity of His creatures, but lets them act according to their nature and thus fulfill the end for which He created them.

So the next time someone tries to make you fall into the trap of believing that God does not exist, you can use the wisdom of St. Thomas to defend our faith, which teaches that God is the first cause of all things, sustains them in their being, but at the same time transcends them.

The destiny of the human being is God

Third, St. Thomas Aquinas was profoundly humanistic. What does this mean? It means that, since God became man in Jesus, the destiny of the human being is union with God, that is, to share in the divine life of God. No other religion or philosophy or sociological theory has upheld so lofty and so worthy a goal for man. That is why, says the Master of Aquino, there is something inviolable about the human person. How important this teaching is when euthanasia is practiced (now, I don’t know if you knew this, but with a 3-D printer they have created a machine made to measure for the person, in which one lies down, presses a button and it kills you… and so one chooses the day and hour of one’s death, free of pain; that is something terrible, very contrary to our faith: life is sacred and God is the owner of life). In how many places, for example, what is called a ‘preventive war’ is practiced, which means that a country is attacked because it is believed that a military conflict, although not imminent, is inevitable and that delaying the attack would be a great risk. It is like killing for the sake of killing, just in case! And so a great many people die, many children are left orphans with no schools to go to, with nothing to eat… Our faith teaches us that life is sacred.

[Peroration] You have the grace and the privilege of coming to this school, of having families who love you and care about you, the grace of being Christians, of getting up each day and making the sign of the cross on your foreheads, the grace of receiving a careful Christian education, of coming to Mass; that is why you must make the most of these years, this time of formation.

The bones of St. Thomas are in that golden casket in Toulouse I told you about, but his mind and his spirit must inform the life of each one of you, so that you may be the voice the Church needs in the world.

The patron of this school, Blessed Pier Giorgio Frassati, was very well aware of the tremendous importance of truth firmly supported by faith; that is why he said: “to live without faith, without a heritage to defend, without the continual struggle for the Truth, is not to live but merely to exist”.

May the Virgin Mary, Seat of Wisdom, grant to all the students of this school, to all their teachers, volunteers, religious and the families of these children the grace to raise with firmness the banner of the Truth of the Incarnate Word, the courage to defend the sacredness of life, and the eloquence of the saint we celebrate today to proclaim with conviction the existence of our Lord and Creator.

We ask for that grace now in the Mass.
