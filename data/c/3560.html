Dear Monks:

On the very happy occasion of the 30th anniversary of the founding of the Contemplative Branch of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, I wish to convey to you, in my own name and in that of the entire Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, our most sincere appreciation and our very best wishes to all the religious of contemplative life scattered throughout the world.

Truly, our whole Religious Family joins gladly in the common hymn of praise and gratitude to the Lord for the gift He has given to our Institute and, in fact, to His whole Church in the Contemplative Branch of the Institute of the Incarnate Word.

The pages of this commemorative magazine for the thirtieth anniversary of contemplative life in our Institute, which I have the pleasure of presenting today, do nothing other than illustrate the importance and the very significant role that you carry out in ecclesial service for the benefit of all the members of the people of God, and the spiritual enrichment you bring to our Institute.

1. An eminent role

Indeed, in the decree Perfectae Caritatis, the Second Vatican Council did not limit itself to affirming that contemplatives still retain today their meaning and a fully valid foundation; it said that the place they occupy in the Mystical Body is “eminent”1.

It is undeniable that the demands of evangelization today are many and urgent. But we would be mistaken if, starting from the observation of even urgent apostolic needs, we were to judge a form of life dedicated exclusively to contemplation as outdated. That is why our Constitutions say: “the best way to carry out an effective apostolate is the closest union with the Incarnate Word”2, which constitutes the principal end of the monk3 and through which you contribute effectively to the apostolic activity not only of the Institute but of the whole Church4.

Looking back over these 30 years, it is consoling to think that precisely in these times, when the needs of evangelization are greater, our monks have had a qualified expansion. In this fact there is an indication from the Spirit that reminds all of us, frequently tempted by the allure of efficiency, of the supremacy of supernatural means over merely human ones. Indeed, how eloquent for all is your witness—the witness of our monks—who, “renouncing everything and aiming directly at the End”5, tell us that “God is all and must be all in all”6.

2. Guardians of the spirit

If the Magisterium recognizes in contemplatives “the glory of the Church and a wellspring of heavenly graces”7, you are no less so for us. For our monks are—as our Proper Law explicitly states—“key pieces of the apostolic endeavor of our Institute”8, of “the greatest importance”9, since they are “magnets of God’s grace and lightning rods of His wrath”10, who occupy a “preferential place in our Institute”11.

From this also follows the most important mission that has been entrusted to you: “to be at the vanguard of our Institute and to be the guardians of its spirit” 12.

You know well that our beloved Religious Family has not been immune to difficulties and that it advances “like a pilgrim amid the persecutions of the world”13 and the contradictions of good people14.

But it seems to me that we too can see applied to ourselves the words of St. John Paul II when he said: “In a not-so-distant historical period, it was almost feared that your orders would be suppressed, but the fidelity of the monks of that time, sustained by divine help and by the protection of Most Holy Mary, enabled them to overcome the trial and not die”15.

Therefore, with great confidence I turn to you, the noble souls of our contemplatives, to entrust to the ardor of your charity this great mission, which I would like you always to keep very much in mind: the safeguarding of the charism of our Institute depends in no small measure on you, on your fidelity, on your joyful acceptance of the demands of silent immolation, on the intensity with which you live our proper spirituality. In a word: much depends on your configuration to Christ the Victim16.

Be tenaciously faithful to the patrimony of the Institute, to the evangelical intention of our Founder, and by your example “spur many to welcome the charism of the vocation into their hearts”17.

And thus, “bearing the weaknesses of many, without faltering before flattery or threats, and remaining above the ups and downs of fortune or failure, keep your soul always ready to accept death, if necessary, for the good of the Institute in the service of Jesus Christ”18.

May your fidelity—of which this anniversary speaks to us so persuasively—be today and always the consoling bulwark of protection for our Institute.

And may your prayers and sacrifices be the fortification that silently rises around us to keep us solidly united, founded on the Rock that is Christ19.

May the “communal witness of poverty and fraternal charity itself”20 of our monasteries make them beacons of joy that enlighten all the members.

May tender devotion to the Mother of Christ, which silently interweaves prayers and sacrifices offered as caresses to this Blessed Mother, be the mantle that wraps us when the storm rages and that preserves in all of us “spiritual fervor, the joy of evangelizing, even when we must sow in tears”21.

Persevere always in your mission of being vigilant sentinels of the Absolute, in constant prayer with God, so as to present to Him “the joys and the hopes, the griefs and the anxieties”22 of our beloved Institute, and thus open the floodgates through which may pour forth that torrent of graces we so greatly need “to to prolong the Incarnation in all things”23.

In other words: be always faithful to your vocation; zealously preserve the gift that has been given to you and make it bear fruit. We expect much of you!

Finally:

To you who, by your particular consecration, carry forward the whole work of the Institute24, who extend our apostolic works with a mysterious fruitfulness25, and who by your hidden immolation enrich the whole Church with most abundant fruits of holiness26, I want to say in the name of our whole Religious Family: Thank you so very much! And “thanks be to God, who endowed you with so faithful a heart… Let these works shine before men; let them see them, celebrate them, and glorify the Father who is in heaven”27.

Invoking upon all of you the maternal protection of Mary, I wish you, in the name of the whole Institute, a great abundance of blessings and great fruitfulness in vocations.

A very happy anniversary!

In the Incarnate Word and His Most Holy Mother,

Rome, December 2018
