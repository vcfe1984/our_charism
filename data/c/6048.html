That all men may discover
the attraction and the longing for divine beauty

Cf. Constitutions, 254, 257

One of our fundamental commitments, which can at times go unnoticed or not be understood in all its fullness and potential, is the one contained precisely in our formula of profession, in which we say that we make an oblation to God of our entire self so that “all men may discover the attraction and the longing for divine beauty”1. A succinct phrase in which is implicit the challenging and, at the same time, thrilling task of “knowing how to call, teach, direct, accompany and select priestly, diaconal, religious, missionary and secular vocations”2.

As we are only a few days away from celebrating the luminous mystery of the Transfiguration of the Lord, this solemnity seems to remind us once again that the icon of the transfigured face of Christ continues even today to exercise a powerful attraction over countless souls, and that it is our unavoidable task to rekindle with great strength, above all in young people, that deep longing for God, thus creating the proper setting for vocations to spring up as a generous response.

1. Urgency

It is nothing new that “the vocational problem constitutes the fundamental urgency of the Church”3 and, therefore, it is so for our Institute as well, because the subject of vocations “affects the whole Church in one of her fundamental marks, which is her apostolicity”4. For vocations are “a proof of her spiritual vitality and the very condition of that vitality. They are the condition of her mission and of her development”5. Truly, “the problem of vocations is an authentic challenge that directly questions Institutes, but which concerns the whole Church”6. In this sense, we understand that what is said in terms of vocations about the vitality and development of the Church refers not only to the universal Church, but also to each of the particular Churches and, analogously, also to our Institute.

Since “vocational ministry” –as the Magisterium of the Church defines it very well– “is the mission of the Church ‘intended to care for the birth, discernment and accompaniment of vocations, especially vocations to the priesthood’7”8; in that definition we recognize the “urgent” call “to strive, through explicit proclamation and suitable catechesis, to foster in those called to consecrated life the free, but prompt and generous, response that makes the grace of vocation operative”9.

This urgency becomes even more pressing, above all if one hears that “the annual average of persons who leave religious life is more than 3,000 people, about 10 a day; which represents 0.3% of all consecrated life”10.

Aware that vocations are not the fruit of spontaneous generation nor of an activism that relies only on human means, vocational ministry has been central to the mission of the Institute. Simply because we understand that, although each vocation is a gift of God, like all His gifts, they come through human mediations.

By the grace of God, we can rejoice that the Institute has many vocations in formation (about 500) coming from some 50 different countries, which not only enrich the life of our Religious Family in the various jurisdictions and particular Churches, but are a sign of the vitality of religious life in our Institute, as well as the result of the faithful and devoted ministry of its members.

However, this does not mean that the commitment to raising up and accompanying vocations to the priesthood and to consecrated life has come to an end. Our Proper Law points this out to us explicitly: “we would like God to give us the gift of being able to discover and guide so many vocations that we could fill all the good seminaries and novitiates of the whole world”11.

Today as yesterday “the call of Christ: come and follow me”12 remains current and, therefore, our requirement remains current of a “work with vocations that is active, constant, full of drive and vitality, committed and urged on by the charity of Christ13, and necessarily opposed to a mentality of ‘ordinary administration or bureaucratic slowness’14, which negligently waits for vocations to knock at the door. For God, by grace, sows the seeds of vocation with full hands15”16.

In a world that so frequently seeks fulfillment in material comforts and in the manipulation of power, in a world that strives to seek happiness without a clear reference to God, we members of the Institute of the Incarnate Word must be like signposts pointing to higher values, showing our contemporaries that we are ambassadors of Him who proclaimed the true ‘newness’ of life they are seeking when, on the summit of the Mount, He raised the standard of the Beatitudes. That is why the Spiritual Father of our Religious Family insisted with great emphasis that priests and religious –monks, brothers, seminarians– be concerned with raising up priestly vocations and forming them17, and that they dedicate themselves to the marvelous work of vocational ministry “with passion and discretion”18.

This, although it is the task of all priests –religious or not, contemplative or not– and of all consecrated persons in general, is imperative and “indispensable”19 for us, since the ministry of vocations “is intrinsic to the end of the evangelization of culture and to the charism of the Institute”20. Therefore, no member of the Institute can remain indifferent or claim that it is not his concern to pray for this intention and to raise up vocations of special consecration. Indeed, whoever does not do something, however small, for vocational ministry will never be able to enjoy that “most abundant apostolic and vocational fruitfulness”21 to which we aspire, and his contribution to the cause of evangelization would be gravely restricted, however much apostolic zeal he may have in other areas.

Here we highlight the preponderant role that the contemplatives of the Institute have in this regard, who have as their very special mission to pray for “the increase in quantity and quality of priestly and religious vocations”22, and on whose generous and joyful fidelity depends in large part the abundance and quality of vocations for the Institute and the whole Church.

2. A leap in quality

Now, if from the members of the Institute is expected “a ministry of proposal, an incisive ministry”23 in all areas of the apostolate, with how much greater reason do these qualifiers apply to vocational ministry which, as is well known, is a pastoral priority24 and an “obligatory dimension”25 of every pastoral plan. This is the concern not only of those ‘in charge’ in the various jurisdictions, but it must be considered one of the primary objectives of the Institute, and all the members must feel involved in it. For those ‘in charge’ will fulfill their mission all the more effectively the more all the members –according to their abilities and offices– help to make their own the commitment to increase the priests and consecrated persons that the Church needs today.

The decline in vocations, or the abandonment by many consecrated persons that we mentioned earlier, led St. John Paul II long ago to ask himself: “What is the cause?”26. Among the various reasons he enumerates, we list the following:

  • “It is likely that the cultural changes of our time provide a certain explanation, but it is also necessary to think of the process of secularization that affects religious life. And there is also a form of presence in the world that leads slowly but inexorably to the suppression of every specific and perceptible aspect of religious life. This specific character consists in being a ‘sign of contradiction,’ not against man but against what is inhuman in contemporary society; not against the modern human universe, but in order to save it. […] At times concern for a poorly understood human development or for community well-being, and the existence of a certain bourgeois comfort, have weakened the search for the one thing necessary which must constitute the witness of religious life”27.
  • “The vocational crisis must not be attributed principally to a lack of generosity on the part of young people, but rather to the fact that a prophetic sign of the presence of God, which is precisely the first dimension of religious life, is not sufficiently perceived in religious life”28.
  • “The proposals that tend to ‘laicize’ the ministry and priestly life, to replace ‘sacramental’ ministers with other ‘ministries,’ judging that they respond better to today’s pastoral demands, and also to deprive the religious vocation of the character of prophetic witness to the kingdom, orienting it exclusively toward functions of social animation or even of directly political commitment”29.
  • If religious/priests “do not bear witness to the Transcendent […] if they are not a sign of what lies beyond this world and its transience, and of what is much higher –divine and eschatological realities–, they will not be able to exercise an authentic attraction to religious life on young people”30.
  • The spread of “erroneous opinions about the value of consecrated life, which produce perplexity and disorientation especially among young people”31.
  • The fact that priests/religious make “rereadings of the Gospel according to the times, conforming themselves to everything the world asks for”32.

For these reasons, and given the historical and cultural circumstances of our world and the particular circumstances the Church is going through, we feel compelled to make a leap in quality in the vocational ministry of the Institute. For without enough laborers for the harvest, it will not be possible for us to make a reality of Christ’s command: Go into all the world and preach the Gospel to every creature33 (which is the very reason for our existence and mission in the Church), nor to renew each day the Eucharistic sacrifice: Do this in memory of me34.

Therefore, the vocational ministry we are attempting is not exhausted in occasional or extraordinary initiatives, but aims to be a constant concern of all the members in all our apostolates. For notwithstanding the present difficulties, it is palpable that throughout the world there are young people more than eager and open to live an authentic Christian radicalism, to give themselves and dedicate themselves to selfless service of the poor, the sick, children, etc., and we must know how to see in the demands and desires of these young people a summons and even an opportunity for the mission of our Institute.

How do we bring it about that “all men may discover the attraction and the longing for divine beauty”35?

That is what we will try to answer in the points that follow.

3. Attraction through fidelity

The young people of our time have great need of the spiritual visibility and transparency of religious communities. Therefore, to the extent that we preserve the physiognomy of our Institute, and that each one of us is like an incarnation of its charism and spirit in the realities we are called to live, young people will feel especially attracted.

This is what experience has shown us: very often, the example of a religious man or woman acts in a decisive way in the vocational orientation of a young person, who discovers in that religious’s fidelity, in the joy that springs from “preferring nothing to Christ”36, the concrete realization of an ideal of life. We have heard it so many times: example draws others along.

In this sense our Proper Law points out that “religious institutes must maintain a firm and clear sense of their own identity and mission”37. The more fervent the members of the Institute are in practicing with conviction the non-negotiables attached to the charism, in living faithfully the religious vows according to the spirit of the Institute, and in carrying out all our proper apostolates with zeal and in union with the Incarnate Word, in communion with the pastors and with a full ecclesial sense, the more inevitable will be –barring divine permission– a flourishing of vocations for the Institute and the whole Church.

In other words, we consider that the example is decisive of “religious and priests who serenely live their vocation day by day, faithful to the commitments they have made, humble and hidden builders of the Kingdom of God, from whose words, behavior and life radiates the luminous joy of the choice they made… it is precisely they… who by their example will spur many to welcome into their hearts the charism of vocation”38. By preaching with good example young people are led to embrace a life of total consecration and service, because it is only possible to beget new vocations spiritually when one lives “firm and faithful to the call, to the vocation, above any other claim of this earth”39. And we are convinced that the consistent witness of Christ represents in itself a vocational proposal.

Consequently, as our Proper Law expresses very well: “every counter-witness, every inconsistency between how values or ideals are expressed and how they are actually lived, every seeking of oneself and not of the Kingdom of God and His justice40, every falsification of the Word of God41, “are frequently strong obstacles for those who feel the call of Christ: come and follow me…”42.

4. Attraction through the radicalism of the Gospel

“Today young people need to see living examples of those who, leaving everything, have embraced as their ideal the life according to the evangelical counsels”43, said St. John Paul II. That is, they need to see priests and religious of the Incarnate Word who accept with joy, for the love of God, privations even in necessary things44; who are ready to live “the radicalism of the self-emptying of Christ and of His condition as a servant”45; who, by dying to the world, are “ready for martyrdom out of loyalty to God, which constitutes the full and total rejection of the evil world”46; who are ready to leave their family to become part of the Family of the Incarnate Word; in short, who live in “a process of continual conversion”47, “resolved to attain holiness”48.

Let it be well understood that it is real sincerity in the radical following of the Incarnate Word that will continue to attract vocations to our Institute, since young people are seeking precisely that evangelical radicality. To the extent that young people get to know us more closely and see us as generous followers of Christ and radical in the choice we have made –given that our way of life offers no material rewards nor accommodates itself to the models of the world– they will be strongly attracted to Christ’s firm and thrilling challenge to leave everything to follow Him.

Far be it from us to make adaptations in our way of life or spirituality that are born of laxity or lead to it. That would not only mean the loss of the spirit of the Institute, but it would be a bleeding wound for our mission in the Church and a regrettably effective way of driving away new vocations.

5. Attraction through joy

Our Proper Law already says it: the “witness of joy arouses an enormous attraction toward religious life, it is a source of new vocations and a support for perseverance”49. For it is through a collective witness of authentic consecration, lived in the joy of personal self-giving to Christ and to others, that young people feel attracted.

Note that priests, deacons, contemplatives, brothers, novices, seminarians, all of us, absolutely all, have a special responsibility in awakening and sustaining vocations. If we give a joyful witness of serving Christ; if we spread hope through the faith that animates us, even in spite of the weariness of the apostolate and of the struggle; if we are truly given over to souls and are capable of teaching others to pray, how can we doubt that vocations will arise around us? Has that not been the experience of many of us?

If we are “sad and discouraged, impatient or anxious”50 religious, bad-tempered or irascible, we will do little or nothing to encourage young people to embrace the call to special consecration. We must be aware that our witness as religious “encourages or discourages, arouses the desire for God or constitutes an obstacle to following Him”51. Seeing us, young people must perceive that “God does not call only to a life of sacrifice and renunciation, but also to a life of intimate joy and fullness”52. We must be persuaded that young people are in search of an Absolute that a good number of adults and even some religious are incapable of revealing to them. That is why our mission toward them is indispensable; they hope that we will prepare them for a life worth living and that we will put them in contact with the Transfigured Christ and His Gospel that does not pass away. Therefore, it is necessary to make every possible effort so that young people come to place Christ at the center of their search and to follow docilely His eventual call.

6. Attraction through fraternal charity

Community life is an inseparable element of religious life in our Institute and is for us a great richness thanks to the benefit of the complementarity of gifts.

The simple austerity of our lives united to ardent fraternal charity anchored in the interior richness of obedience is one of the conditions for vocations to flourish everywhere in a world dominated by consumerism and selfishness53.

That is why there is asked of each member of the Institute “a new and determined evangelical witness of self-denial and sobriety, a style of fraternal life inspired by criteria of simplicity and hospitality”54. But also and above all, the practice of “fraternal charity, as in a true family”55, since without any doubt young people feel attracted by the discovery of communities united in charity.

We want to highlight here the great value that the practice of hospitality has as a means of raising up vocations. Let us remember, then, that “in our houses, provided it does not gravely affect the internal order of the house and the area of cloister of the religious is respected”56, the practice of this work of mercy is very desirable. For “not infrequently have we observed that this work of mercy has led many souls to friendship with God through conversion or to discover their vocation of consecration to the Lord”57.

St. John Paul II already said it, and it seems to us worth emphasizing: “Do you want a key to apostolic fruitfulness? Live unity, the source of great apostolic strength58. In fraternal communion lies, in fact, the guarantee of the presence of Christ and of His Spirit, for putting your responsibilities into practice, following the rules of your institutes. The Church needs the example and witness of religious who live evangelical fraternity. […] The young people who knock at your doors wish to find an ecclesial life characterized by fervor in prayer, by a family spirit, by apostolic commitment. […] Be capable of welcoming them and guiding them, carefully cultivating new vocations, the search for which must be one of your principal concerns”59.

Therefore, the more community spirit there is among the members of the Institute, the more effective our ministry will be. Where there are living communities, where the members live together fraternally, that is where there are more vocations.

7. Attraction through service to the poor

Our task, as has been well expressed in our Constitutions, “is to love and serve, and to make Jesus Christ loved and served… especially [in] the poor”60. That is why we are commanded “to give priority to the care of the poor, the sick and the needy of every kind… concretely practicing charity”61.

Let us open the doors of our missions to young people so that they can collaborate in our activities: parish catechesis, care of the poor, oratories, popular missions, volunteer work, home visits, etc. Let us give them the opportunity to give themselves and dedicate themselves to the selfless service of the poor, of the little ones, of the needy and the sick62.

How enriching is the experience of the many young people who have been able to collaborate as volunteers in the various homes the Institute runs, in the popular missions accompanying the religious or helping in whatever way, or assisting in simple parish tasks. In fact, several of our own discovered their vocation precisely after such an experience.

Therefore, it is desirable that young people be increasingly involved in our apostolates and that they perceive up close the religious’s forgetfulness of self for the spiritual good of his neighbor. For witness remains the most convincing force of attraction at our disposal. The idea is that, seeing our good works, young people may follow in our footsteps for the glory of our Father who is in heaven63.

8. Attraction through solid formation

Young people today, as yesterday, seek the certainty of the truth that does not pass away. They want to give their life to the Lord, not to have it squandered. That is why our Proper Law points out that “without good formation God does not bless with an abundance of vocations”64. This implies that “intense efforts must be made to foster vocations and to procure the best priestly formation possible in the seminaries”65.

At present the Institute has about 250 major seminarians under the care of some 35 formators, not counting the itinerant professors who often visit the seminaries, procuring the best possible formation, according to the norms of the Church in force, as the mission entrusted to us in the Church requires.

Likewise, some 215 priests already hold an academic degree obtained at pontifical universities and there are about 30 more studying to obtain one (that is, more than half of the priest members have some academic degree-level preparation), which in a certain way implies an investment by the Institute in vocational ministry. For we are convinced that vocational ministry also presupposes and needs a careful and concrete follow-up of vocations. And this requires that each of the priests especially prepare himself spiritually, theologically and pedagogically, in an ongoing way, for this important ecclesial mission66.

9. Attraction through prayer

“The ‘center of all vocational ministry’ is prayer”67, our Proper Law points out. Jesus made it clearly understood in the Gospel. When calling the disciples to send them throughout the world, He urges them above all to look on high: Pray, therefore, the Lord of the harvest to send laborers into his harvest68. “The vocational pedagogy –affirmed St. John Paul the Great– that the Lord uses shows that a ministry unbalanced toward action and promotional initiatives runs the risk of proving ineffective and without prospects, because every vocation is, above all, a gift of God”69. “Seeing a priest make his meditation before Mass does more for an altar boy’s vocation than thousands of vocational propaganda pamphlets”70, wrote Fulton Sheen.

That is why today more than ever we must be promoters and drivers of a great movement of prayer, counteracting the wind of secularism that pushes us to give priority to human means, to efficiency-mindedness and to a pragmatic approach to life.

What a blessing it would be if the faithful of our parishes and “the key pieces of the apostolic endeavor of our Institute”71 –that is: the monastic communities, the sick, the religious brothers– as well as the families of the Third Order and those who suffer, were to raise to God every day a fervent prayer for vocations. Let us take the time to exhort others to pray; let us create spaces of prayer for vocations, especially before the Most Blessed Sacrament of the Altar or with a Rosary in hand.

10. To the young people of the Voices of the Word

In the first place, we want to thank you for your generous commitment to spreading the Gospel in the different spheres of daily life. But, in addition, today we want to make you our confidants and tell you that a symphony of many voices is needed to express the one Word72 across the vast horizon of this world. For this it is first necessary to remove from the ears of the soul the noises that often prevent one from catching the divine voice73, in order to give Jesus room in the heart and let oneself be seduced by the Eternal One, as the prophet Jeremiah said: You have seduced me, Lord…; You were stronger than I, and You prevailed74.

“Let yourselves be fascinated by the Incarnate Word, the Infinite One who appeared among us in a visible and imitable form. Let yourselves be drawn by His example, which has changed the history of the world and directed it toward an exultant goal. Let yourselves be loved by the charity of the Holy Spirit, who wants to turn your eyes away from earthly models, in order to begin in you the life of the new man, created according to God in justice and true holiness75.

Fall in love with Jesus Christ, so as to live His very life, so that our world may have life in the light of the Gospel”76.

Today “there is a lack of workers for the Gospel on the outskirts of the great metropolises, in rural areas, among the inhabitants of the heights of the Andes, [in the Russian steppes, in the African deserts, in remote islands] and in the immensity of the jungles. There is a lack of servants of the good news who will dedicate themselves to young people, to families, to the elderly and the sick, to workers, to intellectuals, to the builders of a society, as well as to the poorest and most marginalized. The presence of a greater number of priests and religious is urgently needed in parishes, in apostolic movements, in basic ecclesial communities, in schools and universities, and in so many other fields […]. On the other hand, looking at the wide horizons of the universal mission entrusted to the Church, there is also a lack of men and women missionaries who will go beyond the borders, to proclaim to the world the unsearchable riches of Christ”77.

In a word: the Church needs today more than ever “radical and total self-giving, new and courageous impulses”78.

Take heart “to discern the will of God through familiarity with the Word of God, read and studied in the Church, under the guidance of her legitimate Pastors”79. Do your best to make the Spiritual Exercises every year, which is one of the most qualified means for ordering one’s life according to God and, accordingly, in many cases, for discerning the divine plan for one’s life. Seek spiritual direction frequently. Take part whenever you can in the popular missions or in the volunteer opportunities the Institute offers. It is an enriching experience from every point of view. And at all times, be men and women of prayer.

Finally, let this be an explicit invitation addressed not to the Voices of the Word as a group, but to each one of you, whatever country you may be in and whatever the circumstances of your life at the present moment. It is the invitation to put out into the deep80, taking seriously, thoroughly, the demands of the Gospel: go, sell all that you have…81 so as to be ambassadors for Christ82, reproducing Him, becoming like Him, being configured to Him according to the charism of the Institute.

“Consider the immensity of this world,” Blessed Paolo Manna used to tell young people and newly ordained priests, “do not be one of those who see nothing beyond the narrow limits of their own country. There is a great part of the world about which no one thinks because it lies asleep in error and sin. […] Go, then, you who have wept when you contemplated the Blood of Christ poured out in abundance and apparently with little result… Go to the missions and apply the merits of this Most Precious Blood to the countless souls who have no one to make them sharers in such blessings. Save them and console the Heart of Jesus, which desires nothing more than that.

Go with a pure heart burning with charity, you whose love –you say– is without limits, go to the mission. […] Go to the mission, chosen soul who are nourished by the graces of the Eucharist, soul who desire nothing more than to spend yourself for the love of God. Go to the mission. […] Enlist to go to the mission, you who like to work. […] You whose sins have been forgiven, consecrate yourself for the rest of your life to the work of the apostolate among the pagans, thereby making reparation for a youth spent, perhaps, far from God. […]

If God really calls you, do not be afraid, go to the mission, keeping in mind the promise that the workers of the last hour will receive the same pay as those who worked from the first. […] Let those who until now have resisted the voice of the Lord now say: ‘Yes, I will be a missionary. / I will not resist God’s call a moment longer. I will not hinder God’s great plan to save all men. I will not deprive the Church of my humble services. I will not postpone the promptings of my conscience to generosity; I will be a missionary. / I wish to contribute with all my strength and with my very life, if it were necessary, to extending the kingdom of Jesus. I wish to immolate myself to promote His glory and to procure salvation for so many needy souls who perhaps await the Savior in me.’

What joy there will be in heaven if any of you makes such a decision!”83.

Let all the members of the Institute remember that “in these times of few vocations, those who do not have them often consider it a sin to have many vocations, and mercilessly attack those who have them. That is why one must know how to be holily determined not to tolerate anything that could impede them. For this one must be ready even for martyrdom, if it were necessary, knowing how to maintain an unshakable firmness in order to be faithful to God, who is the Author of every vocation and the one principally interested in their flourishing. In other words, one must not place obstacles to the work of God”84.

We must be convinced that the exemplary character of our religious life by virtue of our proper charism, the spiritual atmosphere of our communities, the austerity and manner of life, the solid and sure formation that prepares candidates to ‘bite into reality,’ and fervor in the works of the apostolate will be a cause of edification for the whole Church and will attract to our beloved Institute ever more numerous vocations of generous young people, who aspire not to mediocrity in the following of Christ, but to radicalism in their consecration to Him.

We entrust to Our Lady of Luján, Queen of the Institute and Mother of vocations, all the young people of the world, as well as the pastoral work of all our members on behalf of vocations of special consecration.

May the sweetness of this Kind Mother draw young people to her Immaculate Heart so that they may understand with her the beauty and joy that await them if the Incarnate Word calls them to His intimacy, to make them witnesses of His Love and to render them capable of gladdening the Church with their consecration.
