The humanity of Christ is our happiness

Recalling the thought of our Founder

During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the fourth day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in Jesus Christ, true man, we call to mind Fr. Buela’s thought on the subject, taken from one of his sermons entitled:

The humanity of Christ is our happiness

The humanity of Christ is our happiness». It is a phrase of St. Thomas Aquinas found at the beginning of the part of the Summa Theologiae in which he speaks of Jesus.

[…] St. Anthony of Padua [for his part] begins a homily by saying: «Christmas: behold paradise». When, two thousand years ago, Mary gave birth in Bethlehem: behold paradise. Happiness that ceases to be a promise, an expectation, a hope, that is no longer glimpsed from afar. Happiness made flesh was present. It was visible. When He came forth from His mother’s womb, visibly happiness, that is, paradise, the supreme delight (as Dante says: «and thus may the supreme delight unfold to him»), the supreme delight had Himself come out to meet man: behold paradise.

[…] The Incarnation

Two thousand years ago, then, happiness came: behold paradise. Happiness came: it ceased to be a promise, it ceased to be pointed to as the end of the human journey. Happiness came, paradise came. It came in the flesh so that it might be seen, so that it might be touched, so that it might be embraced. So that St. Augustine can say: «I knew that happiness was God, but I did not enjoy You [because one does not enjoy knowing; one enjoys when one is embraced], but I did not enjoy You until, humble, I embraced my humble God Jesus». This is the experience of happiness on earth: humbly to embrace my humble God Jesus. Not God as a distant destination, but God made a child, a tiny little child: thus paradise, happiness, came to meet us; thus happiness drew near, thus it came within reach of our gaze, within reach of our heart, within reach of our hands, of the hands that can embrace it. Paradise on earth is He: […] God is faithful, by whom you have been called into the fellowship of His Son Jesus Christ, our Lord (1 Cor 1:9). The fellowship is with His Son Jesus Christ, our Lord […]. Jesus Christ is the happiness of man. It is that man, in His singularity, I would say in His individuality: that man. The fellowship of His Son Jesus Christ, our Lord.

[…] I wish to read a passage which, in my opinion, is one of the most beautiful and most comprehensive of Giussani1, in which he says what this humble relationship with the humble Jesus is, this humble embrace with the humble Jesus, this humble embrace with happiness here on earth, this fellowship of the Son of God, this possibility of familiarity with His Son Jesus Christ. Giussani says: «Your relationship with Christ does not have to be already developed, experienced, mature, for your personality to be born from it and for you, starting from that, to know how to create a companionship [to know how to love. When we are loved gratuitously we can freely, that is, gratuitously, love]. It is enough -how shall I put it- the surprise that John and Andrew had [who were the first two who, at the beginning of His public life, met Him], who understood nothing [who understood nothing and yet had understood everything, for Andrew meets his brother Peter and says to him: We have found the Messiah (Jn 1:41). What they were waiting for, they had found, and therefore they had found everything, because what the heart waits for is everything; consequently they had understood everything. It is enough, the surprise that John and Andrew had, who understood nothing]; the surprise is enough, an initial devotion is enough, wonder is enough. More precisely: it is enough to ask for it.» […] Giussani goes on to say: «More precisely, it is enough to ask for it [because wonder is what makes you ask for it], that embryonic perception of what He is is enough, which makes you beg for it, because of which you ask for it». To begin the experience of happiness on earth, to embrace happiness on earth, to embrace, humble, my humble Jesus, that embryonic perception because of which you ask for it is enough, that embryonic wonder, that embryonic sweetness because of which you beg for it. This is enough to begin to embrace happiness on earth.

[…] I end by reading a passage of St. Augustine [says Fr. Buela] on the beauty of Jesus: «For us believers, the Word of God appears beautiful everywhere / pulcher Deus, Verbum apud Deum, / beautiful as the Word with God, / pulcher in utero virginis, / beautiful in the womb of the Virgin, where He did not lose His divinity and took on humanity; beautiful born a child, because even while He was very small, nursing, being carried in arms, the heavens spoke, the angels paid Him tribute of praise, the star guided the Magi, He was adored in the manger, and at all times He was the food of the meek. So He is beautiful in heaven and beautiful on earth; beautiful in the womb, beautiful in the arms of His parents [of Mary and Joseph], beautiful in His miracles, beautiful in the scourging. [Yes, also in the scourging, because -Augustine says- in the scourging, when He was all disfigured, let us think why He was like that, why He had let Himself be beaten like that by the scourges; let us think of the mercy that made Him come to this for you, for love of you; He was beautiful even in the scourging]. When Mary took Him dead in her arms beneath the cross («vidit suum dulcem natum moriendo desolatum / she saw her sweet newborn, her sweet Son, die alone, alone on the cross»), when she took Him in her arms, there was nothing more beautiful than her Son, that disfigured Son. When the good thief said to Him: Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom (Lk 23:42), he had never in his whole life found anything more beautiful than that moment, at the moment of death, when Jesus answered him: Today you will be with me in paradise (Lk 23:43)]. Beautiful in His miracles, beautiful in the scourging, beautiful inviting us to follow Him, beautiful not concerning Himself with death, beautiful giving His life, beautiful rising again / pulcher in ligno, pulcher in sepulcro, pulcher in coelo / beautiful on the cross, beautiful in the tomb and beautiful in heaven».

[To Mary, Mother of the Incarnate Word, we ask the grace humbly to embrace this God who counted it as nothing to empty Himself so that we might begin to embrace happiness already here on earth.]
