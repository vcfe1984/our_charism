Memorial of Blessed Concepción Cabrera de Armida

[Exordium] Today we celebrate the memorial of a great woman, the memorial of Blessed Concepción Cabrera de Armida. She was born in San Luis Potosí, Mexico, in 1862 and died in Mexico City (DF) in 1937. She was a daughter, fiancée, wife, mother, widow, grandmother, foundress, mystic, writer, tireless apostle of Jesus Christ, and, by a special indult of St. Pius X, without ever leaving her family setting, she even died canonically a religious, in the arms of her children. She was declared Venerable by St. John Paul II on December 20, 1999, in Rome, and was beatified on May 4, 2019 (just 6 years ago) in the Basilica of Guadalupe.

Wife and mother

From her childhood she felt drawn to Jesus; later to Jesus Crucified, but in an original, unprecedented way: by the interior sufferings of Christ, Priest and Host, Priest and Victim in His least actions, from His “Ecce venio” to His “Consumatum est” upon the Cross. And so, little by little, this woman, simple in her outward appearance, married, and the mother of nine children, became identified with Christ. All who knew her declare that she was the most normal woman in the world. One of her daughters stated: “She always seemed to me the most normal woman in the world. She had to admonish me many times. We never saw her in ecstasy.” And the youngest of her children said: “She was very affectionate, but she reprimanded firmly when one misbehaved.” Yet she lived her daily life transfigured. And although nothing in her outward appearance betrayed the wealth of her interior life, reading her diary or her writings reveals to us a mystic of the highest caliber. It is beautiful to read, for example, the resolutions she made in her spiritual exercises—very concrete, very practical. Let me read you a few: “With my husband: I will take care not to lose his trust, but rather to win it more and more; informing myself about his business affairs, I will ask God for light to counsel him rightly. I will strive that he always find in me holy consolation, sweetness and complete self-denial.” “With my children: I will exercise special care and vigilance. I will not weary them by loading them with prayers and making piety burdensome to them; quite the contrary, I will try to make it pleasant in their eyes, so that they seek it naturally, beginning to give wings to the soul with little ejaculatory prayers.” That is why her message, the example of her life, is for everyone: for lay people, for religious, for priests themselves.

Just think: at 10 she received her First Communion; at 22 she married Pancho, as she affectionately called her husband, Mr. Francisco Armida; at 35 she already experienced spiritual marriage with Christ; at 39 she was widowed; at 44 she received the extraordinary grace of the mystical incarnation.

This young married woman, overflowing with love for her husband and her children, had an irresistible desire to love Christ, Master of her heart and supreme rule of all her loves. But for Conchita her desire to imitate Christ was not a utopia, a dream up in the air, but something very concrete in her daily life: “I must reproduce Jesus in myself by means of the transforming virtues, that is, by means of the Cross, which is what most likens us to Him. What Jesus wants of me is not a Christ in the poverty of Bethlehem… Not a Christ in the hiddenness of Nazareth, not a Christ in the course of His public life, but a Christ in the ignominies, abandonments and crucifixions of Calvary and of the Eucharist. I must therefore reproduce in myself Christ Crucified”1. She has a phrase that our founder always liked to repeat, especially when someone was going through times of particular difficulty: “The cross makes fruitful everything it touches!” And he would say it as a prelude of good omen, as if something good were about to happen, as if much fruit were going to be gained…

The mystical incarnation

Well then, one March 24, Jesus said to Conchita: “I want you to be mine…, to belong to Me entirely; I want all your affections to be for Me; I want that place of my rest to be clean of all dust: your heart,” and He granted her the extraordinary grace of the mystical incarnation. Although it is an infrequent grace, it is a grace of transformation in Christ received in seed at baptism and to which all of us are called, as St. John of the Cross, for example, repeats in his works: that God has called us to be deiform, to be “God by participation,” etc., for which it is necessary to pass through the nights, through the purifications of the cross. Her soul being now purified, our Lord said to Conchita: “The Word became incarnate, and becomes mystically incarnate in souls, only in order to be sacrificed. That is the end of all mystical incarnations… Your Word has just become mystically incarnate in your heart… in order to be sacrificed constantly on an altar, not of stone, but in a living temple of the Holy Spirit[…]. The Father wills that I, united with your victim soul, should bring you to sacrifice and immolate Me with that same love of His on behalf of a world that needs a shock and a grace of this kind in order to come to its senses, embrace the Cross and be saved”2.

This extraordinary grace of the mystical incarnation made her aware of her priestly character in a singular way, and Jesus Himself taught her: “You are altar and priest at the same time, for you have with you the sacrosanct Victim of Calvary and of the Eucharist, whom you can offer constantly to the Eternal Father for the salvation of the world. This is the most precious fruit of the great favor I have worked in you by becoming incarnate in your heart… You are my altar and you will also be my victim: in union with Me, offer yourself and offer Me at every moment to the Eternal Father for the most noble end of saving souls and giving Him glory. Forget everything, even yourself, and let this be your constant occupation. You have a sublime mission: the mission of the priest; look upon my goodness and be grateful for it, for without your knowing it, it has given you what you so longed for, and even more: the power to be a priest, not holding Me in your hands, but in your heart, and without ever separating Me from it. But fulfill the magnificent end of this grace, which, as you see, is not only for you but universal, obliging you to be, with all the purity that can exist, at once altar and victim, which consumes in whatever holocaust may please Him the other Victim, the Only One who can save the world”3.

Blessed Conchita received this extraordinary grace, but all of us too—religious, lay people and priests—are called to offer on the altar of our lives, of our hearts, a sacrifice pleasing to God. In this sense the Second Vatican Council teaches: «for all their works, prayers and apostolic undertakings, their married and family life, their daily work, their rest of soul and body, if they are carried out in the Spirit, and even the hardships of life if they are patiently borne, become ‘spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ’ (1 Pet 2:5)”4.

Conchita, very humbly, asked Christ to teach her how to make use of that grace, to which Jesus replied: “The principal end of that grace is transformation, uniting your desires to mine, your will to mine, your immolation to mine. You must, all pure and sacrificed in your body and in your soul, offer yourself and offer Me to the heavenly Father at every moment, with every breath if possible, on behalf first of all of my priests and of my Church, …of the whole world, of the good and of the wicked. You must be transformed into charity, that is, into Me, who am all charity, putting to death the old man and having with Me a single heart and mind. […] Reproduce my life in you, but with the tint of sacrifice, being a living holocaust for my glory. Alone you are worth nothing, but in union with Me you will fulfill your mission on earth, saving souls in the secret holocaust that God alone sees”5. And He adds: “I want you to be my host and to renew your intention many times, night and day, of offering yourself in union with Me on all the patens of the earth: so that, transformed into Me by suffering, by love and by the virtues, this cry of your soul may rise to heaven in union with Me: ‘This is my Body, this is my Blood’”6.

[Peroratio] And that, in the end, is our program. The marvelous and most noble office to which God has called us who are religious, and those who, like Blessed Concepción Cabrera, are lay people in His Church: to make of our lives and our deaths a holocaust pleasing to God. To fuse our life into that of the Incarnate Word, or put another way, to empty ourselves completely of ourselves so that the Word Himself may pour Himself into us and transform us into Him.

And that is the grace that today, in this Holy Mass, we ask of our Lord through the intercession of Blessed Concepcion Cabrera de Armida.
