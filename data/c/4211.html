Mk 13:33-37

[Exordium] My dear all: it is with great joy in my soul that I celebrate this holy Mass with you on this precious first Sunday of Advent and in the setting of this magnificent occasion: the inauguration of the first monastery in the thriving Province of Nossa Senhora da Aparecida.

Here in this place the petition that the prophet Isaiah made in the first reading seems to be fulfilled today: Oh, that you would rend the heavens and come down, making the mountains quake at your presence1.

For what is a monastery if not a “sign of the presence of God in the world”2? That is why special attention is deserved by the fact that today, here, through the work of the loving and most gentle Divine Fatherhood, God officially and publicly establishes a new house where the Incarnate Word himself is to be adored and served.

What a great grace for our Religious Family! For this Province in particular and, truly, for the whole Church!

For as an author said: “a Monastery is the power of God grafted onto the earth, like the Body of Jesus in the bread of the Eucharist and the Word in the womb of Mary. […] From there the power of the Spirit runs through the whole Body of the Church as in a second Incarnation”3. Analogously we can say that this monastery planted in these beautiful lands of Nossa Senhora da Aparecida comes to be like the Word, who in his immeasurable divine wisdom willed to enclose himself in the womb of Mary4 in order to begin his redemptive mission from there.

In the Gospel we have just read we heard our Lord compare himself to a man who goes on a journey, leaves his house and entrusts to each one what he is to do and charges the doorkeeper to keep watch, and then conclude with the final exhortation, telling us: so you too, keep watch.

From these words of our Lord we can certainly deduce the lovingly vigilant attitude that must characterize all of us members of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word. For are we not asked to put out into the deep, so as not to shy away from the missionary adventure, and to conduct ourselves in this life like fishermen, who are humble, hardworking men, who do not fear dangers, vigilant and patient in long night watches5?.

That is why, although the vigilance that Christ asks of us today in the Gospel is common to all the religious of the Incarnate Word, and even more, common to all Christians, it seems to me that we can also read in these same words the mission of these brothers of ours who are to devote themselves with tireless effort to giving “an outstanding witness to the majesty and charity of God, as well as to union in Christ”6, thus imitating “the Word who offered himself to the Father, silent and hidden, in the womb of Mary”7, in order to be true channels through which may descend the holiness that God wants to communicate to this nation and, why not, also to the whole Church and to the whole world.

The Incarnate Word entrusts to each one what he is to do, and to our monks is entrusted

– “to be in the vanguard of all the apostolic works of the Institute”8. That is, that they go ahead with their prayers to implore with tender accents before the throne of God what the psalmist asked in the psalm we have just sung (heard): Lord, God of hosts, turn your eyes: look upon your vine and visit it, protect the stock planted by your hand, the shoot that you yourself cultivated. That is being in the vanguard! That is, keeping watch in prayer so that God may be pleased to make his vine bear fruit, the vine that his missionaries strive so hard to cultivate, and so that they themselves may not abandon it, discouraged by difficulties or cast down by failures. To be in the vanguard is to open new paths so that the message of Christ may penetrate hearts, families, the world of education, lines of thought, that is, the turning points of culture9, so that in all hearts the name of God may be more fully praised.

That is why our monks play such an essential and important role in the Church, because they sustain the activity of our missionaries and of so many others who work on the many frontiers of evangelization.

– Likewise, to them is also entrusted being “guardians of the spirit of the Institute, showing to all the primacy of the love of God and the value of the mortifying virtues of silence, penance, obedience, sacrifice and oblative love”10. That is why the mission of our contemplatives is so inspiring, because it implies a “greater participation in and imitation of the radicalism of the self-emptying of the Word of God”11. A mission that necessarily entails “death like the grain of wheat, in order to see Christ in all things”12.

How much humanity needs the witness of contemplative monks, to spur it to consider eternal and perennial values! How much it needs the serenity that you enjoy and that springs from intimate union with Christ!13.

Look, our dear John Paul II once said: “In the history of evangelization, the destiny of an entire people was radically transformed for time and eternity because of the fidelity of someone who was faithful to his call to the end”14. So too, and I would like you to understand this well, I want to tell you that “God is counting on you; that He makes his plans, in a certain way, depending on your free collaboration, on the oblation of your lives and on the generosity with which you follow the inspirations that the Holy Spirit gives you in the depths of your hearts”15.

Our blessed Catholic faith here in Brazil has been bound forever, according to God’s plan, to the fidelity of Frei Galvão –a man of peace and charity16– and of St. José de Anchieta –the apostle of Brazil–. But from now on we can also say that some aspects of God’s plan will be bound to your fidelity, to the fervor with which you say yes to the Word of God in your lives, to the genuineness with which you live the spirit of the Institute and according to the style17 of the Incarnate Word. Always remember what the fidelity of these men has meant for Brazil and for the world, and be faithful, you too, to the mission that God entrusts to you as monks and missionaries of the Institute of the Incarnate Word here in these lands.

May this monastery be like an oasis in the desert of this life … a beacon of light that enlightens minds and recalls the truths of the Gospel to all the weary souls who come to it. Always remember that you –our dear monks of the St. Michael the Archangel Monastery– are a focal point of the mercy of God and of the goodness of the Virgin Mary, and therefore it is your task to radiate that merciful fragrance upon all men. Make of this place what our Constitutions ask: “a magnet of God’s grace and a lightning rod of his wrath”18. Be, with your joyfully penitent, silent and hidden lives, those flames that shine in the darkness through the charity of Christ that burns in your souls. How much we need magnanimous souls who, with their sacrifices and prayers, rend that heaven so that the waters of grace and consolation –those that lead to eternal life– may flow upon our missions, upon our souls and upon those of so very many other souls who do not yet know Christ or live as if they did not know Him!

That is why I would like to repeat to you the same words that the great mystic and doctor of the Church, St. John of the Cross, wrote to some nuns who had just founded a monastery in Córdoba, Spain, and which I believe fit this occasion well. He writes to the prioress so that she may pass them on to the other nuns, and he says: “Tell them, then, that our Lord has taken them as first stones, that they should consider what they ought to be, since the others are to be founded on them as on the strongest; that they take advantage of this first spirit that God gives in these beginnings to take up anew the way of perfection in all humility and detachment, within and without, not with a childish spirit but with a robust will; let them pursue mortification and penance, wanting this Christ to cost them something, and not being like those who seek comfort and consolation, whether in God or outside him; but rather suffering in God, and outside him for him, in silence and hope and loving remembrance”19.

[Peroratio] My dear all, dear Fr. Rossi, my brother priests, seminarians, novices, monks, dear mothers and sisters, I would not want to conclude without telling you, as St. Paul says in today’s second reading, that I continually thank God for the divine gifts he has granted you in Christ Jesus. I encourage you to keep working together and with great spirit because, as our Constitutions say, “in the [epic] task of evangelizing the culture, individual efforts or those of a single generation are not enough; rather, a great movement is needed that keeps growing in extent and depth”20.

This is the resounding idea: to sacrifice oneself21!.

May the foundation of this monastery here in the Province be a starting point for a new apostolic fruitfulness.

May God bless you with many, very many vocations who wish to leave everything to give themselves to Him who is the All.

May Most Holy Mary, Nossa Senhora da Aparecida, the first to welcome the Word in order to offer it to the world, make the communities of our Religious Family here in this blessed Brazilian nation burning centers of the new evangelization.

To Her also I earnestly entrust each one of you. May Our Lady always protect you, make you ever more her own, and give you that caress in the soul that little ones need as an encouragement for their perseverance.

To all of you I say, as the Spiritual Father of our Religious Family said when he visited Brazil back in the ’80s: “Pray, pray much for those who also pray, for those who cannot pray, for those who do not know how to pray and for those who do not want to pray.” And I too commend myself personally and very much to the prayers of all of you. Always count on mine.

May Our Lady bless you. In the Name of the Father, and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.
