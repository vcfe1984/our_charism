Mt 16:24-28

We find ourselves here in the Co-Cathedral of Priverno, reliquary of one of the most precious relics of him who “espoused the Truth”1 and under whose magisterium we wish to be formed2: St. Thomas Aquinas.

And within this framework I would like to recall the enormous importance of the formation given in our houses in fidelity to the charism. A formation that from the beginning has been conceived as definitively Thomistic.

St. John Paul II, in Vita Consecrata, teaches: “Formators […] must be persons expert in the ways that lead to God, so as to be capable of accompanying others on this journey […] but above all they will show the beauty of following the Lord and the value of the charism in which this following is made concrete”3.

Instilling the charism

Certainly it is a duty of all the members of the Institute to bear witness to and transmit the charism in all its purity, but it is especially incumbent on formators to instill in the members in formation “living the charism proper to the Institute”4. That is, to infuse with determination and by every means the charism and spirituality of our beloved Institute.

This, in our particular case, implies forming the candidates according to the very rich patrimony of the Institute, with all the non-negotiable elements attached to the charism, in such a way that all may “be thoroughly imbued with the charism and its purpose”5, and that nothing and no one may erase from any of them the seal of our “particular style of sanctification and apostolate”6.

This point is immovable for us. Each of the aspects of our formation, in all its stages and with all its nuances, is a non-negotiable whole. In all of them must shine forth the DNA of a religious of the Incarnate Word, magnificently described in number 231 of our Constitutions, which should be the great charter of presentation of each of our members.

That on the one hand.

A consistently Thomistic formation

But also, if we are to be faithful to the charism received, which identifies us and manifests our reason for being in the Church, our formation must be consistently Thomistic. This is of capital importance.

I insist: we “have received the immense grace of Thomism, of being inserted into the current of the perennial philosophy. And with it a great responsibility, that of forming a school”7. Because, as our Proper Law very rightly says, “holy priests and religious who are metaphysicians are needed”8.

Which is the same as saying priests and religious who work so that the public and social life of peoples may be subordinated to God as to its ultimate end, and who do not end up adapting Christianity to modern civilization. Priests and religious who do not dilute faith into the rational, nor turn the sacred into the profane; who do not transform theology into anthropology, nor dissolve the eternal into history. Rather, may they work for the Christianization of the world, not for the worldliness of Christianity. That is, may they evangelize the culture not nominally, but from within, under the influence of grace9, in such a way that it may be ruled by Christ.

It is imperative that we form complete men, heroic, of firm convictions, with “a solid mentality of faith”10 that enables them “to be servants of the Gospel and teachers of the people of God”11. Our formation has nothing to do with making our candidates mere commentators in whom confusion reigns between theology and philosophy, or sociology, or psychology, and who, “ignoring the living tradition of the Church… interpret in their own way the doctrine of the Church, even the Gospel itself, spiritual realities, the divinity of Christ, His Resurrection, or the Eucharist, practically emptying them of their content and thereby creating new forms of gnosis”12, as St. Paul VI said.

By the mission we have received, it falls to us to have a strong cultural formation, one that takes a stand before modern culture13. For which, my dear friends, what is needed is a “metaphysics with grip,” one that bites into reality. And, in this sense, our formation cannot but be Thomistic.

Our vocation as members of the Institute of the Incarnate Word “presupposes a firm Christian identity”14 and the learning of a living Thomism. And this certainly brings with it the cross of which the Incarnate Word speaks to us today: Whoever wishes to come with me must deny himself, take up his cross and follow me. So that thiscross, which was inseparable from the Incarnate Word, is inseparable from our lives as members of this Institute. Far be it from us to be ashamed of it.

That is why today we ask the Blessed Virgin for the grace to remain faithful in our “act of integral faith and total self-surrender [to Christ], with the formal decision not to make pacts, not to compromise, not to capitulate, not to negotiate, not to concede, nor to make deals with the spirit of the world”15, but rather to remain always firm on the Rock16from whom our spirituality and our pastoral work derive at their very root17. And may this always be the attitude in which all our members, present and future, are formed.
