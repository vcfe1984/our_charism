Pier Giorgio Frassati Novitiate (Italy)

[Exordium] Dear Fr. Jesús, dear novices: first of all, I thank you very much for the invitation to celebrate this Holy Mass with you on the day on which we commemorate the 30th anniversary of the beatification of Pier Giorgio Frassatti, Patron of this novitiate.

If all the saints “impel us to seek the city that is to come… perfect union with Christ, that is, holiness”1, certainly the example of the holy patron of the Novitiate, “fascinated by the beauty of the Gospel of the Beatitudes”2, becomes particularly relevant for all the novices of this house, since as members of the Institute they are called to bear “witness that the world cannot be transformed and offered to God without the spirit of the beatitudes”3, as the Constitutions ask of us.

The life of Blessed Pier Giorgio Frassatti “was entirely enveloped by grace and by the love of God, and it was consumed, with serenity and joy, in passionate service to Christ and to his brothers. Young like you, he lived his Christian formation with great commitment and gave a simple and effective witness of faith”4. That is to say, Pier Giorgio was consistent between what he believed and the way he behaved. By his example, he teaches us that “the life lived in the Spirit of Christ, the Spirit of the Beatitudes, is ‘blessed,’ and that only the one who becomes a ‘man of the Beatitudes’ succeeds in communicating love and peace to others. He affirms that it is worth sacrificing everything to serve the Lord”5.

That is why today I want to give you three thoughts of the Blessed, in the hope that they may serve to enkindle in you the great impetus toward holiness that must prevail in every member of the Institute, already from the novitiate.

1st Thought

– Pier Giorgio said: “We must hold on tightly to faith; without it, what would our whole life be? Nothing; it would pass by uselessly. The faith that Baptism gave me tells me with a sure voice: alone you will do nothing, but if you have God as the center of all your actions, you will reach the end”6. “This is the principal occupation of the novice: to seek God, the God revealed by faith”7. “We must live by faith”8, our Proper Law tells us; that is why we must ask for and strive “to have a faith that is living, firm, intrepid, eminent and heroic”9.

Holiness does not consist in “acquiring the manners and behavior of others; nor in carrying out exterior works to perfection”10. Careful! “these things are not bad; what is bad is to neglect seeking God with faith in prayer and to be content with simply ‘doing things well’”11. “Doing things well −while it is important and very good− comes in second place and will be the consequence of the love of God”12 that moves us interiorly. That is why St. John Paul II, on the day of Pier Giorgio’s beatification, highlighted precisely this: Pier Giorgio was an “‘interior’ man”13, a man of faith who manifested by his works the faith that animated him interiorly. Thus, for example, he would keep night vigils before the Blessed Sacrament, he helped the poor… but, as our Spiritual Father pointed out: it was faith in God and love of God, the “true driving forces of his life, [that] made him an active worker”14.

So faith, the interior life, our love of God, comes first. Otherwise it may happen that outwardly “one gradually puts on the clothing of a religious, but inwardly remains still immature”15. Hence one of the first points of our Constitutions emphasizes “the primacy of the spiritual in all our thinking, feeling and acting”16 and how “we must put nothing before his love”17. In other words, if you will accept a piece of advice as you begin religious life, it is this: always give priority to everything that concerns the interior life.

2nd Thought

– Taken from a letter of Pier Giorgio: “The end for which we have been created points out the way to us, strewn, if you will, with many thorns, but in no way sad. It is joyful, even through suffering”18. That is why the Directory of Novitiates says: “All those of simple and upright heart who seek God and live in his grace must always be full of joy, and with joy possess the divine peace that surpasses all understanding”19. “It is in eutrapelia that generosity is seen and good spirit is verified; not in amusement in itself, which can be the fruit of human enthusiasm, but insofar as it is supernatural joy, which can exist even in the company of trials or interior dryness”20. In this sense, the more charity one puts into participating in community activities and in eutrapelia, the more of God’s goods one will receive: and it can be seen that those who are most generous in recreation are the ones who perform best in their other activities21, as our Proper Law points out, particularly for novices.

Several times in the beatification homily John Paul II highlighted Pier Giorgio’s joy, for example: “in the serene acceptance of the difficulties of life, including those of his family, and in the sacrifice of chastity lived as a joyful discipline”22. A great example for all the members of the Institute, because crosses will not be lacking, nor does God want them to be lacking, but joy is one of the characteristics of the Christian (it is his gigantic secret, as Chesterton said) and, even more, one of the non-negotiable elements attached to our charism.

That is why it is important to cultivate, that is, to strive to acquire, interior joy, which does not depend on temperament, nor necessarily on external events, but on knowing oneself loved, chosen and sought by God. The joy that comes from a life well lived. The more deeply we are imbued with this thought, the more joyful and the more at peace we will be. One must even find what is called the joy of the Cross, which comes with the science of the Cross of Christ.

3rd Thought

– “Always remember what the Lord said: The good you do to the poor is the good you do to me. Around the sick, the wretched, around the unfortunate, I see a special light that we do not have,” Pier Giorgio told a friend. Thus our Constitutions teach: “care for the poor, the sick and the needy of every kind must be given priority: the charity of Christ urges us23, practicing charity concretely, as a witness, in the first place, to all the members of our Institutes that ‘charity, only charity will save the world. Blessed are those who have the grace to be victims of charity!’24. It must be very clear to all the novices and to all the members of the Institute that “charity is indispensable for evangelizing25 and the principal business of our life.

In the life of Pier Giorgio “faith was fused with charity: he was firm in faith and active in charity, because faith without works is dead”26. No less is asked of us. Pier Giorgio was a champion of charity and, as such, he is a precious model to imitate. May God grant us the grace to have a great heart like that of Pier Giorgio… a heart conformed to the heart of Christ.

[Peroratio] Finally, Verso l’alto, your holy Patron used to say: the goal must be set very high, the ideal very noble; do not be petty when it comes to resolutions of holiness27, and do not become discouraged, because if holiness is possible, it is because it is above all the work of God.

May Most Holy Mary, whom Pier Giorgio Frassati loved so much, guide you and protect you so that you may always advance with perseverance in the struggle to attain holiness. We ask her this in this Holy Mass.
