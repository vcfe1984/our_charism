[Exordium]«The Cross of Jesus and the Mother of Jesus were the two poles of his life and of his ministry. And that is why his life, so brief in itself, was at the same time so full. And that is the reason why his ministry, which covered only the Vendée, Poitou and Brittany and lasted barely twelve short years, has outlived him by more than two centuries and has spread through many countries. And that is because Divine Wisdom, that Wisdom to whose guidance he had entrusted himself, made all his labors fruitful and crowned all his activities, which were only apparently interrupted by death. The work was entirely God’s work, but it bears the seal of the one who was His faithful collaborator.» With these words Pope Pius XII spoke of St. Louis Marie in an address to the faithful on the day after he canonized him, July 20, 1947.

As we well know, from the very beginning our way of living religious life included the «fourth vow of Marian slavery according to St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort,» as our Constitutions expressly state in number 17. What is more, he is the first saint named in the Constitutions1. It may also serve as a fact that his name is explicitly mentioned 4 times in the text of the Constitutions and 2 times in the Directory of Spirituality. His works are quoted some 12 times in those two very important documents of our Proper Law, but they are also quoted in another 6 directories2 (at least). Even more, the only book by a saint that our Proper Law recommends must be read is precisely the Circular Letter to the Friends of the Cross3, written in his own hand by our beloved St. Louis Marie. I say this so as to give a sense of the importance of the celebration of this great Saint and of the significance his doctrine and his figure have for us.

St. Louis Marie, said Pius XII in the homily of his canonization, «showed the world the true mettle of a priest of Christ… his life was short, but it was an incredibly active life, rich in (apostolic) fruits, despite being singularly turbulent.»

That is why I wanted to take advantage of this sermon to highlight three great loves of St. Louis Marie which were –in my view– the driving forces of that magnificent work that God was pleased to accomplish through him: love of the Cross, love of the Blessed Virgin and love for the missions.

1. Love of the Cross

During his life he not only experienced the Cross himself –such as his family’s financial difficulties in his childhood, his choleric temperament and his tendency to violence, which cost him so much to overcome, the difficult apprenticeship of common life in the seminary of Saint-Sulpice, the great difficulties he suffered in the various apostolates he undertook, the misunderstanding of his superiors, bishops, brother priests…– but it was also the doctrine he preached, because he understood that this was the doctrine of Christ. At every popular mission he preached he appeared holding a cross, and at the end of the mission he planted a cross. The Calvary of Pontchâteau, the Cross of Poitiers with the inscription «love of the Cross. Desire for crosses. Contempt. Sorrows. Outrages. Insults…», the Circular Letter to the Friends of the Cross itself: all this bears silent but eloquent witness to St. Louis Marie’s love of the Cross.

During his life the Cross stretched out before him, tracing his path and giving an unsuspected dimension and extent to his struggles. Thus, for example, in one of his letters he writes to Sister Catherine of St. Bernard: «If you knew my crosses and humiliations in detail, I doubt you would be so eager to see me. In fact, I cannot arrive anywhere without making my best friends sharers in my cross, often in spite of me and in spite of themselves. (For) Anyone who defends me or declares himself in my favor has to suffer for it»4. And to this same nun he says in another letter: «your battles take place within yourself and are not seen outside your community. Mine, on the other hand, explode all over France»5.

Love of the Cross and love of Incarnate Wisdom are one and the same, and that is why St. Louis Marie could not preach the one while leaving out the other. In that precious work, The Love of Eternal Wisdom, he wrote: «Incarnate Wisdom loved the Cross from His tenderest years… As soon as He entered the world, He received it from the hands of the Father in the womb of Mary. He placed it in His heart as sovereign… He espoused it with ineffable love in the Incarnation. He sought it and carried it with unspeakable joy throughout His whole life, which was a continual Cross, and, after having made so many efforts to reach it, He died on it on Calvary… (But) Let us not think that, after His death, Wisdom detached Himself from the Cross or rejected it in order to triumph better. Quite the contrary! He has united Himself and, as it were, incorporated Himself with it, in such a way that neither angel, nor man, nor any creature of heaven or earth can separate Him from the Cross. Their bond is indissoluble, and their covenant eternal. (Then he lets out that irrepressible cry from the depths of his soul): Never the Cross without Jesus, nor Jesus without the Cross!»6, a phrase which, as you know, our Directory of Spirituality makes its own7.

That Cross, with Jesus Crucified upon it, was the driving force of his life. «I am espoused to Wisdom and to the Cross,» he writes to his mother8, «they are all my treasures, temporal and eternal, earthly and heavenly.» That is why the words he wrote in a letter to Sister Catherine take on such force here, so much so that it seems he is saying them to us today too: «Courage! … Carry your Cross well wherever you are. I will try to carry mine well with the help of divine grace. You and I, without lamenting or complaining, without grumbling or throwing the Cross far away, without making excuses or crying like children»9.

2. Love of the Blessed Virgin

The second great love of the Saint, and the secret of his apostolic drive to win souls for Jesus Christ, was without doubt devotion to Mary Most Holy. Convinced that «it was through the Most Holy Virgin Mary that Jesus Christ came into the world, and it is also through Her that He must reign in the world»10, he wrote the Treatise on True Devotion to Mary, proposing to all the total consecration to Jesus through Mary, in the condition of a total slave of Mary, as a most effective means of attaining holiness, following in the footsteps of Jesus Christ, who subjected Himself completely to her when He became incarnate in her womb.

Even more, St. Louis Marie understood devotion to Our Lady «as the best means and the most marvelous secret for acquiring and preserving divine Wisdom»11. He himself explains it when he writes: «Mary is the mold in which not a single feature of the divinity is lacking. Whoever casts himself into it and lets himself be molded will receive all the features of Jesus Christ, true God»12. And he adds: «Mary is a holy place. She is the Holy of holies, where saints are formed and molded»13.

Into that same mold the holy missionary abandoned himself, making filial devotion to Our Lady the spring of living water that made all his activity fruitful and the reason for all his confidence. To that same filial abandonment in the lap of Mary Most Holy he invited all the faithful. The devotion to the Mother of God which he sets out so masterfully in his Treatise on True Devotion contains, the Saint says, «what for so many years he has taught in his missions, publicly and privately, with no little fruit»14.

As for us, members of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, our rule exhorts us «to be apostles of Mary, giving ourselves to Her in the maternal slavery of love and doing everything “through Mary, with Mary, in Mary and for Mary”»15.

And, of course, as apostles we too must pass through our nights –St. Louis Marie knows this well– and that is why he says with great realism: «it will be necessary to pass through dark nights, strange battles and agonies, steep mountains, sharp thorns and frightful deserts. But by the way of Mary one advances more gently and peacefully.»

And he adds one of the phrases that, for me, is among the most consoling: «In Mary there is no night»16, because in Her there is no sin. And if a person wants to be devout and to live piously in Jesus Christ17 and, consequently, to suffer persecution and carry his Cross every day (as we say we want to), he will never carry great crosses, or will not carry them joyfully and to the end, unless he professes a tender devotion to the Blessed Virgin, who is the sweetness of crosses»18.

3. Love for the missions

And since whoever loves Jesus and Mary cannot remain passive, we can affirm that the third great love, which allowed him to channel those two great loves and was the propagator of St. Louis Marie’s magnificent work of evangelization, was the missions.

Truly, this «wayfarer of the Gospel,» said John Paul II, «inflamed with love for Jesus and His holy Mother, knew how to reach the crowds and make them love Christ the Redeemer contemplated on the Cross»19, devoting himself with great zeal to preaching popular missions. And although «his works (ended) in apparent failure,» «he was a missionary of extraordinary radiance»20.

St. Louis Marie preached missions in at least 27 towns in France; he was chaplain of the General Hospital of Poitiers, although he was later expelled from that diocese; he then offered himself for the service of another hospital, but he was dismissed from there too, and went to live in a ‘hovel’ (in fact under a staircase) on the rue du Pot-de-Fer; he founded the Congregation of the Daughters of Wisdom for the work of free schools, organized pilgrimages, founded the Company of Mary, was the author of several books, and practically at all times preached missions. This very town witnessed the last mission the Saint preached.

That is why today we also celebrate the wonderful missionary example St. Louis Marie has given us. For we too have been called to undertake with «courageous faith and to bring to completion, without wavering, great things for God and for the salvation of souls»21.

[Peroration] Consequently, we can say that the priestly ideal St. Louis Marie proposed to the world can be deduced from what he himself asked of the Incarnate Word in his Fervent Prayer for Missionaries, for he himself personified what he asked for there.

And this is the grace we ask of him today for us priests who are here and for all the priests of the Institute: «priests free with the freedom [of Christ], detached from everything,» «slaves of His love and of His will» […] «with the staff of the Cross and the sling of the rosary in their hands,» «men always available,» «true children of Mary,» «true servants of the Blessed Virgin, who […] go everywhere with the burning torch of the holy Gospel in their mouths and the holy Rosary in their hands […] and who, by means of a true devotion to Mary […] crush, wherever they go, the head of the ancient serpent,» «priests wholly of fire» 22.

And united to St. Louis Marie, in this very significant celebration, let us make our own his fervent prayer to the Incarnate Word and say: «Remember your Congregation!»23; «It is time to fulfill your promises!»24; «so that we may form, under the standard of the Cross, an army with banners unfurled and well ordered, to attack the enemies of God in concert»25; «who may guard your house, defend your glory and save your souls, so that there may be only one flock and one shepherd»26.

We ask this of him in this Holy Mass.
