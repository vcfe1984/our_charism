Good-night talk of Fr. Buela at the Major Seminary “Mary, Mother of the Incarnate Word”

I am going to take up again a custom I had in the early years. It was a sort of “good night” on very particular topics –which often are not topics suited to a sermon, but even so always bear much fruit–, and I used to give it always at the beginning of the academic year.

The first thing I want to talk about today: we would accomplish nothing good if we do not manage to form young men who know how to be authentically free. To be authentically free, one has to know how to be disciplined; otherwise, under the pretext of freedom, one seeks oneself, and that is not true freedom.

It is not enough for one simply to fulfill outwardly the things one has to fulfill, the things one has to do; rather, there has to be a deep conviction and there has to be a formation of habits. Good habits, so that later in priestly life (I am thinking, for example, of parish life, where one is practically “besieged” all day long) the priest may know how to attend to the things he must attend to, and give time to the important things to which he has to give time.

The thing is this: you will not succeed in forming an authentically free young man, an authentically disciplined young man, a young man who knows how to make good use of his time, if he is not taught, in no uncertain terms, to get up early. When you see a seminary where there is practically no set time for getting up; if one does not get up at the time one has to get up, afterwards one will not be able to put anything in order. I know cases of some consecrated persons who habitually get up late, and their whole day is crooked; they do not have time even to do the fundamental things. Let alone doing some “little thing more,” no.

To get up early you have to go to bed early, there is “no way around it.” As a priest one may sometimes go to bed late, but two days, three days, four days… in the end that priest ends up getting up late. And what suffers? Prayer suffers. Here, in the seminary, what suffers? The next day’s Mass! Someone might think himself “clever” for going to bed late, but the next day he is asleep. Who suffers? The most important thing we have in the day suffers, which is the Mass. Look: –it will have happened to you when you were university students or were in the world; generally when one is in the world one leads a very disorderly life– many go to bed late because they think they gain time that way, but the time they gain at night they lose in the morning. We consecrated souls are delicate flowers; if we do not have method, we die. And there is no way to have method if one does not go to bed early. The English have a saying: “Early to bed and early to raise, makes a man healthy, wealthy and wise” (“Going to bed early and getting up early makes a man healthy, rich and wise”).

That is why, when there is a fixed schedule –which is set by the superiors, which means that the time for going to bed is a studied and tested schedule–, it must be respected; one must go to bed at that time. One cannot appeal to “freedom of spirit” in order to go to bed at any hour. No! Because this is one of the points that must be very clear. If one does not rest enough, what happens in many seminaries happens: they start with stress, or some other similar or related thing. Of course, they have to study, so they stay up at night to study. No, it is better to go to bed at the proper time and, with the permission of the proper person, to get up earlier if one is in a hurry, if one has something urgent; and if not, to sleep with complete peace of mind the hours God gave us for that. Because that Latin apothegm is also very true: “Age quod agis” (do what you are doing). Whoever turns day into night and night into day lives a life upside down. And so, if one has to sleep, then one must sleep; I sanctify myself at this moment by doing what I have to do: sleep. Not study: sleep; and when it is time to study, study. “Age quod agis.” And to Mass, which is the most important thing, come well prepared; and if I get up half asleep: give my head a good soaking –beforehand– with cold water. Mass is the most important thing, and I must prepare myself. I do not know whether you notice it, but one –perhaps it is the years– prepares oneself for the great feasts we have in the Seminary. For example, when it is the feast of the Incarnate Word, does the feast arrive unexpectedly? No; one gradually prepares, one prepares the soul to enjoy, to delight in the Mass, the greetings, the celebration. The same must be done with the daily Mass. When one arrives at the church it seems that the heart begins to beat faster. One thought on any aspect of the Eucharistic mystery is enough for one to profit from the whole Mass. And all the more in these difficult times. We have to pray for peace in Zaire, for peace in Albania, for peace in the places where people go on killing one another like flies. But if the religious does not dispose his soul, if he wastes that moment which is the most important of the day, afterwards, when he is a priest –if he gets to be a priest– he will not know how to profit from the daily Mass.

So remember: I must make good use of time; I cannot make good use of time if I do not order my life, and I do not order my life if I do not go to bed early. You should even know that at some point you may need to sleep a little more: then go to bed earlier. Here there is noise because afterwards a battalion comes in, but one is already in bed, one is settling down. One can also make up for it by sleeping a little longer at siesta time, by going to bed earlier.

Nothing is gained by having stressed, nervous people, who afterwards start to suffer from surmenage. I have seen very gifted young men in seminaries –I am thinking at this moment of a splendid young man who foolishly fell into surmenage, and was left in a bad way for the rest of the journey: his preaching is a “watering can,” one never knows when it is going to end. The intellect is left as if wounded, as if injured, and so it loses the brilliance it had before.

Let us learn to be faithful “in small things,” as the Gospel says, and so we will come to be faithful “in great things.”

We would accomplish nothing good if we do not manage to form young men who know how to be authentically free. To be authentically free, one has to know how to be disciplined.
