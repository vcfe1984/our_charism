[Exordium]My dear friends:Today we find ourselves in the Church of the mother house of the Institute to celebrate this Holy Mass in which several religious of the Institute of the Servants of the Lord and the Virgin of Matará will take the habit.

A celebration that is already in itself spiritually overwhelming and joyful, and which today, being the day of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, takes on even greater dimensions.

Today’s liturgy presents to us the heart of the Mother, first in search, as the Gospel we have just heard says: they went back to Jerusalem looking for him. And once He was found, the heart of the Mother becomes a tabernacle: his mother kept all these things in her heart.

So too the novices who are going to receive the habit today, after going in search of the Beloved and having now found Him, must treasure Him in their hearts, like one who takes refuge within, under the veil of love.

You are already beginning to sense how lovingly provident God has been with us, having prepared even the smallest detail of this celebration.

1. Sign of their consecration

For today these dear sisters of ours will put on the holy habit for the first time, a habit that is not only a “sign of belonging to a particular religious family”1, our beloved Family of the Incarnate Word, but which, moreover, as our Constitutions say, “is a sign of their consecration and a witness of their poverty”2.

“St. Thomas teaches that religion is a state of penance and of contempt for worldly glory; for this reason, lowliness of dress befits religious, and this for two reasons –the saint affirms–: first, for their own humiliation; second, as an example to others, ‘he who preaches penance wears the habit of penance’3. Therefore, we religious of the Incarnate Word bear witness with the religious habit to our total consecration to God”4.

It is interesting to note that “the use of the religious habit implies, on the one hand, the laying aside of secular clothing, [that is, setting aside the clothing they wore until now in the world]. Such abandonment concretely expresses our not being of the world5, our separation from everything that is not God, and it becomes ‘the means of constantly reminding ourselves of our commitment, which contrasts with the spirit of the world’6. But also, and more importantly still, it entails the aspect of being clothed. Hence the imposition of the religious habit:

– speaks of a consecration, that is, of a self-giving to God [as we were just saying. Because it is an evident sign of total consecration to the ideals of the kingdom of heaven].

– signifies the change of life and of mentality (the metanoia of the New Testament), total surrender to God, wholehearted, effective and irrevocable adherence to the Catholic religious tradition, [it is the assimilation of the style of the Incarnate Word].

– signifies also the renunciation of the world, [that is, a definitive separation from merely human and earthly interests] and also a renunciation of material goods [that is why wearing the habit, the cassock, bears witness to a life of poverty joyfully lived and loved in trusting abandonment to the provident action of God].

  • [And finally, also] it implies that one wishes to strive for the perfection of the Christian life, that one denies oneself, takes up one’s cross, and dies with Jesus Christ a mystical death”7.

All these realities are implied, contained, supernaturally imprinted in the habit that you will wear from now on.

2. The wearing of the holy habit

You who receive the habit today for the first time, and all of us who have already been wearing it for some years, must be aware that the wearing of the holy habit “is a silent but eloquent witness before others; it is a sign that our secularized world needs to encounter on its way, and which is, moreover, the sign that many Christians desire’”8. We ourselves must be ever more deeply convinced of this. How many people think of God, of Our Lady, simply by seeing religious in their habit; how many are moved to pray, or at least to ask how to pray; for how many souls are religious, identified by their holy habit, a point of reference in terms of goodness, of sound doctrine, in short, of consistency of life. That is why I dare to say that the wearing of the holy habit that you will receive today renders, in some way, an ecclesial service.

Indeed, our Constitutions, citing St. Francis of Assisi, say that the mere presence of the religious dressed in his holy habit already preaches9. The Spiritual Father of our Institutes, St. John Paul II, also repeated insistently that “we religious, not only by our life in common, or by our way of behaving, but even by our way of dressing –which must always distinguish us as religious– are in the midst of the world a constant and intelligent preaching, even without words, of the Gospel message; and in this way, we religious become not mere signs of the times, but signs of eternal life in today’s world”10.

And this is so because the religious habit, in its simplicity and modesty, attracts the poor and quietly challenges the powerful, preaching to them without words about the very way of life of the Incarnate Word. Silently, a religious in his holy habit challenges others with the message that “this world cannot be transformed without the spirit of the beatitudes”11. He points out to them the transience of earthly things and speaks to them of a supernatural reality, infinitely abundant and eternal.

Let us realize, then, the importance of wearing it in these times: “Signs must be used now more than ever, –say the Constitutions– ‘above all in today’s world, which shows itself so sensitive to the language of images… where the sense of the sacred has been so terribly weakened, people also need these reminders of God, which cannot be neglected without a certain impoverishment of our priestly’12 or religious ‘service’, we could also say.

The value of the habit, dear novices, comes “not only from the fact that it contributes to the propriety of the religious [or of the priest] in her external behavior or in the exercise of her ministry, but above all because it makes evident within the ecclesial community the public witness that every priest or religious is called to give of his or her own identity and special belonging to God”13. Let us never be ashamed of this most worthy identity!

3. Wedding dress

The religious habit is, moreover, for you who are novices, like a wedding dress. Consecrated virginity –which for today’s world is madness– but whose dignity is confirmed in the image of the Virgin of Nazareth and is completely founded on the radicalism of the ideal that the Incarnate Word proposes to whoever is willing to understand, is the ideal of exclusive dedication to the Incarnate Word, analogously to the way a wife dedicates herself to her husband and her children. Therefore, consecrated virginity, which you have embraced, is founded in particular on a profound and constant yes in the nuptial order; on the gift of yourselves out of love, totally and without reserve.

Evidently virginity in its evangelical meaning entails the renunciation of marriage and therefore of physical motherhood. But this renunciation does not cause frustration –as many think, even members of our own families– because virginity opens one’s whole being to a motherhood according to the spirit; to a spiritual motherhood which is expressed in many ways. Let it be well understood: virginity does not deprive a woman of her proper characteristics: the spousal love which she nourishes for Christ makes her open to each and every person.

The Magisterium of the Church in Lumen Gentium has expressed this truth perfectly; pay attention: “And let no one think that religious, by their consecration, become strangers to men or useless to earthly society. For, although in some cases they do not directly serve their contemporaries (like contemplatives), they nevertheless keep them present in a more intimate way in the heart of Christ and cooperate spiritually with them”14.

Therefore, dear sisters, this sublime vocation, which is at once maternal, nuptial and virginal, you must learn to live in the school of the Immaculate Heart of Most Holy Mary. Your identity from now on is that of consecrated women, and this identity is illuminated and enriched in the light of the teachings of this Kind Mother, who wants us, her children, also to be seekers of God, lovers of God, happy to live separated from the world, yet solicitous toward our brothers and sisters who live in the world and united to them through the bond of charity in Christ, happy to live in the ‘house of God’ as in a family, rooted in obedience and in charity.

Many times the Polish Pope pointed out that we religious are “a living sign of the world to come”15 –and he added– “a sign that takes root by means of the habit”16. In such a way that the religious habit becomes a consistent and serene, but also courageous, witness to that for which we live. That is, “to imitate the Incarnate Word, chaste, poor, obedient and son of Mary”17; it speaks of our mission, which “is founded on the conviction that He must reign”18; it speaks of the fact that “all our wealth consists in giving ourselves to the Word”19 because, as St. John of the Cross says, “all the best of here below, compared with those eternal goods for which we were created, is ugly and bitter”20, and thus, by means of all this, we wish to attain the highest end, which is union with God.

That is why the taking of the habit is in some way a celebration that carries us into the future, which begins, yes, here today, but which extends throughout our whole life and points toward eternity, toward that new heaven and new earth21 that will not pass away.

Our Constitutions, with accents of the gentlest paternal charity, which contain at once a fervent longing of the heart and a loving precept, ask us to “love, then, the habit, which must become our very skin”22. May this be our fervent desire today and always.

*****

[Peroratio] To conclude, I would like to tell you, with the words St. Clare of Assisi once wrote, that: “Among the other benefits we have received and receive each day from our Giver, the Father of Mercies, for which we are bound to glorify Him with lively thanksgiving, great is that of our vocation”23.

Always remember that the gifts and the call of God are irrevocable24and if we are unfaithful, He remains faithful25 . Strive to bear witness all together to the power of grace, and show by deeds the generosity, even to the point of heroism, that can be born of a heart conquered by the love of Christ and that prefers nothing to this love. Keep your hearts always open to the Church and make yourselves available, as true Servants, to the action of God through your self-giving to Him.

May the Mother of the Incarnate Word make you –as St. Paul said– blameless in His presence, in love26.

We also pray with gratitude for the parents of these novices, that God may reward with abundant blessings the generous, selfless offering of their daughters that they make with great faith.

May Our Lady bless you all.
