30th priestly anniversary

[Exordium] My dear friends:

I have the grace of being able to celebrate this Holy Mass with all of you in this beautiful church dedicated to the Immaculate Heart of Mary on the day on which I celebrate the 30th anniversary of my priestly ordination. I want to thank very especially Fr. José for allowing me to celebrate this Mass, and all of you for having come to share this day and to thank God and Our Lady for this very great grace He has granted me of being a priest of the Incarnate Word.

Since I was ordained a priest, I must say that my life, and especially my whole priesthood, has been intimately intertwined with our Most Holy Mother, whom I learned to love and honor from early childhood, thanks to my parents and very especially to my mother.

That is why I wanted, in this homily and at the feet of this beautiful image of the Immaculate Heart of Mary that presides over our church, to speak of her, the mother of priests.

1. The mother

I see here among you many who are mothers of families, and many others whom I call ‘mothers of the heart,’ who, although they have no children of their own, devote themselves to others with an exquisite maternal love from which I have not infrequently benefited (as is the case with the sisters).

The reality of a mother on this earth is an immeasurable gift… Mothers are the supreme consolation that God has given us in this valley of tears… I believe the mystery of a mother is so intimate and profound that on this earth not even they realize the greatness of this vocation.

For the mother, in a special way, is everything to us (and I believe that we religious live this reality in a fuller and more beautiful way than other children). Because in large part, everything one is, one’s way of being, of thinking, of believing, everything good one has, one owes principally to one’s mother. Who could doubt that our mothers’ contribution to our lives has been decisive! That is why I dare to say that, among all human relationships, there is none that can equal this one. Because, as St. John Paul II taught, “although parenthood belongs to both the father and the mother, the mother is the special and more qualified ‘part.’”

For every time a child is born, a mother is also born, and the relationship established between them is unique, most intimate, unrepeatable and eternal, since no other future relationship, however deep it may be, nor the most tortuous circumstances, nor the most humiliating human weaknesses, not even death itself, will ever be able to erase the sacred bond between mother and child. For a child, his mother will always be his mother, and for a mother, her child will always be her child.

The mother is, in some way, like our Providence on earth in the first years of life, our firmest support in the following years of childhood, our most tender and most loyal friend in youth, and our most inseparable and unconditional companion throughout our whole life (and even when she is no longer among us). A good mother is a blessing from God and the supreme consolation that has been given to us in this valley of tears.

She shows us the way when we go astray. She lifts us up when we fall. She encourages us when we are downcast. She sweetens life on earth and magically turns thorns into roses. God conveys to the world the consolation of love through the soul of the mother. That is why a mother’s love has no boundaries. She can renounce her own interests generously and suffer with a smile on her lips because she loves; she is capable of renouncing, for love of her child, all the grandeurs of the earth. She dedicates her life to fighting for her children and rejoices to see them happy.

That is why, for a child, there is no place in the world, however beautiful, like this one: his mother’s heart. There is no creature in this life to whom we are more united than to the heart and soul of our mother. It is said of the Heart of Our Lady and that of Our Lord that they beat in unison, and one author says that this happens with all mothers, with the difference that in that beating it is the mother who always sets the rhythm, and that is why she always knows or senses what is happening in her child’s heart. Who has not experienced this? That is why Hebrew women would counsel one another that making the decision to have a child is of crucial importance: “it is deciding forever that your heart will walk outside your body”; and the proverb says that “whoever takes a child by the hand, in the end, takes a mother’s heart.”

That is why it happens with good mothers as with the saints: the work she does with us is so great that all that remains is to praise God for the gift of having given her to us, because she has been, is, and will continue to be, even when she is no longer here with us, the image of the providence and love of God on this earth.

The mother is the heart of the family; it is the mother who in a certain way creates and constitutes the home and protects its unity. Who has not seen this? Around her, everything is ordered and unified, everything is harmonized and grows.

That is why, when a mother departs, nothing is ever the same. She weaves the bonds among everyone, sends the children toward the father and the father toward the children. The mother is in a particular way the one who listens, consoles, encourages, forgives, reconciles and gives each one his place. She spreads the balm of love and tenderness over all family relationships. She has a constant concern for each one and does not rest until all are content.

How many of us can say of our mothers –whom God Himself has entrusted to us– that they have been and are the precious gift through which we are given to see how full of love Divine Providence is! For they, considering “good example as their first mission,” “with their maternal fiat (‘be it done unto me’),” taught us that there is no greater happiness than doing the Will of God. And in the shelter of the sanctuary of their hearts they taught us that what is most noble, pure and full is generous self-giving, without conditions, without time limits, limitless self-giving to the point of forgetting oneself out of love. That is why our mothers will always be the most luminous brushstroke that God set upon the book of our lives and the most tender caress through which we came to know the paternal gentleness of God.

Therefore, any one of us, wherever he may be, as he caresses his good mother with his heart, can say with St. John of God: “God has deposited the treasure of His love on earth in three coffers: in the Eucharist, in Confession and in my mother’s heart.”

St. Angela Merici said that “mothers, even if they had a thousand children, would always carry each one of them engraved on their hearts, and would never forget any of them, because their love is exceedingly genuine.”

* * *

Now, if we say this of our earthly mothers, how much more must we say of the Mother of God, who is Mother of Jesus the High Priest and of all priests. Who could measure the length, the height and the depth of the love contained in that Immaculate maternal Heart? And even more so in relation to priests

St. Manuel González, a Spanish saint, said: “The fire of the sun, reinforced by the fires of all the suns of creation, is not even a pale imitation1 of the fire of love accumulated in the heart of the priestly Mother… Love of the Mother of God, who can measure you? Love of the mother of the priesthood, who will dare to count your degrees?”2. And yet, how much she had to suffer!

For all Christians, but particularly for us, the Most Holy Virgin is everything.

Who can doubt that, given the perfect communion of spirit and heart that always exists between good mothers and good children, and in a most excellent way between that Mother and that Son, the scene of Gethsemane had two settings: the garden where the Son sweated drops of blood, and the hidden dwelling where the Mother wept drops from her heart; and that the sacrifice of His heart and of His affection for the Son had as its echo the sacrifice of the heart and of the affection of the Mother?

From the sacrifice of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, Mother of priests, all Christians, but especially we priests, must learn to love as the Mother does, even those who outrage her Son, and to shed tears for the salvation of their souls, and even to sacrifice ourselves like her, who, without altering the serenity of her countenance, lets herself be called upon by them.

2. My priestly chasuble

I was ordained a priest on a day like today in 1993, and from that moment I took it as settled that Our Lady would be everything to me. That I would live for her and would try to die next to her. That if I was with her, whatever happened, I would always be safe, consoled and, in the end, happy (as happens with our mothers).

That is why I chose that the image of Our Lady should be very present on my ordination chasuble, which is the one I am wearing now. On the front and on the back.

Ecce venio

Where do these words come from? Ecce venio ut faciam Deus voluntatem tuam. They are taken from the letter to the Hebrews, and do you know what? They represent the offering that Jesus made upon entering this world. It is the priestly consecration of our Lord upon entering this world; it was the beginning of His priesthood, and I wanted that to be my priestly motto: that I might always do the will of God on this earth, whatever it might cost, whatever I might suffer, and that I might always try to live with Jesus. But to do so I also knew that I needed the help of the Most Holy Virgin; hence the image of the Annunciation, when the ecce venido of our Lord was united to the fiat of our mother. Fiat michi secundum verbum tuum, be it done unto me according to your word. May that be the model of my life, that of the Mother; I asked Jesus to have the same sentiments as He and my Father had in order to fulfill His will. Such was the beginning of the Savior; so too did the Most Holy Virgin begin her mission as Mother of the Redeemer; and so I too wanted to begin my priesthood.

Ecce Mater

On the front I placed an image of Our Lady of Sorrows; it is the Virgin of the Macarena, venerated in Spain, where my grandparents came from before emigrating to Argentina. She is a Sorrowful Virgin, or better said, serenely sorrowful.

It was at the foot of the cross that Our Lady, as the model of a priestly mother, standing beside her Son, won for us with her tears what Jesus was winning for us with His blood. It was Our Lady of Sorrows who was given to us as mother: Ecce Mater… ecce mater tuam… She, in her sorrow, was saving us, and in a particular way, in being given to the apostle John, she was granted the grace of co-redeeming the world. That is the model: her offering of her life and of all that one does and suffers for the salvation of souls.

At my first Mass, on the 10th in the afternoon (it was a Sunday), a priest preached the first Mass for me and spoke to me about this, something I will never forget… And I always thought that this would have to happen, and so I should always be very close to Our Lady of Sorrows. That is how it is: every priest must go through this, and this is the sublime degree of our vocation. The holy bishop Manuel González teaches: “Think that many times you will have to go to Gethsemane, many times in the course of your ministerial life! And there, look, think about what you are going to give and what you are going to receive… You: your clean, upright, unmeasured affection, your work, your word, your talent, your sweat, your money, your comfort, everything that is yours… yourself; all of you!

And what are you going to receive? A handful of faithful, generous, loyal souls, but few… very few indeed. Around you?… The same ones who were around the Master,” the bishop goes on to say: “Good people who soon tire and fall asleep, or who are frightened even sooner and flee. Friends who sell or deny… Around these, or mixed in with them, the envious, the ambitious, the Pilates, who do not protect the one unjustly persecuted, those who spit, scourge and slander. And afterward, or in the midst of it all, the cross! And to carry it and face them all, a frail man, a rebellious flesh and a heart of clay!”3. That is the sacrifice of the priest’s heart in imitation of his Master.

However, just as Christ in the garden of Gethsemane was consoled by an angel, so in the Gethsemane of our priestly life, and beside the altar where our priestly heart is immolated −what joy!−, it is not an angel, but the very Mother of the Incarnate Word who, with her Heart in her hand, helps us to say: Ecce venio! Which means: Here I am, I come to do your will4, and which is my priestly motto, taken from the Letter to the Hebrews.

[Looking at the image] My Mother, give the heart of this your priest son, in his hours of Gethsemane, strength to love much and give much, expecting nothing! With a sacrificed heart! Because, as St. John of the Cross teaches: the one in love [with God], the more wounded he is, the more he is repaid5.

Now, taking advantage of the fact that we are here as among very dear friends: first, I want to thank you very much for having welcomed me so well −as if we were family− when I arrived at the parish, and I also want to express my gratitude for all the support and prayers you have given me since I arrived. But at the same time I would like, if you will allow me, to express a wish to you.

3. A wish

Providentially we are here in this beautiful church dedicated to Mary. God could have taken us to other parishes, but He wished to place us under the patronage of her Immaculate Heart (which is also a Sorrowful Virgin; look at that pierced heart). And we cannot fail to see in this a special design of mercy on the part of our Lord. But at the same time, the honorable duty of loving our Mother tenderly, of giving her consolations, not sorrows.

That is why my wish, which is at the same time my prayer, is that you love the Most Holy Virgin very much, tenderly and trustingly. I know that you already love her very much. But at times I fear that our devotion to Our Lady may be intermittent: we remember her only now and then, or only on her feast, or we pray the Rosary only once in a while.

Our Lady wants you to be entirely hers. And, like every mother, she wants you always beside her, every day; in every circumstance, be always Mary’s. And may you become apostles of Mary, that is, may your true and heartfelt devotion to the Mother of God radiate that silent influence on other souls that makes them want to be devoted to Mary as well.

I have always liked to watch the interaction between mothers and their children. Have you noticed what mothers do with their little children before sending them off to school? They wash their faces, comb their hair, prepare a snack for them to take, and they may forget many things. But one thing they do not forget before parting: to plant a kiss on their forehead; and the children run off to catch the bus or go into the school while the mother watches them go, bathed in the waves of a gaze that is all affection and satisfaction.

In the same way, how happy it would make me if every day the Virgin Mary saw you set off to your work, to school, to the club, to a gathering of friends, but not before passing in front of her Image to give her a kiss or make the sign of the cross, and to commend yourselves to her with a prayer. With what satisfaction and affection our Mother would look at you. But it does not end there. Because that would be a purely external devotion if it were not accompanied by a sincere interior life, nourished by the sacraments, which has charity toward others and which every day silently tells its rosary beads. For that is doing everything with Mary. How much the Most Holy Virgin will bless those souls, those homes, those parishes! And that, in the end, is what I wish for you: that you may have the greatest consolation in this valley of tears, which is to live and die embracing this Immaculate Heart that loves us so much.

[Peroratio] Allow me now to turn this sermon into a prayer to Our Lady:

Receive, Most Holy Mother, my fiat, now as a prayer, now as an act of faith in doubt, as an act of hope in fear, and always as an act of love.

Fiat! I am in your hands, my Mother! Suffering, exalted, humbled, useful for something or useless to all, I will always love you and I will always be yours, Beloved Mother! No one will separate me from you! In joys and in sorrows I will always be your son.

Lonely and unknown like the flower of the desert, wandering like the bird without a nest, always, always, my Lady and most sweet love of my soul, from my lips will come your submissive answer to God’s plan: Fiat! Be it done unto me according to your word!

Present my offering before the throne of your Son and grant me the grace to live and die saying: ecce venio, like Jesus, the Incarnate Word.

So be it!
