[Exordium] What a joy and what a blessing to be able to be in the largest minor seminary of the Institute! How much I wanted to be here! Thank you very much for the invitation. It is truly a great consolation.

When I was thinking about what to speak to you about in this goodnight talk, what message I wanted to leave you, there immediately came to mind that phrase of the Directory of Minor Seminaries which says: “Every Christian must be joyful; for the child [or for the young person] in particular, this is his life”1. And it seemed to me very important to highlight it, because it is not only one of the non-negotiable elements of the Institute, that is, something that must characterize all of us, but it must especially stand out in the minor seminarians.

This joy2, which must be distinctive of the minor seminarians, goes hand in hand with the family atmosphere that must reign in this house3. Our Proper Law says it explicitly: “another characteristic of this house must be youthful joy”4.

Many here have perhaps heard or read how St. John Bosco greeted his boys by saying to them: “Be joyful.” That is the watchword for all the minor seminarians of the Institute. But let us make clear that it is not just any joy; it is not the euphoric joy of having won a match (although that also makes us happy, of course), nor is it the ‘joy’ that one was chosen for something nice and the other was not; that cannot even be called joy.

However, there are certain characteristics or concrete manifestations of what this joy must be like.

1. An affectionate and joyful family atmosphere

The first mark of joy reads as follows: life in the “Minor Seminary must unfold in an affectionate and joyful family atmosphere: the seminarian learns to love his companions as true brothers and his Superiors as true fathers”5. That is, this joy we are speaking about is born of charity, of charity toward the other seminarians, of charity toward the superiors, toward the prefect… Feeling loved always brings joy. That is why, in the same way that each one of us likes to experience that we are loved and appreciated by others, we have to love and appreciate others. This is not a boarding school. This is the house where the youngest members of our Religious Family live, and therefore the family spirit must reign; this means that there must be familiarity with one another, and, as in every family, this is a house where we know each other well –with our virtues and defects– and even so we love each other, and love each other very much; we trust each other and know how to appreciate and value the gifts and the efforts of others, and we also know how to accept their corrections, their miseries… And because we are a family, we take care of one another, we respect one another and avoid by every means harming a companion, as can happen, for example, when we mercilessly criticize another seminarian, or leave him out, or do not want to share with some, or cruelly ridicule them… That damages the family spirit, and if we act that way we are opening the doors to sadness. Do you understand me?

You have to be promoters of joy, loving the superiors as your fathers –and therefore speaking with them with complete trust–, and loving the other minor seminarians as your brothers: defending them, taking care of them, helping them in everything you can, being a support for them. And at the same time as we strive to be a cause of joy for others, we must avoid being the cause of sadness for others: that ‘no one be disturbed or saddened in the house of God’”6 , said St. Benedict. I say the same to you. That is why, if we want there to be joy in our house, the first thing is that there be charity. If this is lacking, however beautiful a house we may have, even if we eat ice cream every Friday and get the highest grades, we are not going to be happy.

2. A spiritual joy

Another very important characteristic of this joy is that it is a spiritual joy. St. Tarcisius died a martyr for Jesus, that is, he sacrificed his life to console Jesus; it is not that he spent his time playing, or going on outings and singing songs. Because this joy is rooted above all in a deep sense of faith, in which the presence of the Lord as one who loves and saves always dominates and stands out7. Christ is our Friend, who only wants our good, and in fact the greatest good possible. How could we not be filled with joy!

This does not mean that we will not have setbacks, difficulties, sufferings, temptations or sorrows… but rather that, since our joy is supernatural (it is supported by and anchored in God), all those sufferings and sorrows are borne with a smile, or I would almost say while forgetting that they are sorrows or sacrifices. Because, since God is our Father, we know that He takes care of all our affairs, and that is why, not only as seminarians but as simple Christians, we must “rejoice always and in everything”8. At the same time we must know how to flee, as from a serpent’s temptation, from everything that threatens joy: discouragement because of difficulties –whatever they may be: in study, in prayer, in living together with the other seminarians–, entertaining thoughts about what we have left behind, worries about family, and anything else that makes you less happy, because that is not from God. And if it ever happens to you that thoughts of sadness come to you, or you are worried, or you experience some pain or sorrow, you must know how to open your soul to your spiritual director or to the Father Rector with complete trust, because we all need help in the struggle…

A very beautiful example of this spiritual joy is given to us by the martyr St. José Sánchez del Río, who is the patron of our Minor Seminary in the United States. When they took him prisoner, knowing that they were going to kill him, he wrote a letter to his mother which reads as follows:

“My dear Mom:

I was taken prisoner in combat today. I believe that at this moment I am going to die, but it does not matter, Mom. Resign yourself to the will of God; I die very happy, because I die in the ranks of our God. Do not worry about my death, which is what pains me.

Rather, tell my two other brothers to follow the example of their youngest brother, and you do the will of God. Have courage and send me your blessing together with my father’s. Greet everyone for me for the last time, and you receive, finally, the heart of your son who loves you so much and wished to see you before dying.

José Sánchez del Río.”

Notice the supernatural and spiritual joy in the words of this martyr, who was barely 14 years old! I die very happy… The technological society in which many young people live has multiplied, perhaps, the occasions of pleasure and comfort, but it finds it very difficult to generate joy. Have you noticed? And that is because true joy is spiritual.

3. Cultivating and promoting joy

Finally, our Proper Law, in a very fatherly way, also invites us to cultivate and promote joy. And for that, here in the Minor Seminary, you have thousands of opportunities! In recreation, in games, in competitions, in the floral games, in the conviviums, in the feasts of the saints, in the feasts of the Religious Family… all of that must not only be done, but must fire us up with joy.

I remember when I was a prefect in the minor seminary in Argentina, I would see the minor seminarians on the weekends getting up with more energy than ever, and it was because on those days they could play soccer; you would look at them and get the impression that they wanted to play more than they wanted to live. And afterward they were exhausted, but happy, and many times, if during the week there had been some friction or disagreement between seminarians, in the game they forgot everything and were the best teammates. And that joy of playing and jumping and shouting over a game stayed soaked into their souls and served them as motivation to face the daily struggle in school, in their duties, and in everything they had to do, so as to do it with a magnanimous spirit and generosity. I think they spent their time waiting for the weekend to experience that delight again and recharge the batteries of joy that lasted them the whole week.

The news of the Religious Family around the world is also an occasion for rejoicing. Now we have the ‘missionary adventure’ videos that come out weekly, and it is always a reason for joy to see all the good that the other members are doing in other parts of the world.

“Everything must contribute to joy”9, says the Directory of Minor Seminaries. Whether I study, whether I work, whether I am on duty, whether I am an altar server, whether we go on some outing together… everything must contribute to making joy reign not only in my soul or in my community, but in our dear Religious Family and in the Church herself. That is how Christ rejoices: seeing that we strive to be joyful, to cultivate joy by doing good to others, fulfilling His most holy Will.

They say that St. Mary Mazzarello was so interested, and so continually, in the joy of the girls that, in imitation of Don Bosco, she always asked them: “Are you happy?” For she considered the joy of her daughters the principal proof of their holiness10. If her daughters were happy, then she was doing things well. The same rule applies to us.

That is why this joy is also asked of those whose main apostolate is working in the formation of the Minor Seminarians. For if those under them perceive self-denial and true joy in their formators, they will feel moved to imitate them. But it must also be said that it is the responsibility of the formators to create the atmosphere so that festivity and joy may be lived, without fearing to spend money on everything that contributes to the celebration11. May the boys always be happy! That will also be our joy.

[Peroratio] With the hymn to Our Lady, who is the Cause of our joy, we ask her to increase our joy through the concrete practice of charity and our union with Christ. And at the same time, let us give thanks to God for the beautiful vocations of minor seminarians with which He has blessed the Institute in this dear country. And I especially ask Our Lady to always take care of you and to be consoled in seeing you always joyful.

God bless you!
