The unity of Christ

Recalling the thought of our Founder

During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the fifth day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in the union of both natures, we call to mind Fr. Buela’s thought on the subject, taken from his book “El Arte del Padre.”

The unity of Christ

So that, irrefutably, according to Sacred Scripture, the following four truths […] are certain:

  1. Christ is a single being, one single Person, true God and true man at the same time.
  2. The Word is united to the flesh by an intrinsic, physical and substantial union. He is not a bearer of God, but truly God.
  3. The divine and human properties of Christ are not divided between two persons, but are referred to the «one Lord Jesus Christ» (1 Cor 8:6).
  4. The Virgin Mary is Mother of God.

«We have been redeemed only if Jesus Christ shares in His person the full divine life». «In saying that “we believe in one God,” that “Jesus is Lord” (1 Cor 12:3; Rom 10:9; Jn 20:28), we break with everything else that claims to set itself up as absolute, and we destroy the idols of money, of power, of sex, those that hide in ideologies, “lay religions” with totalitarian ambition».

From this central truth of our faith derive enormous consequences that concern not only the spirituality of man, but the very civilization of humanity. It is curious, for example, what can be seen in Taiwan. The Museum of Taipei, in Taiwan, has a very large hall where the history of mankind is presented in charts, in photographs and in various other ways, and in which Chinese culture is compared with Western culture. There one can clearly see that Chinese art did not evolve with respect to the human figure, whereas Western art and Eastern Christian art did, particularly after the Incarnation of the Word.

In short, Christ is one beyond compare, in eight ways, as Nicholas of Gorran says very well: «It must be noted that Christ is one beyond compare, in many ways:

– Nature of God: In the first place, God is one alone because of the divine nature; hence, no one is God but God alone.

– One sole Almighty Creator: In the second place, He is one sole creator because of His infinite power: “One is the Most High, creator of all things” (Sir 1:8).

– Holiness of the Incarnate Word: In the third place, He is unique as man because of the singular eminence of His holiness. “There is none who does good…” (Ps 13:1); “it is fitting that one man die for the people” (Jn 11:50).

– Government of the world: In the fourth place, He is one sole Lord because of His governance of preeminence: “One Lord, one faith…” (Eph 4:5). And in this way there is one sole Lord, Jesus Christ.

– One sole Teacher: In the fifth place, one sole teacher because of the infusion of knowledge: “Call no one teacher, for your only Teacher is the Christ” (Mt 23:10).

– One sole Father: In the sixth place, one sole Father because of the production of all things: “One is your Father who is in Heaven” (Mt 23:9)38.

– One sole Shepherd: In the seventh place, one sole Shepherd because of His common care of the faithful people: “Let there be one flock and one shepherd” (Jn 10:16).

– One sole Victim: In the eighth place, one sole victim because of the singular price of our Redemption: “For by a single offering He consecrated forever those who are sanctified” (Heb 10:14).

The unity of Jesus Christ is a barrier against all false oppositions and divisions, for example, against the separation of the Word and Christ, of Christ and the Kingdom, of the Christ of faith and the Christ of history… In short, «God willed to unify all things, both natural and supernatural, in Christ Jesus, “so that He may have first place in all things” (Col 1:18)».

Consequences of the hypostatic union

Here are some consequences that derive from the hypostatic union in Christ, set out schematically:

  1. The person of Christ is of infinite dignity, since He is a divine person, the second of the Trinity.
  1. All that Jesus Christ does and all that He possesses, whether as man or as God, must be attributed to God the Son, because it is always God the Son who does it and who possesses it. Thus His words, His sufferings, His blood, His heart, His wounds, are those of the Son of God: consequently, all these things are divine.
  1. All the actions of Jesus Christ are of infinite dignity, because actions are attributed to the person, and therefore they are divine actions, actions of God, and are, for that very reason, of infinite value and merit. They are of «a superabundant grace for infinite worlds, if they exist».
  1. All the actions of Jesus Christ are infinitely holy.
  1. Everything that belongs to Christ, humanity, flesh, blood, heart, is divine and as such adorable. There is no room for any «co-adoration».
  1. Being the only Son of God, He is infinitely pleasing to God the Father.
  1. His humanity is filled with all the gifts of God, that is, with the fullness of grace –personal grace and capital grace– and of truth –beatific knowledge, infused knowledge and acquired or experiential knowledge–, and of His fullness we have all received. Therefore: «Everything that has been truly said belongs to us Christians» (St. Justin); «Let every good Christian understand that any truth, wherever it may be, belongs to his Lord» (St. Augustine); «Every truth, by whomever it is spoken, proceeds from the Holy Spirit» (St. Ambrose). And St. Thomas: «no one can say anything true unless moved by the Holy Spirit, who is the Spirit of Truth»; «there is no knowledge of the truth except in the Holy Spirit, who works in the intellect and preserves it»46; «everything good and everything true is from the Holy Spirit». «Every truth, spoken by whomever, comes from the Holy Spirit».
  1. To the one Person are attributed divine and human properties. This is what is called the «communication of idioms» (from the Greek ίδιος, which means proper, property, what belongs to one).
  1. Jesus Christ is the natural Son of God the Father even as man; only we mere creatures are adopted children.

Let us always ask for the grace to be always great contemplators of the Incarnate mystery, that we may know how to present it courageously to our brothers and, in a special way, that we ourselves may live it in its fullness, confessing the same and only Lord Jesus Christ as true God and true man. [We ask this of the Virgin, Mother of the Incarnate Word, with the hymn.]
