[Exordium] Our Proper Law teaches that “Everything is in the Passion. It is there that the science of the saints is learned”1.

The Passion is, par excellence, the work of Jesus Christ, the work of the Incarnate Word. You did not want offerings or sacrifices, but you have given me a body; holocausts and sacrifices for sin did not please you. Then I said: ‘Behold, I come to do your will, O God’ (Heb 10:5-7). Fr. La Palma teaches that“The Passion of our Lord, from whatever side we look at it, whether from the side of the person who suffers, or of the things He suffers, or of the end for which He suffers them, is the highest and most divine thing that has happened since God created the world, nor will its like happen until the world’s end”2. That is why St. Paul of the Cross, quoted in our Proper Law, tells us: “Everything is in the Passion”3.

However, the mystery of Jesus Christ has not been fully considered or understood unless, indissolubly united to it, we consider the mystery of the Mother, His Mother, whom we can also properly call “our” Mother, by virtue of that wonderful transformation wrought on the day of sorrow. Precisely that most intimate union with Christ –which culminates at the foot of the Cross– and the motherhood which at that moment She assumes toward us are the first reason why our Proper Law tells us that the Virgin Mary must be one of our great loves: “Because of her union with Christ and with the Church. Because she begot us, the members, together with the Head. Because she was given to us as Mother when she stood at the foot of the Cross: Behold your son (Jn 19:26)”4.

In this sermon on the solitude of the Blessed Virgin, in which our gaze and imagination turn to the afternoon of Good Friday and to the events of Golgotha, I would like to set before our consideration and contemplation three moments of the Friday of the Passion. And it seems important to me here to consider this whole drama from another angle, from that other great focal point of Golgotha: Standing by the Cross of Jesus is his Mother (Jn 19:25). To see how this profound sorrow of Our Lady was offered in union with the sacrifice of her most adorable Son out of love for us, so that She might cooperate in the great work of the Redemption; all of which should fill us with confidence and increase in us fervor in our devotion to Her whose slaves of love we are, and to whom we can have recourse, confident in her intercession for us5.

The Blessed Virgin is the second figure in importance in this drama, and so we will now consider these three moments, which are like three paintings, but seen this time not from the mystical side of the Redeemer but from the point of view of the Co-redemptrix.

1. Mary at the foot of the Cross

At the foot of the Cross, standing, as an image of faith,
with her soul pierced by the sevenfold sword,
stood the Virgin Mary.

By the Cross of her Son stood his Mother, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene (Jn 19:25).It is a spectacle at once dramatic and marvelous. Let us leave aside, since they are now behind us, all the central parts of the drama: the nails, the agony, the seven last words (among which was the third, in which His Mother became our Mother), the mockery of the Jews, the weeping of Magdalene. Longinus, with terrible fierceness, has just struck the final blow: the lance, and has pierced the open side of the Savior, from which flowed blood and water (Jn 19:34). Christ has died, the drama is consummated; the blood begins to clot, breathing can no longer be felt, and the body grows cold… icy. Wondrous is the spectacle that now presents itself to our eyes. That Jesus who preached life, who brought a new life to this earth, is dead, though at His side someone still lives, and it is His Mother, bathed in tears. She weeps, she contemplates, and she redeems.

Many wondrous things were happening in Jerusalem. Darkness would still be covering the earth; the earthquake that had been felt would still be stamped on the faces of the inhabitants of Jerusalem; in the distance one would hear the clamor of the Jews on learning that the veil of the temple had been torn from top to bottom, with all that this meant; the people were going back beating their breasts (Lk 23:48). Yet someone remained. All the rest mattered little. Mary was still at the foot of the Cross.

How edifying this image is! The Co-redemptrix was fulfilling her mission to the utmost, and in the absence of her Son, with her heart shattered by all that had happened, she remained standing, as if it were now She who bore on her shoulders the whole drama of the Redemption. Here the solitude of Mary begins.

The union of Christ and His Mother was most intimate. Christ was dead, His blood clotted; little that was divine or royal could be perceived in Him. The death of Our Lady on the day of the Passion was spiritual, but it was a death as real as the death of her Son. She won for us with her tears what Jesus won for us with His blood. Her heart was pierced not by the two-edged sword but by the sevenfold sword.

As members of this Religious Family in which we wish to imitate Jesus Christ as perfectly as possible “until we can truly say to others, Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ (1 Cor 11:1), it is no longer I who live, it is Christ who lives in me (Gal 2:20)”6, we too must die with Christ, like His Most Holy Mother, to the point of completing in the flesh –as the Apostle Paul teaches– what is lacking in the Passion of Christ (Col 1:24). “The sacrifice of oneself, following the example of Christ and united to Him, is most efficacious for cooperating in the redemption of the human race”7. “If in the present economy of salvation the Passion of Christ was necessary, our suffering will also be necessary. If there were another way to go to heaven, Jesus Christ would have followed it and, what is more, He would have taught it”8.

It is true that, as our Directory of Spirituality teaches, we “add nothing substantial to the sacrifice of Christ, but in the order of operation we complete it in some way. In His sacrifice Christ the head offered to the Father His own sufferings and those of all the members of His Mystical Body; He took upon Himself not only the sins of all men, but all their sufferings; these were sufferings that His members would have to suffer for love of Him; they would be persecuted because they served Him9”10.

By the grace of God we too can be “co-redeemers,” provided we unite our sufferings to those of Christ: “All the co-redemptive efficacy of our sufferings depends on their union with the Cross, and is in the measure and degree of that union”11.

Let us see the sublime way in which Our Lady immolates herself together with her Son, so as to follow her example and cooperate with our humble service in this magnificent work of the Redemption.

2. The Pietà

Mary’s inmost being is pierced with new sorrow
by the hammers that unnail Jesus from the high Cross.
Nicodemus gives the nails to the Virgin, so that they may go
from the body of her Son to crucify her soul.

The ceremony of the deposition begins; Christ is taken down from the Cross; they begin to unnail the completely disfigured body, a “holy skein,” as Lope de Vega calls it in one of his poems. The body of the Savior hung lifeless on the Cross… at anyone’s mercy. But above all it belonged to His Mother. She alone had given Him that by means of which Jesus had accomplished His Redemption; She alone made it possible for Jesus to exist; She alone made Him the new Adam.

Joseph of Arimathea arrived; immediately afterward came Nicodemus. These two men took part in this claim of Mary: Nicodemus, the secret disciple of Jesus, and Joseph of Arimathea, who gave up his new tomb for Him. The latter had asked Pilate for the body of our Lord, and Pilate granted it to him. The wealth, rank and position of these two men were notable; one of them had heard the crucified One speak of how one day He would be lifted up (Jn 3:14); the other came from the land of weeping, from the place of Rachel’s tomb. Centuries before, Isaiah had prophesied that our Lord would be rich in his death (Is 53:9); now He is handed over to the rich Joseph of Arimathea. These two men, after they took our Lord down from the Cross, after unnailing Him and removing the crown of thorns, laid Him in the arms of His Most Holy Mother. Death. The Pietà. Christ dead, or stronger still, God dead in His human nature at the hands of merciless men.

“When Adam was driven out of Paradise, after the punishment of labor had been imposed on him, he wandered in search of the food he had to earn by the sweat of his brow. Once, during his search, he came upon the lifeless body of his son Abel. Then he lifted him up, put him on his shoulders and laid him on Eve’s knees. However much Adam and Eve spoke to their son Abel, he did not answer. Never in all his life had he been so silent. They lifted his hand, but it fell limp upon his mother’s body. Never had he acted like this. They looked into his eyes, and they were cold, glassy, mysteriously elusive. His parents had never seen him like this, so passive and unfeeling. Then they asked themselves what had happened, but they could find no answer, until they remembered the words: Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for when you eat of it you shall surely die (Gen 2:16-17). That, Abel’s, was the first death in the world.”

“Centuries passed, and Christ, the new Abel, was condemned to death by His brothers of the race of Cain, poisoned with jealousy. The life that had come forth from the infinite depths was preparing to return to the Father’s house. The new Abel too was laid on the knees of the new Eve. This was the death of Death! When the tragic moment came to fulfillment, Mary, weeping, must have thought she had returned to Bethlehem. The head crowned with thorns, which knew not where to lay itself except on the wood of the Cross, would seem in Mary’s vision to be that little head she pressed to her bosom in the days of Bethlehem. Those eyes, which on closing had darkened the sun and the moon, would be for Her those little eyes that gazed at her from among the straw of the manger crib. The lifeless feet, pierced by the nails, would be for Her those of the Child before whom gold, frankincense and myrrh were laid. The lips, now disfigured and reddened with blood, would again be for Her those full lips which long ago, in that distant Bethlehem, had been nourished by the Eucharist of her body. The hands that now could hold nothing but a wound would seem again the small hands of the Child who in Bethlehem could not even reach the necks of the cattle. The embrace at the foot of the Cross would recall the embrace beside the manger. In that sad hour of death, which often makes us think of birth, it would seem to Mary that she had returned once more to Bethlehem.”

“Let us pray to her, then: No, Mary, Bethlehem has not come back! This is not the manger, but the Cross; here there is not a birth but a death; this is not a day on which one rejoices gladly with shepherds, but the moment of a death in the company of thieves. No, it is not Bethlehem, it is Calvary.”

Nicodemus and Joseph anointed the body with a hundred pounds of myrrh and spices, and wrapped it in the whitest linen. In the place where he was crucified there was a garden (Jn 19:41). In that garden was the tomb in which no one had ever been buried (ibidem).There the Savior was laid. And an enormous rock hid Him from His Mother’s eyes.

In the Pietà, at least she held Him in her arms, but now not even that. Mary was completely alone. Without Christ living and without Christ dead.

3. The Solitude of the Blessed Virgin Mary

Look and see if there is any sorrow like my sorrow (Lam 1:12)

Grown pale, the roses of your anguished lips; withered, the purple lilies of your tearful cheeks; Remembering the joyful hours long gone in Bethlehem,
with no consolation now, and no good to fill her solitude…
See her, where she comes, daughters of Jerusalem!

The return begins. What Jeremiah had lamented centuries before was being fulfilled: How lonely sits the city! How sad is she who lived so joyfully in this life with her Son! The Lady of the nations has become like a widow! (Lam 1:1).They begin to go toward the upper room; Our Lady went almost by force; her body was moving away from the tomb, but her heart remained within it. They take her to the Cenacle, where Jesus Christ celebrated the Passover the night before. Those streets of Jerusalem were walked by the Mother of the Savior, sad, anguished and alone. “O wounded woman! Alone and forsaken, what will you do? With whom will you console yourself? To whom will you tell your sorrows? What heart is strong enough to keep you from fainting, having lost such a Son and having seen with your own eyes Him suffer so many torments, and so blamelessly!”

The Virgin alone weeps for the death of Christ, weeps for the solitude of the world.
Without a spouse, because Joseph was held captive by death,
without a father, because He hides, without a son, because He is dead

“Bethlehem is Jesus as you, His sinless Mother, knew how to give Him to the world. Calvary is Jesus as the sinful world knew how to give Him back to you. Something has come between your giving Him beside a manger and His being given back to you beside a Cross: what has come between is my sins. Mary, this is not your hour, but mine: my hour of malice and of sin. If I had not sinned, death would not now be beating its dark wings over His body of clotted blood; if I were not full of pride, the crown of thorns would never have been woven, because He suffered in my place; if I had been less rebellious in running the long road that leads to destruction, His feet would never have been pierced with the nails; if I had been more docile to His Shepherd’s voice calling me so as not to let me fall among the thorns and thistles, His lips would never have been so deformed; if I had been more faithful, His cheeks would never have been dishonored by the kiss of Judas. Mary, I find myself between the birth and the redeeming death. When you embraced Him in the
Pietà, He was not white as when He came from the Father, but red because He comes from me. The last drops of blood have already fallen from the chalice of the Redemption, staining the wood of the Cross and reddening the rocks that split apart in horror. Mary, my Mother, intercede with your divine Son for the forgiveness of the sins that have changed your Bethlehem into a Calvary. Ask Him, Mary, in these anguished moments o
f solitude, to grant us the grace not to crucify Him anymore and not to pierce your heart anymore with seven swords. Mary, implore your dead Son that I may live. Jesus has died, Mary, and He has died for me!” Let us too die, so as to be able to “nail in our hearts Him who for us was nailed to the Cross”12.

We must “die totally to our own self,” aspiring to that perfect imitation of Our Lord which is only “achieved through perennial labor. It is a matter of dying in order to live: you have died and your life is hidden with Christ in God (Col 3:3). The life of Christ was a continual death, whose last act and consummation was the Cross”13. “The science of the Cross and of the joy of the Cross is attained only in the school of Jesus Christ”14.

[Peroration] May the Blessed Virgin teach us how to remain at the foot of that Cross. The Incarnate Word has told us that only those who persevere to the end will be saved. But perseverance is often very difficult. Few of us are willing to remain at the foot of the Cross for three full hours until the Crucifixion is finished. Most of us are deserters of Calvary, half-crucified souls, impatient to sit down when we are not nailed to a Cross. Many of us make firm resolutions in the morning, but few of us keep them until the end of the day. The soul of the Mother of Jesus does not falter, because the soul of her Son did not falter. He kept His promise. He finished the work that was given Him. And His Mother herself remained standing to the end on that day of sacrifice. And there she is to help us, and to Her we must go with confidence, as our Proper Law encourages us, to ask her for “fortitude in moments of trial, because she remains at the foot of the Cross of each of her children”15.

May Our Lady intercede for us, then, and obtain for us the grace to remain three full hours on Golgotha, so that when our life has ended, we may pray with Him and with Her: ‘I have finished the work you gave me. Now, my God, take me down and raise me to everlasting union with You.’ Let us ask fervently for the grace of the science of the Cross, of the joy of the Cross.
