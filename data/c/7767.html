The Constitutions, speaking of the formation of the members, devote two paragraphs to the formation of the religious brothers. They are numbers 260 and 261. In the first of them they declare that “they will dedicate themselves to the service of the houses and to rendering spiritual services that do not require the sacramental grace of Holy Orders”1. And in the following number they describe in detail the intellectual formation of the religious brothers in the Institute. For example, they say that “they will take some treatises of Philosophy and Theology… [and that] they will continue their formation until perpetual profession, in the houses to which they are assigned and under the direct authority of the local Superior, [and then continue with] their ongoing formation”2. And I would like to dwell on this point. Because sometimes, even within our ranks, it is thought that the brothers do not study, or that they do not have intellectual capacity and that is why they are brothers; and even some brothers themselves think that because they are brothers they are exempt from ongoing formation or from a more demanding intellectual formation.

The vocation of religious brother

First of all, it must be said that the vocation to be a religious brother in the Institute of the Incarnate Word is something well defined. And like every vocation, it requires suitability, which “must be threefold: physical (and psychological), intellectual and moral (which implies having a right intention)3”4, as is stated in our proper law. Therefore, the fact that someone is admitted into the Institute as a religious brother in no way means that he does not have the intellectual capacity necessary to carry out his vocation, which includes −as the Constitutions well describe− those aspects of study we have mentioned, since the brothers are not governed by different Constitutions 5. And therefore, all the non-negotiable elements attached to the charism also apply to them, including the one that refers to the preferential study of the philosophy of St. Thomas, which will allow him a more effective way to bite into reality according to his vocation as a brother.

So it is important, as the Capitular Fathers said at the last General Chapter, that all the members of the Institute become aware of the gift that the religious brothers are for the Institute, and of the specific character of this vocation, just as it is also important that the brothers themselves be aware of the contributions they are called to make to the mission of the Institute in the Church6. That on the one hand.

The dignity of the religious brothers

Secondly, it must be said that the vocation of brothers in our Religious Family “represents a differentiated participation in the mission of the institute, with services rendered in collaboration with those who exercise the priestly ministry, whether within the community or in the apostolic works”7. Now, this does not mean that they are “second-class” members. Quite the contrary. The Directory of Religious Brothers says it very clearly: “The brothers do not constitute a second order, but are true religious bound to the same perfection, and to exercise the identical apostolate that forms the essence of our Institute. Therefore, the one religious consecration and the identical apostolic mission are the foundation of the equality between the brother and the priest”8. Hence, the dignity of the brothers and the priests is equal, even though their tasks are different9. Each one contributes his service according to his vocation, whether priestly or as a brother, and the greatest respect must be shown for the voice of God, who calls whom He wills to the one or to the other.

Moreover, both the Constitutions and the rest of our proper law acknowledge the religious brothers of our Institute as key pieces for its mission10. In fact, “in many areas [the religious brother] plays an irreplaceable role: [For] ‘There are things that neither priests nor clerics can do, and the brothers do them’, as Don Bosco used to say11”12.

Intellectual formation of the religious brothers

And this very thing leads us to develop the third point, which is the intellectual formation of the religious brothers. Because a brother cannot be a key piece in the mission of the Institute if he is not prepared for the noble task of the evangelization of culture. To be a religious brother in the Institute of the Incarnate Word “requires an appropriate and integral formation: human, spiritual, theological, pastoral and professional”13. And elsewhere the Directory, citing the Constitutions, adds that the best guide for the formation of the brothers is the one followed for the formation of all the religious in the Institute, which embraces human, spiritual, intellectual and pastoral formation14.

In this sense our proper law establishes that “the religious brothers need to form their intelligence. [Since] a ‘certainty of the truth’ is necessary, given only by a sound philosophy, founded on the objective reality of things, since ‘the intelligence… can reach what is’15”16.

So then, “the brothers, as far as they are able, must also have access to a philosophical and theological formation that allows them to face the challenges that ‘come from the values that dominate the globalized culture of our day’17. And that is why it is established that they must do the Studentate, in which they will take some treatises of Philosophy and Theology”18.

Furthermore, they must also study the Catechism of the Catholic Church because this gives them a complete and unified vision of the truths revealed by God in Jesus Christ and of the Church’s experience of faith; hence the twofold requirement of knowing all the Christian truths and knowing them in an organic way’19”20.

All this forms part of the initial formation that they must then continue throughout their whole religious life with ongoing formation, which, as we know, never ends21.

How is this ongoing formation carried out? Through “conferences, retreats, courses, exams, etc., but also through the month-long Spiritual Exercises, through pilgrimage to the Holy Land and to the great shrines of Christendom, etc.22. “But above all”, our proper law points out, the religious brother “must learn to let himself be formed by everyday life, by his own community and by his brothers and sisters, by the usual things, ordinary and extraordinary, by prayer and by apostolic weariness, in joy and in suffering, until the moment of death”23. In this way, initial formation is linked with ongoing formation, each one making himself available to let himself be formed every day of his life”24.

That is why I would like to end with the following exhortation of the document Vita Consecrata, which our proper law makes its own. It says: “No one can be exempt from applying himself to his own human and religious growth; just as no one can presume on himself and lead his life with self-sufficiency. No phase of life can be considered so secure and fervent as to exclude every opportunity of being helped and thus being able to have greater guarantees of perseverance in fidelity, nor is there any age at which the complete maturity of the person can be considered concluded”25.

May the Most Blessed Virgin, Model of every consecrated person, obtain for us the grace to make great use of all the means and occasions of formation that we have daily, in order “to make visible the wonders that God works in the fragile humanity of those who are called”26.

I take this opportunity at the same time to thank all the religious brothers of the Institute −apostolic and contemplative− for the invaluable help they represent in so many of our missions, especially those in the most difficult and most remote places.
