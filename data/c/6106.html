[Exordium] When I was thinking about this homily, I began rereading some books I have of Don Orione. One of them is a collection of letters he wrote to his priests for various reasons. I could speak to you about the excellence of the role of the apostolate you carry out in evangelizing the culture; I could tell you that there is no better witness to Christ than that which proclaims Christian charity with deeds and makes its witness irrefutable1; I could tell you that the spiritual and corporal works of mercy are those most in harmony with the end of our Institute, and are those that most spread the good2, and that therefore everything you do here, the least service you render to any of those here, you render to the very Incarnate Word himself, and it will not go unrewarded… but you already know all that very well. It is not for nothing that you are called the key pieces3 in the evangelizing work of the Institute.

Then I found this piece of advice that Don Orione gives to his own, which seems to me could serve you as well, and it goes like this: “The first charity we must do is to ourselves: we have to pray more, I say it to myself, I say it to you, I say it to everyone: we must pray more, cultivate piety more, humility, dependence, docility of spirit, and the religious spirit”4.

How important this is, isn’t it? Obviously this is true for all priests, which is why the saint says: “I say it to myself, I say it to you, I say it to everyone,” but very especially for those who, like you, are directly dedicated to attending to the needs of others, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Our Proper Law highlights something similar in the Directory of Works of Mercy, where it explicitly says that one “must attend to the needs of men, but never abandon or neglect the service of God through prayer”5. Why? Because “it is precisely prayer that will allow [you] to give yourselves better to the service of others; because prayer makes it possible to discover God in our neighbor”6.

That is why it remains important not only to pray: to pray the liturgy of the hours, to make the daily adoration, to celebrate Mass, to pray the rosary… but, as Don Orione says: “to pray more, to cultivate piety more.” I know that there are few of you here, that you have a great deal of work, which at times can become exhausting; nevertheless, we must not forget that for us, as priests of the Incarnate Word, the life of prayer is essential, and the truth is that nothing “can make us forget that above all we have been called to be with God, to listen to his word, to contemplate his face”7. That is why St. John Paul II once spoke to religious about “the need for long periods of prayer, of recollection, of adoration,” and added: “as consecrated persons we must not only pray, we must be a living prayer. One could also say, we must pray while apparently not praying. We must pray while apparently not having time to pray, but we must pray. It is another paradox. Humanly speaking, this is something impossible. How can one pray without praying? But St. Paul tells us: the Spirit prays in us, and so the matter turns out somewhat differently”8. That is why recollection during the day is important.

Of course, in case of extreme necessity, one must apply what St. Vincent de Paul teaches: “Service of the poor must be preferred to everything, and it must be rendered without delay. Therefore, if at the time of prayer some medicine or any kind of help must be brought to a poor person, go to him with a very tranquil mind and do what is fitting, offering it to God as a prolongation of prayer. And do not have any scruple or remorse of conscience if, in order to render some service to the poor, you have left prayer; to leave the presence of God for any of the reasons listed is no contempt of God, since it is for Him that we do it”9. St. Benedict Cottolengo taught something very similar to his own: “be tireless in the service of the least, without forgetting, at the same time, that prayer is our first work and the most important, because prayer gives life to the Little House”10.

St. Paul of the Cross, for his part, said: “for the sick, one needs a mother or a saint,” referring to the great care and exquisite delicacy one must have in attending to them, as if each were one’s only sick child. Well, that charity of the priest springs from prayer, from the contemplation of the mystery of divine mercy11. And so, when the work in the hogarcitos is born of prayer, that is, when it is supernaturalized, it presents itself as a most beautiful account of the love of God, which manifests in a special way his merciful tenderness as physician of souls and of bodies.

You know better than I, but it seems to me that service to the sick, to the poorest, is an intense experience of God, which constantly leads us to seek the help of Christ in prayer. In that way, even though the occasions for self-denial multiply day by day, you are being purified, you are growing in virtue, and that is, in a certain way, like a confirmation of the value of the charism of the Institute as a path toward the perfection of charity, which is what we have come to do in this life.

Don Orione also points out the importance of practicing in our lives “humility, dependence, docility of spirit, and the religious spirit.” For there is no doubt that “service of one’s neighbor neither divides nor separates the religious from God”12. And that is why in another letter he tells them: “be assiduous together in the practices of common life, and do your work together, bearing one another’s burdens and each one putting up with the defects of the others for the love of Jesus Christ: always joyful in heart, in spirit, in words, and also with serenity on your face as in your expressions: content to serve God, always content in perfect joy in Domino, in prayer, in work, in recreation, at meals, always and in everything with joy and simplicity of heart, praising God and serving God. And the Lord will do the rest, and will multiply blessings upon your steps, and your number”13.

[Peroratio] Now, to conclude, I would like to express to you from the heart our gratitude and great appreciation for all that you do here; know that it does not go unnoticed, and it is very edifying for everyone to see all that you do in the Home (I have seen photos14 of the Easter camps, of the stations of the cross, of an outing with the kids to the park…). I am more than convinced that it is not easy, so, as Don Orione used to say: “Courage! and always forward! Ave Maria and forward!” I truly congratulate you.

Let us ask the Heart of Jesus in this Holy Mass that charity may always increase in your souls, allowing you to bear witness ever more faithfully and eloquently to the Divine Samaritan through a holy and fervent life, sustained by constant prayer and aided by the maternal hand of the Blessed Virgin. And we also ask Her to send vocations that have the courage to do good as you do.
