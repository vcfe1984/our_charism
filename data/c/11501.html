Our Proper Law states with complete clarity that “the books used for proclaiming the liturgical texts in the vernacular, with the people or for their benefit, must possess such dignity that their outward appearance moves the faithful to greater reverence for the Word of God and for sacred things. Therefore, it is necessary to move beyond, as soon as possible, the provisional stage of sheets and leaflets, wherever this occurs. All books intended for the liturgical use of celebrating priests or deacons must be of a size large enough to distinguish them from the books for the personal use of the faithful”1.

That is, when dignified and fitting liturgical books are available, one should avoid missals or lectionaries that are coming apart (detached from the cover), with frayed ribbons, so small that a magnifying glass is needed to read them, leaflets or ring binders with loose sheets, or tablets (or a digital missal), etc. What is more, our Founder advised: “Never proclaim the Word of God from hand missals or loose sheets”2 and added: “Do not treat the lectionary as if it were a novel”3.

So, even though people may have ‘little missals’ or apps on their phones to take part in the Mass, the celebrant should not use them to read the readings, much less to celebrate the Mass from them. For, as Sacramentum Caritatis points out: “The ars celebrandi should foster a sense of the sacred and the use of outward forms that educate us in it”4. In this sense, the General Instruction of the Roman Missal commands: “Special care must be taken that the liturgical books, especially the Book of the Gospels and the Lectionary, which are intended for the proclamation of the Word of God and therefore enjoy special veneration, truly be in the liturgical action signs and symbols of supernatural realities, and hence be truly worthy, beautiful and fitting”5.

Moreover, Fr. Buela always recommended to us that, in addition to the Lectionary and the Roman Missal, we should have the Missal and Lectionary of the Blessed Virgin, the Missal of the Holy Land, and that all the liturgical books be of the best editions. He placed special emphasis on our sacristies never lacking a beautiful Book of the Gospels.

Regarding the use of the liturgical books employed at Mass, we recall here that:

  • The Lectionary must be placed on the ambo, open and marked at the corresponding reading6. That is, the Lectionary is not carried in the entrance procession7.
  • The Missal must be marked before Mass. Fr. Buela taught us to mark the proper (prayers, rites, introductions, universal prayer), the Preface and the chosen Eucharistic Prayer. Furthermore, the Missal, once marked, should be placed “next to the priest’s chair”8. However, once the universal prayer is over, everyone sits and the offertory chant begins, the acolyte or another lay minister places the Missal on the altar9.
  • The Book of the Gospels may be set out on the altar, facing the faithful, before Mass begins10. But when there is an entrance procession, let the Book of the Gospels also be prepared, and once the altar is reached: “it is praiseworthy to place the Book of the Gospels on the altar”11. During the Liturgy of the Word, “if the Book of the Gospels is on the altar, he takes it and, preceded by the lay ministers, who may carry the thurible and the candles, goes to the ambo, carrying the Book of the Gospels slightly elevated”12. The Book of the Gospels is incensed with three swings of the thurible13. The Book of the Gospels (like the altar) is also venerated with a kiss. “In more solemn celebrations the Bishop, as circumstances suggest, imparts the blessing to the people with the Book of the Gospels. Finally, the Book of the Gospels may be carried to the credence table or to another suitable and dignified place”14.

In short, as the Directory of Parishes rightly says: “there is a wealth of liturgical signs that should be made use of with a view to an ever more fruitful active participation”15, and this concerns all of us, whether parish priests or not.
