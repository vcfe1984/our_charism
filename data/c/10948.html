THE ENCOUNTER WITH THE LIVING JESUS CHRIST

Dear brothers and sisters: In the account we have just read, Luke describes in a beautiful way one of the encounters our Lord had with His disciples after He had risen.

I – EMMAUS

This account holds a most important teaching for our lives, since in these disciples we must know how to see ourselves. In them the evangelist reveals three states or moments:

  • Before contact with the risen Christ: Cleopas, with his sad face, said to Him: are you the only one who does not know what has happened in these days? ….. We had hoped that He would be the one to set Israel free... And our Lord would reproach them: Men slow of understanding, how hard it is for you to believe all that the prophets announced. They were sad, discouraged, and this because their faith was as if dead (it seemed to have died with Jesus Christ on Golgotha); and so they had no hope; before the cross they had despaired of Jesus Christ. And that is why they were returning as if defeated to Jerusalem.
  • The encounter: It took place in a way similar to how it happens in our lives, under the veil of Faith. Certainly in a different way, but a similar one: … Jesus Himself drew near and went on walking with them… but something kept their eyes from recognizing Him. In fact they will recognize Him by effects proper to Jesus Christ, by two things that only Jesus Christ could do in that way: make hearts burn with His words, and break the bread.

John Paul II: “There is no doubt that St. Luke, in narrating this episode, especially the decisive moment in which the two disciples recognize Jesus, makes an explicit allusion to the accounts of the institution of the Eucharist, that is, to the way Jesus acted at the Last Supper (cf. Lk 24:30). The evangelist, to relate what the disciples of Emmaus tell the Eleven, uses an expression which in the nascent Church had a precise Eucharistic meaning: They had known Him in the breaking of the bread (Lk 24:35)” (Ecclesia in America).

  • What remained in their souls: Joy: at that very moment (before, they had said it was late and the day was ending) they returned to Jerusalem. Spiritual consolation: were not our hearts burning while He explained the Scriptures to us?

II – EMMAUS IN THE TIMES OF THE CHURCH

How can we repeat that experience of the disciples of Emmaus?

John Paul II: (explain in my own words) “The Church is the place where men, encountering Jesus, can discover the love of the Father: indeed, whoever has seen Jesus has seen the Father (cf. Jn 14:9). Jesus, after His ascension into heaven, acts through the powerful action of the Paraclete (cf. Jn 16:7), who transforms believers by giving them new life. In this way they begin to be capable of loving with the very love of God, which has been poured into our hearts by the Holy Spirit who has been given to us (Rom 5:5). Divine grace, moreover, prepares Christians to be agents of the transformation of the world, establishing in it a new civilization, which my predecessor Paul VI rightly called the ‘civilization of love.’”

So that the search for Christ present in His Church may not be reduced to something merely abstract, it is necessary to show the concrete places and moments in which, within the Church, it is possible to encounter Him.

1- In the first place, “Sacred Scripture read in the light of Tradition, of the Fathers and of the Magisterium, deepened in meditation and prayer.” That is why we must seek knowledge of the Gospels, in which is proclaimed, in words easily accessible to all, the way Jesus lived among men. The reading of these sacred texts, when it is listened to with the same attention with which the crowds listened to Jesus on the slope of the mount of the Beatitudes or on the shore of the Lake of Tiberias as He preached from the boat, produces true fruits of conversion of heart.

Sacred Scripture, even the Old Testament (beginning from Moses and going through the prophets … all that had been said about Him). “Ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Jesus Christ” (St. Jerome). How many times our world complains of the silence of God … and yet it leaves in oblivion the revealed Word of God, in which God teaches us the fundamental mysteries of our existence.

2- A second place for the encounter with Jesus is the Sacred Liturgy. To the Second Vatican Council we owe a very rich exposition of the multiple presences of Christ in the Liturgy (SC 7): Christ is present in the celebrant who renews on the altar the same and unique sacrifice of the Cross; He is present in the Sacraments, in which His efficacious power acts. When His word is proclaimed, it is He Himself who speaks to us. He is present moreover in the community, by virtue of His promise: Where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I in the midst of them (Mt 18:20). He is present “above all under the Eucharistic species.” My predecessor Paul VI thought it necessary to explain the uniqueness of the real presence of Christ in the Eucharist, which “is called ‘real’ not by exclusion, as if the other presences were not ‘real,’ but par excellence, because it is substantial.” Under the species of bread and wine, “Christ whole and entire is present in His ‘physical reality,’ even bodily.” (St. Paul VI, Mysterium fidei)

3- Scripture and the Eucharist, as places of encounter with Christ, are suggested in the account of the appearance of the Risen One to the two disciples of Emmaus. Moreover, the text of the Gospel on the last judgment (cf. Mt 25:31-46), in which it is affirmed that we will be judged on our love for those in need, in whom the Lord Jesus is mysteriously present, indicates that a third place of encounter with Christ must not be neglected: “Persons, especially the poor, with whom Christ identifies Himself” (29). As Pope Paul VI recalled at the closing of the Second Vatican Council, “in the face of every man, especially if it has become transparent through his tears and his sorrows, we can and must recognize the face of Christ (cf. Mt 25:40), the Son of man.”

III – CONCLUSION

Dear brothers and sisters, we must strive to encounter the risen Christ as those disciples did. Christ lives; He is our contemporary, and to the extent that we unite ourselves to Him we will have eternal life and therefore also the true joy of the soul.

However, John Paul II says: “The Lord’s invitation always respects the freedom of those He calls. There are cases in which man, on encountering Jesus, closes himself to the change of life to which He invites him. There were numerous cases of contemporaries of Jesus who saw and heard Him and yet did not open themselves to His word. The Gospel of St. John points to sin as the cause that prevents the human being from opening himself to the light that is Christ: The light came into the world and men loved darkness rather than light, because their works were evil (Jn 3:19). The Gospel texts teach that attachment to riches is an obstacle to welcoming the call to a generous and full following of Jesus. Typical in this respect is the case of the rich young man (cf. Mt 19:16-22; Mk 10:17-22; Lk 18:18-23).”

That is why it is not enough to encounter Christ; He also asks that we seek the things that are above, so that one day He may call us from death to life. If you believe that you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above; or as St. Peter says: So that your faith and hope may be set on God.

Let us ask this grace of the Most Blessed Virgin, she who will always give us Jesus Christ when we ask her for Him; for that was, is and will be her vocation: to give Jesus Christ to the world.
