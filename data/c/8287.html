the immortality of bliss becomes credible

[Exordium] We are gathered here to celebrate the most important solemnity of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, which, truth be told, is the primordial and fundamental truth that the Second Person of the Trinity became man. And therefore this occasion is most solemn for the whole Catholic Church.

Since we who are here are religious of the Religious Family of the Incarnate Word, some members of the Third Order, the young people of the Voices of the Word, and very dear friends of our Institutes, it seemed to me that it would be good, since we are in the intimacy of our family, to reflect on a sentence that appears twice in the principal documents of our Proper Law, which are the Constitutions and the Directory of Spirituality, and which in turn the Directory of the Third Order repeats precisely. The sentence is this: “Grounded in the mystery of the Incarnation, wrought by the Spirit in the Virgin Mary, we must always sing the mercies of God1 because ‘through the Incarnation of the Word the immortality of bliss becomes credible’2; we must have a clear awareness that without Jesus Christ we can do nothing3, and we must strive with all our strength always to advance in virtue”4.

Let us explain a little what this means.

1. Grounded in the mystery of the Incarnation, wrought by the Spirit in the Virgin Mary

As you know, Jesus in His first coming became incarnate in the most pure womb of the Virgin Mary by the work and grace of the Holy Spirit. The Latin text says: et Verbum caro factum est , hence the initials you see on the coat of arms of the Institute, which mean: and the Word became flesh5. Grounded, or rather, anchored in this mystery, we as a Religious Family want “to launch ourselves boldly to restore all things in Christ6”7, that is, to make Christ reign, first in our souls, and then in other souls and in the whole world, that is, in culture, in families, in education, in the means of social communication and among men of thought… in everything8.

Now, although “the work of the Incarnation is common to the three divine Persons, it is attributed specifically to the Holy Spirit”9, and this for three reasons: 1) because its cause is the supreme love of God: God so loved the world that He gave His Only-begotten Son10, says the Gospel of John; 2) because the human nature was not assumed by its own merit, but by grace alone, which is attributed to the Holy Spirit; and 3) because Jesus Christ is the only Holy One and Son of God; that is why the Gospel of Luke says: what will be born of you will be Holy, He will be called Son of God11.

And what does this have to do with us? someone might ask. Well, this has several implications for us, not only for religious but also for you. In the first place it should lead us “to act moved by the Holy Spirit, being supremely docile to Him”12. For which three things are necessary; pay attention:

  • “Attention to the inspirations of the Holy Spirit, for which one must work against habitual dissipation, lack of mortification and disordered affections”13.
  • “Discernment of spirits in order to accept and second the motions of the Holy Spirit and reject those of the evil spirit”14.
  • “Docility and promptness in carrying out what the Holy Spirit asks, because slow calculations are foreign to the grace of the Holy Spirit’15”16.

This “being grounded in the mystery of the Incarnation” also implies living the evangelical beatitudes17, which is what the Incarnate Word came to teach us; that is, we must be poor in spirit, meek, merciful, pure of heart… And finally, it also follows from this mystery that, just as Our Lady “gave her ‘yes’ as a slave”18 and then the Word took the form of a slave, being made in the likeness of men19 in her most pure womb, so we too make a vow of slavery to Our Lady20. Are you following me?

The second part of the sentence says:

2. We must always sing the mercies of God

And why sing? Because “song springs from joy, and, if we look at it closely, it is born of love: acting is proper to love. Whoever, then, knows how to love the new life that Christ came to bring us by making us children of God, also knows how to sing the new song”21. For it is in the Incarnation of the Word that the Mercy of God is most plainly manifested. In fact, one of the invocations of the litany of Divine Mercy honors God by saying: “Divine Mercy, descending to earth in the Person of the Incarnate Word, I trust in you.”

That is why it is proper to us, listen well, “to praise and give thanks, to sing and rejoice in God, Mighty, Holy, Merciful, Savior and Faithful”22, “even in the most difficult situations and in the most adverse conditions”23.

St. John of Avila, a Spanish saint and Doctor of the Church, understood this very well, for he said: “If we let His heart do what it wants for us, everything would be showing us mercy, because showing mercy is proper to Him; if He punishes, He punishes as though forced, and against His nature: He does not afflict willingly, nor gladly grieve the children of men24. When God brings someone low, He does not do it from His heart, but as though forced; like a father who sees his son being bad, he punishes him with love, and it is the son who makes him punish him”25.

Therefore, the man, the woman, the young person, the religious sister who lives consistently with her Catholic faith will never cease to sing the infinite mercy of God: I will sing forever the mercies of God26, says the psalm.

And this brings us to the third part of the sentence of the Proper Law, which says, quoting St. Augustine:

3. Through the Incarnation of the Word the immortality of bliss becomes credible

First of all we must clarify what bliss St. Augustine is speaking of here. It is true bliss, the bliss we are to enjoy in eternal life, in heaven, the endless happiness that the Son of God won for us by His death and resurrection.

That is why the Saint of Hippo says that “if all men desire to be happy, and this is their sincere desire, they must without doubt want to be immortal; otherwise they could not be blessed”27. For the happiness to which we refer here is not an earthly happiness, in danger of being lost at any moment, limited, intermittent −happy at times, unhappy at times−, nominal, happy only in appearance but without being true, deep and completely full.

Nevertheless, how much we men strive to be happy “according to the world,” or in this ephemeral world. In the face of this, another Spanish Doctor of the Church, St. John of the Cross, tells us (and it is worth listening to this attentively): “all the delights and pleasures of the will in all the things of the world, compared with all the delights that God is, are supreme pain, torment and bitterness. And so, whoever sets his heart on them is held before God as deserving of supreme pain, torment and bitterness. And thus he will not be able to come to the delights of the embrace of union with God [that is, where bliss is immortal], being himself deserving of pain and bitterness”28.

That is also why Our Lady said to St. Bernadette: “I do not promise to make you happy in this world, but in the next.”

For man to live fully happy, it is necessary that he live. But in this earthly and miserable life we are passing through, and therefore any ‘happiness’ is passing, and even if it lasted a hundred years it must end with this life. The Incarnate Word has won for us eternal life, true life, where we will experience the delights of the embrace with God. Because, to put it in a few words, “life cannot be truly happy unless it is eternal”29.

Now, the Son of God by nature became son of man out of compassion, out of mercy for us, the children of men −because that is what the words the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, men, mean−, in such a way that by His grace we may be made children of God −and therefore co-heirs with Christ30− and may one day dwell in God, in whom and through whom alone we can be blessed and sharers in His immortality. To convince us of this truth, the Son of God became a sharer in our mortality, says St. Augustine31.

That is why on this beautiful solemnity the Proper Law invites us to think: What is more fitting to revive our hope, so often weighed down, submerged in the heaviness of our existence, so often bewildered, than for God to show us His high esteem and the immense love He has for us? Is there a more luminous and convincing proof than this infinite condescension of the Son of God, good without change, who, remaining in Himself what He was (God) and taking from us what He was not (human nature), became one of us, and took upon Himself our sins, without having committed any fault Himself; so that, believing in the excess of His love and hoping against all hope, He might grant us, out of pure liberality, His gifts without any merit of ours?32.

That is why all the afflictions we may have in this life, the dark nights and the perplexities that God permits us to go through, the losses in our families, the very heavy loneliness, the criticisms we have to suffer, poverty itself, illness, the collapse of our plans, our very miseries and sins… all of this is lost like a drop in the ocean before the immense mystery of the Incarnation of the Son of God, who came to give us immortal bliss, the bliss that does not end, that is full, that is immense and cannot be contained. That is why the Apostle said: the sufferings of the present time are nothing compared with the glory that is to be revealed in us33. Whatever crosses we may suffer, we must know that they are preparing for us an eternal weight of incalculable glory34.

Contemplating one day the immense mystery of love that we celebrate today, and which identifies us as members of this Religious Family, St. John of the Cross said:

“O souls created for these grandeurs and called to them! What are you doing? With what are you occupying yourselves? Your aims are base things and your possessions miseries. O wretched blindness of the eyes of your soul, since you are blind to so much light and deaf to such great voices, not seeing that, while you seek grandeur and glory, you remain miserable and base, made ignorant and unworthy of so many goods!”35. Which means: how is it that we occupy ourselves with base things, with the glitter of this world, and set our heart on miseries, if we have been called to immense bliss… to heaven itself, where we will be transformed into the very beauty of God36 and will be inebriated with love, hidden forever in the most adorable breast37 of Christ, the Son of God. The Incarnation of the Word tells us that our place is in heaven, in the very Heart of Christ, where all delights are found.

“And one must not hold it to be impossible that the soul should be capable of so lofty a thing”38, St. John of the Cross goes on to say, because precisely “so that it might come to this, [God] created it in His image and likeness39.” That is why the Proper Law goes on to say:

4. We must have a clear awareness that without Jesus Christ we can do nothing40, and we must strive with all our strength always to advance in virtue

That is the message of the Incarnation of the Word for each of the members of this Religious Family, here or wherever they may be, whether religious or lay, contemplative or apostolic, and whatever the field of mission Providence has assigned them. Because ours is “to be another Incarnation of the Word”41, imitating the Incarnate Word in earnest; working not for ephemeral or passing things, but for ‘the most divine work among the divine,’ which is the eternal salvation of souls and the glorious resurrection of bodies42, and striving to attain the perfection of our state43.

*****

[Peroratio] Therefore, on this day we ask the Mother of the Incarnate Word for the grace always to keep our eyes fixed on the heaven that her Divine Son won for us, and that, whatever the circumstances of our pilgrimage, she may help us always to sing, with our lives more than with words, the song of the new man, which only new men can learn, renewed from their former condition by the work of grace and already belonging to the New Testament, which is the Kingdom of Heaven44.

A happy feast of the Incarnation of the Word to all!
