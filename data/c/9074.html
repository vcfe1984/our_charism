“The Heavens are distilling joys of honey”

One of the mysteries of the life of our Lord Jesus Christ that is sweetest, most devout, and most full of wonders and teachings is this one of His glorious birth.

“On this day, the heavens are distilling drops of honey over all the world.” Let us look upon Jesus, brothers and sisters!

Not only with the crown with which God the Father begot Him from all eternity, but with the one with which His Mother crowned Him when she gave birth to Him and clothed Him with our humanity. Come and see God! no longer in the bosom of the Father, but in the arms of His Mother; not amid the choirs of angels, but amid lowly animals; not seated at the right hand of the Majesty, but laid in His poor manger; not thundering and flashing lightning in the heavens, but weeping and trembling with cold in a humble stable.

Come and see the Christ of God! That hour has come which was so desired by all peoples, so awaited in all ages, so promised in all times, so sung and celebrated in all the divine Scriptures. Come and see the Son consubstantial with the Father! That hour has come on which depends the salvation of the world, the restoration of heaven, the victory over the devil, the triumph over death and sin, the hour for which all the saints wept and sighed.

It was midnight, far brighter than midday, when all things were in silence and enjoyed the calm and repose of the still night. At that blessed hour the Only-begotten Son of God comes forth from the virginal womb of the Blessed Virgin. Let us contemplate Him!

At that most blessed hour, that Omnipotent word of God, the Eternal Word, the Divine Son, having come down from heaven, appeared clothed in our flesh, like us in all things but sin. Let us look upon the Sweet Child! He already knows good and evil, He knows weeping, He knows sorrows, He knows tears, He knows hardships, pains and groans; and He goes through all this out of love for us. Let us marvel at the Holy Manger! The goodness, the mercy and the love of God toward men went so far that He exchanged His Holy Temple for the stable and heaven for the Manger, out of love for us: works full of love which the very ones for whom He did them regarded as madness: “the preaching of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing; but to those who are being saved -to us- it is the power of God” (Cor 1:18).

Let us learn by looking at the Divine Infant! Here He condemns greed, the root of all evils. From the cradle of the manger, from that heavenly chair, this is the firm lesson He gives us. O glorious manger, where the word of God, struck dumb, speaks all the more clearly the more silently it warns! Look, brother, do not depart from this stable where the word of God, keeping silent, weeps; but this weeping is sweeter than all the words of men and even than all the music of the angels of heaven.

Let us rejoice in seeing the Merciful Child! It is a sweet thing to contemplate Him, and not only sweet, but powerful and effective for healing our wounds. He was in glory and I in misery. He admirable and I miserable; but He bowed the heavens, came down to earth and drew me out of vanity, and washed me and clothed me and restored me and strengthened me. He gave me His hand when He was born, He drew me out when He preached, He washed me when He died, He clothed me when He rose again, He restored me when He ascended to heaven, He strengthened me when He sent the Holy Spirit. But who is this one, so high and so low, so great and so small? Small in the flesh… manger… stable, but great in heaven, where the stars served Him; great in the air, where the angels sang; great on earth, where Herod and Jerusalem were afraid. Be enraptured by the humility and greatness of Jesus! It is great humility for God to be conceived, but greater glory to be conceived of the Holy Spirit. It is great humility to be born of a woman, but great glory to be born of a Virgin, and such a one as Mary. It is great humility to be born in a stable, but great glory to be announced by a star. It is great humility to be among animals, but great glory to be sung and praised by the angels. It is great humility to be circumcised, but great glory is the name of Savior given Him there.

All this was so that we might know what He came for. If you look at who He was, all glory and all honor befitted Him, because He was the Son of God; but if you look at what He came for, all humility and all poverty befitted Him, because He came to heal our pride. With the one He confirms our faith, with the other He builds up our conduct.

Let us look upon Jesus! We will learn humility of heart, contempt for the glories of the world, the nakedness and poverty of spirit so celebrated in the Gospel.

Let us look upon Him! He is most beautiful in heaven and most beautiful in the stable, most beautiful on the throne of His Church and most beautiful in the manger of Bethlehem, most beautiful amid the choirs of angels and most beautiful between the little donkey and the ox.
