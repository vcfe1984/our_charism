Second Reading

From the Homily of St. John Paul II, pope, at the beginning of his pontificate

(October 22, 1978: AAS 70 [1978] 945-947)

Do not be afraid! Open the doors to Christ!

Peter came to Rome! What was it that guided him and led him to this City, the heart of the Roman Empire, if not obedience to the inspiration he received from the Lord? Perhaps this fisherman from Galilee would not have wanted to come here; perhaps he would have preferred to stay there, on the shores of the Lake of Gennesaret, with his boat, with his nets. But guided by the Lord, obedient to his inspiration, he came here.
According to an ancient tradition, during Nero’s persecution Peter wanted to leave Rome. But the Lord intervened; he came to meet him. Peter turned to Him and asked: “Quo vadis, Domine?: Where are you going, Lord?” And the Lord answered him at once: “I am going to Rome to be crucified a second time.” Peter went back to Rome and stayed here until his crucifixion.
Our time invites us, urges us and obliges us to look to the Lord and to immerse ourselves in a humble and devout meditation on the mystery of the supreme power of Christ himself.
He who was born of the Virgin Mary, the Son of the carpenter – as he was thought to be –, the Son of the living God, as Peter confessed, came to make all of us “a kingdom of priests.”
The Second Vatican Council has reminded us of the mystery of this power and of the fact that the mission of Christ – Priest, Prophet-Teacher, King – continues in the Church. All, the whole People of God, share in this threefold mission. And perhaps in the past the tiara, that triple crown, was placed on the Pope’s head to express, by means of such a symbol, the Lord’s plan for his Church, namely, that the whole hierarchical order of the Church of Christ, all the “sacred power” exercised in it, is nothing other than service, a service with a single aim: that the whole People of God share in this threefold mission of Christ and always remain under the power of the Lord, which has its origin not in the powers of this world but in the heavenly Father and in the mystery of the cross and of the resurrection.
The absolute and yet gentle and sweet power of the Lord responds to what is deepest in man, to his loftiest aspirations of intellect, will and heart. This power does not speak with the language of force but expresses itself in charity and in truth.
The new Successor of Peter in the See of Rome today raises a fervent, humble and trusting prayer: O Christ! Make me become, and be, a servant of your unique power! A servant of your gentle power! A servant of your power that knows no setting! Make me a servant! Indeed, the servant of your servants.
Brothers and sisters! Do not be afraid to welcome Christ and to accept his power!
Help the Pope and all who wish to serve Christ and, with the power of Christ, to serve man and the whole of humanity!
Do not be afraid! Open, and even more, open wide the doors to Christ! Open to his saving power the boundaries of States, economic and political systems, the vast fields of culture. of civilization and of development. Do not be afraid! Christ knows “what is in man.” He alone knows it!
So often man today does not know what he carries within, in the depths of his soul, of his heart. Many times he feels unsure about the meaning of his life in this world. He feels overcome by doubt that turns into despair. Allow, then – I beg you, I implore you with humility and with trust – allow Christ to speak to man. He alone has words of life, yes, of eternal life!

Responsory

R. Do not be afraid: the Redeemer of man has revealed the power of the cross and has given his life for us. * Open wide the doors to Christ.
V. In the Church we are called to share in his power. * Open wide the doors to Christ.

Prayer

O God, rich in mercy, who willed that St. John Paul II, pope, should guide your whole Church, we ask that, instructed by his teachings, you grant us to open our hearts with confidence to the saving grace of Christ, the sole Redeemer of man. Who lives and reigns.

Collect

O God, rich in mercy,
who willed that St. John Paul II, pope,
should guide your whole Church,
we ask that, instructed by his teachings,
you grant us to open our hearts with confidence
to the saving grace of Christ, the sole Redeemer of man.
Who lives and reigns.

Liturgy of the Word

First Reading

Isaiah 52:7-10

How beautiful it is to see, running over the mountains,
the messenger who announces peace,
the messenger who brings the good news,
who proclaims salvation,
who says to Zion: “Your God is king”!

Listen: Your watchmen raise their voices
and all together shout for joy,

for they see with their own eyes the Lord
returning to Zion.
Burst into shouts of joy, ruins of Jerusalem,
for the Lord ransoms his people, he comforts Jerusalem.
The Lord bares his holy arm
in the sight of all the nations.
The whole earth will see
the salvation that comes from our God.

Responsorial Psalm

From Psalm 95

R. Let us sing the greatness of the Lord.

Let us sing a new song to the Lord,
let the whole earth sing to the Lord;
let us sing to the Lord and bless him. R.

R. Let us sing the greatness of the Lord.

Let us proclaim his love day after day,
let us announce his greatness to the peoples,
his wonders from nation to nation. R.

R. Let us sing the greatness of the Lord.

Praise the Lord, peoples of the world,
acknowledge his glory and his might
and pay honor to his name. R.

R. Let us sing the greatness of the Lord.

“The Lord reigns,” announce to the peoples,
by his power he made the world firm,
with all uprightness he rules the peoples. R.

R. Let us sing the greatness of the Lord.
