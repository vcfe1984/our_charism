October 22

The Saint

At Rome, in Saint Peter's Basilica, Saint John Paul II, Pope, who governed the Church for twenty-seven years, brought his missionary presence to every part of the earth, nourished doctrine through many illuminating documents, and called the people of our age to open their doors to the Redeemer.

The Institute's Proper Law

Constitutions

5. Specific Purpose. “Secondly, we specifically pledge all our strength to inculturate the Gospel, that is, to extend the Incarnation ‘to all men, in the whole man, and in all of the manifestations of man,’ in accordance with the teachings of the Magisterium of the Church. In this respect, His Holiness Blessed John Paul II teaches: ‘The term “acculturation,” or “inculturization” may well be a neologism, but it expresses very well one of the elements of the great mystery of the Incarnation.’”1

Directory of Spirituality

“… The contemplative life, as Blessed John Paul II teaches, is ‘the nucleus starting from where you prepare to give a full sense to your religious life, as ambassadors of Christ amidst this world.’ […] Blessed John Paul II says: ‘To be devoted to God is... the noblest and highest form of activity for the human being, insofar as this concentrates completely on the adoration of the Infinite Being who desires the salvation of all humanity... you have here, thus, the precise form of collaboration that... you offer to the Church for the good of souls.’ …”2

The Founder

“The priest, an icon of the Father, must reflect God's goodness and mercy. That is why they call us ‘Father!’ Thus, as Saint John Paul II says, to achieve pastoral effectiveness in the ministry requires firmness of doctrine, but above all goodness of heart. Put on, therefore, the sentiments of Jesus and proclaim to everyone what the author of the Letter to the Hebrews wrote: ‘Let us go therefore with confidence to the throne of grace: that we may obtain mercy and find grace in seasonable aid’ (Heb 4:16, Douay–Rheims). A priest with firmness of doctrine, but without goodness of heart taking precedence, is an absurdity, something senseless, an aberration.”3

Excerpt from Sacerdotes para siempre. Working translation of the Founder excerpt; Scripture follows an existing published English version.
