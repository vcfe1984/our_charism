[Exordium] Here, beside our common Mother, the Most Holy Virgin of Succor, I would like first of all to greet with great affection my dear brother priests, the religious men and women present here, and all the friends who have come to celebrate the happy anniversary of the presence of the monks of the Incarnate Word in this beloved monastery of “Our Lady of Succor” here, on “the island of eternal spring.”

I want to tell you that it is a great joy for me to be able to celebrate this Eucharist with you. This is a privileged place of spirituality, and a true ‘capital of grace’ not only for the local Church –being the first men’s monastery in the diocese– but also for the whole Institute of the Incarnate Word and for the entire Church.

That is why, within the framework of the celebration of another anniversary of the inauguration of the monastery –so beautifully built by the Jorge Dorta brothers: Don Juan, Don Hipólito and Don Vicente, on the graced lands of Güímar, and which they built with the purpose that it might be a point of reference for spirituality, prayer and expiation for sins– I would like to pause and ponder with you, if you allow me, the grace that a monastery signifies.

One may well ask why the Jorge Dorta brothers wanted a contemplative monastery for men and not a parish, or a home for the elderly or for children, or any other work of mercy. Why a monastery? What is it that monks do that made them prefer a monastery above all the other works of piety they could have undertaken? That is what we will try to answer in this sermon.

Someone may ask:

1. What is a monastery?

And on this occasion we will answer this question by paraphrasing St. Luigi Orione and say that a monastery is like an “oasis in the desert of this poor life, to which souls come like weary and wounded pilgrims, and often dying along the way”1. How many souls, how many of you here today, burdened by the trials of this life, have come to the doors of this monastery to find consolation, seeking rest to heal the spirit, and at the feet of the Most Holy Virgin of Succor have left the wounds of the heart… That is why we can also say that monasteries, and in particular this monastery dedicated to the Most Holy Virgin, “is a beacon of light that enlightens minds and recalls the truths of the Gospel.It is a center of radiation of divine mercyand of the maternal goodness of Mary. It is a source of spiritual elevation, of faith, of Christian morals for discouraged and weary souls. A monastery is also like a flame that shines upon the mountains, and moves hearts to lofty sentiments, to thoughts of heaven. In the dark gray of earthly life, a monastery is a lighthouse –like the one on the coast of Güímar– that guides the little boats of souls in danger toward the harbor of salvation”2.

When the Lord Bishop Don Felipe Fernández García delivered the homily at the inauguration of the monastery, he said that “a monastery… is like the heart in the body of the Church: an invisible organ but of primordial importance.” Because a “monastery represents the very intimacy of a Church, the heart, where the Spirit always groans and pleads for the needs of the whole community and where thanksgiving rises unceasingly for the Life that He gives us each day”3.

A monastery is, then, something very singular in the life of the Church. Look around you. How can we fail to recognize, inscribed on the walls of this church, in the silence of the cloister, in the freshness and serenity of its gardens, the pure and iron-strong faith that motivated its construction and that continues to sustain this house of prayer and school of faith?

I think you will agree with me if I say that it is here, in a monastery, that souls –with different states of life, at different stages of their spiritual life– find a fruitful space, far from daily concerns, to recollect themselves and recover spiritual vigor, to take up again the path of faith with greater ardor, and to seek, find and love Christ in the ordinary life of the family, of work, of school, in the midst of the world.

Dear friends who have come today to celebrate these years of grace upon grace: may you too be the ones who draw others to come to the monastery to pray with the monks, to spend a day in silence before the Blessed Sacrament –that Physician of souls who works wonders in the serenity and silence of the soul that offers Him its friendship–; come yourselves and invite others to make the Spiritual Exercises, to consecrate themselves to Our Lady and, in short, to encounter God and be united to Him.

Many of you come regularly to the monastery and will have seen the monks pray, work… and you have some idea of what contemplative life is, but perhaps few know what it means to be a monk of the Incarnate Word. And this leads us to answer in part the second question we asked at the beginning:

2. What does a monk of the Incarnate Word do?

Well, “in imitation of the Word, who willed to live in the silence of Nazareth for thirty years, they consecrate themselves to Him in the contemplative state of life”4.

That is to say, they consecrate their life to praying, to doing penance, to immolating themselves in the silence of the cloister to ask God for the conversion of sinners –above all of consecrated souls–, to praying for the intentions of the Holy Father, for the increase in quantity and quality of priestly and religious vocations, and for the perseverance of all the members of the Church5.

Therefore, if, as we said a moment ago, a monastery is the house of God, “the monastery is the place which God guards6; it is the dwelling of His singular presence. And its monastic community can be compared to Moses, who in prayer determines the outcome of Israel’s battles7, and to the watchman who keeps vigil in the night awaiting the dawn8”9. That is why we say that they are at the vanguard of the missionary work.

Did you know that the prayer of monks “serves as suffrage for the souls of the departed, strengthens the weak, heals the sick, frees the possessed, opens the doors of prisons, loosens the bonds of the innocent, forgives sins, wards off temptations, makes persecutions cease, consoles the downcast, delights the magnanimous, guides pilgrims, calms storms, feeds the poor, leads the rich onto the right path, raises up the fallen, sustains those who are about to fall, and makes those who are standing hold firm?”10.

Because prayer is the only thing that has power over God.

Contemplative monks are those who, with their silent immolation, in the observance of regular discipline, and above all, in the offering of prayers and sacrifices, in the divine office and in the worthy celebration of Holy Mass, open new paths so that the message of Christ may penetrate the hearts, the mentalities, the customs and the cultures of peoples. And that is why a monastery, and a contemplative community in a diocese, near your home, on this most beautiful island, is an honor for its Church, a torrent of heavenly graces and, listen well, a lightning rod against divine wrath.

This is why the Jorge Dorta brothers built this monastery, as Don Hipólito explained well in a letter to his uncle, saying that they were building this monastery “as an expression of the value of prayer and of the dogma of the Communion of Saints” and, he added, that they wanted to offer to this “diocesan community the space of a vital need within it,” that is, a place where our Lord is adored, a place that is primarily a house of prayer. Hence the importance and preeminence of a monastery over all the other works of piety, which, although very important and very necessary, do not have the supremacy of a purely spiritual work through which all the others are obtained and bear fruit.

Therefore this anniversary also invites us to see the merciful Providence of our Lord, who has willed to grace His Church of Nivaria with this immense capital of spiritual grace. And, we must also say it, it is for the Institute of the Incarnate Word a blessing, a singular privilege to form part of this magnificent history.

3. The Virgin of Succor

Daily, in the silence and hiddenness of the cloister, the monks of the Institute of the Incarnate Word, with the profession of the evangelical counsels and their vow of Marian slavery, give themselves to the service of the Church in Güímar, so that in all hearts the name of God may be more fully praised and His Mother, the Most Holy Virgin of Succor, queen of these lands, may be loved more tenderly. Hence the devotion and trusting prayer that day after day our monks address to the Mother of God.

For a monk of the Incarnate Word who does not love the Blessed Virgin tenderly and devoutly is inconceivable. Her help is indispensable for all of us, in every field.

That is why at this point this sermon becomes a prayer in which I wish to entrust all of you, and those who will come after you, to the Blessed Virgin, with all your struggles, with all your needs, and whatever the vicissitudes of life you may be going through, that She may grant you her maternal protection and her heavenly succor. Mary is the Queen of heaven, close to God, but she is also the mother close to each one of us, who loves us and listens to us and is ready to give us her help. Always have recourse to Her, trust in Her. “If Mary protects you –said St. Bernard– you have nothing to fear; under her guidance, you will not know weariness; thanks to her favor, you will reach the goal”11.

To her intercession we also entrust the souls of those who are no longer with us today, especially the Jorge Dorta brothers, illustrious founders of this monastery, and the souls of all who with great effort collaborated so that the first contemplative monastery for men in the history of this diocese might become a reality.

And now, if you allow me, I would like to leave a final message to our dear monks, here in the Monastery of Our Lady of Succor: Know that to your prayers and to the holiness of your life this generous land of Nivaria is entrusted in a particular way, toward which you have responsibilities and duties. Contribute with the strength of your prayer to sustaining the local Church and gladden it with the fruits of loving faith. You have been called to bear witness here to the primacy of the charity of God. And as you know very well, witness means martyr, and the Church is faithful in martyrdom, in witness. Feel yourselves sharers in this mission especially when dryness and the nights of the soul make you suffer.

May the Blessed Virgin, to whom you are so devoted, console you in your daily labors and nourish your hope. Be always faithful to your vocation; jealously guard and make fruitful the gift that has been granted to you. And as the Mystical Doctor, St. John of the Cross, said: see that the Lord “has taken you as first stones; so see what you must be, for as on the strongest the others are to be founded; take advantage of this first spirit that God gives in these beginnings to take up very anew the way of perfection in all humility and detachment within and without, not with a childish spirit, but with a robust will, wanting this Christ to cost you something”12.

May Our Lady bless you, and may these first years of the monastery be only the beginning of many more anniversaries. To God and His Most Holy Mother we offer the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass in thanksgiving for the many gifts He has been pleased to bestow on His children in Güímar by planting His house here.
