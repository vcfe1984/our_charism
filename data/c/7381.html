Homily preached on the occasion of the pilgrimage of the

Religious Family to the Virgin of Valle Grande – Argentina

[Exordium] My dear all, what joy and what consolation it is to be able to celebrate this Holy Mass here with you at the feet of our Most Holy Mother.

I know that it has been no small sacrifice to get here, and I am sure that our Mother in Heaven looks with pleasure upon all the efforts you have made to come and honor her today, on the day when the Church commemorates our Mother under the title of Our Lady of the Rosary.

“To go on pilgrimage is to project oneself, or to set out on the way to Heaven, through self-denial”1. That is why this pilgrimage, although it has its penitential character, also speaks to us of the immeasurable consolation the soul will experience upon reaching Heaven and meeting, as today, our Most Holy Mother with her arms outstretched and her infinitely tender heart.

But even more: the Virgin Mary not only awaits us at the end of the road, but she herself guides us and accompanies us all along the pilgrimage.

She is always a cause of consolation and hope, because “she shines forth before her pilgrim children on this earth as a sign of sure hope and consolation until the day of the Lord comes”2. That is to say, the most pure Virgin also makes the pilgrimage with us.

Her pilgrimage began at the Annunciation, because that was the starting point of her journey toward God3. And on that journey toward God she met with many sorrows: such as the foretelling of the sword that would pierce her soul4, the tortuous paths of the exile in Egypt and of interior darkness, when she ‘did not understand’ Jesus’ attitude at the age of twelve in the temple, arriving later at the summit of her pilgrimage on Golgotha, when she sees her Son die5. That is to say, our beloved Mother is a mother who knows the sorrows of this life, and although she already enjoys the eternal reward, she does not forget her children who are still on pilgrimage, weighed down by this world. That is why ‘this valley of tears’ –as we say in that most beautiful prayer, the Salve Regina– is transformed into a valley of hope, of sweetness, of joy through her consoling presence.

This merciful Mother walks with us, because it is proper to mothers to suffer with their children and to want to share their trials, their sadness and their sufferings. And it is well known that our most tender Mother has never failed to come to the aid of any child who has invoked her help.

But we too must walk with Mary, as we committed ourselves to do when we made the fourth vow of Marian consecration. That is, to accept ‘the companionship (of Mary) and (to act according to) the model that must guide “all our intentions, actions and operations”’6, as our Constitutions say.

1. Accepting her companionship

Accepting her companionship means being constantly at home with our Mother. I mean that this walking with Mary implies having a serious spiritual life, not a sentimental one; applying oneself to prayer; being ready to pass through “the purifications of the senses and of the spirit, active and passive”7. For an “intense spiritual life” is the duty of every Christian, but it is “the first and principal duty of all religious”8.

But not only of them. In fact, St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort wrote: “I personally believe that no one can attain intimate union with Our Lord and perfect fidelity to the Holy Spirit without a very close union with the Most Blessed Virgin and a true dependence on her help”9. A filial and tender relationship with the Mother of God is, for every Christian, the privileged path to fidelity to the vocation received –whatever it may be– and a most effective help to advance in it and live it to the full, that is, to attain holiness10. “There is, then, no interior life or spiritual action possible that does not depend on her”11.

To the dear religious men and women who work in our houses of mercy, striving to show the fatherly face of God and the motherly face of the Church12: it is by being grounded in an intense spiritual life, one that sinks its roots into the mystery of the Incarnate Word and has as its fundamental pillar a filial and tender relationship with the Mother of God, that we will be able to carry out an authentic Catholic pastoral work13.

It is with Mary that one learns to found all apostolic enthusiasm14 solely on the Rock which is Christ15. Not on our poor physical strength, not on our organizational ability, not on the little or much money we may have at our disposal. For that would be to shut ourselves up within human horizons. From our Most Holy Mother, who praised the Lord for knowing herself ‘looked upon’ by Him, despite her ‘lowliness’, her littleness16, we learn that for the works of God the most important thing is to abandon oneself with full confidence to His Providence. And the fathers and sisters who work in our homes know this very well…

It is with Mary that one learns “to do the things of God promptly17. To serve one’s neighbor even in the simplest tasks”18. Everything –whatever the task may be– has a higher sanctifying efficacy if one always acts with Mary. Always keep this in mind.

2. Acting according to the Model: Mary

Now, this walking with Mary on the pilgrimage of this life also implies acting with her. Not only sometimes, not intermittently, but always. In fact, our proper law explicitly points us to “doing everything with Mary”19. And this acting with Mary in everything “is to take the Most Blessed Virgin as the perfect Model of Christian conduct”20.

Therefore, the Virgin Mary, besides being our Mother, who delights in our company, is also our Model.

The Virgin Mary is above all the Model of those theological virtues that are characteristic of the Christian: faith, hope and love21. Model of that faithful perseverance in the Gospel that allows us to travel with her “the pilgrimage of faith”. Model of an apostolic self-giving that allows us to cooperate in the spreading of the Gospel and in the growth of the Church22. Model of a life committed to God and to men, to the designs of salvation and to fidelity to her children.

Mary is also the Model of “fortitude in exile23 and in sorrow24. Sublime Model of poverty borne with dignity and trust in the Lord25. Exemplary Model of watchful care for her Son, from the humility of the crib to the ignominy of the Cross26. Model at once of virginal purity27 and of strong and chaste spousal love”28. That is why there is no Christian who cannot have this beloved Mother as Model.

Moreover, St. Paul VI affirmed that “with these virtues the children will be adorned who, with steadfast purpose, contemplate the example of their Mother in order to reproduce them in their own lives”29.

By invoking her with the words of the angel and by going over her Gospel life in the praying of the Holy Rosary, all will always have before their eyes the perfect model of the Christian30, which is Most Holy Mary.

***

[Peroration] My dear all:Behold your Mother, Christ told us before dying. I too repeat it today to all of you who have come here to venerate our most beloved Mother, the Virgin of Valle Grande. Open the doors of your souls and of your lives to the Mother of God. Accept her companionship and follow her as Model. She will teach you the paths of the Gospel. She will make you know Christ and love the Church. She will show you the way of life and encourage you in difficulties.

To all the sick and to the young people and children who are with us today: know that you are the favorites of Our Lady. Love her with tender and simple devotion and make others, too, love our Mother dearly.

On this day when the whole Church venerates the Virgin Mary under the title of Our Lady of the Rosary, I invite you, at the end of this Holy Mass, to pray the Holy Rosary as a family and for the family, here at the feet of the Mother of the Incarnate Word, who is also our Mother.

Of her we must ask, for all the members of our Religious Family, the grace of an ever greater and more convinced fidelity to our charism. Together with the grace –like her– “to strive with all our strength always to advance in virtue”31 and “to preserve and cultivate spiritual fervor and the joy of evangelizing, even when we must sow in tears”32.

My dear all: may we live today and always “through Jesus and through Mary, with Jesus and with Mary, in Jesus and in Mary, for Jesus and for Mary”33.

May Our Lady bless you.
