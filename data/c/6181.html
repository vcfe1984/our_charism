Good Night Talk for the Monks of the Monastery “St. Michael the Archangel” – Brazil

Contemplatives of the Eucharist1

[Exordium] First of all, thank you very much for the invitation and the warm welcome. It is a pleasure to be here. How much this place has grown! Congratulations!

I have titled this good night talk Contemplatives of the Eucharist, which is a phrase from the Directory of Contemplative Life. For if there is something that must characterize our monks, it is precisely that adoration of the Incarnate Word present truly, really and substantially in the Eucharist2, where one grows in familiarity with Him3 day after day. And while it is true that this Eucharistic imprint is common to all the members of the Institute, the monks are (or must be) the adorers par excellence in our Institute.

Now, I would like to point out that the ‘of’ in the phrase has a double meaning. On the one hand, it refers to contemplating the Eucharist, to being devoted to it as the holiest act we can perform4. But, on the other hand, the preposition can be interpreted as belonging or possession5. The contemplative becomes so attached to Christ in the Eucharist, goes about the monastery so recollected “so that dissipation may find no incentive”6, that whatever he does, his soul remains captivated by Christ, to the point that with great reason it can be said that this contemplative belongs to the Eucharist: Diego belongs to the Eucharist, José belongs to the Eucharist… And in this way he fulfills what our Constitutions say in number 209 (pay attention): “Jesus Christ, Son of God and of the Blessed Virgin, must be the center of all spiritual life: No one comes to the Father except through Me7. In the church, in the cloister, in the cell, in the refectory, in recreation, they must go about sighing for Jesus Christ. The song and the laughter of the members of our Religious Family must be to weep for Jesus Christ. This is their office. The sweeter the melody they sing in choir, the better they will manifest the deep and interior sigh of their souls longing for Jesus Christ. The memory and remembrance of Him must never depart from their hearts. With this, sleep must come, and of this they must dream while sleeping. The heart always melted in love of Him, and the memory occupied with nothing other than Him.” Interesting, isn’t it?

How is this achieved? How do we bring it about that the memory and remembrance of Christ never depart from our hearts? By contemplating Christ in the Eucharist. By uniting oneself to the Eucharistic Christ. That is where the soul becomes steeped in the very sentiments of Christ, where it drinks of the divine Wisdom that God whispers in the silence of the soul, where it finds the Friend, to embrace Him and never let Him go.

That is our noble office, says our Proper Law, but especially it is that of the monk of the Incarnate Word. You have been called to the service of the Most Blessed Sacrament, to serve the adorable person of Jesus Christ in the Most Blessed Sacrament.

But the monk of the Incarnate Word must not be content with that alone; rather, carried along by a boundless love for Christ and by zeal for his glory, he must also strive to make all men love, adore and serve Him8, and that everywhere a throne may be raised to Him and faithful adorers gathered for Him9. This is so because if the monk “truly seeks God10, he must necessarily share in the ardent love of Christ for souls”11; he cannot forget his neighbor12. And when we say neighbor, we do not refer only to the thousands of people in the world who do not yet know Christ, but very especially to the members of the Institute, “as the order of charity requires, according to which one must love –affectively and effectively– those who are closest more”13. And here, if you will allow me, I want to give you a threefold charge:

Love the Congregation with a filial love; always defend it. Pray much for the Congregation when you contemplate the Incarnate Word in the Eucharist. Consider “that the graces a Congregation gives are never repaid as they deserve: we are always debtors to our mother. Each day’s labors are a small thing beside the debt, which is immense”14. To look no further, think of the immense gift God has given us in bringing us to this Religious Family to consecrate ourselves to Him, with all the means it gives us for our sanctification… In short, for all that the Congregation gives us, the only thing it asks of us in return –so to speak– is that we be faithful to its rule, to the charism, to the patrimony that has been bequeathed to us, to its sound traditions. St. Peter Julian Eymard told his own: “Keep, then, your rule, in order to preserve the life of your mother the Congregation”15. It is the same message I want to repeat to you. Just as in the Middle Ages, when the monasteries were the place where the cultural riches of the past were preserved16, so too may you today be the true guardians of the spirit17 of our Institute. Be the faithful escort of the Incarnate Word. Notice that our Constitutions say that the spirit of our Religious Family, of our Institute, is identified with the Holy Spirit18. Now, that spirit is manifested or lives in a body, that is, through us. If we separate ourselves from that spirit, in some way we are departing from God (the Holy Spirit), from what He wants for us. And what happens to a body when it is separated from the spirit (from its soul)? It dies. That is why it is very important not only that we know this spirit well, but that we live it, that we do not depart from the spirit of our Institute in a single point, and that we know how to defend it with clear arguments, with courageous witness, and even with a look.

Help our Congregation to expand. How? Well, the best way to do it –which is at the same time the best tribute of honor you can give to Christ in the Eucharist– is the loving practice of the rule. The faithful fulfillment of what is commanded of us not only greatly consoles Christ, but contributes to the prosperity of our Congregation and draws new members to it. And it is not enough to do what we are commanded; we must also perfect ourselves in what falls to us to do. The Congregation will be judged by us. If we are lax, if we have no apostolic zeal, if we are too bureaucratic or, on the contrary, totally disorganized, if we do not study seriously, if we chase after every novelty that comes along, people will think that this is what the Congregation is like. Conduct yourselves in such a way that those who see you may say: “there is a true religious of the Incarnate Word.”

Lastly, always be generous and available19 to achieve the end of the Institute, that is, the inculturation of the Gospel. May being contemplatives of the Eucharist lead you not to shy away from the missionary adventure, that is, not to refuse a priori this or that mission, to be capable of offering yourselves personally to collaborate in whatever is needed for the good of the Institute and the cause of Christ.

[Peroratio] St. Peter Julian Eymard asked some religious while preaching the Spiritual Exercises to them: “What will make you saints? The rule, your religious rule, the rule of the congregation to which you belong. For you, holiness comes down to practicing it, because the rule is the will of God. […] What [God] wants to see in you is perfect religious of this Institute, and its living incarnation. In heaven you will be crowned, not as holy men, but as holy religious”20. And we could add: as holy religious of the Institute of the Incarnate Word.

We cannot become saints except according to what God inspired in our Founder, which is set down in the Constitutions and in the Directories; you know that very well. If any of you says that he loves Christ very much but does not love the Congregation, he is lying. Because, as our Proper Law says: a true religious keeps fidelity and shows a great love not only when he appreciates the gift of religious life but also when he loves and defends his own Institute, which includes in a particular way the founder and the Constitutions. Read it yourselves in the Directory of Consecrated Life21.

That is why we must be forewarned, for there will be no lack of those who believe themselves called to reform the Founder and to do better than he did; but God blesses only the one he has chosen to found, and never those who oppose him22. We must be prepared for the hardest trials, and for that we must be firmly planted on the Rock that is Christ23, who lives among us in the Eucharist, from where every day He tells us: I am with you always, until the end of the age24.

With the hymn to Our Lady, we ask Her to intercede for all of us, for the Institute, and I personally ask her to bless you always and to make you very holy.
