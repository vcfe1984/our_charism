«But who do you say that I am?»

Recalling the thought of our Founder
During the Novena of the Annunciation

On the third day of the novena, on which we pray specifically for our faith in Jesus Christ, true God, we call to mind Fr. Buela’s thought on the subject, taken from his book “El Arte del Padre.”

«But who do you say that I am?»

Very different is the impression made on us by the judgments about Jesus pronounced by those who were His Apostles and disciples, and by their successors. Here everything is unity, clarity, joyful certainty, truth, homogeneous development, consideration of all aspects without silencing or denying any of them. It is the calm sea of faith, the clear sky of evangelical simplicity, the firmness of the rock, the solidity of good foundations; in short, light after darkness.

– «This is the Son of God» (Jn 1:34), confesses St. John the Baptist, and he evidently means a sonship by nature, because if it were by mere adoption it would express nothing singular, since all the Jews knew themselves to be adopted children of God.

– «The beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, Son of God», St. Mark sets down at the start of his account (1:1).

– St. John begins his Gospel directly by affirming: «In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God and the Word was God», by which he affirms the preexistence of the Second Person of the Most Holy Trinity, His distinction from the Father and His divinity. And in his first epistle he declares that Jesus Christ «is the true God and eternal Life» (5:20).

– St. Thomas the apostle, falling prostrate, adores Him, saying: «My Lord and my God» (Jn 20:28).

– «I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God» (Jn 11:27), exclaims St. Martha.

– St. Paul speaks to us of his «hope in the glorious manifestation of our great God and Savior, Christ Jesus» (Titus 2:13), of that Christ «who is above all things, God blessed forever» (Rom 9:5)77.

Let us now turn to the Prince of the Apostles, as the Lord Himself did: «Who do you say that I am?» St. Peter answers: «You are the Christ, the Son of the living God» (Mt 16:16). Glorious confession, in which the Catholic Church begins and on which she is built!

And what do the successors of Peter and Vicars of Christ tell us? The Fathers gathered at the First Ecumenical Council, at Nicaea, in the year 325, proclaimed the faith of the Church «in one Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God… God from God and Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten, not made, consubstantial with the Father (homooúsios tõ Patri), through whom all things were made…» (Dz. 54). As Hermann Josef Sieben says: «No Council, either before or since, has made, even remotely, a dogmatic decision so fundamental and so important in its consequences».

The «homooúsios» is the key word, the term a thousand times blessed that should sound to us like heavenly music, the watchword of Catholic orthodoxy forever and ever. It expresses the unity of substance, numerically one, between the Father and the Son, and therefore that the one is as much God as the other.

[…] And leaping across the centuries we come to St. Paul VI, in his Solemn Profession of Faith, on June 30, 1968, who once again confesses the now twice-millennial faith of the Catholic Church in the divinity of Jesus Christ: «We believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God. He is the eternal Word, born of the Father before all ages and consubstantial with the Father, or homooúsios tõ Patri, through whom all things were made. And He became incarnate by the work of the Holy Spirit, of Mary the Virgin, and was made man: equal, therefore, to the Father according to the divinity, less than the Father according to the humanity, entirely one, not by confusion (which cannot be) of substance, but by unity of person».

[…]

The Catechism of the Catholic Church, promulgated by St. John Paul II on October 11, 1992, also affirms clearly and unequivocally faith in the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, as well as in His sacred humanity and in the mystery of the hypostatic union. This is the faith of the Church!

The same Great Pope approved «with certain knowledge and by his apostolic authority», in the heart of the Year of the Great Jubilee, a fundamental document on the Christological faith of the Church and the salvific unicity and universality of Jesus Christ, issued by the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith: the declaration Dominus Iesus. Against many present-day errors this great document also reaffirms faith in the definitive and complete character of God’s revelation in Jesus Christ; the personal unity between the eternal Word and Jesus of Nazareth; the unity between the economy of the Incarnate Word and the action of the Holy Spirit; the salvific unicity and universality of the mystery of Jesus Christ; etc…

Hence we, counting on the backing of the ecclesiastical Magisterium of all times, in spite of our nothingness and sin, confess with all the strength of our soul and of our heart the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, who is «one single person in two natures» (Dz. 429), and we do so not without a certain belligerence, «as if giving heresy a wet willy,» as Ignacio B. Anzoátegui put it, with the holy pride of the children of God who know that «flesh and blood did not reveal this to them, but the Father who is in heaven» (Mt 16:17), and that it is a truth by which we live and for which we are ready to die. [We sing to the Virgin, asking for this grace.]
