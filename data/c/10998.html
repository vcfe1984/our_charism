Feast of Saints Philip and James, apostles. Philip, who, like Peter and Andrew, had been born in Bethsaida and was a disciple of John the Baptist, was called by the Lord to follow him. James, for his part, was the son of Alphaeus, surnamed “the Just,” regarded in the West as the kinsman of the Lord; he was the first to govern the Church of Jerusalem, and when the controversy over circumcision arose, he sided with the opinion of Peter, so that that ancient yoke would not be imposed on the disciples who had come from the Gentiles. Very soon he crowned his apostolate with martyrdom (1st c.).

Proper Law

Directory of Spirituality

  1. Ecclesial communion is at once invisible and visible. As an invisible reality, it is the communion of each man, on the one hand, with the Father through Christ in the Holy Spirit and, on the other, with other men, who are also sharers in sanctifying grace, in the Passion of Christ, in the same faith, in the same Spirit. All these realities are intimately related to the visible communion founded on the teaching of the Apostles, on the sacraments and on the hierarchical order. This relationship between the visible and the invisible in ecclesial communion is constitutive of the Church as “universal Sacrament of salvation”
